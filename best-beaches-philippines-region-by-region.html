Best Beaches in the Philippines, Region by Region

Ren Calago 28 October 20260

If you only want the shortlist: Saud Beach and the Calaguas Islands in Luzon, Boracay, Bantayan and Siquijor in the Visayas, Port Barton and Balabac in Palawan, and the island-hopping circuit off Siargao plus White Island in Camiguin for Mindanao. Each one earns its place for a different reason, and the best of them depends heavily on the month you travel.


That last point catches a lot of first-time visitors. The Philippines has two monsoons that hit opposite coasts, so a beach that is glassy in March can be churning in August. Picking a region first, then a beach, saves you from booking a west-facing island in the middle of the southwest monsoon.


Below, each region gets a well-known headline beach and at least one quieter partner nearby. The idea is simple: go for the famous one if you like, but add the second stop, because that is usually where the trip slows down in a good way.




Luzon: The Far North and the Bicol Islands


Luzon's best sand sits a long way from Manila. Plan on a full travel day for most of the picks below, and treat the beaches near the capital as weekend fillers rather than the main event.


Saud Beach, Pagudpud (Ilocos Norte)


Saud is a broad curve of pale sand at the northwestern tip of Luzon, with far less building along it than the big resort strips further south. Vans and buses from Laoag City cover the 70 to 80 kilometres in a couple of hours. Swimming is gentle in the dry months; once the monsoon winds arrive, the surf picks up and the beach is better for walking than wading.


Palaui Island and Anguib Beach (Cagayan)


Off Santa Ana, Palaui is a protected island with grassy hills, quiet coves and the Cape Engaño lighthouse. There are no resorts on it, so you arrive by boat from San Vicente port and plan around the tide and the boatman's schedule. Anguib Beach, also in Santa Ana, is a softer option on the mainland side with white sand and very little development. Cagayan faces typhoons more often than most provinces, so keep a spare day in your plan.


Calaguas Islands (Camarines Norte)


Calaguas is the classic off-grid beach trip in Bicol: a long white shore, clear water and almost nothing built on it. Boats leave from Vinzons or Paracale, and most visitors camp, so bring a tent, water and a power bank. Boat fees and camping rules change, so message the municipal tourism office before you set off, and skip weekends if you would rather share the cove with fewer groups.


Near Manila: Batangas and Zambales


For a short break, Batangas has Calatagan, Laiya and Nasugbu, all reachable by car in a few hours. In Zambales, Anawangin's pine-backed cove is a favourite overnight camp. Know before you go that the sand there is grey from volcanic ash, so it photographs well but does not match the white beaches further south.


Western Visayas: Boracay and Its Quieter Neighbours


Boracay's White Beach in Aklan remains the country's benchmark for fine sand and long sunset walks, and the island is run under tighter rules than it once was, with limits on beach parties and smoking. Station 1 and Station 2 give you the best swimming; Station 3 is where cheaper rooms tend to be. For more space on the same island, Puka Shell Beach on the northern tip trades the powder for coarse shell fragments and a lot fewer people.


If Boracay feels too busy, Iloilo's Gigantes Islands offer white sand framed by karst rock, with day tours leaving from Carles and usually including a seafood lunch. In Antique, Mararison and Nogas islands make easy day trips with almost no crowds and very few facilities, so pack food and shade.


Central Visayas: Cebu, Negros Oriental and Siquijor


Cebu's best sand is off the main island. Bantayan, in the far north, is flat and unhurried, with wide beaches and resorts at sensible prices. Malapascua has small, pretty coves and is better known for its thresher shark dives. On the Camotes Islands, the islet of Tulang Diot off Pacijan has a white sandbar that shows itself at low tide; it has very little shade, so bring water and a hat.


Negros Oriental works on two very different beaches. Apo Island, off Dauin, is a community-run marine sanctuary where you come for turtles and coral, not for lounging, since parts of the shore are pebbly. Manjuyod White Sandbar in Bais City is the reverse: a long ribbon of fine sand that appears only when the tide drops, about 15 minutes by boat from Capiñahan Wharf, with stilt cottages on it and dolphin watching in the Tañon Strait on the way out.


Siquijor rounds out the region. Paliton Beach on the west side has soft sand and some of the island's best sunsets. Salagdoong, in the northeast, is busier and built for cliff jumping. For a quieter swim, Kagusuan Beach in Maria sits below the main road on the southeast coast and stays calm even in the peak months.


Eastern Visayas: Sandbars and Open-Ocean Swell


This side of the Visayas gets fewer visitors and rewards the ones who make the effort. Kalanggaman's long double sandbar, off Palompon in Leyte, is the region's postcard, with sand stretching from both ends of a small wooded island. Canigao Island in Matalom, southern Leyte, is smaller and easier to fit into a day. Surfers can look to Calicoan Island in Guiuan, Eastern Samar, which faces the Pacific and sees far fewer boards in the water than Siargao.


Palawan and Romblon


El Nido and Coron are the names everyone knows, El Nido for its limestone cliffs and lagoon tours, Coron for lakes and wreck dives. If you want white sand without joining the boat traffic, Port Barton's White Beach is the calmer pick: swim in the morning, grilled fish at night, and very little light after dark.


At the southern end of the province, Balabac has shallow turquoise sandbars and almost no one on them. Getting there takes planning, with boats out of Rio Tuba or Buliluyan and permits arranged ahead, so this suits a longer trip rather than a weekend. Further north, Romblon Island is slow, clear-watered and easy to like. Bonbon Beach there links to a small islet by a sandbar at low tide.


Mindanao: Siargao, Camiguin and the Davao Gulf


Siargao is best known for the surf at Cloud 9 in General Luna, yet non-surfers spend most of their time on the island-hopping run to Daku, Guyam and Naked Island. Daku has coconut groves and lunch stalls, Guyam is a tiny round island made for swimming, and Naked Island is a bare strip of sand with nothing on it. On the east side, the Magpupungko rock pools in Pilar fill at low tide, so check the tide table before you ride over.


Camiguin, the small volcanic island off northern Mindanao, has two offshore picks. White Island is an uninhabited sandbar off Mambajao that changes shape with the tide and is best before the late-morning wind. Mantigue Island has soft coral sand and decent snorkelling on its edges. Neither has overnight stays, so both work as day trips by local boat.


Around Davao, Samal Island has white-sand resorts a short ferry ride across the gulf, and Dahican Beach in Mati, Davao Oriental, is a long stretch known for skimboarding and nesting sea turtles. Further south, Gumasa Beach in Glan, Sarangani, has a long run of white sand on the Celebes Sea. Advisories differ by province in Mindanao, so stay within established tourist areas and read the current guidance before booking.


Matching the Beach to the Month


The northeast monsoon, or amihan, runs roughly from November to May and keeps the western coasts calm: Boracay's White Beach, Palawan, Zambales and Ilocos are all at their best then. The southwest monsoon, or habagat, takes over from June to October and turns the west choppy, while the eastern coasts get their turn. Siargao's main surf season lands around August to November, and Baler on Luzon's east coast is another option in those months.


A rough rule for budgets and crowds: the more boat transfers a beach needs, the fewer people you will share it with and the more the transport costs. Islands with their own airports, such as Boracay, Siargao and Camiguin, save travel time but charge more for rooms. Places reached by bus and small boat, like Calaguas and Gigantes, stay cheaper and quieter.


One Region, Two Beaches


The easiest way to plan is to pick one region, stay longer than you think you need, and pair the famous beach with a quiet one close by: Boracay with Antique, Siargao with a free day on the islets, Dumaguete with Siquijor or the Bais sandbar, El Nido with Port Barton. You spend less time in transit and more time in the water, and you see a side of the coast most itineraries skip.
