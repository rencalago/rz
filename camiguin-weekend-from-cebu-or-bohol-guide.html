Camiguin Weekend From Cebu or Bohol: Routes and Costs

Ren Calago 16 October 2026

Camiguin is a small volcanic island off the north coast of Mindanao, and a weekend there is realistic from Cebu or Bohol, but only if you let the transport set the plan. The quick route is a Cebu Pacific flight of about an hour. The cheaper route goes through Bohol, where the ferry from Jagna sails only on Mondays, Wednesdays and Fridays. Pick the route first, then build the days around it.


Camiguin's ring road is about 64 kilometres around, enough to drive in roughly an hour without stops, so two days cover the headline sights comfortably. The harder part is getting on and off the island on the days you have free.


If you are reading this in mid-October, note the timing. The island's 47th Lanzones Festival runs 18 to 25 October in Mambajao, with a parade on opening day, nightly concerts and a drone light show billed as Camiguin's first. Room rates climb during festival week, and typhoon risk in October and November can disrupt both flights and ferries, so book changeable tickets.




Fly From Cebu


Cebgo, Cebu Pacific's turboprop arm, flies Cebu to Camiguin in under an hour, with one or two flights a day depending on the day. Fares swing widely. Seat-sale tickets start around ₱1,200 to ₱1,900 one way, while ordinary and peak fares can reach ₱3,000 or more. From Camiguin Airport in Mambajao, a tricycle to town costs from about ₱50.


For a Friday-to-Sunday trip this is the simplest option. Fly out Friday, fly back Sunday, and you have two full days on the ground. Because the schedule is thin, a cancelled flight can strand you, so avoid pairing it with a tight onward connection on Monday morning.


Go Through Bohol


The overland route is cheaper and more of a journey. From Cebu City, take a fastcraft to Tagbilaran, about two hours, then a bus to Jagna, about two more at roughly ₱100 to ₱150. Super Shuttle's RoRo leaves Jagna for Balbagon in Camiguin at 1:00 PM on Mondays, Wednesdays and Fridays and takes about four and a half hours. The return sails from Balbagon at 8:00 AM on the same days. Economy runs roughly ₱800 to ₱1,000 and tourist class costs more, with a fuel surcharge on top at times. Tickets are usually sold at the port, so ask about advance booking through the operator.


Those days shape the weekend. Leave Cebu on the earliest fastcraft on a Friday to make the afternoon sailing, spend Saturday and Sunday on the island, and take the Monday 8:00 AM boat back, which reaches Jagna early afternoon. Sailings can be cancelled in rough weather, and the crossing can be choppy from November to February, so keep Monday flexible. If the days do not line up with your plans, a Bohol stop on the way works well, as in our Bohol day trip from Cebu or the Balicasag and Virgin Island guide.


A third route exists from the Mindanao side: the Balingoan to Benoni RoRo crosses in about an hour with sailings through the day, and a foot passenger pays about ₱200 to ₱360. It is useful if you are already in Cagayan de Oro, less so from the Visayas.


A Two-Day Plan on the Island


  • Day one, evening: Check in, then catch the sunset at the Sunken Cemetery, a roadside viewpoint over the marker cross that stands in the sea where a village sank after the 1871 eruption. It is free to view, and boats can be hired for snorkelling. Finish at Ardent Hot Springs, warm rather than scalding and best in the evening, with an entry fee of about ₱50.
  • Day two, morning: Visit Katibawasan Falls, about 70 metres tall, for roughly ₱75, then White Island, a sandbar reached by a 10 to 15-minute banca ride from Yumbing. Go at low tide. A boat costs about ₱550 for up to six people, plus an environmental fee of ₱50 to ₱75 each, and there is no shade or shop on the sand.
  • Day two, afternoon: Walk the steep Old Volcano trail past the Stations of the Cross, then cool off at Tuasan Falls or Sto. Niño Cold Spring.

Mt. Hibok-Hibok, the island's active volcano at 1,332 metres, needs an accredited guide and a permit, so it is a poor fit for a short weekend.


What It Costs


Island costs are modest. A scooter rents for about ₱350 to ₱450 a day, a shared multicab across the island costs about ₱25, and a carinderia meal runs ₱80 to ₱150. Rooms range from ₱800 to ₱1,500 for a basic one to ₱2,800 to ₱5,000 at a mid-range beach resort. A basic three-day trip with shared costs can come in around ₱5,500 per person before the trip to the island. Prices shift without notice, so treat these as a guide and confirm on the spot.


Practical Things to Sort Before You Go


  • Visitor registration: Camiguin's Smart Tourism Ordinance has required visitors to get a QR code before entering through its airport and seaports, and the rule was reinstated in 2024 after a court order. Ask the Provincial Tourism Office how to register for the current requirement, and do it in advance to avoid a queue.
  • Cash: ATMs are in Mambajao only and can run dry, and cards are patchy outside town.
  • Fuel: Fill the scooter in town, since stations are scarce on the ring road.
  • No ride-hailing: Agree tricycle and habal-habal fares before you ride.
  • Best months: February to May brings the driest weather and calmest seas. For how the calendar works for the wider region, see our look at October to December peak season in Cebu and Bohol.

Is a Weekend Enough?


It is enough for the headline sights if you fly, and tight but doable by ferry. The price of the ferry route is time: you lose most of Friday and part of Monday to travel. If your dates are fixed and the ferry days do not match, fly. If you have a long weekend and like the idea of seeing Bohol on the way, the ferry is the more interesting trip.
