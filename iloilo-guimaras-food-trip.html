Iloilo and Guimaras Food Trip: What to Eat and How to Go

Ren Calago 18 October 2026

Iloilo is the rare Philippine city where the famous dishes sit a short ride apart, and where a quick boat ride puts you on an island known for one fruit. A food trip here is less about a long list of restaurants and more about a handful of bowls, each tied to a particular district, and then a sweet detour across the strait to Guimaras.


The city earned formal recognition for it. Iloilo was named a UNESCO Creative City of Gastronomy on 31 October 2023, the first city in the Philippines to receive that title for food. In practice that means you can eat your way across the city on foot, by jeepney or by a short Grab ride without planning much.


This guide covers what to order and where each dish belongs, how to cross to Guimaras, and how to reach Iloilo from Cebu. Prices and schedules change often, so treat every figure as a rough guide.




La Paz Batchoy: Start in La Paz


Batchoy is the dish most people name first, and the place to eat it is the district it takes its name from. A bowl combines egg noodles in a pork-based broth with pork offal, crushed chicharon and often a raw egg stirred in at the table. The flavour is savoury and a little rich, which is why it works as a breakfast for locals and as a late snack for everyone else.


Several names come up again and again. Netong's sits by the La Paz public market on Rizal Street and is usually eaten with a piece of puto. Ted's Oldtimer, on the same street, traces itself back to 1945 and offers noodle choices and a beef version. Popoy's runs from inside the market with long shared tables, and Deco's, on Mabini Street in the city proper, is often credited with the dish's origins. Bowls are generally priced for everyday eating, but check the menu board before you order, as add-ons like marrow or extra egg raise the bill.


Go earlier rather than later. The market stalls are livelier in the morning, and by afternoon some of the smaller counters have already sold out.


Pancit Molo: Dumplings in Clear Broth


Pancit molo has no noodles in the usual sense. It is a clear broth with pork-filled dumplings, usually with shredded chicken, and it comes from Molo, a district that began as a Chinese community in the seventeenth century. The soup is mild, so it makes a good contrast after batchoy rather than a second heavy bowl.


Eat it while the wrappers are still firm, and skip heavy chili sauce, which drowns a delicate broth. If you are short on time, many Ilonggo restaurants list it as a starter, but a stop in Molo itself gives you the home-style version. Pair it with a walk around the old church area, which is a calm break between meals.


KBL and the Sour Side of Ilonggo Cooking


Ilonggo cooking leans sour more than most travelers expect. KBL combines kadyos (pigeon peas), baboy (pork) and langka (jackfruit) in a broth soured with batwan, a local fruit, and it is tied to Jaro. Kansi, a beef shank soup soured the same way, and laswa, a plain vegetable soup, round out the comfort-food side of the menu.


If you only try one sour dish, make it KBL. The sourness cuts through grilled meats and fried food, which is why it pairs so well with chicken inasal at dinner. For the second half of the day, look for a restaurant that serves traditional Ilonggo dishes in one place rather than chasing separate stalls.


Sweets and Pasalubong


Iloilo is also a city of bakeries. Biscocho, a twice-baked, buttered and sugared bread, is the best-known take-home snack, and barquillos, thin rolled wafers, are another. Buy a small pack first to test the sweetness before committing to a large box, since styles differ between bakeries. Hot chocolate made from tablea, with a simple biscuit or puto on the side, is a good reason to slow down in the afternoon.


If you are headed home by air, pack pasalubong in your checked bag where possible, since carry-on limits on low-cost flights are tight.


Crossing to Guimaras


Guimaras is a short hop across the Iloilo Strait, and the quickest way over is by pumpboat from the wharf in Iloilo City. Boats are listed from both Ortiz Street and Parola, and they land at Jordan Wharf in Jordan, the island's main gateway. The crossing takes about 15 to 20 minutes. One operator listing shows departures roughly every 30 minutes from early morning into the evening, with a regular fare around PHP 40 and a slightly higher night rate. Fuel surcharges can push fares above the published price, so budget a little extra and carry cash.


A RORO ferry also links Iloilo with the port at Hoskyn in Jordan, which suits travelers with a vehicle. For foot passengers, the pumpboat is simpler. Weather can delay or adjust sailings, so keep your return plan flexible and avoid leaving the last boat as your only option.


What to Eat in Guimaras


Guimaras is known for its mangoes, and the island's restaurants build menus around them. The best-known stop is The Pitstop Restaurant in San Miguel, Jordan, a few blocks from the provincial capitol. Its mango pizza is a layer of melted cheese and ripe mango on a crust, topped with chopped cashews and thin slices of bell pepper. Travelers have compared the taste to a sweet-and-tangy mango pie. The restaurant describes its pizza as the only one of its kind, which is its own claim rather than something verified independently.


The same menu includes mango adobo, mango beef bulalo and a mango sisig. A pizza has been listed in the low-to-mid hundreds of pesos depending on size, but those prices come from earlier reviews, so expect them to have moved. Mango dishes taste best in the mango season, generally the hotter months of the year, so ask before you go if you are visiting at another time.


For take-home snacks, the Trappist abbey on the island, Our Lady of the Philippines, sells mango products, including jams and dried mango. Check opening days before you go, since monastery shops keep their own hours.


Getting to Iloilo From Cebu


By air


The direct flight from Mactan-Cebu to Iloilo takes about 45 minutes to an hour. Philippine Airlines lists the route, and other carriers may also fly it, so compare before booking. Round-trip fares have been listed from about PHP 1,795 at the low end, and more for holiday weekends and festival dates. Iloilo's airport is out of town, roughly 45 to 60 minutes from the city center by taxi or van, so factor that into a same-day plan.


By sea


An overnight ferry between Cebu and Iloilo has been listed at around 12 hours, with a very limited number of sailings per week, so confirm the day directly with the shipping line. It only makes sense if you have time to spare or want to bring a vehicle. For most travelers the flight is the practical choice.


A Sample Plan: One Day or Two


If you have two days


On the first day, eat batchoy in La Paz in the morning, take pancit molo in Molo before lunch, and spend the afternoon on sweets and tablea. Finish with KBL and inasal for dinner. On the second day, cross to Guimaras early, have mango pizza for a late lunch, visit the abbey shop, and take a boat back before the evening rush.


If you have one day


Pick two bowls rather than five. Batchoy plus either pancit molo or KBL covers the city well, and then you can still make the Guimaras crossing before the afternoon. Keep a buffer before any flight home, because the road to the airport takes close to an hour.


Practical Tips


Carry cash for markets, pumpboats and tricycles on Guimaras. Wear light clothes, since the city is hot and humid. If your stomach is sensitive, start with cooked soups and grilled food and drink bottled water. And if you are comparing regional food trips, our guide to Cebu's best local food beyond lechon is a good counterpart, while Bacolod's MassKara weekend shows what another Western Visayas city brings to the table. If you want a beach add-on after Iloilo, see how the flight-and-jetty route to Boracay works.


So, Which Do You Start With?


Start in the city and finish on the island. Iloilo gives you the savoury and sour end of the table, and Guimaras gives you the sweet end, with one short boat ride between them. That contrast is the whole trip, and it fits comfortably into two days from Cebu.
