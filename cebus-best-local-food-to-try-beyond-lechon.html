Cebu's Best Local Food to Try: Beyond Lechon

Ren Calago 29 September 2026

Cebu is famous for many things, but food is definitely near the top of the list. Ask someone what to eat in Cebu and lechon will probably be the first answer. The city's roasted pork has earned a reputation across the Philippines and beyond. But if lechon is the dish that introduces you to Cebuano food, it should not be the dish where your food trip ends.


Cebu has a much wider food culture built around street stalls, neighborhood eateries, seafood, grilled food and simple meals that locals have enjoyed for years. If you want to explore Cebu beyond the famous roast pig, here are some local favorites worth looking for.



Puso: The Perfect Partner to Cebuano Food


Puso, or hanging rice, is one of the simplest and most recognizable parts of eating in Cebu. Rice is wrapped in woven coconut leaves and cooked inside the pouch, creating a compact serving that is easy to carry and eat.


You will often find puso beside grilled meat, barbecue, seafood and other street food. It is not necessarily the star of the meal, but it is one of the things that makes a Cebu food experience feel distinctly local.


Ngohiong: A Cebu Favorite for Merienda


Ngohiong is another food that deserves attention. It is a crispy fried roll with a seasoned filling and is commonly served with a dipping sauce. The dish has Chinese culinary influence, but it has become strongly associated with Cebuano street food.


It is especially good for a quick merienda or an inexpensive snack while exploring the city. Order a few pieces, add some puso, and you already have a simple Cebuano-style meal.


Sutukil: Three Ways to Enjoy Seafood


If you love seafood, sutukil should be on your list. The name comes from three cooking styles: sugba, or grilled; tuwa, referring to a soup or stew; and kilaw, where fresh seafood is cured using vinegar and other ingredients.


The appeal of sutukil is that one seafood meal can give you several different flavors and preparations. It is especially enjoyable when shared with family or friends, making it a natural choice for a relaxed Cebu food trip.


Tuslob Buwa for Something Different


For a more unusual Cebuano food experience, try tuslob buwa. The name roughly refers to dipping into a bubbling mixture, traditionally served with puso. The dish is strongly associated with Cebu City and has roots in communities such as Pasil and Suba.


Tuslob buwa is not necessarily for everyone, but that is part of what makes it interesting. It is less about a polished restaurant presentation and more about experiencing a food tradition that developed within Cebu's local communities.


Siomai, Barbecue and Everyday Cebu Food


Cebu's food scene also includes plenty of everyday favorites. Siomai, particularly the style associated with Tisa, has become a popular inexpensive snack. Barbecue stalls offer grilled pork, chicken, liver and other choices, usually enjoyed with puso and a vinegar-based dipping sauce.


These foods may not have the same international recognition as lechon, but they are an important part of how people actually eat around the city. They are affordable, casual and easy to find while exploring different neighborhoods.


Don't Forget Cebu's Pasalubong


A Cebu food trip does not have to end when you leave the restaurant. Dried mangoes, dried fish such as danggit, otap and other local snacks are popular choices to bring home. Our guide to what to buy in Cebu covers where to find them and how to pack them for a flight.


These are particularly useful if you want to share a little bit of Cebu with family and friends after your trip. They also show another side of the city's food culture: not every Cebuano specialty needs to be eaten immediately.

A Cebu Food Trip Is Bigger Than Lechon


Lechon may be Cebu's most famous food, but it is only one part of the story. Puso, ngohiong, sutukil, tuslob buwa, barbecue, siomai and local pasalubong all add something different to the experience.


If you are visiting Cebu for the first time, make room for more than one famous dish. Walk through local food areas, try something you have never ordered before and do not be afraid of the smaller stalls. Some of the most memorable Cebuano meals are the simple ones.


Because ultimately, the best way to understand Cebu's food is not just to taste its most famous dish. It is to discover what people eat when there is no special occasion at all.
