Davao City Itinerary: How to Spend 3 Days

Ren Calago 1 November 20260

Three days is a good fit for Davao City. You get one day downtown for parks, museums and the night market, one day crossing to Samal for sea and caves, and one day in the cooler uplands of Calinan for the Philippine Eagle Center. The city is spread out, but each of those days stays inside one area, so you are not zigzagging across town in traffic.


This plan assumes you arrive the evening before Day 1 or early that morning, and that you stay in or near the downtown area around Poblacion, Bajada or Lanang. It keeps the pace relaxed, with time to eat well, which in Davao is half the point of going.


Mount Apo is left out on purpose. The climb needs permits, guides and several days of its own, so treat it as a separate trip. If you only have a weekend, do Day 1 and Day 2 and save the uplands for next time.

Day 1: Downtown Davao on foot


Start at People's Park, a four-hectare green space built on the old PTA Grounds. Mornings are the best time, before the heat builds. Look for the Kublai Millan sculptures of indigenous people around the paths and the durian-shaped dome that serves as its visitors' centre. The National Museum of the Philippines–Davao is inside the same compound and usually opens from 9 a.m. to 5 p.m. Entry is free, but bring an ID.


From there, take a short ride toward City Hall. Museo Dabawenyo, the city's own museum, covers Davao's tribes and its growth from a small settlement into a large city, and it is a good way to make sense of what you saw in the park. San Pedro Cathedral, the city's oldest parish, is a few minutes away. Check museum hours before you go, since government museums are often closed on Mondays and holidays.


Davao afternoons are hot, so slow down after lunch. Rest at your hotel or find a café and save your energy for the evening.


At night, head to the Roxas Night Market on Roxas Avenue. It is the easiest place to sample Davao street food in one stroll, with grilled skewers, fried snacks, fresh fruit and stalls selling secondhand clothes. Bring small bills, and keep your phone and wallet in a front pocket in the crowd.



Day 2: A full day on Samal


The Davao–Samal bridge is still being built, with completion targeted for 2028, so for now the way across is by boat. From downtown, get a Grab or taxi to Sasa Wharf in the north of the city and take a passenger ferry to Babak on Samal. The crossing takes only minutes, though queues build on weekends and holidays. If you are bringing a car, the barges also take vehicles.


Hire a tricycle or habal-habal for the day once you land, and agree on the route and the price before you set off. A good loop covers the Monfort Bat Sanctuary, home to a huge colony of fruit bats, and Vanishing Island, a sandbar that shrinks as the tide comes in. In the afternoon, choose between Hagimit Falls or a lazy few hours at a beach resort that sells day passes. Our guide to Samal Island beyond the resorts has more on each stop and how to link them in a day.


Head back to the wharf by mid-afternoon if the weather turns. Boats run into the evening, but the last trips fill up and you do not want to be racing the sunset.



Day 3: The uplands of Calinan


Leave early for Malagos in Calinan, about an hour from downtown and noticeably cooler. The Philippine Eagle Center is the main reason to come. It cares for the country's national bird, a critically endangered raptor, and its trail lets you see the eagles up close in large enclosures. Arrive near opening time, when the birds are more active and the trail is quieter.


Malagos Garden Resort is a few kilometres away. It has a bird dome and a butterfly sanctuary, and the Malagos name is also known for locally grown cacao and chocolate, which makes a good gift to bring home. Have lunch in the area and head back to the city by mid-afternoon to beat the traffic.


For your last evening, buy fruit. Davao is famous for durian, and pomelo and mangosteen are easy to find too. If the smell of durian is too much, start with durian candy or ice cream. Many hotels ban the fresh fruit in rooms, so eat it at the stall.



Where to stay for this itinerary


For a short stay, the downtown area works best. Poblacion and the streets around City Hall put you close to the Day 1 stops and the night market. Lanang and the area near SM Lanang Premier are closer to the airport and Sasa Wharf, which saves time on Day 2. Bajada sits between the two and has plenty of restaurants. Avoid booking too far south or west, since Day 2 and Day 3 both leave from the north side of the city.



Getting around and practical tips


  • Use Grab or metered taxis. Davao's taxis are known for using the meter, and Grab works well for longer rides such as Malagos.
  • Follow the city's rules. Davao enforces a smoking ban in public places, a firecracker ban and speed limits, and the police do act on them.
  • Plan for rain. Showers can come in any month, so carry a light umbrella and keep the Samal day flexible.
  • Carry cash. Night market stalls, tricycle drivers and fruit vendors take cash only.
  • Visit in December for the lights. The city's Christmas season adds parades and lit parks to Day 1. See our guide to Pasko Fiesta in Davao City.

Fees, ferry schedules and opening hours change, so check with each site or the city tourism office close to your trip.
