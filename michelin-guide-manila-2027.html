Michelin Guide Manila 2027 Results: Stars and Bib Gourmands

Ren Calago 28 October 2026 Updated 29 October 20260

The Michelin Guide Manila and Environs & Cebu 2027 was announced on the evening of 29 October 2026 at the Manila Marriott Hotel in Newport World Resorts. All nine restaurants that held stars last year kept them, and one newcomer joined: Flow, a Peruvian–Filipino restaurant in Makati, with one star. Helm, also in Makati, is still the only two-star restaurant in the country.


The second edition lists 127 establishments in total: ten starred restaurants, 31 Bib Gourmands and 86 Michelin Selected. Every starred kitchen is in Metro Manila or a nearby province. Below you'll find the full starred list, the Manila-area Bib Gourmands by city, this year's special awards, and how to turn the list into a few days of eating.


Planning around the Cebu side of the guide instead? Our Michelin Guide Cebu 2027 results page covers the 20 Cebu restaurants.




What changed in 2027


  • Stars: 10, up from 9. No restaurant lost a star, and Flow is the only new one.
  • Bib Gourmand: 31 across Manila, the environs and Cebu, with 10 new names.
  • Michelin Selected: 86, including 18 restaurants new to the guide.
  • Moved down: Hálong and Your Local, both Bib Gourmands last year, are now listed as Selected.

The inspectors kept every star where it was, so last year's starred list still holds, with one addition. That makes the 2027 guide a dependable one to book from.



Manila's Michelin-starred restaurants for 2027


Seven of the ten starred kitchens are in Makati, so the city is still the easiest place in the country for a Michelin dinner.


  • Helm (two stars) – Makati, creative cooking
  • Flow (new, one star) – Makati, Peruvian–Filipino
  • Celera – Makati, contemporary Asian
  • Hapag – Makati, Filipino
  • Inatô – Makati, contemporary Filipino
  • Kása Palma – Makati, contemporary Filipino
  • Toyo Eatery – Makati, contemporary Filipino
  • Gallery by Chele – Taguig, creative cooking
  • Linamnam – Parañaque, contemporary Filipino
  • Asador Alfonso – Alfonso, Cavite, contemporary Spanish

Filipino cooking still runs through the list. Five of the ten starred places cook Filipino food in some form, and Flow mixes Peruvian and Filipino cooking. If you only have one fine-dining night in Manila, any of those six gives you something you can't easily find outside the country.


What a starred dinner costs


Almost all of these restaurants serve set tasting menus rather than à la carte. At last check, menus ran from around ₱3,000 at the low end to more than ₱8,000 per person, before the 10 percent service charge and drinks. Linamnam sits toward the more affordable end, while Helm and Hapag sit near the top. Prices often rise after a good result, so treat these as a floor and confirm on each restaurant's booking page.



The Bib Gourmand list for Manila and the environs


For most people, the Bib Gourmand list is the more useful one: good cooking where a full meal costs a fraction of a tasting menu. Of the 31 Bibs, 24 are outside Cebu.


Makati


La Pita in Poblacion, Lampara, Manam at the Triangle, Pilya's Kitchen, Sarsa, Taquería Franco and The Underbelly all kept their Bibs. With the seven starred kitchens in the same city, that's 14 Michelin-listed places within a short ride of each other. Our BGC and Makati first-timer's guide covers where to stay and how to get around.


Taguig


Bolero, Brick Corner, COCHI, Em Hà Nội and Los Tacos all kept their Bibs. Most are in or near BGC, so you can walk between them in the evening, which is rare in Metro Manila.


Quezon City, Manila and Parañaque


Morning Sun Eatery, Palm Grill in Diliman and SOME THAI on Tomas Morato hold on in Quezon City. Cabel in the City of Manila and Kumba in Parañaque also kept theirs.


New on the Bib list


Seven of this year's ten new Bib Gourmands are outside Cebu: Apag Marangle, Azela, Balay Dako, Bawai's Vietnamese Kitchen, Butcher Boy, C.H.E. Bacolod Chicken House Express and Tito Boy in Pampanga. New names usually get busy fast, so book or go outside peak hours.



Special award winners


Four people were recognised this year:


  • Young Chef Award: Francis Lacson of Digámo in Marikina, one of the new Selected restaurants
  • Service Award: Clyde Joshua Zafra of Tito Boy in Pampanga
  • Exceptional Cocktails Award: Ron Job Sanchez of Automat in Makati
  • Opening of the Year: Frances Tariga of Tadhanà


Making it a trip


Book early, then book again


Starred restaurants in Manila take reservations directly, through their own websites or social pages. Several have only a few counter seats, so tables go quickly. Demand jumps after each new edition, and December adds holiday crowds on top. Book two to six weeks ahead for a starred dinner, and check the cancellation policy, since many ask for a deposit.


Plan around traffic


Two tasting menus on the same day is too much for most people. A Bib Gourmand lunch and a starred dinner in the same district is the easier pattern: Makati for both, or BGC for both. Crossing from Quezon City to Parañaque at rush hour can take longer than the meal.


Asador Alfonso as a day trip


The one starred restaurant outside Metro Manila is in Alfonso, Cavite, on the cooler ridge near Tagaytay. A late lunch there combines easily with views of Taal. Our Tagaytay and Taal day trip guide has the transport options and timing. Weekend traffic up the ridge is heavy, so go on a weekday if you can.


With every star kept and a new one-star in Makati, the 2027 guide gives Manila an eating plan you can rely on through the holidays and into next year, from a Bib Gourmand bowl in Quezon City to a long Filipino tasting menu in Makati.
