What to Buy in Cebu: Local Products Worth Bringing Home

Ren Calago 15 October 2026

Cebu makes a lot of things people want to take home, from sweet biscuits and dried fruit to handmade jewellery and ukuleles. The trick is choosing the few items that are worth the suitcase space, and knowing which ones will survive the trip. A good pasalubong list is short, specific and a little bit local.


This guide covers the snacks and sweets that are worth buying, the chocolate and tablea, the crafts and music pieces, and the places to find them. It also covers what to pack for a flight abroad, since some favourites cannot travel. Prices change and stocks vary by shop, so treat the notes here as a starting point and check the labels and the price on the day.


Plan your buying for the last day or two of your trip. Many snacks are sealed, but heat, humidity and crushing in a bag are common reasons for things arriving damaged.


Sweets and Snacks Worth Buying


Rosquillos are a dependable choice. Titay's Flagship Store in Poblacion, Liloan is a well-known place to buy them, and you will also find them in supermarkets and pasalubong centres across the city. They come in boxes and packets, so pick a size that suits how many people you are buying for.


Otap is another easy choice, sold in packs and boxes in most supermarkets and pasalubong shops. Peanut brittle, known locally as browas, is worth a look in souvenir stores and supermarkets. Dried mangoes come in many styles, from plain strips to chips and chocolate-dipped pieces, so taste a sample before you commit to a large bag.


Chicharon and Savoury Snacks


Chicharon, the crisp pork rind, is one of the most popular savoury buys on the island. Packs are easy to find in supermarkets and local shops, and they make a good gift for anyone who likes a salty snack with their drinks. Look for packaging with a clear manufacturing date and keep the bag sealed, since the crisp texture fades quickly once it is open.



Chicharon is a domestic buy only. Pork products are generally restricted when you travel abroad, so keep it for the trip home within the Philippines or as a gift for someone you will see in person.


Chocolate and Tablea


Tablea is pure cacao made into round discs, traditionally used for hot chocolate and champorado, the sweet rice porridge. It is a sensible buy for anyone who likes to cook, and it keeps well when sealed. Local chocolate bars, including dark bars with cacao nibs, are also widely sold in Cebu.


The Chocolate Chamber in Robinsons Galleria is one option, and Tablea Chocolate Café has branches in several areas, including Bonifacio District, JY Square Mall and Banilad. Check the shop's current stock and ask about packaging if you are taking the chocolate on a plane.


Crafts, Music and Clothing


Handmade ukuleles are one of the most distinctive buys. Several makers on Mactan and in Lapu-Lapu sell them directly, and mall kiosks are convenient but usually charge more. An instrument is fragile, so take it as hand luggage if you are flying and confirm the airline's rules for the size you are carrying.


For smaller pieces, look for handcrafted jewellery, magnets and keychains from mall pop-up stores and souvenir shops. The Freedom Park and Carbon Market area is a good place to browse, and haggling is normal at market stalls. Local music is another souvenir option, and albums by Cebu artists can be bought from record shops such as 22 Tango Records at Mango Square Mall.


What to Pack for a Flight Abroad


Pork and other meat products are generally not allowed to leave the country, so leave chicharon and lechon at home for an overseas trip. Dried fish is usually allowed if it is packed well, and double or triple bagging helps contain the smell. Cuttings and seeds of any plant are likely to cause problems at customs.


Declare everything when you are asked. Hiding items can lead to bigger penalties than the item is worth, and the rules change, so check the destination country's current customs guidance before you buy. Keep receipts, and keep the most delicate or valuable items in your hand luggage.


Where to Shop and How to Spend


Supermarkets are the most predictable choice for sealed snacks, since prices are fixed and the stock is clear. Pasalubong centres and tourist shops are convenient, but compare prices before you buy a large order. Markets offer the best prices on bulk items, but the quality varies, so buy from stalls that look busy and clean.


If you want a simple budget, pick three items for family, one or two treats for friends, and one piece that reflects the trip, such as a ukulele or a set of local crafts. That keeps the list manageable and stops the suitcase from filling before you reach the airport.


Who This List Suits


This guide suits first-time visitors who want practical gift ideas, families with a long list of people to remember, and anyone who likes food and music as much as souvenirs. It is less useful if you are buying perishable food for an overseas trip, since the rules are strict and the shelf life is short.
