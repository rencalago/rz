Sakada Cafe and Farm in Pamplona, Negros Oriental: What to Know Before You Go

Ren Calago 11 Feb 2023 Updated 22 October 2026

Sakada Cafe and Farm is a mountain-top restaurant and cafe in Barangay Abante, Pamplona, Negros Oriental, a town about 37 km northwest of Dumaguete. It is known for a 360-degree view, a row of reclining seats that face the hills, and a menu we keep going back to. We have been four times, and this guide pulls everything from those visits into one place: where it is, the opening hours, what we ordered, what it costs, and what the road is like.


Most of the places we have written about in Negros Oriental got one visit, maybe two. Sakada is the exception. The reason is simple: the view is good enough to justify the drive, and the food holds up its end.


The setting is a hill farm, so the first thing you notice is green in every direction, then the mountains behind it, then the natural formations of the land. Photos help, but they flatten it. The view only really works when you are sitting in it.




Where Sakada Cafe and Farm is and how to get there


Sakada sits at the top of Barangay Abante, one of the 16 barangays of Pamplona. The town itself is roughly 37 km from Dumaguete. When we drove up from Tanjay City, the trip took about 25 minutes, which is short enough to fit into a half-day outing.


How steep is the road?


There are steep stretches near the top, but they were manageable for us, and noticeably gentler than the climb to Cafe Alicia, another Pamplona cafe. Fill up on fuel and check your brakes in town first, because the descent is where they work hardest. If you do not have your own vehicle, ask around the Pamplona town center about rides to Abante before you commit to the trip, because the last stretch is not somewhere you want to improvise.


Opening hours and walk-ins


When we first wrote about Sakada in 2023, it was open every day from 9 AM to 6 PM. You do not need a reservation; just walk in. Hilltop cafes adjust their schedules, so check the Sakada Facebook page or message them the day before.


The view and the reclining seats


The 360-degree panorama is the main draw, and the easiest way to enjoy it is to take one of the four reclining seats that face the back of the property. You can lean back and look at the hills as far as the eye goes, which makes it a good place to nap, pray, meditate or just do nothing for an hour. Ask whether they are free when you arrive.


A mountain view depends on the sky. Cloud and mist can wipe out the far hills, so check the forecast the morning of your visit, and keep a flexible plan if you are driving up from far away.








What to eat and drink at Sakada


On our latest trip we arrived between lunch and dinner, so we ordered light: pad thai, carbonara, choros and a few drinks. Everyone at the table was satisfied. The pad thai is the dish we keep going back for, and it is the first thing we would tell a new visitor to order. We plan to work through more of the menu next time.


Coffee and prices


Coffee is priced closer to a big city chain than a small-town cafe. A cappuccino ran about PHP 150 when we last went, and it tasted as pricey as it sounds, though still good. Our guess is that the location explains part of it, since everything has to be brought up the hill. Prices change, so treat that figure as a rough guide and check the current menu when you arrive.


Tips for visiting Sakada Cafe and Farm


  • Check the weather the same morning. The view is the reason to go.
  • Gas up and check your brakes in town before the climb.
  • Go hungry if you can. A late lunch, like ours, can leave you ordering too little.
  • Confirm the hours on their Facebook page before a long drive.

Making a day of it around Pamplona


Sakada works well as the anchor of a short loop. Our guide to Negros Oriental towns beyond Dumaguete covers what else Pamplona and its neighbours have to offer, and the Negros Oriental weekend getaways post helps you fit the trip into a longer stay. If you are basing yourself in the city, our Dumaguete food trip is a good counterpoint to a day in the hills.


Why a cafe on a mountain still fills tables


Business school taught us to put a restaurant where the customers already are. Sakada does the opposite. It sits up a mountain road, and people go anyway because a good view and a few photos travel further than a roadside sign. For a travel blogger that is the interesting part: the place is a reminder that, today, the audience will come to you if you give them something worth the drive.


If you have a free afternoon in Negros Oriental, book a table, bring someone you like talking to, order the pad thai, and take the reclining seat if it is free.
