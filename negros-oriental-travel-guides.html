Negros Oriental travel guides

Negros Oriental covers the eastern half of Negros Island, a long strip of coast backed by mountains and volcanic highlands. Dumaguete is the capital and the easiest base, but most of what makes the province worth the trip is outside the city: towns where the main road still passes rice fields and small markets, and a coastline with far fewer resorts than Cebu or Bohol.

Head north and you reach Bais, known for dolphin watching and the Manjuyod sandbar, and Tanjay, with views from the hills above town. Inland, Mabinay has dozens of caves, and Pamplona and San Jose have farm cafes in the cooler uplands. Head south and the coast runs past Zamboanguita, the jumping-off point for Apo Island, toward Siaton and the beaches beyond. Along the northern coast are quiet beach towns such as La Libertad, and inland, Canlaon City sits on the slopes of Kanlaon Volcano, so check the current alert level before planning anything on that side.

Public transport is mostly buses and vans along the coastal highway, with tricycles and habal-habal for the last stretch. That works for the main towns, but caves, waterfalls and mountain cafes are easier with a hired car or driver, especially in the rainy months when side roads turn muddy. The weekend getaways for different budgets and places worth the drive are good places to start.

The stories below follow the province beyond the city limits, newest first. Distances look short on a map, but mountain roads are slow, and opening hours at smaller attractions change, so confirm before you set out. For food, budgets and events in the city itself, see the Dumaguete guides.
