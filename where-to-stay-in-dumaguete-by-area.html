Where to Stay in Dumaguete by Area: Neighbourhood Guide

Ren Calago 4 November 20260

Dumaguete is small, flat and mostly walkable, so no part of the city leaves you stranded. What the area you choose really decides is how your evenings feel. Stay on the boulevard and dinner is a stroll along the seawall. Stay near Silliman and your mornings start with coffee and a quiet campus. Stay on the north side and you trade a short tricycle ride for more space and a calmer night.


This guide goes through each part of the city in turn, from Rizal Boulevard to the quieter north side and the mall area to the south, then the two places outside the city that many visitors use as a base: Dauin for the beach and diving, and Valencia for cooler mountain air.


Room rates below are rough 2026 guides and climb during busy periods, especially Buglasan in October, fiesta week in late November and the Christmas to New Year stretch. Check current rates when you book.




The Areas at a Glance


  • Rizal Boulevard and downtownBest for a first visit, an early ferry and walking everywhere.
  • Around SillimanBest for cafés, a university-town feel and quieter nights close to the centre.
  • Piapi and BantayanBest for longer stays, more space and a calmer base north of downtown.
  • CalindaganBest for mall access and the road south to the beaches.
  • DauinBest for divers and beach resort time, outside the city.
  • ValenciaBest for a cool night in the hills near the waterfalls.

Rizal Boulevard and Downtown


This is the Dumaguete most people picture: a seaside promenade lined with trees, benches and food stalls, looking out over the water. Behind it, the downtown grid holds the belfry, the cathedral, Quezon Park and the public market, all within an easy walk. The port for ferries to Siquijor and Cebu is at the southern end of the boulevard, which makes this the most practical base if you have an early boat.


Evenings are the draw. Students, joggers and families fill the seawall at sunset, and the tempura and kwek-kwek vendors set up as the light fades. The trade-off is noise. Traffic runs along the boulevard into the night, and weekend evenings can be loud. Rooms here also tend to cost more for their size, because you are paying for the location.


Expect budget inns from roughly ₱800 to ₱1,500 a night, mid-range rooms from about ₱1,800 to ₱3,500 and boutique stays above that. The Bricks Hotel, right on the boulevard, is a well-known option for anyone who wants the seawall at the door. For a short first trip, this is the area to choose.


Around Silliman University


The campus of Silliman University sits at the northern end of the boulevard, and the streets around it, including Hibbard Avenue and Silliman Avenue, feel like a university town inside the city. Expect cafés, bookshops, laundry services and small guesthouses that cater to students and visiting families. The campus itself is worth a slow walk, and our Silliman University heritage walk covers the buildings and the museum.


Nights here are quieter than on the boulevard, but the seawall is still close enough to walk to for dinner. You lose the sea view, and mornings bring school traffic. In return you get a calm base in the middle of things, which suits couples, slower travellers and anyone visiting the university.


Piapi and Bantayan: The North Side


North of the campus, the city opens up into residential barangays with more space and lower rents. Piapi, along EJ Blanco Drive, is home to the provincial tourism office and Piapi Beach, where a cluster of open-air bars and restaurants draws a younger crowd at night. Further along, Bantayan sits on Flores Avenue and the streets around it, with apartment-style stays, guesthouses with kitchens and a few resorts.


The Henry Resort on Flores Avenue, with its infinity pool, is the best-known name on this side. It is about a 25-minute walk from the boulevard, which gives a sense of the distances involved: comfortable for a morning walk, but most evenings you will take a tricycle into the centre. The ride is short, and short trips in the city stay close to the minimum fare.


Choose the north side for stays of three nights or more, for a kitchen, or for a quieter base where you can still reach downtown in minutes. There is no great beach here, so if swimming matters, look south to Dauin.


Calindagan and the South Road


Calindagan, on the south side of the city, is where you will find Robinsons Dumaguete, which opened a large new wing in September 2026, and Go Hotels Dumaguete nearby. It is not a sightseeing area, but it is practical. You get a cinema, supermarkets, restaurants and indoor entertainment under one roof, and you are already on the road south toward Bacong and Dauin.


It suits business travellers, families who like mall convenience on rainy days, and anyone splitting time between the city and the southern beaches. Downtown is a short tricycle ride away.


Dauin: Beach and Diving


Dauin is a separate town about 20 to 30 minutes south of the city, but most visitors treat it as Dumaguete's beach side. The coast here is dark volcanic sand, lined with dive resorts, and it is the usual starting point for trips to Apo Island. Expect mid-range to luxury prices, with many resorts running their own dive centres and pools.


The catch is distance from the city. Evenings in Dumaguete mean a ride each way, so Dauin works best when diving or beach time is the main point of your trip and you plan only a day or two in town.


Valencia: Cool Air in the Hills


Valencia sits inland and uphill, about 20 to 30 minutes from the city, and is noticeably cooler at night. It is the base for Casaroro and Pulangbato falls and the hot springs, and a night here is a good change of pace after the heat of the coast. Accommodation is mostly small resorts and guesthouses along the road up, often listed under Dumaguete on booking sites. You will need your own transport or a hired ride to get around.


A Three-Night Plan for First-Timers


  1. 01Night 1Rizal Boulevard

    Arrive, walk the seawall at sunset and get your bearings downtown.

  2. 02Night 2Silliman or the north side

    Cafés, the campus and a quieter night a short ride from the centre.

  3. 03Night 3Dauin

    Move south for the beach, a dive or an early boat to Apo Island.


If you only have one or two nights, stay on the boulevard and do day trips from there. Moving hotels is only worth it once you have three nights or more.


Booking Tips


Before you book

  • Watch the festival calendarRates and demand rise in October for Buglasan, in late November for fiesta week and Charter Day, and from Christmas to New Year.
  • Match your base to your boatIf you are heading to Siquijor, a room near the boulevard lets you walk to the port. Our Dumaguete to Siquijor guide covers the crossing.
  • Check the map, not the nameMany places in Dauin and Valencia list themselves as Dumaguete. Look at the pin before you book.
  • Ask about noiseOn the boulevard, a room at the back of the building is often worth more than a partial sea view.

Pick by How You Want to Spend Your Evenings


Dumaguete forgives a wrong choice, since almost everything is a short ride away. Pick the boulevard for sunsets and street food, Silliman for coffee and calm, the north side for space on a longer stay, Calindagan for convenience, Dauin for the sea and Valencia for the hills. Then spend less time in the room and more time outside it, which is how this city is best enjoyed.
