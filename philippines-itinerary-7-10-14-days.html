Philippines Itinerary: 7, 10 or 14 Days Across the Islands

Ren Calago 20 October 2026

Most Philippine trips go wrong at the planning stage, not on the beach. Someone sees El Nido, Boracay, Siargao and the Chocolate Hills on the same mood board, books all four into nine days, and spends half the holiday in departure lounges. The country has more than 7,000 islands, and every new one you add costs you a morning of packing, a transfer, and usually a flight.


The fix is to plan around a hub. Mactan-Cebu International Airport has direct flights to Caticlan for Boracay, to Siargao, to Busuanga for Coron, and to El Nido, while fast ferries reach Bohol in about two hours. Base your route on Cebu and you can reach the big-name islands without routing every leg through Manila.


Below are three routes, one each for 7, 10 and 14 days, plus the timing, transfer and budget calls that decide whether a plan actually works once you land.




Count Transfer Days Before You Count Islands


A simple ratio keeps an itinerary honest: two islands for a week, three for ten days, four for two weeks. Each move between islands eats most of a day once you add the ride to the port or airport, check-in, the trip itself and the transfer to your hotel on the other side. Five islands in seven days sounds ambitious on paper. On the ground it means five half-days lost and very little time in the water.


Book the longest flights first, then the ferries, then the day tours. Holy Week and the stretch from about 20 December to early January fill up fast and fares climb, so give yourself six to eight weeks of lead time if your dates fall there.


7 Days: Cebu, Bohol and Boracay


This is the easy first trip. It mixes city food, countryside and one famous beach, and it needs only one round of flights.


Days 1 to 2, Cebu. Land at Mactan, spend an afternoon on the beach side of the island, then cross to Cebu City for Magellan's Cross, the old streets around it and a proper lechon dinner.


Days 3 to 4, Bohol. Take an early fast craft to Tagbilaran. Spend one day inland for the Chocolate Hills, the tarsiers and a lunch cruise on the Loboc River, then sleep in Panglao so the next morning can go to snorkelling around Balicasag.


Days 5 to 7, Boracay. Fly to Caticlan, cross to the island, and give White Beach two full days: a sunset sail, a lazy island-hopping trip, and time doing nothing at all. Fly back to Cebu for your outbound connection.


Boracay collects an environmental fee and a terminal fee at the Caticlan jetty on top of the boat ride. Keep the receipts and any QR code they hand you until you leave the island.


10 Days: Cebu, Moalboal, Bohol and El Nido


Ten days lets you add Palawan's lagoons, the picture most people have in mind when they think of the Philippines.


Day 1, Cebu. Arrive, sort out a local SIM and some cash, and rest.


Days 2 to 3, Moalboal. The ride south takes roughly three hours by bus or van. Snorkel the sardine run straight off the shore at Panagsama, and give a separate day to canyoneering down to Kawasan Falls with a registered guide.


Days 4 to 5, Bohol. Back through Cebu City for the ferry to Tagbilaran. Do the Chocolate Hills loop one day and an island-hopping boat to Balicasag and Virgin Island the next.


Days 6 to 9, El Nido. Fly to El Nido, either direct or with a Cebu connection. Four nights covers two boat tours, usually Tour A for the lagoons and Tour C for the hidden beaches, plus a free day at Nacpan Beach. If the direct fare looks steep, flying into Puerto Princesa and taking the van north is cheaper but costs most of a day on the road. Our El Nido, Coron and Puerto Princesa comparison helps if you are still deciding which part of Palawan to target.


Day 10, out. Fly El Nido to Cebu and connect home.


14 Days: Siargao, Palawan, Boracay and Bohol


Two weeks is enough for a loop that rarely doubles back: Cebu, Siargao, Palawan, Boracay, Bohol, then Cebu again.


Days 1 to 2, Cebu. Recover from the long-haul flight.


Days 3 to 6, Siargao. Take a morning flight and stay in General Luna. Watch the surfers at Cloud 9, book a boat to Naked, Daku and Guyam islands, swim at Magpupungko when the tide is right, and save a day for riding a scooter up the coast road toward Pacifico. If you have never ridden a motorbike, this is not the place to learn; hire a habal-habal driver instead. The Siargao flight and ferry guide covers airport transfers and where to stay.


Days 7 to 10, El Nido and Coron. Fly to El Nido, usually with a stop in Cebu, and spend two days on the water. Then take the morning ferry to Coron and give one day to Kayangan Lake and Twin Lagoon.


Days 11 to 12, Boracay. Coron to Caticlan normally means a connection through Manila or Cebu. Two nights on White Beach is the reward.


Days 13 to 14, Bohol and home. Fly back to Cebu and cross to Panglao for a last quiet night, or skip it if your flight home is early.


Want less flying? Drop Coron, spend four days in El Nido, three in Boracay, three in Bohol and two in Moalboal. That cuts the domestic flights from five to three.


Pick the Right Months


November to May is the safest window for island-hopping in most of the country, with calmer seas and clearer water. Palawan and Boracay are at their best from roughly December to April. Siargao runs on a different calendar: the bigger surf usually arrives between about August and November, while December to February brings more rain to the eastern side of the country. Surfers and beach-sitters should book Siargao for different months.


June to October is typhoon season. A storm signal can cancel ferries and small-plane flights with little warning, so leave a spare day before any international departure.


Ferry or Flight?


Fly the long legs. Siargao, El Nido, Coron and Caticlan have no practical ferry chain out of Cebu, and the overnight boat-and-bus alternatives cost you a full day or more.


Take the boat for the short hops. The Cebu to Tagbilaran fast craft lands in the middle of town, which often makes it quicker door to door than flying once you count airport transfers and waiting time. The El Nido to Coron ferry also beats any flight between the two, since flying usually means a detour through Manila.


Check the total fare, not the headline one. Promo seats look cheap until checked baggage is added, and a surfboard or a big backpack can double the price. Open-jaw tickets, flying into Cebu and out of Manila for example, sometimes cost about the same as a return and save you a final backtrack.


Budget and Fees


As a rough guide before flights, backpackers on dorm beds, carinderia meals and shared tours can expect around PHP 2,200 to 3,500 a day. A private air-conditioned room, café meals and split private tours push that to about PHP 5,000 to 8,500 a day per person. The Philippines travel budget breakdown goes line by line.


Set aside money for the small charges too. Boracay, El Nido, Coron and Siargao all collect environmental or eco-tourism fees, ports add terminal fees, and some lagoons charge their own entrance. Across the 14-day loop these add up to somewhere near PHP 1,200 to 1,800 per person. Amounts change, so check them at each terminal. Keep the El Nido eco-fee receipt with you on boat tours, because rangers ask to see it.


Carry cash in small bills. Tricycle and boat crews rarely take cards or e-wallets, and ATMs on smaller islands can run dry over long weekends, so withdraw in Cebu City or Manila before you head out.


Which Route Fits You?


A week and a first visit: Cebu, Bohol and Boracay, with no heavy flying. Ten days and lagoons on your list: add Moalboal and El Nido. Two weeks with surf in the picture: run the full Siargao and Palawan loop, or the lighter version with fewer flights. In every case, let the plane carry you over open sea and let the ferries handle the short crossings, and leave one day loose for the weather.
