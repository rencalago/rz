Bantayan Island Guide: Getting There, Beaches and Planning a Slow Week

Ren Calago 13 October 20260

Bantayan Island sits off the northwest tip of Cebu, and most visitors reach it by road to Hagnaya and a short ferry crossing. It is not a place for a packed schedule. The appeal is the long, sandy coastline, the slower pace and the sense that the day is set by the tide and the sun rather than by a timetable.


This guide covers how to reach the island, what the crossing costs and how long it takes, where the main beach areas are, and how to get around once you arrive. Fares and schedules on this route have changed in 2026, and quoted figures do not always agree, so confirm the current figures with the port operators before you travel.


Plan on the crossing being the most uncertain part of the trip. Published sailing times are a guide rather than a guarantee, and the weather can suspend services at short notice.



Getting from Cebu City to Hagnaya


From Cebu City, the usual route is a bus from the North Bus Terminal to Hagnaya Port in San Remigio. The road trip takes roughly three to five hours, depending on traffic and stops. A Ceres bus fare of about ₱165 from the North Terminal has been quoted, though that figure may be out of date, so check it with the terminal before you buy. Buses from Mactan-Cebu International Airport connect to the North Terminal by the MyBus, which runs frequently during the day.


Leave Cebu early enough to reach Hagnaya by early afternoon. This leaves time for a delay if the sea or the schedule shifts. If you are making a same-day connection to a flight or a hotel check-in, build in a generous buffer rather than planning around the last sailing.


The Hagnaya to Santa Fe Crossing


Passenger and vehicle ferries run from Hagnaya to Santa Fe Port on Bantayan. Island Shipping and Super Shuttle are among the operators on this route. The crossing takes about an hour to an hour and a half once loading and docking are included. Sailings run several times a day, but published timetables differ, so ask the port office for the day's departures when you arrive.


Fares are the least certain part of the trip. A fare rollback for passengers and vehicles took effect on 11 May 2026, which means older price posts may now be wrong. For planning only, an adult crossing is roughly ₱300 to ₱450, before any port or environmental charges. Treat that as a rough range and check the exact amount on the day, especially if you are taking a vehicle, since vehicle rates depend on the class of vehicle.


Once you land at Santa Fe, expect to pay an environmental or tourism fee at the tourism office. An older guide lists this as ₱30, so confirm the current amount. A tricycle or a resort shuttle takes you to your accommodation.


When the Ferry Does Not Sail


Weather can close both Hagnaya and Maya, the other northern port. One 2026 article notes that both were suspended during a tropical storm in February. Check the weather forecast the day before you travel, and keep one flexible day in your plan rather than a fixed return flight the next morning.


If you do get stuck at the port, ask the operator what they offer for rebooking and whether the terminal has a waiting area. Keep some cash on hand, since port stalls and small shops may not accept cards.


The Main Beach Areas


Santa Fe is where most visitors stay. It is known for its sunrises and for wide, white sand, and the beach area has the largest number of resorts and guesthouses. Sugar Beach is where many of the listed resorts are located. Alice Beach faces east, so it suits people who want to watch the sun come up.


Kota Beach, also called Camp Sawi, faces south and is harder to reach. It is a tropical stretch worth the effort, though the approach is not easy and it has few facilities. Bring everything you need for the day, and check how you will get back before you set out.


Bantayan town, on the western and central part of the island, is quieter than Santa Fe and is known for its sunsets and a historic church. Madridejos, a fishing village, has the Kota Ruins, Kota Park and a Baywalk.


Getting Around the Island


Tricycles and motorbikes are the main transport. The minimum tricycle fare is quoted as ₱10, though the price for longer trips should be agreed before you set off. Jeepneys and multicabs run between Santa Fe and Bantayan, but the trips are limited, so do not depend on them for a late return.


Renting a motorbike is a common way to explore. Rates are about ₱350 a day for a motorbike and about ₱150 for a bicycle, usually arranged through a resort or hotel. Check the fuel arrangement and the helmet situation before you rent, and carry your licence if the rental requires one. A four-hour tricycle tour, quoted at about ₱1,000 for three people, is an easy option if you do not want to drive yourself.


When to Go


January to June is the best window, which fits the drier months in northern Cebu. The rainy months generally bring heavier seas and the risk of cancelled ferries, so the crossing becomes harder to plan. Check the forecast before you book, and keep the trip flexible if you travel in the second half of the year.


Weekends and public holidays can be busy in Santa Fe, so book accommodation early if you plan to visit at those times. Midweek stays are quieter and often easier to arrange on short notice.

How to Plan a Slow Stay


Bantayan rewards a slower pace. Plan one or two things each day, such as a morning swim, a walk along the beach and an evening meal by the water, rather than a long list of stops. Three or four days is enough to see the main areas without rushing.
