Dumaguete Is Changing: 5 Things That Feel Different in 2026

Dumaguete has always had a particular character. It is small enough to feel familiar, but busy enough to attract students, travelers, entrepreneurs, professionals and families from different parts of Negros Oriental and beyond.


In 2026, however, some parts of the city feel noticeably different. New commercial spaces are appearing, pickleball has grown into a major local activity, business development is expanding, and the city is having conversations about what some of its important public spaces could look like in the future.



Not all of these changes are completed projects, and some are still proposals or developments in progress. But taken together, they show a city that is gradually adding new layers to its identity.


1. Pickleball Has Become Part of Dumaguete's Identity


Perhaps the most unexpected change is how visible pickleball has become around the city.


What was once a relatively unfamiliar sport has grown into a major community activity. Recent reports put the number of pickleball courts in Dumaguete at more than 300, with thousands more across Negros Oriental.


The growth has attracted attention from the Philippine Sports Commission, which has expressed support for Dumaguete's development as a major pickleball center.


More importantly, the sport is no longer limited to a small group of players. Families, older residents, younger players and visitors are increasingly becoming part of the local pickleball community.


That changes the way the city can think about sports and tourism. Dumaguete is no longer simply a place people pass through on the way to beaches or diving destinations. It is beginning to develop an activity that can bring people into the city specifically to play.


2. Robinsons Dumaguete Is Much Bigger


Another very visible change in 2026 is Robinsons Dumaguete. The mall has undergone a major expansion, adding four floors and more than 17,000 square meters of gross leasable area.


The expanded complex brings additional shopping, dining, entertainment and lifestyle options to the city, making the Robinsons development noticeably larger than the version many Dumaguete residents have known for years.


The change is important not only because of the number of new stores. It also reflects the growing demand for larger commercial spaces in Dumaguete.


For residents, that means more choices without having to travel to Cebu or another major city for certain shopping, dining and entertainment experiences.


3. Dumaguete Is Becoming More of a Business Hub


The changes around Robinsons are not limited to retail. Business and office development is also becoming a more noticeable part of Dumaguete's growth.


Robinsons Land has identified Cybergate Dumaguete as a Grade A office development being delivered to support the expansion requirements of an existing BPO tenant.


This adds another dimension to Dumaguete's economy. The city has long been known as a university and tourism destination, but office and BPO activity provides another source of employment and commercial demand.


It also means that some of the people spending money in Dumaguete may increasingly be living and working in the city, rather than simply visiting it.


4. The City Is Thinking About Bigger Public Facilities


Another change is less visible because it is still at the planning stage.


In May 2026, public consultation was held regarding a proposed new four-storey public market and twin two-storey City Hall extension building.


These are proposals rather than completed developments, but the discussions are significant because public markets and government buildings are among the facilities that shape how residents experience a city every day.


A modernized market could potentially change the way people shop for everyday goods, while additional government space could provide room for city services as Dumaguete continues to grow.


Whether and when these proposed projects move forward will depend on the city's planning, approvals, funding and implementation.


5. Dumaguete's Tourism Story Is Getting Broader


Dumaguete has always benefited from tourism, but the city's role in the region is becoming broader than simply being a convenient base for visiting nearby attractions.


The city's calendar increasingly includes sports, festivals, food activities, cultural events and community gatherings. Buglasan, for example, brings activities from across Negros Oriental into Dumaguete and turns the city into the center of the province's annual festival season.


The growth of pickleball adds another potential tourism connection. A visitor who comes for a tournament or sports event can also stay in local hotels, eat at restaurants, explore the boulevard and visit other attractions.


That creates a different type of tourism—one based not only on sightseeing but also on events, activities and experiences.


What Has Not Changed


For all these changes, Dumaguete still has many of the qualities that have made it attractive in the first place.


The boulevard is still a major part of everyday city life. Students continue to shape the atmosphere around the universities. Cafés and restaurants remain important meeting places, while the city's relatively compact layout continues to make it easy to move between different parts of town.


The changes happening now are therefore not necessarily replacing the old Dumaguete. They are being added on top of it.


A City Growing Without Losing Its Character


That may be the most interesting thing about Dumaguete in 2026. The city is clearly developing, but the changes are happening in several different directions at once.


Pickleball is creating a new sports identity. Robinsons is expanding the city's commercial options. Office development is adding another layer to the local economy. Proposed public projects are raising questions about the city's future infrastructure, while tourism is becoming increasingly tied to events and experiences.


None of these changes alone completely transforms Dumaguete. Together, however, they make the city feel different from the one many residents remember a few years ago.


Dumaguete is still Dumaguete. But in 2026, it is becoming a little bigger, a little busier and more diverse in the ways people live, work, play and visit.
