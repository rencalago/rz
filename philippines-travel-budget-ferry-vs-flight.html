Philippines Travel Budget: Daily Costs and Ferry vs Flight

Ren Calago 18 October 2026

The Philippines is cheap until you start moving between islands. A day in one place, with a fan room, carinderia meals and the odd jeepney ride, costs very little. Add a plane ticket, a baggage fee and an airport transfer, and one travel day can cost more than the three days around it. If you want a trip budget you can trust, the daily spending figure is only half of it. The other half is how you cross the water.


This guide gives rough daily ranges for three travel styles, then puts ferries and domestic flights side by side on the routes that most visitors use around Manila and Cebu. All prices are typical ranges in Philippine pesos and they move with season, fuel costs and how early you book, so treat them as planning numbers and check the operator's current fare before you pay.


The short version: fly when time is tight or the route has no ferry, and take the boat when you have a spare day, heavy bags, or a short crossing like Cebu to Bohol.




What a Day in the Philippines Actually Costs


Backpacker: about PHP 1,800 to 2,850 a day


At this level you sleep in a dorm or a basic fan room (PHP 500 to 1,000), eat pan de sal and coffee for breakfast, a carinderia lunch at PHP 80 to 150 and a street barbecue dinner at PHP 150 to 250, and move by jeepney (PHP 15 to 25), tricycle (PHP 50 to 150) and the occasional provincial bus (PHP 100 to 300 between towns). The big-ticket item is a shared island-hopping tour, usually PHP 800 to 1,500, and most people do one every two or three days, which spreads the cost across the trip.


Mid-range: about PHP 3,500 to 6,000 a day


A private aircon room with its own bathroom runs PHP 1,500 to 3,500. Meals are cafe breakfasts (PHP 200 to 350), restaurant lunches (PHP 250 to 400) and dinners (PHP 300 to 500). Rides in a Grab car or a private van are PHP 300 to 700 each. A private island-hopping boat can run PHP 3,000 to 6,000 for the whole group, which works out to PHP 1,000 to 2,000 each if three people split it. Add PHP 1,800 to 2,500 per dive if you dive, or PHP 1,200 to 2,000 for a guided tour.


Family or comfort: PHP 6,000 to 10,000 and up


Two rooms, or one family room, take PHP 3,500 to 7,000 of that. The rest goes to better restaurants, more bottled water, private vans and airport transfers, and private boats where schedule and safety matter more than saving a few hundred pesos. Resort-based island hopping and private tours push this range higher.


The real swing factor is transport, not food


Moving from backpacker to mid-range mostly changes your bed and your boat. Food barely moves the total. What pushes a trip budget up is flying, especially last-minute fares and the weeks around Holy Week and Christmas. Remote destinations such as Batanes and Siargao often cost more to reach than Cebu to Manila. Expect environmental fees, terminal fees and small boat tickets to add roughly PHP 200 to 500 on any day you change islands.


What brings it down: staying longer in one place (guesthouses often discount weekly stays), eating where locals eat, sharing vans, booking flights four to six weeks ahead, and avoiding the big holiday weeks.


Flights: Fast, but Read the Fine Print


Manila to Cebu takes about 1 hour 20 minutes to 1 hour 35 minutes in the air, with several airlines flying it through the day. Promo fares can sit around PHP 1,200 to 2,000, while regular one-way fares often land between PHP 2,500 and 5,000. Cebu to Tagbilaran in Bohol is a 25 to 30 minute hop at about PHP 1,000 to 2,500. Cebu to Siargao takes roughly an hour at PHP 1,500 to 5,500, and Cebu to Caticlan, the gateway for Boracay, takes about an hour at PHP 1,200 to 4,500.


The ticket price is rarely the final price. A checked bag can add PHP 500 to 1,200, seat selection costs extra, and domestic fares carry a fuel surcharge that varies by distance. A PHP 1,200 promo fare can easily become PHP 2,500 once you add a 20 kg bag and a seat. Hand luggage is usually limited to around 7 kg and checked bags to about 20 kg, and sports gear costs extra.


Flying also has a time cost people forget. You should be at the airport two to three hours early for most domestic departures, and you still need to get from the airport to town on the other end. Weather can ground flights too, so a fixed plane ticket does not guarantee a fixed arrival day.


Ferries: Slower, Cheaper Per Bag, Sometimes Cheaper Overall


Manila to Cebu by 2GO takes about 21 to 23 hours overnight. Fares run from roughly PHP 2,859 for the cheapest open-bunk class to PHP 8,615 for a private suite, and some classes include meals. Cebu to Tagbilaran by fast craft takes about two hours for PHP 800 to 1,200. Cebu to Iloilo is an overnight crossing of roughly 11 to 13 hours, priced anywhere from PHP 800 to 2,500 by class. Cebu to Surigao, the usual ferry route toward Siargao, takes 8.5 to 10 hours at PHP 800 to 1,200 for economy or PHP 1,500 to 2,500 for a cabin, and then you still need a fast craft from Surigao to Dapa for another PHP 400 to 800 and two to three and a half hours. Coron to El Nido by boat takes four to five hours at PHP 1,800 to 3,500.


The ferry advantages are real. There is no strict baggage weight limit, so large backpacks, surfboards and even motorbikes (for an extra fee) can go along. An overnight sailing also covers a night's lodging. The costs are time, seasickness risk, fewer departures, and cancellations when the Coast Guard holds boats during bad weather, which is a particular risk in the typhoon months and during the northeast monsoon from December to February.


Route by Route: Which One Wins


Cebu to Manila


A flight at PHP 1,200 to 5,000, plus PHP 300 to 600 to get into the city, beats the ferry on time by about a day. The 2GO ferry (PHP 2,859 to 8,615 with a bed and, in some classes, meals) only makes sense if you are carrying a lot of luggage, hate airports, or want the voyage itself.


Cebu to Bohol


Here the ferry often wins door to door. A flight looks quick on paper, but airport transfers at both ends and the check-in window can eat two hours. A fast craft from the Cebu pier at PHP 800 to 1,200 usually puts you in Tagbilaran in similar total time for less money. Our Bohol day trip from Cebu guide walks through that crossing.


Cebu to Siargao


The ferry route totals about PHP 1,200 to 2,000 but takes 11 to 13 hours counting the Surigao connection. A direct flight takes about an hour at PHP 1,500 to 5,500. Unless your budget is very tight, fly. We compare the options in detail in Siargao from Cebu.


Cebu to Boracay


Flights to Caticlan at about PHP 1,200 to 4,500 are the sensible choice for most visitors. Read Boracay from Cebu for the rest of the journey from the airport to the island.


Manila to Puerto Princesa


Fly. Flights take about an hour and a half at PHP 1,500 to 5,000, and there is no direct ferry. The sea alternative is a chain of boats via Coron, which only suits travelers with plenty of time.


When to Fly and When to Take the Boat


Fly if you have under a week and want to see more than one island, if you travel between December and May and need reliable timing, if you carry light bags and booked an early promo fare, or if you are going somewhere with no practical ferry, such as Batanes. Take the ferry if you have time, want to skip a night of accommodation, are hauling big luggage or sports gear, or are on a short, well-served crossing such as Cebu to Bohol, Cebu to Iloilo or Coron to El Nido.


A blended trip usually works best. Fly into one hub, take short ferries between neighboring islands, and fly out from the last stop. In the Visayas, the Cebu, Bohol, Siquijor and Dumaguete chain suits that approach, and our guide to Cebu to Dumaguete by sea or road covers one leg of it.


Money-Saving Moves That Work


Book flights four to six weeks out, and avoid Holy Week and the Christmas to New Year stretch, when fares can double. Always add up the full price, with bag, seat and surcharge, before comparing airlines. On overnight ferries, pay the extra PHP 500 to 1,000 for a tourist or cabin berth over the open bunks, which are noisy and less secure.


Withdraw cash in Cebu City or Manila before you head to smaller islands, since ATMs there can be few, capped or empty on weekends. GCash and Maya are accepted at many mid-range hotels and tour desks, but tricycles, small boats and terminal fees are usually cash only, so carry PHP 20, 50 and 100 bills. If you are crossing in typhoon season or the northeast monsoon, travel insurance with weather cover can save rebooking costs. Before an overnight sailing, take seasickness medicine about 30 minutes before departure, sit midship on a lower deck and skip heavy, oily food.


The Budget Test Before You Book


Compare door to door, not ticket to ticket. A PHP 1,200 flight plus a PHP 600 airport transfer, PHP 500 for a bag and a three-hour airport wait can lose to a PHP 1,200 fast ferry that leaves from a city pier. Use that test for every leg and the right choice usually becomes obvious.


For a ten-day trip from Cebu, one workable mix is a fast ferry to Bohol (about two hours, PHP 800 to 1,200), a short onward hop toward Siquijor, then flights only for the longest legs, such as Cebu to El Nido or Coron. Planned this way, a mid-range traveler can hold daily spending near PHP 2,500 to 4,500, with flights taking a few large bites out of the budget instead of nibbling at every day.
