Tumalog Falls in Oslob: The Steep Walk, Fees and Whether to Swim

Tumalog Falls is one of the easier-going waterfalls in the Oslob area, and it is often visited on the same day as the whale shark trip. It drops over a limestone cliff into a wide basin, and the setting is quieter than the more famous falls further south. Expect a short but steep walk, a small entrance fee and a visit that takes about an hour.


This guide covers how to reach the falls from Oslob, what it costs, the walk down and what the pool is like, the swimming and safety points, and the timing that makes a difference. Sources agree on the basic fee and hours but differ on fares and the best season, so check the details on the day.


The falls are simple to enjoy if you arrive with the right shoes and a plan for the road. The steep part is the one thing that catches first-time visitors out.



Getting There from Oslob


Oslob town is the usual base. One guide puts the drive from the town centre to the jump-off point at around 10 minutes by scooter, or 5 to 10 minutes by vehicle. Scooters can be rented along the main road, and one source gives a typical price of about ₱400 a day with fuel, though rates change with the season.


The last stretch is the difficult part. The road down to the falls is very steep, and cars cannot drive all the way. Scooters and motorbike taxis can. A habal-habal return trip from the junction is quoted at around ₱50, and two sources agree on that figure. Taxis from town were quoted at roughly ₱300 to ₱500, depending on the driver and how long they wait, so agree on the price before you set off.


From Cebu City, one guide describes hourly buses from the Cebu South Bus Terminal to Oslob, quoted at around ₱150. That fare and the distance of about 130 kilometres come from a single source, so check the bus fare with the terminal before you buy a ticket. The sources did not describe the route from Bato or Moalboal.


Entrance Fee and Opening Hours


The entrance fee is ₱50 for a visit without a tour, and the sources we checked agree on that figure. No separate guide fee was stated. The falls open daily from 7:00 in the morning to 4:00 in the afternoon. One source adds that the site closes on one Wednesday each month for a clean-up, which is every third Wednesday, so confirm the closure before you plan a trip that depends on that day.


Plan to arrive in the morning. The road and the falls are quieter before the day-tour groups arrive, and the basin is easier to enjoy before the afternoon heat.


The Walk Down and the Falls


From the junction, the path to the falls is roughly 500 metres and runs steeply downhill. There is no formal difficulty rating, so judge it by your own fitness. Some steps lead down to the plunge pool, and the path can be slippery after rain, so wear shoes with a good grip rather than sandals.


At the bottom, water falls in a curtain over the limestone cliff into a large basin. A cave sits on the left side of the swimming area, which is worth a look if you want something beyond the waterfall itself. The site has changing rooms, toilets and a small shop, so you do not need to bring everything with you.


Swimming and Safety


Swimming is allowed in the plunge pool. One guide describes it as mostly no deeper than waist height, and the cascade is not described as strong. The sources we checked did not list any formal swimming rules, so ask the staff at the entrance about where it is safe to go in and whether there are any restrictions on the day.


Wear clothes you do not mind getting stained, since the water can discolour light fabric and swimwear. Leave valuables in a dry bag or with someone at the entrance rather than taking them to the edge of the pool.


If you ride a scooter or a habal-habal, be aware that helmets were not described as provided. Drivers can take the steep road quickly, so ask them to slow down. If you drive yourself, use the handbrake and leave the car in gear when you park, because the slope is steep.


When to Go


Locals quoted in one guide suggest June to November as the best time, when the water flow is highest, particularly after heavy rain. That is also the wetter season, so the path can be slick and the water may run harder after rain. The dry months are more comfortable for the walk, but the water is lower.


Check the weather the day before and avoid a visit on the morning after a storm, when the road and path may be slippery and the water may be running hard.


Who Tumalog Falls Suits


The falls suit slow travellers, photographers and people who want a calm swim after a busy morning. They also work well as a second stop when you are already in Oslob for the whale sharks.


They are less suitable for anyone who needs step-free access, for young children who cannot manage steep steps, or for people who want a long hike.
