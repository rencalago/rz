Tinuy-an Falls Bislig: Fees, Raft Rides and How to Get There

Ren Calago 22 October 2026

Tinuy-an Falls drops over a cliff in Barangay Borboanan, Bislig City, and it is built wide, not tall. The curtain is about 55 meters high and 95 meters across, which is why it is known as the Little Niagara of the Philippines. It does not fall in one plume; it spills in broad sheets over stepped rock into a green basin where bamboo rafts wait.


Bislig is a long way from Cebu, so this trip needs more planning than a typical waterfall stop. Below: what you pay at the falls, how the last leg from Mangagoy works, two ways to reach Bislig from Cebu, and the one factor that can cancel your visit even after you arrive, which is rain.


Prices and opening hours at small LGU-run sites change without notice. Use the numbers here to set a budget, then confirm them at the ticket booth.




What the Falls Are Actually Like


Three tiers can be seen from the main viewing area, and a fourth is tucked out of view. The lower pool is where most people stop: you can wade, swim with a life vest, or take a raft close to the main drop and get soaked by the spray. The upper tiers are calmer, with smaller cascades and shallower pools, reached by paved steps along the side. Guides are expected for the upper tier and are paid by tip, so settle that before you start climbing.


Rocks around the pools are slippery year-round, and the edge of the upper pool deserves respect when the current is running. Rainbows show up on sunny mornings, usually between 9 and 11 AM, which is the best argument for arriving early.


Fees, Raft Rides and Opening Hours


Entrance is PHP 50 per person. A bamboo raft costs PHP 300 per boat for up to seven people, and a life vest rents for PHP 30. Vehicles that park at the site have been charged PHP 50. If a full raft splits the fare, the core costs come to roughly PHP 125 per person: entrance, a share of the raft and a vest.


Older blogs quote a PHP 150 raft fare, which is outdated. Posted opening times differ from one listing to the next, so treat daylight as the working window and confirm at the gate. Bring cash in small bills for the raft, the vest, parking and the guide.


From Mangagoy to the Falls


Mangagoy is the commercial center of Bislig and the place where buses unload. Buses and vans often show MANGAGOY on the sign, not Bislig, so do not assume you are on the wrong vehicle. The falls are roughly 17 to 18 km from the city proper, and a habal-habal or tricycle takes anywhere from 30 minutes to an hour depending on the driver and the road.


Round-trip habal-habal fares have started around PHP 300 to PHP 600 per motorcycle and rise if the driver waits or adds a side trip. Settle the price, the waiting time and the return before you leave Mangagoy. The final stretch was rebuilt in a road project completed in 2018, so it is much smoother than older accounts describe, though it is still a rural road. Private vehicles can park near the entrance.


Reaching Bislig From Cebu


There is no direct way to Bislig from Cebu, so you choose a gateway. Butuan lies about 158 km away and Davao about 208 km. Both work, and the better one depends on your flights.


Via Butuan: Cebgo and Philippine Airlines fly Cebu to Butuan in a little over an hour, and our Britania Islands and Enchanted River guide covers that leg and the Butuan terminals in more detail. From the Butuan bus terminal, the ride to Mangagoy takes about four to five hours by bus or van.


Via Davao: Bachelor Express lists a daily Davao to Bislig aircon bus at about PHP 813, with departures around 5 AM and 8 AM from Ecoland Terminal. Expect anywhere from five to nine hours on the road, so plan on most of a day and do not book a tight connection. This route makes sense when Davao is already on your list, and our Davao travel guide covers what to do there.


Either way, sleep in Bislig the night before. Arriving by late afternoon and reaching the falls early the next morning beats a rushed same-day dash that leaves you at the gate at noon with a night bus to catch.


When the Water Decides Your Day


Bislig is very wet, with around 4,200 mm of rain a year. January is the wettest month, at roughly 710 mm, and August the driest, at about 171 mm. A wet spell makes the falls fuller and louder, and it also turns the pools brown and the rocks treacherous.


It can also shut the site. On 19 February 2026, the Bislig City mayor's office suspended operations at Tinuy-an Falls under a PAGASA yellow rainfall warning, citing the risk of sudden flooding and landslides. No end date was announced at the time. Arriving at a closed gate wastes the whole transport leg, so check the forecast a few days out, ask your hotel whether the site is open the evening before, and keep a spare day in the itinerary.


The raft ride, the wet steps and the river-level swimming are the same hazards covered in our island-hopping safety guide: bring a dry bag, a waterproof phone pouch and shoes with grip, and keep your life vest fastened.


Pairing It With the Enchanted River


Hinatuan lies north of Bislig, roughly an hour away by jeepney from Mangagoy, and the Enchanted River is the natural add-on. The river closes at 5 PM and its fish feeding is now in the afternoon, so do Tinuy-an first thing, head north before lunch and reach the river with daylight to spare. Remember that the main lagoon is for viewing only; the Britania guide above explains the swimming rules.


Before you leave Bislig, look for puto balanghoy and salvaro, two cassava treats worth tracking down. Salvaro keeps well, which makes it the easier pasalubong to carry home.


Is It Worth the Trip From Cebu?


If a wide, powerful waterfall you can ride a raft toward is what you want, yes, with conditions. The falls themselves cost little; the real expense is the time to reach Bislig. Go on a weekday morning, stay overnight, carry small bills and treat the rain forecast as part of the itinerary.
