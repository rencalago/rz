Nacpan Beach, El Nido: Getting There, Costs and Tips

Ren Calago 8 November 20260

Most days in El Nido are decided by a boat. You wake up, find your meeting point, and the tour does the rest. Nacpan Beach is the opposite: it is the one big outing on the northern coast that runs on wheels and your own timing, and the whole point is that nobody is herding you to the next lagoon.


The beach runs for roughly four kilometres of pale sand backed by coconut palms, and it faces west across open water with no island in the way. At its southern end it meets Calitang Beach at a small hill, which is why the pair is called the Twin Beaches. It is about 17 kilometres from El Nido town, a drive of around 45 minutes that gets slower after rain.


This guide covers how to get there, what it really costs, where the sea turns tricky, what to do once you arrive, and how to fit it into a trip that probably also includes the island-hopping tours. Fares and schedules in this part of Palawan shift with the season, so the numbers below are planning ranges, not a price list.




Nacpan Beach at a glance


Nacpan Beach, El Nido

  • Where: northern coast of El Nido, Palawan, next to Calitang Beach
  • Distance: about 17 km north of El Nido town
  • Drive time: roughly 45 minutes, slower on the unpaved final stretch
  • Beach length: around 4 km, shared with Calitang
  • Entrance fee: none at the beach; parking can carry a small charge
  • Best months: November to May, with February to April usually driest
  • Not included in: island-hopping Tours A, B, C or D


How to get to Nacpan Beach from El Nido town


There is no boat option, so the choice is between a seat in a shared vehicle, a tricycle, a motorbike or a private van. Each suits a different kind of day.


  • Shared shuttleRoughly ₱400 to ₱450 one way or ₱700 to ₱800 round trip. Fixed departures and a set last return, so book your seat for the way back before you settle on the sand. Best for solo travellers and couples.
  • TricycleTypically ₱700 to ₱1,500 for the whole vehicle, with the driver waiting, for up to about four people. Settle the price, the waiting time and the return before leaving town.
  • Rented motorbikeAbout ₱350 to ₱700 a day plus fuel. The final stretch is sandy and dusty, so this is for riders with a licence and real experience, not a first try on a scooter.
  • Private vanOften ₱2,000 to ₱3,000 for a group of up to 10 to 12, usually arranged through a hotel or tour desk. It works out cheaper per head than shuttle seats once you are six or more.

Whichever you pick, leave early. The road is mostly paved but bumpy, and the sandy last section slows everything after heavy rain. Riding back in the dark on an unfamiliar road is the one plan worth avoiding.


Coming from Cebu or Puerto Princesa


Starting in Cebu

Fly straight to El Nido

Cebu has a direct flight to the small Lio airport north of town, so you can skip Puerto Princesa entirely. Our guide to getting to Palawan from Cebu compares routes, costs and door-to-door times.

Starting in Puerto Princesa

Van to town first

The van to El Nido takes around five hours. Treat Nacpan as a separate day once you have arrived, not a same-day add-on to the transfer.

Already at Lio airport

Go straight to the coast

If you are staying at a beachfront property on the Nacpan side, arrange the transfer with the property in advance rather than hoping for a ride at the gate.

Things to do at Nacpan Beach


Walk the length of it


The most useful thing about four kilometres of beach is that it thins out. The busiest section is near the main access point and the beachfront restaurants. Walk a few hundred metres either way and the crowd drops off quickly, along with the shade and the facilities, so carry water.


Climb to the Twin Beaches viewpoint


The small hill at the southwest end, where Nacpan meets Calitang, gives the view that appears in most photos of this coast: two long curves of sand on either side of the headland. The climb is short but steep. Wear shoes with grip, skip it in heavy rain, and ask locally whether the path is open before you go, since access can change.


Swim, but read the water first


This is open coast, not a sheltered cove. November to May usually brings calm water, while June to October can bring choppier seas and stronger currents. The area near the point between the two beaches can pull harder than it looks. Swim within sight of beachfront staff, keep children within reach, and do not assume a lifeguard is on duty.


Stay for sunset


Because the beach faces west with an open horizon, the late afternoon is the best light of the day. This is also where day-trippers go wrong: they stay for the sunset and miss the last shuttle. Confirm your return ride before the sky turns orange, or arrange a driver who waits.


Add Duli Beach if you have a driver


Duli sits about 30 minutes further north on a rougher road. It draws fewer people and a steadier swell, which is why surfers use it. Treat it as its own stop, not an extension of the same beach walk, and only add it if your transport is private and you are not racing a shuttle schedule.



Best time to visit Nacpan Beach


Season

November to May

The dry months give the calmest swimming and the clearest skies, with February to April usually the driest stretch. From June to October the sea can turn rough and the road gets harder, so keep the plan flexible and skip the swim if the waves say no.

Time of day

Arrive before 10 AM

Inland tour groups tend to reach the beach by midday. Being there by mid-morning gives you the quietest stretch of sand and cooler walking before the heat builds.

Sunset

Plan the way back first

The evening light is the reward, but the last shuttle and the dark road are the cost. Decide at lunch whether you are staying overnight or leaving before dusk.



Where to eat and stay near Nacpan


Food on the beach is simple: grilled fish and meat, Filipino plates, coconuts and fruit shakes from a handful of mostly locally run places. Sunmai Bar and Restaurant is one of the better-known stops near the main beach area. Supermarkets, pharmacies and banks are all back in El Nido town, so buy sunscreen, snacks and spare cash before you leave.


Cash deserves its own warning. There is no ATM along most of the beach. One machine near Sunmai is the usual fallback, and it can run out, so arrive with more than you think you need.


If you want to sleep by the sea, the options include Nacpan Beach Resort, Nacpan Beach Glamping and the social hostel Mad Monkey Nacpan Beach. Staying here suits people who care more about sunrise, sunset and quiet than nightlife and restaurant variety. For everything else, base yourself in town and see our guide to where to stay in El Nido. Check room rates, meals and the transfer back to town directly with each property before booking.

What to bring


  • Cash in small billsFor transport, food and any parking charge. Do not count on the ATM near the beach.
  • Drinking waterFood is easy to find near the main access point and scarce farther along.
  • Sun coverA hat, sunscreen and a rash guard. Shade is patchy once you leave the palms.
  • Shoes with gripFor the steep path up to the viewpoint, and for hot sand.
  • A dry bagPhones and cameras do not mix well with sand and spray.
  • Your return ride detailsKeep the shuttle booking or driver's number where you can reach it.


Where it sits on the map




Is Nacpan Beach worth the trip?


Yes, if you want one day in El Nido that does not depend on a boat crew. It is long, open and easy to be alone on, and the sunset is a better argument for the trip than any photo. It is less convincing if you only have a day or two in town, dislike bumpy roads, or expect a polished beach resort strip, because the stretch is still fairly simple and cash-based.


The easiest way to fit it in is to keep boat days and beach days separate. Do your lagoon tours first (our breakdown of Tours A, B, C and D helps you pick), then give Nacpan a full unhurried day so neither one feels rushed.



Frequently asked questions


Is there an entrance fee at Nacpan Beach?


There is no gate or ticket for the beach itself. Parking for your own bike or car can carry a small charge, and the El Nido eco-tourism fee applies to the municipality as a whole, not to Nacpan alone.


Can you swim at Nacpan Beach?


Yes, in calm conditions. Waves and currents change with the season and the wind, and the area near the headland between the two beaches can be stronger than it looks. Check the sea, swim near other people and do not rely on a lifeguard.


Is Nacpan part of the El Nido island-hopping tours?


No. Tours A, B, C and D are boat routes around Bacuit Bay. Nacpan is reached by road, so plan it as a separate day.


How far is Nacpan Beach from El Nido town?


About 17 kilometres north, roughly 45 minutes by road, depending on weather and the condition of the last unpaved section.


Can you stay overnight at Nacpan?


Yes. There are resorts, glamping and hostel-style rooms near the sand. Book ahead in the dry season and confirm how you will get to and from town.
