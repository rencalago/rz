Hot Air Balloon Fiesta Clark: Dates, Tickets, Getting There

Ren Calago 25 October 20260

The Philippine International Hot Air Balloon Fiesta runs on the weather, not on the printed schedule. Balloons only fly when the air is cool and calm, which in practice means a short window just after dawn and another in the late afternoon. Everything else in the program fills the hours in between. Plan your weekend around those two windows and the fiesta is easy to enjoy. Plan it like a regular concert and there's a good chance you'll arrive to an empty sky.


The event, known as PIHABF, is held every February in the Clark area of Central Luzon. Since 2024 it has used New Clark City in Capas, Tarlac, under a five-year arrangement, so the field is no longer inside Clark Freeport in Pampanga, where it ran for most of its history. The 2026 edition, the 26th, was held from February 13 to 15. Dates for 2027 come from the organizers at philballoonfest.net and their Facebook page, and they can move with weather and aviation clearances, so check there before you book flights or rooms.


This guide walks through how a fiesta day actually unfolds, what tickets and balloon rides cost at the last edition, how to reach New Clark City by car, plane or bus, and where to sleep so the 4 a.m. start doesn't wreck you.





Why a balloon festival started in Clark


The fiesta began as a recovery project. After Mount Pinatubo erupted in 1991, Clark and the surrounding Pampanga towns needed a reason for visitors to come back. Then Tourism Secretary Mina Gabor, British Airways executive John Emery and Korean balloonist Sung Kee Paik pushed the idea, and Capt. Joy Roa later led the organizing team. The first edition, in 1994 at the Omni Aviation Complex in Clark, had 21 pilots from 11 countries.


Its run since then has been bumpy. It stopped in 1999 over funding and air traffic concerns, sat out 2014 when the Department of Tourism held a separate balloon event, was cancelled in 2019 when government support came too late, moved to San Lazaro Leisure Park in Cavite for 2020, and went quiet from 2021 to 2023 during the pandemic. The 2024 comeback at New Clark City restored the full format and drew around 30,000 people over the weekend. It now bills itself as the longest running aviation sports event in Asia, under the tagline "A Weekend of Everything That Flies."


How a fiesta day unfolds, hour by hour


Before dawn: the mass launch


Gates open in the dark, and the crowd builds fast. Skydivers open the morning by jumping with the Philippine flag, usually around 5:30 a.m., and the balloons start inflating right after. Burners roar, envelopes swell on the grass, and within a few minutes dozens of them lift off together. The window is short. If you walk in at 6 a.m., the best part may already be drifting away over the field. Aim to be through the gate by 4:30 a.m.


Mid-morning to afternoon: everything else that flies


Once the balloons land, the rest of the aviation program takes over. Past schedules have included skydiving, paragliding and paramotor demonstrations, ultralight fly-bys, helicopter displays, radio-controlled model aerobatics, kite flying and search and rescue simulations by the Philippine Air Force. The 2026 lineup added FPV drone racing, drone soccer, a paper plane contest and the public debut of the Hoverbike Air X1, a manned drone built in the Philippines.


On the ground there's a fly market with aviation souvenirs, local crafts and food stalls, plus daytime aviation career seminars. Students thinking about flight school can get more out of those talks than out of the air show.


Late afternoon: the second flight


Winds usually settle again around 4 to 5 p.m., giving the pilots a second chance to fly. This is the session to watch for special shape balloons. Teams from abroad bring designs that look nothing like the classic teardrop, and past fiestas have featured a sunflower, a soda bottle and a giant pink elephant called Princess Nelly.


After sunset: Night Glow


The balloons stay tethered on the field after dark and light up their burners in time with music. Fireworks and a drone light show follow, with several hundred drones forming shapes above the crowd in recent editions. Local bands play the main stage into the evening.


Tickets and what to budget


Tickets are sold online through HelixPay, SM Tickets and Klook, and on site. Prices change every year, so treat the 2026 rates below as a guide and confirm on the official ticketing page:


  • General admission: about PHP 750 to 770 per person per day.
  • General admission with aviation seminar: about PHP 1,020.
  • VIP: above PHP 8,000 per day, with better viewing and priority entry.
  • Free-flight balloon ride: about PHP 25,000 per person for 30 to 60 minutes, minimum height 4 feet.
  • Tethered ride: about PHP 500 for roughly 10 minutes a few meters up, minimum height 3 feet.

Rides are booked at the info booth and depend on wind and pilot availability, so go there first thing if flying is the point of your trip. Children usually need their own tickets, though kid and group discounts are sometimes announced close to the event. Even with a digital ticket, carry cash for parking, food and market stalls.


Getting to New Clark City


New Clark City is in Capas, Tarlac, along the same Clark corridor as Angeles and well signed from SCTEX. It is not in Angeles City itself, which catches out people who book a hotel there expecting a short walk.


By car from Manila: take NLEX to SCTEX and exit at New Clark City or Clark. Expect 1.5 to 2.5 hours on a normal day and longer on festival mornings. If you're driving up for the dawn launch, leave Manila before 2 a.m., or sleep nearby the night before.


By plane: Clark International Airport is the closest airport. From there, a taxi or private transfer to the grounds takes about 30 to 45 minutes.


By bus: ride a bus bound for Capas or Tarlac, either via MacArthur Highway or SCTEX, and get off at Capas Municipal Hall, the KKK gas station area or the Bamban-Capas cloverleaf. Tricycles run from there to the grounds. Agree on the fare before you get in, since prices climb on event days.


Drivers are usually directed to designated parking fields, with shuttle jeeps to the entrance. Traffic advisories go out on the fiesta's official Facebook page in the days before.


Where to stay


Staying in the area lets you catch both flight windows without spending half the day on the road. Angeles City and Clark Freeport have the widest choice of hotels and restaurants. Capas and Bamban put you closest to the field. Tarlac City tends to be quieter and cheaper. Festival packages often name Park Inn by Radisson Clark, Quest Plus Conference Center Clark, Widus, Hilton Clark Sun Valley Resort and Microtel Tarlac, and the ones that bundle tickets with transfers take care of the hardest part, getting to the gate before dawn.


Small things that make the day easier


  • Dress for two temperatures. The pre-dawn field is chilly, and by midday there's almost no shade. Pack a light jacket along with a cap, sunglasses and sunscreen.
  • Expect dust. Wear closed shoes you don't mind ruining, and bring a mask or bandana.
  • Bring something to sit on. A mat or low folding chair makes the long gaps between flight windows much easier.
  • Save things offline. Mobile signal clogs with thousands of people on one field. Download your ticket QR code, hotel booking and maps, and carry a power bank.
  • Pack water. Food booths are plentiful, but lunch lines get long. A refillable bottle helps, and alcohol is usually restricted near the flight line.
  • Leave your drone at home. Visitors can't fly drones during the air shows. Check the advisory before packing one.
  • Plan photos ahead. Use a fast shutter and continuous focus for the launch, and brace your camera or bring a small tripod for Night Glow.
  • Mind the ground with kids and older relatives. Strollers manage, but the field is uneven grass and dirt, and the walk from parking can be long.

When the wind says no


Every flight depends on wind speed and visibility, and the pilots' briefing decides whether balloons go up on time, wait or stay down. A cancelled morning doesn't always mean a lost day, since organizers often try again in the afternoon. Listen to the PA announcements before heading for the exit. If seeing the balloons fly is the whole reason for your trip, book two mornings instead of one. That spare dawn is the closest thing to insurance the fiesta offers.


Making a weekend of it


Capas National Shrine is less than 5 kilometers from New Clark City and fits easily into a gap in the program. Hikers can stay an extra day for the Mount Pinatubo crater lake trek, which starts on the Capas side and ties in neatly with the fiesta's own story. Before heading back to Manila, a stop in Angeles or San Fernando for a Kapampangan food trip rounds things off. If February doesn't work for you, Pampanga's other big sky-lit event, the Giant Lantern Festival, takes place in December.


It's an early, loud and dusty weekend, and the crowds are real. But watching dozens of balloons lift off together against the Zambales mountains at first light is hard to find anywhere else in Luzon. Get there before the sky turns orange, and you'll see why people keep setting 3 a.m. alarms for it.
