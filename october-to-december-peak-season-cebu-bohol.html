Why October to December Is Peak Season in Cebu and Bohol

Ren Calago 15 October 2026

If you are planning a trip to Cebu or Bohol between October and December, expect company. The last three months of the year bring a run of holidays, year-end bonuses and family reunions, and that combination fills ferries, flights and resort rooms quickly.


The tourism department is counting on it. The Philippines recorded 4.42 million international arrivals from January to August 2026, and officials expect the final quarter to carry the country toward its target of at least 6.4 million for the year. In the tourism secretary's words, "The good months are the last quarter."


This guide explains what drives the late-year rush, which dates matter most, what to expect at ports and airports, and how to book so the crowds work around you rather than against you.




Why the Last Quarter Fills Up


Several things pile up at once. Holidays cluster from November to the end of December, so families get several long weekends in a short space of time. Private employers must pay the 13th month pay no later than 24 December, which puts extra money in people's pockets right before Christmas. Many Filipinos working abroad time their leave to be home for the holidays, and students are on break for part of December.


Foreign visitors add to the mix, with many timing beach trips for the drier months from December onward. Then the season rolls straight into January, when Cebu hosts the Sinulog on 17 January 2027. Our Sinulog 2027 update covers what has been announced so far.


The Dates That Drive Demand


These are the holidays and special non-working days from November to December 2026, under Proclamation No. 1006:


1 November (Sunday): All Saints' Day, special non-working day
2 November (Monday): All Souls' Day, special non-working day
30 November (Monday): Bonifacio Day, regular holiday
8 December (Tuesday): Feast of the Immaculate Conception, special non-working day
24 December (Thursday): Christmas Eve, special non-working day
25 December (Friday): Christmas Day, regular holiday
30 December (Wednesday): Rizal Day, regular holiday
31 December (Thursday): Last day of the year, special non-working day


Two stretches stand out. Undas creates a three-day weekend from 31 October to 2 November, and our Undas 2026 guide covers that rush in detail. Bonifacio Day on Monday, 30 November, gives another long weekend. Then Christmas and New Year sit close together, with 24 and 25 December falling on a Thursday and Friday and 30 and 31 December on a Wednesday and Thursday, so many people will take the whole week in between.


Simbang Gabi, the nine dawn Masses from 16 to 24 December, also draws big crowds to churches across Cebu and Bohol in the week before Christmas, so expect early-morning traffic near parishes.


What to Expect at Ports and Airports


Last year's numbers give a sense of scale. The Cebu Port Authority expected more than 560,000 passengers through Cebu ports over the Christmas and New Year period, with its holiday operation running from 20 December 2025 to 4 January 2026. For Undas 2025, it expected more than 350,000. Mactan-Cebu International Airport ran its own holiday operation over the same Christmas dates and urged passengers to plan ahead and arrive early.


At the ports, the advice is consistent every season: buy tickets online and in advance rather than queuing at the terminal, avoid resellers and scalpers, and bring a valid ID. During Undas 2025, Cebu terminals enforced a "No Ticket, No ID, No Entry" rule and asked passengers to arrive at least two hours before their sailing.


Cebu–Bohol crossings are popular with holiday travellers. If you are crossing for a short trip, book the ferry the moment your dates are fixed, and see our Bohol day trip from Cebu guide for how to plan the day around the sailings.


Typhoon Season Is Not Over Yet


Peak travel season overlaps with the tail end of typhoon season, and the Visayas have been hit hard late in the year before. Typhoon Odette struck Bohol and Cebu in December 2021. In November 2025, Typhoon Tino brought deadly flooding to Cebu, submerged parts of Talisay City and Cebu City, and led to more than 160 flight cancellations across the affected areas.


When a storm approaches, vessels are kept in port and sailings can be suspended at short notice, which can leave holiday travellers stuck on either side. Watch the PAGASA forecasts in the days before you travel, keep a spare day in your plans if you can, and choose bookings that allow you to change dates without losing your money.


How to Book Smarter


Book early, but flexibly. Rooms and ferries for the Christmas week go first. Reserve as soon as your dates are set, and favour rates that can be changed or cancelled in case the weather turns.


Travel on the shoulder days. Leaving a day before or after the busiest dates, such as 23 December or 26 December rather than Christmas Eve itself, often means shorter queues and better choice of sailings.


Use November weekdays. Outside the two long weekends, November weekdays are a good window for tours and island trips before the December crowds arrive.


Reserve tours with limits. Some attractions now cap visitor numbers each day, so popular trips can sell out on holiday weekends. Book these alongside your room rather than waiting until you arrive.


Keep cash and documents ready. Carry some cash for terminal fees and small purchases, and keep your ID and e-tickets within easy reach.


Planning Around the Rush


The last quarter is a lovely time to be in Cebu and Bohol, with festive lights, family gatherings and a full calendar of events. It is also the time when a little planning goes furthest. Lock in transport and rooms early, leave room for weather, and travel a day off the peak, and the season's crowds become much easier to live with.
