Cebu BRT Guide: Route, Stations, Fares and Tips

Ren Calago 26 October 20260

Cebu City finally has a bus rapid transit line you can ride. Since March 2026 the Cebu BRT, also written CBRT, has run one line of roughly 13 kilometres between Il Corso on the South Road Properties and Cebu IT Park in Lahug. It links the big malls by the sea, the south bus terminal, Fuente Osmeña, Capitol and Ayala, so a lot of the places a visitor ends up in sit close to a stop.


This guide covers where the line goes, which stations matter for travellers, what you pay, and the habits that make the ride smoother. One thing to keep in mind before you start: the system is still in partial operation. Some stretches run in ordinary traffic, the fleet is smaller than planned, and the long-term route is still being debated at City Hall. Treat the details below as how it works now, and check for changes before a big day out.



Where the Cebu BRT goes


Picture the line as a long hook. It starts at Il Corso on the reclaimed SRP, swings past Nustar and SM Seaside, comes inland along N. Bacalso Avenue past the Cebu South Bus Terminal, climbs Osmeña Boulevard through Fuente and Capitol, then turns toward the Ayala business district and ends at IT Park.




Northbound buses, heading from the SRP to IT Park, stop at Il Corso, Nustar, SM Seaside, SM Arena, Mambaling, SIT/CIT, V. Rama, CSBT, CNU, Fuente, Capitol, Sacred Heart, Ayala South, Pag-IBIG (Ayala), Samar Loop, Negros Road, Luz and IT Park.


Southbound trips run the same corridor in reverse but add City di Mare and El Pardo near the SRP end. If you are heading to one of those two stops, ride the southbound direction.


Buses stop only at stations. You cannot flag one down along the road or ask to be dropped at a corner the way you would on a jeepney, so pick your station before you board.



Stations worth knowing as a visitor


Il Corso, Nustar and SM Seaside


The southern end is all about the waterfront. Il Corso is an open-air mall facing the sea and a popular sunset walk. Nustar is the resort and casino complex next door, and SM Seaside is one of the largest malls in the city. Concert-goers can use the SM Arena stop for shows at the new arena at SM Seaside instead of fighting for a taxi when an event lets out.


CSBT for buses to the south


The CSBT stop serves the Cebu South Bus Terminal, where Ceres buses leave for Carcar, Moalboal, Oslob and the rest of southern Cebu. Provincial bus fares rose on 28 September 2026, and an air-conditioned bus to Moalboal now costs P240. Riding the BRT to the terminal with a small bag is a cheap way to start a beach trip. With a large suitcase, a taxi or Grab is less of a struggle.


Fuente, Capitol and CNU


This is the middle of the old uptown. Fuente Osmeña Circle is a landmark rotunda ringed by hotels, pharmacies and places to eat, and the Cebu Provincial Capitol sits at the top of Osmeña Boulevard. The historic downtown around Colon Street and Magellan's Cross is not on the line. From Fuente, finish that trip by jeepney or taxi.


Ayala and IT Park


Three stops serve the Ayala area: Ayala South, Pag-IBIG and Samar Loop. Use them for Ayala Center Cebu and the hotels around the business park. IT Park is the northern end and the city's late-night food district, with BPO workers keeping its restaurants busy well after midnight. It is also a sensible base if you plan to eat your way through the best lechon in Cebu, since several spots are a short ride away.



Fares and free ride hours


Fares are distance-based and follow the government's rate guide for air-conditioned city buses. At launch, the first 5 kilometres cost P18 and each extra kilometre added P2.97. At those rates, even the full 13 kilometres works out to about P42. Students, senior citizens and persons with disabilities get a 20% discount, with a minimum of P14.40. Students get the discount every day, holidays included.


Free ride hours have also run daily from 6 a.m. to 9 a.m. and from 5 p.m. to 8 p.m. Free windows like these can end with little notice, and public transport fares across the country were raised in late September. Before you count on a free trip, ask at the station or check the Cebu City government's announcements. Carry small bills and coins in case the ride is not free when you board.



What to expect on board


The stations are covered, raised and clearly marked, and the buses are air-conditioned, which already makes the ride more comfortable than a packed jeepney at noon. Speed is another matter. Parts of the route still lack dedicated lanes, and early in operations the city let jeepneys share the BRT lanes to ease congestion. Expect the trip to be steady rather than fast.


Not every planned bus was on the road when service began, so waits between buses can feel long, especially outside the morning and evening peaks. A reasonable plan is to allow at least 45 minutes for a full run from end to end and more during rush hour. If you have a flight or a ferry to catch, build in extra time or take a taxi.


Peak periods are crowded in the way any big-city bus is crowded. Keep your phone and wallet in a front pocket, wear your backpack in front, and give priority seats to older passengers, pregnant women and anyone with a child.



What the BRT does not cover


The line does not reach Mactan-Cebu International Airport, Lapu-Lapu, Mandaue or Talamban. For the airport, MyBus runs air-conditioned routes between the city and the terminals, and taxis and Grab are available at both ends. Hotels in Banilad, Talamban and the north reclamation area are also off the line.


Feeder routes toward Talisay in the south and Talamban in the north are part of the wider plan but are not yet in service. The Cebu City Council has also pushed for the main line to follow its original north to south alignment from Bulacao to Talamban. The Department of Transportation says that route has not been dropped. For visitors, this means the map may change over the next year or two, so check the current route before relying on an older guide, this one included.



A simple half-day ride


Want to try the line without a fixed errand? Board at IT Park late in the afternoon and ride south. Get off at Fuente for a look around the circle and a merienda, then hop back on and continue to Il Corso in time for sunset over the water. The SRP malls have plenty of dinner options, and taxis queue there in the evening for the ride back.


On days when jeepney groups stop running, as happened during the Cebu City transport strike, the BRT corridor becomes one of the few dependable ways across the city. Expect fuller buses, but it is worth remembering as a backup.



Quick tips before you ride


  • Board and get off only at stations; buses will not stop in between.
  • Check which direction you need: City di Mare and El Pardo are on southbound trips only.
  • Carry coins and small bills, and bring your student, senior or PWD ID for the discount.
  • Allow extra time at rush hour, and take a taxi when you have a flight or ferry to catch.
  • Check the latest route and fare announcements before a long trip, since the system is still expanding.

The Cebu BRT is not finished, and it still feels like it in places. For moving between the waterfront malls, the south bus terminal, uptown and IT Park, though, it is a cheap, cool and simple option that many visitors overlook.
