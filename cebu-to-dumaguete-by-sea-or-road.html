Cebu to Dumaguete: Ferry, Bus or Flight, and Which to Choose

Cebu and Dumaguete are separated by the Tañon Strait, and there is no single obvious way to cross it. Some travellers take an overnight ferry, some combine a bus with a short crossing, and others fly in about an hour. Each option trades time for money, and the cheapest one is not always the least tiring.


This guide sets out the main routes, the times and fares we could verify, and a simple way to choose. Schedules and prices on this route change often, and several sources disagree, so confirm the details with the operator or at the port before you travel.


Option One: The Overnight Ferry from Cebu Port


Cokaliong Shipping runs a roll-on, roll-off ferry from Cebu Port to Dumaguete Port. The crossing takes about six hours. A 2026 timetable lists one departure from Cebu at 8:00 PM on Mondays, Wednesdays and Saturdays, arriving at about 2:00 AM. The return leaves Dumaguete at 11:59 PM on Tuesdays, Thursdays and Sundays. Check the days carefully, since there is no daily service.



Economy seats start at about ₱480 one way, and cabins and suites cost considerably more. Fares may rise because of fuel surcharges, so confirm the current price when you book. The ferry suits travellers who want to sleep on the way and do not mind arriving in the small hours. Arriving at around 2:00 AM means you will need a room booked in advance.

Option Two: Bus to Liloan, then a Short Ferry to Sibulan


This is the quickest route by sea. Take a bus from Cebu City to Liloan, at the southern tip of Cebu, and cross to Sibulan on Negros, which sits beside Dumaguete. The crossing takes about 30 minutes, and Maayo Shipping runs the route with vessels for both passengers and vehicles.


Sailing times and fares differ between sources. One 2026 guide lists sailings every three hours from 6:00 AM to 9:00 PM and a passenger fare of ₱250 one way. Another gives a regular fare of ₱120, with nine sailings a day from 4:30 AM to 10:30 PM. Check the current timetable at the Liloan port before you set out. From Sibulan, a tricycle, taxi or ride-hailing car takes you into Dumaguete.


Plan the whole trip as a daytime journey, and allow more time than the crossing itself. A combined estimate for the bus and ferry legs is six to eight hours, though that figure comes from a single source and depends on traffic.


Option Three: Fast Craft


Some guides list a direct fast-craft sailing between Cebu and Dumaguete, with a crossing of four to six hours. We could not confirm one in the timetable we checked for August 2026, so treat it as unverified. Check with the operator before you plan around it. If a direct fast craft does run, check its fare against the overnight RORO before you choose.


Option Four: Fly


Flights from Mactan-Cebu International Airport to Dumaguete-Sibulan Airport take about an hour in the air. One guide lists one-way fares between ₱2,000 and ₱4,000, depending on season and how early you book. The main airlines on the route are Philippine Airlines, Cebu Pacific and AirAsia. Remember the airport transfers at each end, since they can add a long time and extra cost. The airport is not in the city, so budget for a ride into Dumaguete.


Which Option to Choose


If you are on a budget and do not mind an overnight trip, the RORO from Cebu Port is the cheapest option, with cabins available at higher fares. If you want the day with you and a short crossing, the bus and Liloan ferry is the best choice, provided the timetable suits you. If your time is short or you are travelling with a lot of luggage, the flight is more comfortable, though the transfers make it less simple than the air time suggests.


If you are bringing a car, the Liloan crossing takes vehicles, and the Cokaliong ferry is a roll-on, roll-off vessel. Confirm the vehicle rate before you travel, because the fares differ by vehicle type.


Planning Tips


Book the overnight ferry in advance, especially for weekend sailings. Keep cash for terminal fees, tricycles and snacks, because some ports and stalls do not take cards. Arrive at the port early, and keep your ticket and ID handy. Weather can cancel sailings, especially during the rainy months, so keep one flexible day in your plans.
