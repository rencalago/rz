Manila to Baguio by Bus: Routes, Fares and Terminals

Ren Calago 28 October 20260

Three bus companies run most trips from Metro Manila to Baguio: Victory Liner, Genesis (with its premium JoyBus coaches) and Solid North. Buses leave from Cubao, Pasay, Sampaloc, Caloocan, Avenida and PITX, and the ride takes about four to six hours depending on traffic and stops. One-way fares quoted in 2026 range from around ₱650 for a regular air-conditioned seat to about ₱1,000 for a 2x1 premium coach, and over ₱1,500 for Victory Liner's Royal Class.


That is the short version. The longer one depends on where in Manila you are starting, how much legroom you want, and whether you are travelling on an ordinary Tuesday or the Friday before a long weekend. This guide compares the operators, terminals, bus classes and fares, then covers booking, the best departure times and what happens when you arrive in Baguio.


Fares move with fuel prices and government-approved fare adjustments, and they changed more than once in 2026. Treat the figures here as a guide to budgeting, and confirm the exact fare when you book.




The Three Main Operators


Victory Liner


Victory Liner has the widest network and the most terminals in Metro Manila: Cubao, Pasay, Caloocan, Sampaloc and Avenida, among others. In Baguio, its terminal is on Upper Session Road in Marcoville, a short taxi ride or a downhill walk from the city centre. It runs four classes on the route: Regular Aircon, Deluxe, First Class and Royal Class. Express trips take the expressways with fewer stops, and that makes a real difference to travel time.


Genesis and JoyBus


Genesis Transport runs standard Genesis buses and the premium JoyBus line. Departures leave from Cubao, Pasay, Avenida, Sampaloc and other Manila terminals. JoyBus coaches use the TPLEX route via the Rosario exit, and their Executive and Premier classes have 2x1 seating, 28 seats per bus, an onboard toilet, footrests, blankets, and snacks with bottled water. For many travellers, JoyBus hits the sweet spot between comfort and price.


Solid North


Solid North runs Deluxe buses from Cubao and both Deluxe and Luxury services from PITX in Parañaque. That PITX link is handy if you are coming from the airport or from the south of Metro Manila, since it saves a long ride north through EDSA traffic just to reach a bus terminal.


Bus Classes Explained


  • Regular Aircon: 2x2 seating with 45 to 49 seats, no toilet on most buses, and more stops. The cheapest option.
  • Deluxe: 2x2 seating with more legroom, around 41 seats, and an onboard toilet. A comfortable middle ground.
  • First Class, JoyBus Executive and Premier: 2x1 reclining seats, around 28 to 29 per bus, a toilet, snacks and water. Usually express, with no stops or one short one.
  • Victory Liner Royal Class: the top tier, with sleeper-style seats. The priciest ticket on the route.

For a daytime trip, Deluxe is enough for most people. For an overnight trip, a 2x1 seat makes it much easier to sleep, and arriving rested is worth the extra few hundred pesos.


Fares at a Glance


These are one-way fares in pesos quoted between March and August 2026. Fares from Pasay tend to run slightly higher than from Cubao, since the trip is longer.


  • Victory Liner Regular Aircon: about ₱650 to ₱750
  • Victory Liner Deluxe: about ₱785 to ₱870
  • Victory Liner First Class: about ₱1,055 to ₱1,140
  • Victory Liner Royal Class: about ₱1,580 to ₱1,650
  • Genesis Regular Aircon: about ₱650 to ₱670
  • Genesis Deluxe: about ₱765 to ₱795
  • JoyBus Executive and Premier: about ₱970 to ₱1,035
  • Solid North Deluxe: about ₱665 to ₱850, depending on the terminal
  • Solid North Luxury from PITX: about ₱1,080

Students, seniors and persons with disabilities are entitled to discounts on public transport fares with a valid ID. Online bookings sometimes carry a small service fee on top of the fare.


Which Terminal Should You Use?


Choose by where you are starting, not by which terminal is famous. Cubao is the busiest hub and has the most departures across all three operators, which makes it the safest choice if you are flexible. Pasay suits anyone staying in Makati, Pasay or near the airport. Sampaloc and Avenida are convenient for the University Belt and the older parts of Manila. PITX is the best fit if you are coming from Cavite, Las Piñas, Parañaque or straight from NAIA.

Booking Tickets


Victory Liner and Solid North both sell some seats online, and booking ahead guarantees your seat. Genesis and JoyBus post schedules and updates on their official Facebook page, and you can also buy at the terminal.


Walk-in tickets are fine on ordinary weekdays. For Friday nights, long weekends, Holy Week, Christmas, New Year and the Panagbenga festival in February, book several days ahead or expect to wait for a later bus. Return trips on Sunday afternoons and holiday Mondays sell out quickly too.


Best Time to Leave


Night buses are popular for a reason. Leave Manila around 10 p.m. to midnight and you skip the worst city traffic, sleep through the climb, and arrive in Baguio in the early morning with a full day ahead. The trade-off is reaching a cold city before most cafés and hotels are open, so pack a jacket in your hand-carry, and ask your hotel about early check-in or bag storage.


Daytime departures are better for first-timers who want to see the road up, and for families with small children. Avoid leaving Manila on Friday afternoon, when city traffic adds hours before the bus reaches the expressway.


Arriving in Baguio


Buses arrive at their company terminals near the city centre, not at one shared station. From Victory Liner on Upper Session Road, it is a short downhill walk or taxi ride to Session Road and Burnham Park. Taxis wait near the terminals, and short rides around the centre are cheap.


Mornings in Baguio can be cold, especially from December to February. Once you have dropped your bags, a slow walk around the parks suits a tired traveller well. The new Baguio Arboretum next to the Botanical Garden is a quiet first stop after an overnight ride.


Is the Bus the Best Way to Reach Baguio?


For most people coming from Manila, yes. Loakan Airport has very limited commercial service, so the bus is the main public option. It is cheaper than driving once you count fuel, tolls and parking, and far less stressful than finding a parking spot in Baguio on a holiday weekend.


Pick the terminal closest to where you start, choose Deluxe or a 2x1 coach for comfort, book ahead for busy dates, and plan for a cold arrival. For what to do once you are there, browse our Baguio travel guides.
