Bislig City Travel Guide: Tinuy-an Falls and Beyond

Most people reach Bislig for one reason, and it is a good one. Tinuy-an Falls, the wide curtain of water in Barangay Borboanan, is the picture on every Surigao del Sur brochure. But Bislig is a city of close to 100,000 people on the Pacific side of Mindanao, and once you have made the long trip in, there is a bay, a scatter of rock pools and beaches, and a town with an unusual past waiting beyond the falls.


This guide treats Bislig as a destination rather than a waterfall stop. It covers how the city is laid out, what else is worth a few hours, what to eat, how to get in, and a two-day plan that leaves room for the weather.


Small attractions run by barangays or private owners change their fees and hours without much notice. The prices below are for budgeting, and the ticket window, your driver or your hotel will have the current figure.




Bislig at a glance

  • Where: Surigao del Sur, Caraga Region, on the Pacific coast of eastern Mindanao
  • Where you arrive: Mangagoy, the commercial district where buses and vans stop
  • Gateways: Butuan to the north or Davao to the south, both several hours by road
  • Main sight: Tinuy-an Falls, about 45 minutes to an hour from Mangagoy by road
  • Also worth a look: Kawa-Kawa rock pools, Hagonoy Island, Lawigan Beach, the baywalk
  • Time to give it: two nights, with room for a rain delay

Start in Mangagoy, Not the Old Town


First-timers often get confused by the names. Bislig is the city, but the busy part where you will sleep, eat and catch rides is Mangagoy, which grew up beside the old paper mill and the port. Buses and vans often show MANGAGOY on the signboard instead of Bislig, so that is the place to watch for.


Mangagoy has the hotels, banks, the public market, eateries and the habal-habal and tricycle drivers you will need for everything else. Base yourself here. The attractions fan out in different directions, so a central bed saves time even when the room is plain.


Tinuy-an Falls Comes First


The falls deserve your best morning. The main curtain is roughly 95 meters across and about 55 meters high, dropping over several rock steps into a broad basin, and it sits inside a protected landscape of lowland forest. Entrance is ₱50, bamboo rafts carry groups close to the spray, and sunny mornings between about 9 and 11 AM can throw a rainbow across the lower tier.


Our Tinuy-an Falls fees and raft guide goes through the raft prices, the upper tiers and habal-habal fares from Mangagoy in detail, so this guide will not repeat them. The one thing worth saying twice is that heavy rain can shut the site. In February 2026 the city suspended operations at the falls under a rainfall warning, and that is why a spare day matters on this coast.


More Water: Pools, an Island and a Pacific Beach


Bislig has a lot of water that most visitors never see. These are the easiest places to add once the falls are done.


  • Kawa-Kawa sa Awog, Barangay San JoseA string of round rock pools fed by cold water running down from the hills. The name comes from kawa, the local word for a big cooking pan, which is exactly what the hollows look like. Entry has been around ₱20, with cottages for rent. Weekends are busy with local families, so go on a weekday if you can.
  • Hagonoy Island, Bislig BayA small, privately owned island in the bay with fine pale sand. You can walk around it in about ten minutes. Pump boats go from the Mangagoy side, and the caretaker collects a small landing fee. Bring your own food and water. Do not confuse it with the Hagonoy islet on the Britania island-hopping tour in San Agustin.
  • Lawigan BeachThe open Pacific side of the city, about 13 km from the centre, with simple resorts and cottages. Upkeep varies and the shore can collect litter after rough weather, so check recent photos first. The swell here is ocean swell, so watch the water before letting children in.
  • Bislig BaywalkThe city's waterfront promenade and an easy way to spend the hour before dinner. It needs no fee and no planning, which suits an evening after a long day on the road.

The city has more inland falls and caves that turn up on travel listings, such as Sian Falls. Roads and trails to those change with the season, so ask your driver or the city tourism office before you set out, and take a local guide for anything off a paved road.


A Paper Town's Story


Bislig's size makes more sense once you know about PICOP, the Paper Industries Corporation of the Philippines. It began as a logging company in the 1950s, took the PICOP name in 1963 and grew into what was once the biggest pulp and paper mill in Asia, with around 20,000 workers at its height. Roads, the port and much of Mangagoy were built around it.


The mill stopped running in the 2000s after log bans, debt and labour disputes, and it has been tied up in receivership and court cases ever since. Plans to revive it come up from time to time. For a visitor, the story explains why a city so far from the big hubs grew this large and has its own port, and why locals are keen to see tourism fill some of the gap.


What to Eat in Bislig


The Bislig table

Three things to look for

  • Kinilaw and grilled seafoodThis is a fishing coast, so raw fish dressed in vinegar and grilled catch of the day are the safe orders at local eateries.
  • Puto balanghoyA cassava treat sold around the market. Buy it fresh and eat it the same day.
  • SalvaroA flat, crunchy local cookie that keeps for days, which makes it the pasalubong to carry home.

Getting to Bislig and Getting Around


There is no quick way in. From the north, fly to Butuan and take a bus or van south for roughly four to five hours. From the south, buses leave Davao's Ecoland terminal for Mangagoy, and the trip usually takes six hours or more. If Davao is already on your trip, our Davao, Samal and Mount Apo guide covers the city end of that route.


Inside Bislig, habal-habal and tricycles do most of the work. Agree on the fare, the waiting time and the return trip before you leave Mangagoy, especially for the falls and Lawigan, where finding a ride back is harder. A hired van or car makes sense for a group, or for joining Bislig with Hinatuan's Enchanted River to the north. Our Britania Islands and Enchanted River guide covers that stretch of coast.


Two Days in Bislig


  1. 01Day 1The falls and the rock pools

    Leave Mangagoy around 7:30 AM to reach Tinuy-an before the crowds and in time for the rainbow window. Head back for lunch, then spend the afternoon at Kawa-Kawa. End with a walk along the baywalk and seafood for dinner.

  2. 02Day 2The bay or the Pacific

    Take a morning boat to Hagonoy Island while the sea is usually calmer, or ride out to Lawigan for a slow beach day. Pick up salvaro before you leave. If rain closed the falls on Day 1, swap the days around.


Before You Go


Plan ahead

  • Watch the rain, not the calendarBislig gets more than 4,000 mm of rain in a typical year, and January is usually the wettest month. Check the forecast a few days out and ask your hotel the night before whether the falls are open.
  • Carry small billsEntrance fees, rafts, cottages, boats and drivers all want cash, and change is often short outside Mangagoy.
  • Pack for wet rockShoes with grip, a dry bag and a phone pouch will see you through the falls, the pools and the boat.
  • Leave the site cleanerBins are few at the pools and on the island, so bring a bag for your trash and take it back to town.

Is Bislig Worth Two Nights?


If you are already coming for Tinuy-an, yes. The trip in is the expensive part, in hours more than pesos, and one extra night turns a rushed waterfall visit into a proper look at the city: a quiet island in the bay, cold pools in the hills and an evening by the water in a town still working out what comes after the mill. Go in the drier months if your dates allow it, keep a day free for rain, and let Mangagoy be your base.
