Victoria's Mountainview Resort in Tanjay City: Hilltop Dining With a View

Ren Calago 13 Feb 2023 Updated 22 October 2026

Victoria's Mountainview is a hillside restaurant in the uplands above Tanjay City, Negros Oriental, and people drive up for one thing: the view. Lunch or dinner comes with a wide stretch of green countryside below the veranda, and on a clear day nothing gets in the way of it. We went in February 2023 on a bright, sunny day and spent about an hour there, eating, walking around and taking far too many photos.


This post covers what the drive up is like, what you will find when you arrive, when to go for the best view, and how to fit the trip into a day around Tanjay and the towns next to it.




Where it is and how long the drive takes


The restaurant sits outside the Tanjay town centre, up in the hills. Plan on 15 to 30 minutes of driving from the city proper, depending on your vehicle and how relaxed you are on hill roads. Tanjay is on the coastal highway north of Dumaguete, between San Jose and Bais, so a stop here is easy to work into a drive up the coast.


Once you turn off the highway, the roads get narrow. That sounds worse than it is. The whole route is cemented, so there are no dirt tracks or muddy patches to worry about, and traffic is light because the road is used mostly by people who live along it. Take the bends slowly and give way to motorbikes coming the other way.


The climb up feels long the first time. Coming back down to the highway took us roughly half the time, partly because we knew the way by then and partly because it is all downhill.


No car? Arrange a ride from the town centre for the round trip rather than one way, since you cannot count on finding transport waiting at the top when you want to leave.


What it is like when you arrive


Parking is generous and sits at the back of the building, on the side that faces the view. That means the scenery is the first thing you see when you get out of the car, before you have even walked inside.


The place mixes modern finishes with the look of a traditional Filipino home. The roofs are thatched with cogon, the native grass once used on houses all over the countryside, and it gives the dining area a cool, shaded and relaxed feel. It comes across more like a big family house in the hills than a resort.


Our favourite spot was the roofless veranda. It is open to the sky, catches the breeze, and gives you the clearest look at the countryside. Most people end up there at some point, either for photos or just to stand and take in the fresh air for a few minutes.




Food and drinks


The menu is wide enough for a group with different tastes, and there is beer and other alcohol if you want a drink with the view. Dishes and prices change over time, so look at the restaurant's Facebook page for what is being served now.


If you are the one driving, save the drinks for another day. The road down is narrow and winding, and you will want a clear head for it, especially after dark.


Best time to go for the view


Weather decides how good your visit is. Photos of Victoria's Mountainview shared online often show fog rolling over the hills, and we half expected to see some ourselves. We got the opposite: a sunny day with clear air and an open view all the way out. Both are worth seeing, they are just different trips.


If you want a clear, far-reaching view, pick a dry, sunny day and check the sky before you leave town. Low cloud during the rainy months can hide the countryside completely. If you are hoping for fog, your best chance is early in the day or after rain, when the air in the hills is cool and damp.


Lunch or dinner? Midday gives you the brightest, widest view, though the light can be harsh for photos. Late afternoon is softer and cooler, and a good middle ground. Dinner trades the daytime scenery for a quieter evening, but it also means driving down a narrow hill road in the dark, so only do it if you are comfortable with that.




Opening hours and practical tips


When we visited, Victoria's Mountainview was open daily from 7am to 8pm. Hours can change, particularly around holidays, so confirm on their Facebook page before you make the trip up. A few other things that help:


  • Bring enough cash for the meal and your ride. Do not rely on finding an ATM once you are up in the hills.
  • Pack a light layer if you are going early in the morning or staying into the evening, when the uplands are cooler than the town.
  • If you are riding a motorbike, check your brakes and fuel before the climb. The road is steady uphill for most of the way.
  • Give yourself at least an hour once you arrive. We stayed about that long, and it went quickly between the food and the photos.
  • Take your time on the veranda. It is the best spot for photos, and on a busy day it is worth waiting for a quieter moment.

Making a day of it around Tanjay


Tanjay sits right next to Bais, which makes for an easy pairing. Spend the morning on the water with our guide to Bais dolphin watching and the Manjuyod sandbar, then head into the Tanjay hills for a late lunch with the view.


If you like eating somewhere green and away from the highway, Pamplona is just inland from Tanjay, and our post on Sakada Cafe and Farm in Pamplona covers another farm-style food stop in the area.
