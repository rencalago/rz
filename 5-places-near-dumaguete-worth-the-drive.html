5 Places Near Dumaguete Worth the Drive

Ren Calago 10 October 20260

One of the best things about visiting Dumaguete is that you don't have to stay within the city to find something worth exploring. A short drive can take you to mountain waterfalls, forest-covered lakes, quiet coastal towns and marine destinations that feel completely different from the city center.


Whether you're visiting for a weekend or already living in Negros Oriental, these nearby destinations can turn an ordinary day into a memorable trip. Some are easy half-day escapes, while others deserve a full day and an early start. Here are five places near Dumaguete worth adding to your itinerary.


1. Valencia: Waterfalls, Hot Springs and Mountain Air


If you want to escape the coastal heat without spending several hours on the road, Valencia is one of the easiest choices. Located west of Dumaguete, the municipality is known for its cooler upland surroundings, forest scenery, waterfalls and natural hot springs.


Casaroro Falls is one of its best-known attractions. Reaching the waterfall involves a long staircase and a rocky walk beside the river, so wear shoes with good grip and allow enough time for the return climb. The route can become slippery or unsafe after heavy rain, and visitors should follow local guidance about trail access.


Another option is Pulangbato Falls, known for the reddish rock formations around the water. You can also look into nearby hot springs or nature resorts if you prefer a more relaxed visit instead of a demanding trek.


Valencia is roughly 20 to 30 minutes from central Dumaguete by car, depending on traffic and your destination. It works well as a half-day trip, although combining several stops can easily fill most of the day.


2. Twin Lakes: A Scenic Escape into the Highlands


For travelers who prefer mountain views and quiet scenery, the Twin Lakes Natural Park is another destination worth considering. The protected area in the highlands of Sibulan is known for Lake Balinsasayao and Lake Danao, surrounded by forested slopes and rich biodiversity.


The appeal here is different from Valencia. Rather than visiting several waterfalls and swimming spots, you can spend more time appreciating the landscape, taking photographs, enjoying the cooler air and learning about the natural environment.


Depending on current conditions and available services, visitors may be able to explore designated viewpoints, walking areas or lake activities. Check the park's latest visitor rules before setting out, since access and activities can vary.


The mountain road takes longer than a trip to Valencia, and the final journey can be winding. Leave early, bring water and a light jacket, and avoid rushing the drive. Twin Lakes is best for travelers who want nature to be the main event rather than simply another stop on a packed itinerary.


3. Dauin: A Coastal Town for Diving and Slow Travel


Head south from Dumaguete and you'll reach Dauin, a coastal municipality known for its diving sites and marine life. It offers a different experience from the city's waterfront, with dive resorts, shore-accessible marine areas and a more relaxed seaside atmosphere.


Dauin is particularly popular with divers interested in underwater photography and small marine creatures found in sandy and reef environments. You don't need to be an experienced diver to enjoy the area, though: you can also look into snorkeling options or simply spend time at a beachside resort.


If you plan to enter a marine sanctuary, use an authorized operator when required, follow local conservation rules and never touch or stand on coral. Conditions, fees and access arrangements should be confirmed before your visit.


Dauin is commonly around 30 to 45 minutes from Dumaguete by road, depending on where you're going. It makes a good day trip, but it can also be worth staying overnight if diving is the main reason for your visit.


4. Apo Island: A Marine Adventure Worth Planning For


If you want a trip that feels more adventurous, consider Apo Island, off the coast of Dauin and Zamboanguita. The island is well known for its marine sanctuary, coral reefs and opportunities to see sea turtles in their natural environment.


Getting there takes more planning than visiting the waterfalls or nearby towns. Travelers commonly travel by road to the boat departure area at Malatapay in Zamboanguita before taking a boat across to the island. Travel time depends on the departure point, sea conditions and boat arrangements.


Snorkeling is one of the main attractions, but the island is also appealing to visitors who simply want to appreciate its coastal scenery and small-island atmosphere. If you want to snorkel, confirm whether a local guide is required and what fees or equipment are included in your trip.


Apo Island is best planned as a full-day outing. Bring enough drinking water, sun protection and cash, and check the weather and sea conditions before departing. Never touch, chase or feed sea turtles, and avoid stepping on coral while snorkeling.


5. Bais City and Manjuyod Sandbar: A Longer Coastal Day Trip


If you're willing to drive farther north, Bais City and nearby Manjuyod offer a different kind of adventure. The area is known for dolphin-watching trips and the beautiful white sandbar often associated with Manjuyod.


Dolphin-watching trips generally involve an early start and a boat ride, while visiting the sandbar depends on tides and weather. The appearance and size of the exposed sand can change throughout the day, so check the tide schedule and boat arrangements rather than assuming the sandbar will look the same at every hour.


This is a good option for groups or families who want a full day built around the sea. However, it requires more time on the road than Valencia or Dauin, and the boat portion adds another cost to consider. Ask the operator about the total price, boat capacity, waiting time, environmental fees and what happens if weather prevents the trip.


For the best experience, arrange transport and boat details in advance, leave Dumaguete early and avoid squeezing the trip into a tight schedule.


Which Destination Should You Choose?


The best destination depends on how much time you have and what kind of scenery you want to experience.


Choose Valencia for waterfalls, hot springs and a quick mountain escape. Visit Twin Lakes if you prefer forest scenery and a quieter nature trip. Head to Dauin for diving, snorkeling and a relaxed coastal atmosphere. Choose Apo Island for a full-day marine adventure, or travel to Bais and Manjuyod if you're ready for a longer outing that combines road travel and a boat trip. For quieter towns further out, see our five Negros Oriental towns worth the detour.


You don't need to visit all five in one stay. In fact, trying to squeeze them into a single trip would mean spending too much time traveling instead of enjoying each place. Pick one or two that match your interests, then leave enough room for unexpected stops along the way.


Make the Drive Part of the Experience


One reason Dumaguete works well as a travel base is the variety of destinations within reach. In one direction, you can head into the mountains; in another, you can follow the coast toward diving areas and boat departures. Farther north, a longer drive opens up another set of coastal experiences.


Before leaving, check road and weather conditions, confirm current entrance fees and opening arrangements, and agree on transport costs if you're hiring a driver or motorcycle taxi. Public transport may get you to the town center, but individual attractions can be spread out and may require another ride.


The best trips aren't always the ones with the longest checklist. Sometimes, choosing one destination, leaving early and taking your time is enough to discover another side of Negros Oriental.
