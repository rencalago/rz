Bais Dolphin Watching and the Manjuyod Sandbar: A Day Trip from Dumaguete

Bais sits on the Tañon Strait, a short boat ride from the coast of Negros Oriental and within reach of Dumaguete for a day out. Most people come for one of two things: the dolphins that move through the strait, or the Manjuyod sandbar, a long strip of white sand that appears when the tide goes out. Many visitors combine both in a single morning, though the sea and the tide decide how much of each you will see.


This guide covers the practical side of the trip: how to reach Bais, what a boat usually costs, the timing that makes the sandbar worth the trip, and what to expect from the dolphins. Prices and schedules here come from sources that are not always dated, so confirm them with the Bais Tourism Office or your boatman before you set out.


Dolphins are never guaranteed. Sightings depend on the water, the weather and luck, and the best plan is one where the sandbar still makes the trip worthwhile if the pod does not appear.



Getting to Bais from Dumaguete


Buses run from Dumaguete to Bais, and one travel guide describes a Ceres bus departing about every half hour. The trip is short, usually under an hour, though the road and the stops along the way add time. Confirm the schedule at the terminal in Dumaguete before you leave, since departures change and the early ones matter if you want the sandbar at low tide.


Once you reach Bais, head for the wharf where the boats leave. Ask at the Bais Tourism Office first. Local boatmen also approach visitors directly, and the office is a simpler place to compare prices and confirm what the boat includes.


What a Boat Costs


Boat hire is the main expense, and it is shared among the people on board. Published figures vary. One guide puts a shared boat from the wharf at about ₱3,000, while a second source gives a general range of ₱2,000 to ₱5,000 for a boat that includes a dolphin-watching side trip. The Tourism Office has also listed a boat costing ₱3,500 that holds more than ten people, so the cost per person drops when the group is larger.


Ask what the price includes before you agree. Find out whether it covers the dolphin stop, the sandbar, snorkelling and the return, and how long the trip takes. Docking is reported as free. Renting a stilt house on the sandbar for the day is optional and was reported at about ₱1,500, so check that price on the day too.


Manjuyod Sandbar: Timing and What to Bring


The Manjuyod sandbar is about 7 kilometres long and sits roughly 15 minutes by boat from Bais. The sand is at its widest when the tide is low, and the water around it is shallow and clear. The morning is the best time to visit, before the sun is high and before other boats arrive. One source recommends 7 to 10 in the morning, which is a sensible window to aim for.


There are no restaurants on the sandbar, so pack lunch, water and sunscreen. Vendors sell fresh coconut juice, and fishermen sometimes sell sea urchins, fish and oysters at low prices. Take your rubbish back with you. Sandbars are fragile, and the ones that stay beautiful are the ones that stay clean.


Check the tide before you book. The sandbar shrinks or vanishes when the water rises, and a boatman who knows the tide table will plan the stop around it. If you arrive at high tide, you may see the water and the boats but very little sand.


Dolphin Watching in the Tañon Strait


Spotted and spinner dolphins are the species most often mentioned in the strait. The watching happens from the boat. Boats usually keep a distance, and the sightings come when a pod surfaces nearby. Expect to wait. One first-hand account describes an hour on the water before the dolphins appeared, which is a reasonable amount of time to plan for rather than a promise.


Swimming with the dolphins is not allowed, according to the source we checked. That is a sensible rule. Dolphins that are followed or approached by many boats change their behaviour, and the animals are better served by a quiet boat that waits for them to come close on their own.


Some visitors add a whale or dolphin tour from Capiñahan Wharf, which is listed as a departure point in one guide. Whale sightings are less common in this area and are never a certainty, so treat them as a bonus and not the reason for the trip.


Who This Day Trip Suits


The Bais trip works well for people who are already in Dumaguete for a few days and want a quieter morning on the water. It is a good choice for families who are comfortable in small boats, and for anyone who enjoys wildlife and open sea. It suits sandbar lovers who do not mind a basic setting, since the sandbar has no facilities.


It is less suitable if you have a poor sense of balance in small boats, or if you need toilets and a shaded restaurant close at hand. Sun exposure is serious on the sandbar, so people with fair skin or any condition that makes heat a problem should plan carefully and bring a hat and plenty of water.


Planning the Day


Leave Dumaguete early enough to reach the wharf by late morning at the latest. Aim for the boat that will take you to the dolphins first, then the sandbar once the tide allows. Keep cash in small notes, since boatmen and vendors rarely carry change. If the dolphins are slow, use the time to rest on the boat and enjoy the water, rather than chasing a pod.
