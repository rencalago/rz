# Donsol vs Oslob Whale Sharks: Which Should You Choose?

If you want to swim with whale sharks in the Philippines, you will end up comparing the same two names: Donsol in Sorsogon and Oslob in Cebu. Both call their whale sharks *butanding*, both offer snorkeling encounters, but the way they work is completely different.

Donsol is seasonal and wild. Whale sharks come into Donsol Bay naturally to feed on plankton and krill, no one feeds them, and sightings are never guaranteed. Oslob is year-round and fed. Fishermen hand-feed krill every morning to keep a few sharks near the boats, so you are almost certain to see them, but it changes how the sharks behave.

This guide breaks down the real differences — ethics, costs in PHP, rules, best months, and how to get to each from Cebu and Manila — so you can decide which experience fits how you want to travel.

## 1. The Core Difference: Wild Encounter vs Fed Encounter

Donsol has been branded the Whale Shark Capital of the World since the late 1990s when the community stopped hunting them and moved to ecotourism. The local government enforces a strict no-feeding, no-touching, no-scuba policy. Boats go out to search for 2-3 hours, spotters look for fins from the bow, and when a shark is seen the BIO (Butanding Interaction Officer) drops you in the water at a distance. You swim alongside for a few minutes and the shark swims away.

Oslob in Tan-awan started around 2011 when fishermen discovered that feeding attracted whale sharks. The municipality now runs it as a timed activity. You go to a briefing area, get on a small paddle boat, are towed 10 minutes to the interaction zone, and snorkel in a roped area for 30 minutes while feeders in separate boats throw food. It is efficient and photogenic, but peer-reviewed research from LAMAVE (Large Marine Vertebrates Research Institute Philippines) has documented changes in the Oslob sharks’ natural behavior, surface-feeding, boat collision scars, and habituation to people.

**Best for:**
- **Choose Donsol if** you want a wild encounter, do not mind not seeing a shark on day one, and care about non-feeding tourism.
- **Choose Oslob if** you have limited time in Cebu, want near-certainty, and are traveling with kids or first-time snorkelers who need calm water.

**What to do:**
- In Donsol, you will get an orientation video at the Donsol Whale Shark Interaction Center. Listen to the 10-minute briefing — it explains the 3-meter distance rule and how the BIO will pull you out if you get too close.
- In Oslob, you will get a short orientation about not touching, no flash, no sunscreen, and staying 4 meters from the shark. In practice boats are crowded, so keep your fins low and give the animal space even if others do not.

## 2. Best Months: Feb-May vs Year-Round

Donsol season runs from November to early June, with the sweet spot **February to May**. That is when plankton concentration is highest in the bay. Late March to late May reports show 85-95% sighting success on shared boats if you stay 2-3 days. Outside season, boats do not operate for whale sharks — operators switch to firefly watching and Ticao Island manta tours.

Oslob operates year-round, 6 days a week, closed on some Fridays for “rest day” during the pandemic pattern and now mostly daily 6:00 AM to 11:30 AM, with cut-off around 10:00 AM. Best weather in Cebu is December to May, with calmer seas March to May. June to September is habagat season — still operating but rougher and more rain.

**Best for:** Plan Donsol as a destination trip between February and May. Use Oslob as a day trip if you are already in Cebu and cannot align with Donsol season.

## 3. Fees, Permits, and Real Costs in PHP

Rates change yearly, so confirm at the centers, but these are the 2025-2026 figures posted by LGUs and operators:

**Donsol:**
- Registration / environmental fee: PHP 100 per local tourist, PHP 300 per foreign tourist (paid once per day at the Interaction Center, open 6:00 AM)
- Boat rental: PHP 3,500 per boat good for up to 7 persons (includes skipper, spotter, BIO). Newer listing at PHP 5,000 per boat for 4 hours during peak — budget PHP 5,000 now. Price is per boat, not per person.
- Shared boat option: If you are solo, you can join a boat by paying PHP 500 per head and waiting for the center to fill a 6-7 person boat.
- Gear: Mask and snorkel rental PHP 150-300, life vest included. Resorts often include it.
- For a group of 3 sharing: PHP 1,167-1,667 per person boat + PHP 300 fee = PHP 1,467-1,967 per person per day.
- Accommodation in Donsol town: PHP 1,200-2,500 guesthouse, PHP 3,000-5,500 resort.

**Oslob:**
- LGU whale shark watching fee as of March 21, 2025: PHP 500 per local, PHP 1,000 per foreign tourist. This includes boat, guide, and basic snorkel mask. This is the official fee; some tours add their own markup.
- Add-on if not in your tour: Mask rental PHP 100-150 if not included.
- No sharing complexity — you buy an individual ticket at the Tourism Center, watch briefing, wait for number.

Comparison: Oslob looks cheaper on paper — PHP 1,000 vs Donsol’s PHP 1,500+ per person — but Oslob is 30 minutes in a crowded line. Donsol is 3-4 hours searching in open water with only 6 people per boat.

## 4. Rules — What You Can and Cannot Do

**Donsol rules (strictly enforced by BIO):**
- No touching — 3 meters minimum from shark, 4 meters from tail. BIO can end your interaction.
- One boat per shark, max 6 swimmers + BIO in water at a time.
- No scuba diving in the interaction zone (only snorkeling/free diving).
- No flash photography, no feeding, no blocking the shark’s path.
- Do not use flash or selfie sticks that hit the animal.
- Boats must maintain 10 meters and turn off engines when swimmers are in water.

**Oslob rules (posted and announced, inconsistently enforced):**
- No touching — 4-5 meters distance rule posted, but difficult when 10+ boats and 30 snorkelers are in a 50-meter area.
- No sunscreen / sunblock, no flash, no free diving under the shark.
- 30-minute limit, no diving, stay inside designated area.
- Feeders only — tourists prohibited from feeding, but sharks are constantly fed from feeder boats.
- Life vest required for most tourists.

If being told off for getting close bothers you, Donsol’s smaller groups and present BIO feel more controlled. If you want lifeguards pulling people back, Oslob will feel chaotic at peak 8-10 AM.

## 5. How to Get There From Cebu and Manila

**How to get to Oslob from Cebu:**
Oslob is 3-4 hours by bus from Cebu City. Go to South Bus Terminal (CSBT), take Ceres bus to Bato via Oslob (Tan-awan) — PHP 250-350 aircon, hourly 24 hours. Tell conductor “whale shark watching.” Alight at Tan-awan center. Private transfer or hired car PHP 3,500-5,000 one way. Many day tours from Cebu City start 3:00-4:00 AM, include Oslob + Tumalog Falls or Sumilon Island, and cost PHP 1,800-2,800 per person for joiners, PHP 8,000-12,000 private car. You can return to Cebu for dinner same day.

**How to get to Donsol from Cebu — fastest:**
No direct ferry from Cebu to Donsol. Fastest is to fly.
- Flight Cebu (CEB) to Bicol International Airport (DRP) in Legazpi (Albay): 1-1.5 hours, PHP 2,300-8,000 one way on Cebu Pacific / PAL. Some require Manila stopover — direct flights run on some days. Second option is fly CEB-MNL-DRP.
- Legazpi to Donsol: Van from Legazpi Grand Central Terminal or private van at airport — PHP 100-150 per person shared van, 1.5-2 hours, 60-70 km. Private van PHP 1,750-2,500. Total Cebu to Donsol fastest door-to-door: 5-7 hours if flight aligns.

**How to get to Donsol from Manila:**
- Fly Manila (MNL) to Legazpi (DRP): 50-70 minutes, several daily flights, PHP 1,800-5,000 one way. Then van to Donsol as above.
- Bus: Manila Pasay / Cubao to Legazpi 10-12 hours PHP 1,200-1,800, then van to Donsol. Not recommended if short on time. Self-drive Manila to Donsol is 505 km road distance, 10-12 hours.
- Via Masbate: Some backpackers fly Cebu to Masbate, van + boat to Pilar, then to Donsol, but this is 4-6 hours longer than Legazpi route.

**Practical tip from Cebu:** If you only have 4 days and are based in Cebu, Oslob is the realistic option. If you can spend 3-4 days for Donsol — Day 1 fly to Legazpi + transfer, Day 2-3 whale shark attempts, Day 4 return — treat it as a separate trip via Manila or Legazpi, not a Cebu day trip.

## 6. The Experience Itself — What It Feels Like

In Donsol, you may wait. Boats idle, spotter scans, then a shout — *butanding!* You slip in, kick hard, and a 6-to-10-meter spotted giant moves at 3-4 km/h. You get 30 seconds to a few minutes before it dives. The water can be green with plankton, visibility 5-15 meters. There are maybe 2 other boats far away. It feels like you found something.

In Oslob, you see a fin as soon as your paddle boat stops. The water is bluer, visibility better, and there are often 2-4 sharks circling feeders. You also see 20-40 snorkelers kicking, GoPros everywhere, and a line of boats. You are guaranteed photos, but you are also in a queue.

Both offer firefly watching, island hops, and waterfalls nearby — Donsol River at night (PHP 1,250 per boat) and Ticao Island; Oslob with Tumalog Falls, Sumilon Island, and Kawasan Falls in Badian if you have a second day.

## Tips Before You Go

### For Donsol, go early and stay multiple days
Donsol Visitor Center opens 6:00 AM. Be there by 7:30 AM, watch briefing film by 8:00 AM, boat out by 8:30 AM. Search lasts max 3 hours. If you want higher chances, book 2-3 mornings. Most resorts help with joining a boat — ask your accommodation to pre-register you the night before. Peak sighting likelihood is late March to late May.

### For Oslob, arrive before 7 AM to avoid the crowd
Oslob’s briefing area opens 6:00 AM and by 9:00 AM there are often 100+ people in line. Come 6:00-6:30 AM for calmer water, fewer boats, and better light. No sunscreen — use rashguard instead, as lotions are banned.

### Bring reef-safe habits and cash
Neither place reliably takes cards for LGU fees. Bring cash in PHP 100s. Bring your own mask if you have one — shared rental masks leak. No touching, no flash, no blocking the shark’s path. In Donsol, wear a rashguard and strapped sandals for boat transfers.

### Manage expectations with kids
Oslob’s shallow, roped area and 30-minute format is easier for kids who can swim. Donsol requires swimming in open ocean, jumping off and climbing back onto a small boat, and possibly no sighting. Both require life jackets for non-swimmers.

### Ethical check
If ethical concerns matter to you, ask yourself: do you want a guaranteed photo or a natural encounter? Marine biologists and NGOs like WWF Philippines and LAMAVE generally recommend non-feeding encounters like Donsol or Sogod Bay in Southern Leyte. If you go to Oslob, avoid tours that promote touching or riding, and keep distance even if guides do not enforce it.

## So, Which Should You Choose — Donsol or Oslob?

Choose Donsol if you have flexibility in months (February to May), can spend 3 days, and value ethics and wilderness over certainty. It costs a bit more, involves more logistics from Cebu, but gives you a boat with 6 people and a trained BIO, no feeding, and a small-town experience.

Choose Oslob if you are short on time, based in Cebu City, want near-100% chance to see whale sharks tomorrow morning, and are okay with feeding tourism and crowds. It is cheaper, year-round, and logistically simple — 4 hours by bus and you are back.

Many travelers from Cebu do both in different trips: do Oslob as a day tour when you have friends visiting, and save Donsol as a dedicated Bicol trip with Mayon Volcano and Legazpi food after. If you only do one and ethics matter, go to Donsol in peak season.
