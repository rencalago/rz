# Binondo Food Crawl: A Practical Guide to Manila Chinatown

Binondo in Manila is recognized as the oldest Chinatown in the world, established in 1594 as a settlement for Chinese migrants during Spanish colonial times. Today it is less about quiet history and more about busy streets, gold shops, incense-filled temples, and food stalls that have lasted for generations. The heart of it is Ongpin Street, named after Don Roman Ongpin, a Chinese-Filipino patriot who supported the Philippine Revolution.

For travelers based in Cebu, a Binondo food crawl makes sense as a day trip from a Manila layover or as the food highlight of a weekend in Manila. You do not need a tour group to enjoy it, but you do need a loose plan because the district is crowded, shops open and close at different times, and it is easy to get full after two stops if you order too much at once.

This guide covers what to eat — hopia, xiao long bao, fried siopao, beef noodles, lumpia and more — plus a walkable 3-hour itinerary, how to get to Binondo from Cebu via Manila, current prices in Philippine pesos, and practical tips that keep the crawl enjoyable rather than overwhelming.

## 1. Getting to Binondo from Cebu via Manila

**Cebu to Manila flight:** Cebu Pacific, Philippine Airlines and AirAsia Philippines fly from Mactan-Cebu International Airport (CEB) to NAIA (MNL) almost hourly. Flight time is 1 hour 20 minutes to 1 hour 35 minutes. One-way base fares start at PHP 88 to PHP 599 on promos, regular all-in fares around PHP 2,000 to PHP 5,000 with baggage. Distance is about 570 km air.

If you have time, 2GO Travel and Trans-Asia ferries run Cebu to Manila at 23 to 25 hours, PHP 1,200 to PHP 6,000 depending on accommodation class, but for a food crawl weekend, flying is the practical choice.

**From NAIA to Binondo:** Binondo is 11 to 15 km north of NAIA, across the Pasig River, just north of Intramuros.

- **Grab or taxi:** Most convenient with luggage. Travel time 30 to 90 minutes depending on traffic. Cost PHP 280 to PHP 500 from NAIA to Binondo, sometimes higher with surge. Ask driver to drop at Binondo Church or Plaza Calderon de la Barca. Avoid touts inside arrivals; book via app.
- **Bus + LRT-1:** The cheapest way. Take UBE Express or city bus to Baclaran or EDSA, then LRT-1 to Carriedo Station — the nearest station to Ongpin. Cost PHP 40 to PHP 55 total, time 60 to 90 minutes. Carriedo Station is about a 5 to 7 minute walk to Ongpin Street.
- **Ride-hailing to LRT then walk:** Many locals taxi to LRT Baclaran then ride to Carriedo to beat airport traffic.

You can walk from Intramuros to Binondo via Jones Bridge in 10 to 15 minutes, so combining both districts in one day is easy.

**Best for:** Travelers doing a Manila weekend from Cebu who want a food-focused afternoon.

**What to do:** Book an early morning CEB-MNL flight to arrive in Binondo by late morning. Leave large bags at NAIA baggage storage (PHP 150 to PHP 300 per bag per day in Terminals 1 and 3) or at your hotel and bring only a small bag for walking and eating.

## 2. Must-Eat Stops on Ongpin and Beyond

Binondo's food reflects Fujianese roots mixed with Filipino tastes. Portions are for sharing, so go with 2 to 4 people if you can, order small, and split. Most places accept cash; some take GCash or card but cash is still king.

### Hopia at Eng Bee Tin – The Pasalubong Staple

Eng Bee Tin started in 1912 and is now Binondo's most famous hopia maker. The main Chinese Deli is at 628 Ongpin Street. Flavors range from classic mongo and baboy to modern ube, ube langka, ube queso, and premium brownie.

- **Price:** PHP 230 to PHP 315 per bundle of 3, PHP 700 to PHP 900 for dozen bundles. Single hopia around PHP 35 to PHP 50 in-store.
- **Hours:** Daily around 8 AM to 8 PM, longer before Chinese New Year.

**Best for:** First stop to orient yourself and last stop for pasalubong.

**What to do:** Buy small bundles first and save bulk buying for the end — hopia has no preservatives and keeps 5 to 7 days, refrigerated. Try the Tikoy rolls and hopia dice if available.

### Xiao Long Bao and Dumplings – Dong Bei and Tasty Dumplings

Dong Bei Dumplings at 642 Yuchengco Street is famous for xiao long bao (soup dumplings) and kuo tie (pan-fried dumplings). Tasty Dumplings at 620 Ongpin Street is another long-time crowd pleaser.

- **Price:** Dong Bei xiao long bao PHP 180 to PHP 210 for 6 pieces, fried dumplings PHP 200 for 14 pieces, pork chop noodles around PHP 120 to PHP 235. Tasty Dumplings xiao long bao around PHP 170 to PHP 210 for 8 pieces.
- **Hours:** Dong Bei 9 AM to 8 PM daily; Tasty 7 AM to 2 AM daily.

**Best for:** Sit-down break mid-crawl when you want soup and something hot.

**What to do:** Eat xiao long bao while hot. Poke a small hole, sip the broth first, then dip in vinegar with ginger. Do not pop the whole thing at once. Order one basket to share if you are trying many stops.

### Fried Siopao – Shanghai Fried Siopao

At 828 Ongpin corner Bahama Street, Shanghai Fried Siopao sells a Binondo icon — steamed pork buns pan-fried on the bottom until crispy. Different from the usual asado siopao, the filling is minced pork with spring onion, juicy and savory.

- **Price:** PHP 35 each in 2024-2025 (older blogs cited PHP 17 to PHP 22, now PHP 35 due to price increases). Four pieces costs PHP 140. Plan for 1 to 2 pieces per person if crawling.
- **Hours:** Around 6 AM to 7 PM daily, often sells out early afternoon.

**Best for:** The quintessential Binondo street eat — quick, cheap, eat-while-walking.

**What to do:** Eat it hot right in front of the stall. The bottom crisp softens quickly in a takeout box. Bring tissue — it is a bit oily.

### Beef Noodles and Machang / Kuchay Ah

Wai Ying along Benavidez Street and other beef brisket houses serve Chinese beef mami or beef noodles, a hearty soup with tender beef, bok choy and house-made noodles. Machang (sticky rice wrapped in lotus leaf) and kuchay ah (chive cake) are common add-ons.

- **Price:** Beef noodles PHP 200 to PHP 350 per bowl, machang PHP 45 to PHP 80 each, kuchay ah PHP 15 to PHP 25 per piece.
- **Hours:** Most noodle houses open 7 AM to 9 PM.

**Best for:** Travelers who want something soupy after many fried items.

**What to do:** Share one bowl if you are pacing yourself. Ask for extra chili oil and spring onions on the side.

### Lumpia – New Po Heng Lumpia House and Sincerity

New Po Heng Lumpia House is famous for fresh Chinese lumpia with peanut sauce, ground peanuts and garlic. Sincerity Cafe at 519 Quintin Paredes Street is one of Binondo's oldest, known for fried chicken, oyster cake and lumpia.

- **Price:** New Po Heng fresh lumpia PHP 50 to PHP 80 each, Sincerity fried chicken half PHP 240 to PHP 320, oyster cake PHP 180 to PHP 250.
- **Hours:** Sincerity daily 9 AM to 9 PM; lumpia stalls 8 AM to 7 PM.

**Best for:** Vegetable break in a meat-heavy crawl, and for sit-down with family.

**What to do:** Fresh lumpia is best eaten within 15 minutes; the wrapper softens. Ask for extra sauce in a separate small bag if taking for later. At Sincerity, order one signature chicken to share rather than per person.

## 3. A Walkable 3-Hour Itinerary (1.2 km loop)

This route keeps you on or near Ongpin so you do not get lost, fits 6 to 8 tastings, and ends near Binondo Church for easy exit.

**Start: Carriedo Station (0 min)** – Exit and walk north on Evangelista Street to Ongpin. Grab a bottle of water or sugarcane juice from Wongkei (PHP 120) if it is hot.

**Stop 1: Eng Bee Tin (10 min)** – 628 Ongpin. Orient, small snack, buy later. Quick photo of Ongpin arch.

**Stop 2: Shanghai Fried Siopao (25 min)** – 828 Ongpin cor Bahama. 1 siopao each.

**Stop 3: Dong Bei Dumplings (50 min)** – Walk to 642 Yuchengco Street, 5 minutes off Ongpin. Sit, share xiao long bao and fried dumplings. Rest your feet.

**Stop 4: Tasty Dumplings or Wai Ying (80 min)** – Back to Ongpin. Either xiao long bao comparison or beef noodles if you want soup.

**Stop 5: New Po Heng Lumpia House (105 min)** – Fresh lumpia to balance flavors. This is near the corner of Quintin Paredes.

**Stop 6: Sincerity Cafe or desserts (130 min)** – Oyster cake or hopia dice. If you want sweets, try Lord Stow's egg tarts or Miliky bicho at Oishiekun Food Hub (PHP 80).

**End: Binondo Church / Plaza San Lorenzo Ruiz (180 min)** – Sit in the church courtyard, rest, then final pasalubong buy at Eng Bee Tin. From here you can walk to Jones Bridge to Intramuros in 10 minutes or Grab back to your hotel.

Distance walked: about 1.5 km total plus crisscrossing. Time: 3 to 4 hours including queuing.

**Best for:** First-timers who want the hits without backtracking too much.

## 4. How Much Does a Binondo Food Crawl Cost?

- **Cebu to Manila return flight:** PHP 2,400 to PHP 10,000 all-in depending on promo and baggage.
- **NAIA to Binondo Grab one-way:** PHP 280 to PHP 500; bus + LRT: PHP 40 to PHP 55.
- **Luggage storage at NAIA:** PHP 150 to PHP 300 per bag per day.
- **Food budget per person:** PHP 500 to PHP 1,000 is enough for 5 to 7 stops if sharing. Solo budget PHP 800 to PHP 1,200 if eating full portions.
  - Shanghai Fried Siopao PHP 35 each
  - Dong Bei pork dumplings PHP 200 for 14 pcs, xiao long bao PHP 180 to PHP 210
  - Tasty Dumplings porkchop rice PHP 235
  - Eng Bee Tin hopia bundles PHP 230 to PHP 315 (3 pcs), PHP 900 dozen
  - Fresh lumpia PHP 50 to PHP 80
  - Sincerity half chicken PHP 240 to PHP 320
  - Drinks: sugarcane or dragon fruit juice PHP 100 to PHP 120
- **Manila hotel (if overnight):** PHP 1,500 to PHP 4,500 mid-range in Binondo or Intramuros area.

Total DIY day from Cebu excluding hotel: roughly PHP 1,000 to PHP 1,800 for food + local transport when sharing.

## 5. Tips Before You Go

### Best months to go

November to February is driest and coolest, best for walking. Chinese New Year (late January to February) is most festive and most crowded — shops have lion dances, but queues double. March to May is hot, bring hat and water. June to October is rainy season with afternoon downpours; Ongpin floods lightly.

### Cash and timing

Binondo still runs on cash. Bring small bills PHP 100 and PHP 50, plus coins. Go on a weekday late morning Tuesday to Thursday for shorter lines. Weekends 3 PM to 8 PM are peak. Many stalls close by 7 PM; Dong Bei closes 8 PM; Tasty until 2 AM for late cravings.

### Dress and walk smart

Wear comfortable shoes — sidewalks are uneven, with motorcycles parked. Bring hand sanitizer, wet wipes, and a small umbrella. Ongpin and Carvajal Alley are busy with trucks; walk single file. Keep phone and bag in front, especially near Divisoria spillover.

### Safety and etiquette

Binondo is generally safe in daytime with heavy foot traffic and barangay tanods. At night, stick to well-lit Ongpin and Quintin Paredes. Ask before photographing inside temples like Kuan Kong or Seng Guan Temple. For dietary restrictions, most meat dishes use pork and shrimp — always ask if in doubt.

### How to pace yourself

Order for sharing, not per person. Start light (siopao, lumpia), then heavier (xiao long bao, noodles), then sweet (hopia). Skip rice at early stops if you want more variety. Hydrate after fried foods; sugarcane juice helps cut grease.

## So, Is a Binondo Food Crawl Worth It from Cebu?

If you are flying Cebu to Manila anyway, yes. Binondo gives you a concentrated taste of Filipino-Chinese history that you will not find in Cebu — the oldest Chinatown in the world, still cooking recipes from the 1900s, all within a walkable 1 km stretch off Carriedo LRT.

For a Cebu-based food traveler, turn it into a 2-day mini-break: Day 1 fly early, check into Intramuros or Binondo hotel, do late afternoon food crawl when it is cooler, Day 2 morning temple visits and last pasalubong run before your 1-hour flight back to Mactan. Budget PHP 500 to PHP 1,000 per person for food, book flights during Cebu Pacific piso or PHP 88 promo periods, and keep your itinerary to Ongpin and side streets — that is where the must-eats are.
