# Christmas Markets and Night Bazaars Around the Philippines

If you love Christmas shopping after sunset, the Philippines delivers. From October onwards malls turn into holiday villages, streets close for grilled skewers and parols, and tiangges stretch late into the night. For 2026, the big charity bazaars have already locked in their dates, while the nightly city markets in Baguio, Davao, Cebu and Pampanga run on the same rhythm every year. Here is where to go, when to go, and what is actually worth buying.

## Manila and Metro Manila: The Christmas Bazaar Capital

### Noel Bazaar 2026

The longest-running Christmas bazaar returns for its 26th year with the theme Rediscovering the Magic of Paskong Pinoy. Expect more than 600 stalls of fashion, toys, homeware and food under one roof.

For 2026 the organizers confirmed two runs:
- **World Trade Center Metro Manila, Pasay City:** November 25 to 30, 11:00 a.m. to 10:00 p.m.
- **The Filinvest Tent, Alabang, Muntinlupa City:** December 17 to 20, 11:00 a.m. to 9:00 p.m.

This is where you go for curated local brands, gift sets that actually look finished, and food stalls from Metro Manila favorites. Arrive before 2 p.m. on weekends if you want to avoid the longest lines. Bring a tote, the paper bags tear quickly.

### World Bazaar Festival

Also at the World Trade Center, this charity bazaar benefits ABS-CBN Lingkod Kapamilya. For 2025 it ran December 12 to 21, 10 a.m. to 10 p.m., and the 2026 edition is listed for December 11 to 20. It leans more toward variety than Noel: imported snacks, beauty kits, Christmas decor, plus a dedicated food hall. If you only pick one big indoor bazaar in Manila, choose between Noel for local makers and World Bazaar for volume.

### Greenhills Night Market, San Juan

Manila's most famous tiangge flips to night mode every Christmas. Ortigas Malls runs the **Greenhills Christmas Night Market at the GStrip Parking Area** from early November to the end of December, nightly from around 4 p.m. to 11:30 p.m.

What to buy here that is different from the charity bazaars:
- Pearls and silver at V-Mall second floor, then bargain downstairs
- Trendy clothes, phone cases, and ukay finds at tiangge prices
- Christmas lights, parols, wrapping paper in bulk

Food is half the point. Isaw, shawarma, siomai, milk tea and fish balls are grilled right on the parking lot. It gets crowded after 7 p.m. and traffic on Ortigas Avenue crawls, so ride the MRT to Greenhills or come before sunset.

Other Manila fixtures to check in November and December:
- **St. James Bazaar** usually at The Tent at Enderun and Alabang Town Center
- **Zonta Christmas Carnivale** in Filinvest Tent
- SM Supermalls start tree lighting in October. SM Mall of Asia, SM North EDSA, and SM Megamall each host their own nightly Christmas markets with puto bumbong stalls.

## Baguio: Hillside Christmas and Two Markets in One

Baguio does Christmas like Europe but with strawberries.

**Baguio Christmas Market at Rose Garden, Burnham Park** is the city's European-style fair. In 2025 it opened December 5, and in previous years it started as early as November 28. The city tourism council curates wooden chalets with lights, local preserves, woodcraft, and Cordillera coffee. Expect live busking in the afternoon and a light show at night. For 2026, plan for late November to early January at the Rose Garden. Dress for 12 to 18 degrees after dark.

Right next to it, literally across the road, is the **Baguio Night Market on Harrison Road**. This is the everyday bazaar that becomes extra busy in December. It opens nightly at 9 p.m. until around 2 a.m., sometimes 4 a.m. on peak weekends. The stretch of Harrison Road beside Burnham Park closes to traffic.

What shoppers actually go for:
- 100-peso ukay jackets, sweaters, and Christmas sweaters for family photos
- Baguio woven bags, beanies, and strawberry jam to take home
- Hot taho, grilled corn, and strawberry taho while you browse

Tip: Go to the Christmas Market first for gifts, eat there, then cross to Harrison at 9 p.m. for thrift finds. Bring cash in small bills and a headlamp or phone light, lighting is uneven in the ukay rows.

## Cebu: Sugbo Mercado and Business Park Pop-Ups

Cebu does not have a European-style Christmas market square, but its night food markets turn festive in December.

**Sugbo Mercado** is the reference. The main branch is at Garden Bloc, Cebu IT Park, open Tuesday to Sunday 4 p.m. to midnight, and a seasonal **Christmas Bazaar branch at Cebu Business Park**, across Ayala Terraces, operates Thursday to Sunday, 4 p.m. to 10:30 or 11 p.m. from late November through December.

The Business Park bazaar mixes food and gift stalls: local art, potted plants, jewelry, childrens clothes, and lechon, barbecue, and seafood grills. Cebu Business Park is easier to park in than IT Park, and families spread out on the grass. For the IT Park food market, go before 6 p.m. if you want a table. After 8 p.m. you will be standing with a plate.

Elsewhere in Cebu City, Ayala Center Cebu and SM Seaside host nightly tiangge rows in December, usually starting 4 p.m., with bibingka and puto bumbong ovens set up near the entrance. Confirm the exact start date with the mall Facebook page, they announce it in the last week of November.

## Davao: Roxas Avenue Night Market

Davao's nightly institution runs year-round and becomes a Christmas food crawl in December.

**Roxas Avenue Night Market** occupies a closed section of Roxas Avenue in Poblacion District, near People's Park and Ateneo de Davao. Hours are 5 or 6 p.m. to midnight daily. The city sets up more than 300 food and non-food stalls, with masskara-style parols in December.

What to try:
- Grilled tuna panga, chicken barbecue, and isaw with Davao's sweet-spicy sawsawan
- Puto bumbong na overload with cheese, which pops up only in the last quarter
- Durian, mangosteen, and marang in season, December is still good for durian

For gifts, the Roxas stretch is more food than crafts. If you want abaca bags, Mindanao batik, and coffee, add a morning run to Bankerohan Market or Aldevinco Shopping Center, both 10 minutes by taxi.

Safety note: Davao implements a no-smoking and bag inspection rule at the market entrance. No backpacks left on chairs, and keep valuables front-facing when it gets packed before the holidays.

## Pampanga: Christmas Capital Markets

San Fernando calls itself the Christmas Capital, and it shows in December.

- **Giant Lantern Festival, San Fernando:** Mid-December, usually the Saturday before Christmas, at Robinsons Starmills grounds. This is not a traditional market, but the perimeter becomes a huge bazaar for a week, selling parols from small Santa Cruz lanterns to 2-foot capiz ones. If you want a handmade lantern to ship, buy it here, not in Manila. Vendors will pack it in a box for the bus.
- **Clark and Angeles Night Markets:** SM City Clark hosts an annual Christmas bazaar often labeled 100 Days of Happiness or One Town One Product trade fair, typically September 16 to December 15 at the ground level. Clark CityFront Mall and Outlet stores along Friendship Highway set up outdoor food stalls with sisig, puto bumbong, and bibingka from September onwards. Angeles City's Plaza Angel in the Heritage District also runs early bibingka stalls from September, worth a stop if you are doing a food trip.
- **Pampanga Christmas Village** projects in Clark, often called Nights of Lights, combine light installations with a food park from November to January. Confirm ticket and schedule on the Clark Development Corporation page, prices change yearly.

If you are driving from Manila, leave before 3 p.m. on festival weekends. NLEX traffic triples after that.

## What to Buy and What to Skip

**Worth buying at bazaars:**
- Parols made in San Fernando, Pampanga. Ask for capiz shell backing and LED adaptor.
- Handwoven table runners from Baguio and Benguet, Ilocos inabel, and Yakan weaves from Davao stalls.
- Local food gift sets: Benguet coffee, Davao tablea, Pampanga longganisa vacuum packs, Cebu dried mangoes in gift boxes.
- Small-batch soaps, candles, and wooden toys from Noel Bazaar exhibitors, they pack better than plush toys.

**What to think twice about:**
- Cheap Christmas lights without PS marks. They fail fast and are a fire risk.
- Replica bags or watches at tiangges. Customs can be strict if you fly internationally with them.
- Oversized plants from Cebu bazaars if you are flying home, airlines charge for them.

## Food to Try at Night Bazaars

Every market has the same core, but each city has a signature.

- **Manila tiangge:** puto bumbong and bibingka cooked in clay ovens with salted egg and muscovado. Best with extra butter and niyog.
- **Baguio:** strawberry taho, Ube jam on hot puto, and peanut brittle.
- **Cebu:** Tuslob buwa with hanging rice, ngohiong, and lechon belly chopped per order.
- **Davao:** grilled tuna jaw and kinilaw na malison, served in small plastic cups.
- **Pampanga:** sisig tacos, bibingka with salted duck egg, and tibok-tibok in banana leaf cups.

Carry hand sanitizer and tissues. Most stalls have no running water, but they do have covered trash bins. Bring your own eco bag, vendors appreciate it.

## Practical Tips for 2026

1. **Mark the big dates first.** Noel Bazaar November 25 to 30 at World Trade Center and December 17 to 20 at Filinvest Tent Alabang, World Bazaar Festival mid-December at World Trade Center, Greenhills Night Market November 8 to December 31 nightly. These are confirmed by organizer pages for 2026.
2. **Check LGU pages for small markets.** Baguio City Tourism, Davao City Tourism Operations Office, and City of San Fernando Facebook pages post actual road closure maps and stall maps the week before opening.
3. **Go early on weekdays.** Tuesday to Thursday after 5 p.m. is the sweet spot. Friday and Saturday after 7 p.m., you will queue for everything.
4. **Cash still rules at tiangges.** Indoor bazaars accept GCash, Maya, and cards. Harrison Road and Roxas Avenue are almost cash-only. Break your 1,000 peso bills at a convenience store first.
5. **Parking is the bottleneck.** Book Grab early in Baguio and around Greenhills. In Davao and Cebu Business Park, there is official parking but it fills by 6 p.m.
6. **For photos, arrive at blue hour.** 5:30 to 6:15 p.m. gives you lights on and enough sky to still see faces. After that, phone flash washes out the parols.

If you are basing yourself in Manila, you can cover Greenhills and World Trade Center in one weekend. For a longer Christmas loop, fly Manila to Cebu, ferry or fly to Davao, then end in Clark for the lanterns. That route lines up with mid-December festival dates and keeps you out of the worst of the Christmas flight surge.

Cebu travelers looking for a complete itinerary can pair the Cebu Business Park market with a food tour around IT Park. If you want quieter hillside air after the crowds, Baguio's Rose Garden market in the afternoon is a calm reset before Harrison Road gets loud.

Happy hunting, and leave room in your luggage for a parol. It will not fit, but you will try anyway.
