# Zambales Coves: Anawangin and Nagsasa

Zambales coves are where many Manila-based travelers get their first real taste of off-grid camping, and they are worth the detour if you are flying in from Cebu. Anawangin Cove is famous for its pine-like agoho trees growing on what looks like volcanic ash sand after the 1991 Mt. Pinatubo eruption. Nagsasa Cove, just south, is wilder, wider, and often quieter, with a more open beachfront and shallow, swimmable water at low tide.

The classic way to see them is to base yourself in Barangay Pundaquit in San Antonio, Zambales. From there, you hire a boat that takes 30-45 minutes to the coves. There is no road to any of the coves — boat is the only way in and out — which is part of why they still feel remote despite being 4-5 hours from Manila.

If you are coming from Cebu, think of this as a 2-stage trip: fly Cebu to Manila, then bus to Zambales. Budget two days and one night minimum for camping, or three days and two nights if you want to cover both coves plus Capones Island without rushing.

## 1. Anawangin Cove — The Classic Pine Beach Camp

Anawangin is the most developed of the Zambales coves, but developed here means tent pitches under agoho trees, basic toilets, and sari-sari stores that sell ice and canned goods, not resorts. There is no electricity in the cove itself at night aside from generator-powered lights until around 10pm in some camps. Expect poor to no mobile signal.

Campsites are privately run side by side. Entrance is typically P50 per person for a day trip and P100 per person for overnight. Your boatman will take you to the operator he is affiliated with. If you have a preference, tell him before you depart.

**Best for:** First-timers, groups who want a ready tent setup, photographers who like the pine-beach contrast.

**What to do:** Pitch under the trees, not right on the sand line where high tide can reach. Walk the full length of the beach in the morning. Swim, but watch for jellyfish season (May-August). In the evening, join the other campers for a bonfire — firewood is usually sold at P150-P250 per bundle.

## 2. Nagsasa Cove — Wilder, Bigger, Better for Swimming

Nagsasa is about 30-40 minutes by boat south of Anawangin. It has a similar agoho forest, but the cove is broader, flanked by steep hills, and ends in a small stream on the southern end that you can trek to for fresh water (not for drinking without treatment). Many regulars find Nagsasa better for swimming as the water stays calmer than Anawangin on some days.

Entrance fees are similar: P50 per head day trip, P100 overnight. Some operators charge an additional P20 per head municipal environmental fee collected before you enter Pundaquit proper — keep the receipt.

**Best for:** Travelers who want more space, fewer neighbors, and a longer stretch of beach for sunset.

**What to do:** Trek to the Nagsasa waterfalls and small river (around 20-30 minutes inland, easy trail). Ask your boatman to include Subic Beach on the way back for a white sand stop — it is often part of an Anawangin-Nagsasa combo.

## 3. Capones Island and Camara Island — The Island Hopping Add-ons

Capones Island is the rocky island with the old Spanish-era lighthouse built in 1890. There is no entrance fee to the island itself, but you pay for the boat that stops there and you need to hike 15-20 minutes uphill to the lighthouse. Wear closed shoes; the path is sharp limestone. Camara Island is the tiny islet near Capones with clearer water for snorkeling, good as a mid-boat stop but not for camping.

**Best for:** Lighthouse views, quick snorkeling, filling in the boat ride between Pundaquit and the main coves.

**What to do:** For a day tour from Pundaquit, get a boat that does Capones + Camara + Anawangin in one loop. If you are camping overnight in Anawangin or Nagsasa, most boatmen will include Capones on your return trip on day 2 if seas allow.

## 4. How to Get There from Cebu via Manila

This is the most practical route for Cebu-based travelers.

**Step 1: Cebu to Manila flight.** Daily flights from Mactan-Cebu (CEB) to NAIA or Clark (CRK). Cebu Pacific and Philippine Airlines fly this hourly. Book early, fares are often P900-P2,500 one-way. From NAIA, take a Grab or bus to Cubao or Pasay Victory Liner terminals. From Clark, take a bus to Olongapo first.

**Step 2: Manila to San Antonio, Zambales by bus.** At Victory Liner Cubao or Pasay, take a bus bound for Iba or Sta. Cruz, Zambales. Tell the conductor you will get off at San Antonio Public Market. Fare is P350-P400 (aircon), travel time 4-5 hours depending on Manila traffic. Alternative: Manila to Olongapo P320, then Olongapo to San Antonio P80.

**Step 3: San Antonio to Pundaquit by tricycle.** From San Antonio market, tricycle to Pundaquit is P40-P50 per person, or P150-P200 per tricycle for special trip. Travel time 20-25 minutes. You will pass the Pundaquit environmental fee checkpoint: P20 per head.

**Step 4: Pundaquit to coves by boat.** Boats are on-demand, no fixed schedule. Go to the boatmen association area near Pundaquit Beach. Rates as of 2025-2026:

- Small boat (1-4 pax): Anawangin only P1,200-P2,000 per boat roundtrip; Nagsasa only P1,800-P2,500; Anawangin + Capones P1,300-P2,500; Anawangin + Capones + Camara P1,800-P2,500; Anawangin + Nagsasa + Capones + Camara full loop P3,000-P4,000 per boat.
- Large boat (5-10+ pax, group rate): P200-P300 per person for basic cove, P400-P500 per person for full loop including Nagsasa.
- Camping packages (tour operators): Day tour from Pundaquit only usually P800-P1,200 per head inclusive of boat and entrance. Overnight package with tent, meals, boat: Solo P2,700-P3,500, 2 persons P1,450-P1,850 per head, groups cheaper.

Boats leave early — 8am to 11am is the busy window. If you arrive in Pundaquit at 9am, you can be in Anawangin by 10am. Return boats from coves usually ask you to be ready by 8am-10am on checkout day.

## 5. Camping — What to Expect and How Much

Most campsites offer tent rental P400-P600 per night for a 2-person tent, P800-P1,000 for 3-4 persons. Bring your own tent if you can — you save and you control quality. Cottages/kubo P500-P1,000 per night. Table rental P100-P150. Shared toilet and bath: usually free with your entrance, but bring your own tabo. Showers with fresh water: sometimes P25 per use.

There are small stores in Anawangin and fewer in Nagsasa. Prices are 1.5x town prices. Drinking water is critical: bring at least 2 liters per person per day, or buy 6L jugs at P80-P100 in Pundaquit before boarding.

Bonfire area is central. Quiet hours are generally observed around 10pm-11pm by most camp owners.

## 6. Costs Breakdown for 2D1N from Manila (Per Person, Group of 4)

This is the typical DIY budget. Costs in PHP:

- Victory Liner bus Manila-San Antonio roundtrip: P700-P800
- Tricycle San Antonio-Pundaquit roundtrip: P80-P100
- Environmental fee: P20 (one-time) + P20 municipal
- Boat full loop Anawangin + Nagsasa + Capones + Camara roundtrip divided by 4: P750-P1,000 per head
- Entrance fee overnight (Anawangin or Nagsasa): P100
- Tent rental if not bringing own: P200-P300 per head shared
- Food and water (buy in San Antonio market): P400-P600
- Cottage/table share: P75-P125

Total DIY estimate: P2,300-P3,200 per person for 2D1N excluding flight Cebu-Manila. With Manila-Zambales tour package with food and tent, expect P1,800-P2,500 per head from Pundaquit, or P1,500-P2,000 per head all-in from Manila van tours.

Add Cebu-Manila flight roundtrip P1,800-P5,000 depending on promo.

## 7. Best Months to Go

November to May is the recommended camping window. Best months are December to March when seas are calmer and skies clearer. January to February is peak season for camping — book boat ahead via your camp operator. 

March to May is summer: very hot (35C+), but calm seas good for Capones stop. Off-season is June to September (habagat/southwest monsoon) — waves can cancel boat trips, especially for Capones Island which faces open sea. Avoid when there is a storm signal in Zambales.

If you want that iconic cloudy-mountain backdrop without the heat, late November to early December often gives good light without the Christmas rush.

## Tips Before You Go

### Pack for no electricity

Bring power bank (20,000mAh minimum), headlamp, dry bag for electronics. Solar chargers help but agoho shade limits sun. No ATMs in Pundaquit beyond San Antonio town, so withdraw cash in San Antonio market area.

### What to bring checklist

Tent footprint, ground sheet, mosquito repellent, sun protection, 2L water minimum, ready-to-eat food, cooking set if you plan to cook (check if camp allows), trash bags (leave no trace policy enforced), aqua shoes for Capones limestone trek.

### Book the boat the night before

If you arrive late in San Antonio, stay in a Pundaquit guesthouse (P1,000-P1,800 per night) and arrange the boat that night. This avoids queuing at 9am with day trippers. Many operators run Facebook pages — confirm price, boat size, and whether Capones is included.

### Travel light but prepared

You have to wade to get on/off the boat. Waterproof your bags. Leave your big Manila luggage in San Antonio storage or Manila.

### No resorts means no resort services

You are camping. Bring toilet paper, basic first aid, and allergy meds. The nearest hospital is in San Antonio or Iba.

## So, Is It Worth Going from Cebu?

If you are based in Cebu and want a different beach profile than the usual white sand islands in Visayas, Zambales coves offer something else: pine-shaded sand born from lahar, overnight camping with no signal, and an old lighthouse you can hike to. It is not a luxury escape, and that is the point.

For most Cebu travelers, the sweet spot is fly to Manila on a Friday evening, night bus to San Antonio, camp in Nagsasa Saturday to Sunday, and fly back Sunday evening via Manila to Cebu. It is a logistically heavier weekend than, say, Bantayan or Moalboal, but it feels far more remote. If you want two coves, give it three days.

For quiet, pick Nagsasa over Anawangin. For convenience and first-time camping, start with Anawangin. Add Capones Island if weather is good — it is the visual highlight that ties the trip together.
