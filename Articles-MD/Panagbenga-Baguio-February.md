# Panagbenga, Baguio (February)

Panagbenga is Baguio's flower festival — Panagbenga means season of blooming in Kankanaey — held every February to mark the city's recovery after the 1990 earthquake. The whole month is a festival, but two weekends are the highlight: the grand street dancing parade and the grand floral float parade.

The 30th edition in 2026 ran February 1 to March 8, with street dance on February 28 and float parade on March 1. For 2027, organizers have not published final dates as of September 2026, but the pattern holds: month-long events February 1 to 28, with main parades on the last weekend of February to first weekend of March. Confirm with Baguio Flower Festival Foundation (BFFFI) and Philippine Information Agency before booking.

Here is how Panagbenga works, what to see, how to get to Baguio from Cebu, and how to enjoy it without getting stuck in traffic.

## 1. What Happens During Panagbenga

**Month-long events February 1–28:** The festival starts with opening ceremonies at Panagbenga Park and Harrison Road, then weeks of activities: flower exhibits at Burnham Park, Session Road in Bloom (Session Road closed to cars and lined with stalls), cultural shows at Baguio Athletic Bowl, and competitions.

**Grand Street Dancing Parade:** The main street dance, with flower-inspired costumes and drums, usually on the last Saturday of February. Dancers move from Panagbenga Park through Session Road to Athletic Bowl. Free to watch from sidewalks, but arrive early — Session Road fills by 7 AM.

**Grand Floral Float Parade:** The signature event, floats covered entirely in fresh flowers — sunflowers, chrysanthemums, roses, orchids — parading the next day, usually the first Sunday of March or last Sunday of February. Floats are judged, and the best ones are huge — trucks covered in thousands of blooms.

**Session Road in Bloom:** For about a week, Session Road — Baguio's main road — is closed to vehicles and becomes a market and food fair. Good for pasalubong like strawberry jam, Benguet coffee, and woodcraft.

**Best for:** First-time Baguio visitors, flower lovers, photographers, and families who want a daytime festival rather than a night street party.

**What to do:** Watch the street dance from Session Road for the closest view, then the float parade from near Burnham Park or Athletic Bowl for wider shots. Bring a hat — Baguio sun at 1,400 meters is strong despite cool air.

## 2. How to Get to Baguio from Cebu

There is no direct Cebu to Baguio flight — Baguio's Loakan Airport has limited commercial flights. You fly to Manila or Clark, then take a bus.

**Cebu to Manila or Clark, then bus to Baguio:**
- **CEB to MNL:** Hourly flights CEB to NAIA, 1h20m–1h35m, PHP 1,200–5,000 one-way. From NAIA, take a taxi or Grab to a bus terminal — Victory Liner, Genesis, or JoyBus in Pasay, Cubao, or Caloocan. Bus Manila to Baguio takes 4 to 6 hours, PHP 600–1,200 for deluxe or JoyBus with restroom, PHP 900–1,500 for premium.
- **CEB to CRK (Clark):** Direct flights by Cebu Pacific and PAL, about 1h30m. From Clark, Genesis and Victory Liner buses to Baguio take 4 to 5 hours, PHP 500–1,000. Clark is less congested than Manila, often faster overall.
- **Direct bus Manila to Baguio:** Victory Liner, JoyBus, and Genesis JoyBus run hourly from early morning to late evening. Night buses are popular — you sleep on the bus and arrive Baguio early morning.

**Total Cebu to Baguio:** About 7 to 9 hours including flight and bus, if connections line up. Book bus tickets in advance for Panagbenga weekends — buses sell out Friday to Sunday.

**Best for:** Travelers combining Manila and Baguio, or those who want to see Baguio's cool weather after beach islands.

**Practical tip:** If you fly to Clark, pre-book the Baguio bus online — Genesis and JoyBus allow online booking. For Manila terminals, use PITX or Cubao to avoid city traffic.

## 3. Where to Stay and Costs

**Accommodation:** Book at least 3 to 4 weeks ahead for the last weekend of February. Hotels near Burnham Park, Session Road, and City Center fill first. Budget inns PHP 1,500–3,000 per night, mid-range hotels PHP 3,500–6,000, higher-end like The Manor or Azalea PHP 7,000–12,000. Many require 2 to 3 night minimum during float weekend.

**Food:** Baguio has strawberry taho (PHP 30–50), Benguet coffee, and Session Road in Bloom food stalls. Restaurants like Good Taste and Hill Station are popular — expect queues during festival.

**Entrance fees:** Panagbenga parades are free to watch. Burnham Park and Athletic Bowl have no fee, but grandstand seats for parades may be ticketed PHP 300–1,000 if you want reserved viewing.

**Transport in Baguio:** Taxis are metered and honest — PHP 50 flag down. Jeepneys PHP 15–20. Traffic is heavy during Panagbenga, especially around Session Road and Burnham — walking is often faster than driving for short distances.

## 4. What to Bring and How to Enjoy It Without Crowds

**What to bring:** Jacket or hoodie — Baguio February mornings are 12–18°C, cooler than Cebu. Comfortable walking shoes for cobblestones and hills, hat, sunscreen despite cool air, reusable water bottle, and small bills for market stalls.

**Avoiding crowds:**
- **Arrive early for parades:** For street dancing, be on Session Road by 6:30 AM. For float parade, near Burnham Park by 7 AM.
- **Watch from Athletic Bowl for float parade:** Less crowded than Session Road, with better wide shots.
- **Visit Burnham Park on weekdays:** Flower exhibits are less crowded Monday to Thursday.
- **Book return bus early:** Sunday afternoon buses after float parade are packed — book a Monday morning return if you can.

**Best months:** Panagbenga is February, but Baguio is pleasant December to March. If you want flowers without the biggest crowds, go first two weeks of February — Session Road in Bloom is already open, but parades have not happened yet.

## Tips Before You Go

### Book accommodation and bus tickets early

The last weekend of February sells out in Baguio. Hotels and buses need advance booking, especially JoyBus with restroom.

### Dress in layers

Baguio February is cool mornings, warm midday, cool evenings. A hoodie plus t-shirt works — you will not need heavy winter gear.

### Walk, do not drive, in the center

Session Road and Harrison Road are closed or heavily congested during Panagbenga. Taxis get stuck — walking is faster for short hops.

### Bring cash

Market stalls at Session Road in Bloom and Burnham Park are cash-only. ATMs in Session Road have long queues during festival.

## So, Is Panagbenga Worth the Trip from Cebu?

Yes, if you want a break from beaches and a flower-focused festival that is daytime, family-friendly, and free to watch. The grand floral float parade is unlike any other festival in the Philippines — floats made entirely of fresh flowers, not just decorated.

From Cebu, fly to Manila or Clark, take a 4 to 6-hour bus to Baguio, and base yourself near Burnham Park for walking access. Aim for the last weekend of February for the two main parades, or first two weeks of February for a quieter flower experience.
