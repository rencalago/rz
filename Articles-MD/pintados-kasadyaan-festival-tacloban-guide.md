# Pintados-Kasadyaan Festival Tacloban: Complete Guide

If you are planning to be in Leyte in June, Tacloban's biggest cultural week should be on your calendar. Pintados-Kasadyaan blends body-painted street dancers honoring ancient Visayan warriors with a full provincial showcase called the Festival of Festivals. It is loud, colorful, religious, and very local, held right around the city's fiesta for Señor Santo Niño de Tacloban.

Here is what it is, when it happens, where to watch, and how to make the trip work.

## What Pintados-Kasadyaan Actually Is

Two celebrations share one name.

**Pintados** refers to Tacloban City's own performance. The word means painted, the name Spanish chroniclers gave to Visayans they met with extensive tattoos from face to foot. For warriors, those tattoos recorded bravery, rank, and status. Dancers today recreate that look with intricate paint in patterns that mimic armor, covering arms, legs, chest and face.

**Kasadyaan** means merriment in Waray. It is Leyte province's Festival of Festivals. Each contingent comes from a different town and brings its own local festival to the stage: Buyogan Festival of Abuyog, Lingganay Festival of Alangalang, Karatong Festival of Dulag, and others. Think of it as a highlights reel of Leyte's culture in one afternoon.

Together, the event is officially called the Leyte Pintados-Kasadyaan Festival, often shortened to Pintados-Kasadyaan. Nightly events, trade fairs, and pageants run for most of June at the Pintados-Kasadyaan Festival Park, also known as RTR Plaza, but the main showdown happens in the last week.

## History in Brief

Tacloban used to celebrate its fiesta on the third Sunday of January like many Santo Niño cities. The date moved to June 30 after a local devotion history tied to the return of the Santo Niño image in 1889, which the community linked to the end of cholera. The city kept June 30 as its patronal fiesta.

In 1986, civic business leaders formed the Pintados Foundation, Inc. to build a cultural event around the province's tattooed heritage and devotion to Señor Santo Niño. The first Pintados Festival was staged on June 29, 1987.

Years later, the provincial government combined its Kasadyaan gathering of municipal festivals with the city event. That merger created the show you see today: a ritual dance competition that is both a religious tribute and a tourism platform for Leyte.

After a hiatus during the pandemic, the festival returned in full in 2024 at the Leyte Sports Development Center, locally called the Grandstand, with around ten contingents and thousands of delegates.

Note on 2025: organizers announced that the Kasadyaan Festival of Festivals 2025 competition was postponed to 2026 after only three towns confirmed participation following the May 2025 elections. The city fiesta activities around June 29 to 30 went on, but the grand Festival of Festivals competition did not. If you are planning for next June, check the official Facebook pages of the Tacloban City Government and Pintados-Kasadyaan for the final date.

## When It Happens

Pintados-Kasadyaan is a June festival.

* The city fiesta is fixed on **June 30**, feast of Señor Santo Niño de Tacloban.
* The competition day, sometimes called the ritual showdown, usually falls in the **last week of June**, often June 27, 28 or 29, depending on the year.
* Month-long activities start early June at RTR Plaza.

If you want the painted dancers and the full competition, target **June 27 to 30** and stay flexible. In recent years the parade and ritual presentation started at 1 to 2 pm at the Grandstand, with street parades in the morning when permitted.

## What to Expect on the Ground

### 1. The Painted Dancers
This is the signature image. Masks, headdresses with feathers and abaca, and body paint in black, blue, gold and white. Movements blend warrior choreography, folk dance and theater, with drums and agung gongs driving the pace. It is not just a dance: each contingent tells a story, from pre-colonial legends to fishing life to local heroes.

### 2. The Festival of Festivals Competition
Each town gets 15 to 20 minutes to perform at the Grandstand. Judges score choreography, costume, musicality and theme. Winners often go on to represent Leyte at national stages like Aliwan and Sinulog. Expect big props, chanters, and a lot of crowd cheering for hometown groups.

### 3. Street Parade (When Allowed)
In some years, contingents parade through downtown Tacloban, starting and ending at the Grandstand and passing through major avenues before the ritual showdown. In other years, due to weather, traffic permits or logistics, all dancing stays inside the sports complex. The city tourism office announces the parade route a few days before.

### 4. Side Events at the Park
Around RTR Plaza: nightly shows, food stalls, garden and pet shows, trade fairs, and the searches for Mutya ng Kasadyaan and Miss Pintados. The Santo Niño Church at Real and Zamora Streets draws pilgrims throughout the month, especially on June 30 for the fiesta Mass.

## Where to Watch and Get a Good Spot

**Leyte Sports Development Center, Grandstand, Tacloban City.** This is the main venue for the ritual showdown. Seats fill early. Arrive at least two hours before the published start if you want covered grandstand seating. Bring a hat, water, and a small fan. The sun hits hard even inside the open stadium.

**RTR Plaza / Pintados-Kasadyaan Festival Park.** Free, open, and best for evening chill after the main event.

**Street-side.** If a morning parade is announced, line up along Imelda Avenue or the route published on the city's page. Locals bring umbrellas both for sun and for quick June showers.

Tickets: general admission at the Grandstand has often been free or ticket-controlled by the organizers. Some sections require invitations for LGU guests. For the best chance, ask at the Tacloban City Information Office or your hotel front desk a day before for ticket distribution points.

## How to Get to Tacloban for the Festival

**By air:** Daniel Z. Romualdez Airport (TAC) receives daily flights from Manila and Cebu. From there it is 15 to 20 minutes by taxi or van to downtown. Book flights early for the last week of June, fares rise and flights fill with returning residents for the fiesta.

**By land or sea:** Van and bus from Ormoc, Catbalogan, and Samar cross the San Juanico Bridge. Ferry connections to Matnog and Cebu run through nearby ports.

**Around the city:** Jeepneys, multicabs and tricycles handle short trips. Traffic rerouting is common on competition day, so leave extra time if your hotel is near the parade route.

## Practical Tips for First-Timers

**Book early.** Hotels near downtown sell out around June 28 to 30. Look also in nearby Palo and Alangalang if Tacloban proper is full.

**Prepare for heat and rain.** Late June is hot, crowded and humid, with sudden showers. Carry a poncho, not just an umbrella, because crowds make umbrellas hard to use in the stands.

**Protect your gear.** Body paint, colored powder and water spray are part of the fun in street areas. Keep cameras in a quick-cover bag.

**Respect the religious core.** The festival honors Señor Santo Niño. The Mass at Santo Niño Church on June 30 is the heart of the celebration for locals. Dress modestly if you enter the church, and avoid blocking processions.

**Food and cash.** RTR Plaza has street food, but ATMs run low during fiesta. Bring cash. For full meals, Tacloban's downtown has seafood and Waray dishes like tinapa and moron. Lines are long at night, lunch is calmer.

**Combine with nearby sights.** If you have an extra day, the San Juanico Bridge viewpoint, the Santo Niño Shrine and Heritage Museum, and Palo's historic sites are easy add-ons without leaving Leyte. If you are coming from Cebu, this makes a good Visayas loop with Sinulog season being January and Pintados-Kasadyaan being June.

## Is It Worth It?

Yes, if you enjoy cultural Festivals that remain truly local. It does not have the scale of Sinulog or Ati-Atihan, and organization can shift year to year between full street parades and stadium-only shows. That unpredictability is part of Leyte's charm. You go for the painted warriors, the drumlines, and the way towns put their pride on stage, not for polished stadium perfection.

Check the final schedule a week before you fly, arrive early for shade, and give yourself two days in town: one for the competition, one for the church fiesta and the park.
