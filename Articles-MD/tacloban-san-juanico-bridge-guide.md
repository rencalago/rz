# Tacloban and San Juanico Bridge Guide

Tacloban City is the gateway to Eastern Visayas and the most practical base for seeing two landmarks most travelers come for: San Juanico Bridge and the Leyte Landing Memorial in Palo. Tacloban itself is a compact, walkable city on Cancabato Bay — no beach resort strip, but a working port city with good food, easy jeepney routes, and quick access to both Leyte and Samar.

San Juanico Bridge is what made the region famous beyond history books. At 2.16 kilometers long, it is the longest bridge in the Philippines spanning a body of seawater, part of the Pan-Philippine Highway that links Leyte to Samar across San Juanico Strait. Built from 1969 to 1973 with Japanese engineering support, it has a distinctive S-curve and arch truss in the middle for ship clearance. Locals also call it the Bridge of Love.

Here is a practical guide focused on Cebu-based travelers — how to get to Tacloban from Cebu by direct flight or ferry via Ormoc, what it actually costs now, what to do at San Juanico Bridge and MacArthur Park, and what to eat in between.

## 1. How to Get to Tacloban from Cebu

You have two realistic options from Cebu, and both are straightforward.

**By direct flight — 55 minutes, fastest option:**
Cebu Pacific, Cebgo and Philippine Airlines fly Cebu Mactan (CEB) to Tacloban Daniel Z. Romualdez Airport (TAC) daily. Flight time is 55 minutes to 1 hour 5 minutes. One-way fares range from PHP 800 to PHP 1,500 on promo to PHP 2,000 to PHP 3,800 on regular dates, PHP 4,500+ around holidays. Baggage is extra on low-cost. Tacloban Airport is 10 to 15 minutes by taxi to downtown. Grab is limited in Tacloban; airport taxis charge PHP 150 to PHP 300 to downtown, regular taxis on meter start around PHP 40 flagfall. From downtown, San Juanico Bridge is 12 km away.

**By ferry and van via Ormoc — 8 to 12 hours, overnight possible:**
This is the backup when flights are expensive or cancelled. From Cebu Pier 1, Cokaliong Shipping and Roble Shipping run overnight ro-ro passenger ships to Ormoc City. From Cebu Pier 3, OceanJet runs fastcrafts to Ormoc (2.5 to 3 hours). Slow boat fare is PHP 900 to PHP 1,600 for economy to tourist, PHP 1,800 to PHP 2,800 for cabin. Fastcraft is PHP 1,100 to PHP 1,700 one-way. Travel time: 8 to 10 hours for slow boat, often overnight 9 PM to 5 AM, 2.5 hours for fastcraft plus transfers.

From Ormoc, take a van or bus to Tacloban. Vans from Ormoc Terminal to Tacloban New Bus Terminal in Abucay take 2.5 to 3 hours, PHP 250 to PHP 400. Ceres and other buses are PHP 200 to PHP 350. Roble Shipping also runs a direct Cebu to Tacloban route via MV Claudine Star three times a week (Tue/Thu/Sun from Cebu, return Mon/Wed/Sat from Tacloban), fare PHP 1,200 to PHP 2,200 economy, schedule changes with weather so confirm at the pier.

**Best for:** Fly if you have limited days. Take the ferry-van combo if you are on a budget, want to bring a motorbike, or want a Visayas sea route experience.

**What to do:** If you fly, book morning departures — Tacloban gets afternoon cloud and thunderstorms are more likely to delay late flights. If you ferry, bring cash, power bank, and earplugs for overnight boats.

## 2. San Juanico Bridge — The Longest Bridge Spanning Seawater

San Juanico Bridge connects Barangay Cabalawan in Tacloban City, Leyte to Barangay Sta. Rita in Samar. It is free to cross — no toll. You can cross by car, taxi, jeepney or motorbike. Walking is allowed on the sidewalks but is not formally encouraged for long stretches because there is no shade and vehicles pass fast. Many visitors hire a habal-habal from Tacloban for a half-day loop: Tacloban - San Juanico - Palo.

The best views are not from the middle of the bridge itself but from the approaches. On the Leyte side, there is a small park and viewing deck near the arch where you can take photos without stopping on the carriageway. On the Samar side in Santa Rita, there is a viewpoint with San Juanico Strait and small islands below. Sunset is the best light — the bridge lights up at night after the PHP 80-million illumination project, with LED color changes on weekends.

A car crossing takes 3 to 5 minutes. If you want photos, do not attempt to stop in the middle lane. Use the side lay-bys at both ends. Motorcycle rental is PHP 400 to PHP 600 per hour with driver, PHP 800 to PHP 1,200 for a half-day bridge + Palo tour. Van with driver is PHP 2,500 to PHP 3,500 for a half-day (up to 10 pax).

**Best for:** First-time visitors, photographers who like infrastructure and strait views, and anyone crossing from Leyte to Samar on a road trip.

**What to do:** Go early morning for fewer vehicles and cooler air, or around 5 PM for sunset. Bring a cap — it is windy and exposed.

## 3. Leyte Landing Memorial — MacArthur Park in Palo

Ten kilometers south of Tacloban downtown is the MacArthur Leyte Landing Memorial National Park in Palo, Leyte. This is where General Douglas MacArthur fulfilled his “I shall return” promise on October 20, 1944. Seven larger-than-life bronze statues stand in chest-deep water — MacArthur, President Osmeña, and others — marking the historic landing.

Entrance to the park itself is free. It is open 24 hours as an open park, best visited at sunrise, late afternoon, or at night when lit. Small museum beside it charges PHP 30 to PHP 50. Food stalls sell drinks and snacks. Parking is PHP 20 to PHP 50 for motorbikes and cars. Jeepney from Tacloban downtown to Palo is PHP 20 to PHP 30, 20 to 30 minutes. Tricycle from Palo market to the memorial is PHP 50 to PHP 100.

Beside the memorial is the Leyte Landing Memorial Museum and Library which reopened after Yolanda renovations. If you have time, continue to the Hill 120 / Hill 522 Japanese shrines nearby — remnants of WWII fighting.

**Best for:** History buffs, families with a half-day in Tacloban, and anyone combining Tacloban with a San Juanico loop — the memorial is on the way.

**What to do:** Do not wade out to the statues during high tide for a photo — the footing is slippery and the water has sharp shells. Take the classic shot from the seawall.

## 4. What Else to See in Tacloban City

Tacloban is not a beach city but it has a few quick stops worth your time:

**Sto. Niño Shrine and Heritage Museum:** Built in 1979-1981 as one of the Marcos resthouses, it showcases art, antiques and religious icons. Entrance PHP 50 to PHP 100, 8 AM to 5 PM daily. 5 km from downtown.

**Price Mansion (Capitol):** The former Leyte Provincial Capitol served as seat of the Commonwealth government in 1944. Now a museum on Tacloban’s WWII role. Entrance free to PHP 30 donation.

**Tacloban Astrodome and City Fish Port / Magsaysay Boulevard:** Good for an evening walk, sunset over Cancabato Bay, and grilled seafood. Budget PHP 250 to PHP 500 per meal.

**New Bus Terminal Abucay:** If you are continuing to Samar, Ormoc or Maasin, this is the hub.

Hotels: Basic pension houses start at PHP 800 to PHP 1,500 per night. Mid-range business hotels near downtown like Hotel Alejandro, Ironwood Hotel, Summit Hotel Tacloban (formerly Leyte Park) run PHP 2,200 to PHP 4,500. Power and water are stable post-Yolanda rehabilitation but bring a universal adapter — outages happen in heavy rain.

## 5. What to Eat in Tacloban

Two things you will see everywhere in pasalubong shops along Zamora Street and Real Street:

**Binagol:** Taro (talyan) mashed with coconut milk, condensed milk, egg yolks and nuts, steamed inside a coconut shell and wrapped in banana leaves. Sweet, sticky and rich. PHP 80 to PHP 150 per shell.

**Moron (Chocolate Moron):** Suman-like sticky rice cake made with glutinous rice, coconut milk, cocoa, sometimes peanuts, rolled into a chocolate-vanilla swirl and wrapped in banana leaf. PHP 15 to PHP 25 per piece, PHP 150 to PHP 250 per bundle of 10-12.

Other regulars: kinilaw na latot (seaweed ceviche), tinola na saang (spider conch soup), lechon with Waray-style vinegar dip, and fresh crabs from San Juanico Strait. For a full meal, budget PHP 200 to PHP 400 per person at local eateries like Ocho Seafood Grill, Patio Victoria, or the public market. Seafood is cheaper than in Cebu if bought at the fish port.

**Best for:** Travelers who like coconut-based desserts and want a portable pasalubong that lasts 2 to 3 days unrefrigerated.

**What to do:** Buy binagol and moron on your last day — they spoil after 3 days without refrigeration. Vendors near Savemore Tacloban and at the airport sell vacuum-packed versions.

## 6. Best Months and Costs to Budget Right Now

**Best months:** Tacloban has a tropical rainforest climate with rain even in the dry months. The relatively drier window is March to May — fewer heavy rains, calm seas for San Juanico Strait views, 26°C to 32°C. Next best is December to February — cooler, still good for walking the bridge and Palo. Avoid July to October peak typhoon season; June to October is when flights and ferries get cancelled often. November 8 is the Yolanda anniversary — solemn events but some shops close early.

If you want to time festivals: Pintados-Kasadyaan in late June (Tacloban’s biggest, colorful body-painted dancers) and the Balyu-an rites in early June reenacting the merging of Leyte and Samar peoples near San Juanico.

**Costs from Cebu (per person, one-way, 2025-2026 levels):**
- Flight Cebu to Tacloban: PHP 800 (promo) to PHP 3,800 regular
- Airport taxi to downtown: PHP 150 to PHP 300
- Ferry Cebu to Ormoc slow boat: PHP 900 to PHP 2,800 depending on class
- Fastcraft Cebu to Ormoc: PHP 1,100 to PHP 1,700
- Direct Roble Shipping Cebu to Tacloban: PHP 1,200 to PHP 2,200
- Van Ormoc to Tacloban: PHP 250 to PHP 400
- Jeepney downtown to Palo/Memorial: PHP 20 to PHP 30
- Habal-habal half-day bridge + Palo tour: PHP 800 to PHP 1,200
- Van with driver half-day: PHP 2,500 to PHP 3,500 (split)
- Hotel: PHP 1,000 to PHP 4,500 per night
- Meals: PHP 200 to PHP 500 per person
- Binagol/Moron pasalubong: PHP 80 to PHP 250

For a 2-day weekend from Cebu flying both ways, budget PHP 6,000 to PHP 10,000 all-in for budget to mid-range, including flights, one night hotel, food, and local transport. Ferry via Ormoc can bring that down to PHP 3,500 to PHP 6,000 but costs you a day in transit.

## Tips Before You Go

### Bring cash and small bills
Many jeepneys, habal-habal drivers and pasalubong stalls do not take GCash or cards. The airport ATM charges PHP 250 per foreign card.

### Do not stop in the middle of San Juanico Bridge
Guards may call you out, and trucks have limited shoulder space. Use the designated viewpoints at both ends for photos.

### Charge and download offline maps
Signal drops on parts of San Juanico Strait and Samar side. Download Leyte and Samar maps on Google Maps before crossing.

### Respect the Palo memorial
It is both a tourist spot and a memorial. Do not climb the statues, do not leave trash in the lagoon, and keep voices down near the museum.

### Check bridge status before a Samar road trip
San Juanico Bridge had load restrictions in 2024-2025 that barred heavy trucks and some buses. As of late 2025, two-way traffic reopened but occasional maintenance closures happen at night. If you are taking a van to Samar, ask your driver the night before if the bridge is fully open.

## So, Is Tacloban Worth a Weekend from Cebu?

If you want a city that is easy to navigate, with two national landmarks within 15 kilometers and food you cannot find in Cebu supermarkets, yes. San Juanico Bridge alone is worth the short flight if you like bridges, straits, and sunset views — it is not just longest-by-numbers, it feels long when you cross it with wind on both sides.

Go for two days: Day 1, fly in early, do downtown, Sto. Niño Shrine, then San Juanico for sunset. Day 2, morning at Leyte Landing Memorial in Palo, market lunch, buy binagol and moron, fly back. If you have three days, add a day trip to Kalanggaman Island via Palompon (2.5 hours to Palompon + 1 hour boat, PHP 1,500 to PHP 2,500 joiner) or Cuatro Islas.

Just pick the drier months of March to May or December to February, keep cash ready for vans and habal-habal, and give the bridge the late afternoon it deserves — that is when the light hits the arch and the strait turns gold.
