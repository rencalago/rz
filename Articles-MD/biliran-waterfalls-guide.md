Intro:
Biliran is not the island people think of first when they hear "waterfalls" — but it should be. This small island province just off northern Leyte has over 30 named waterfalls packed into one loop road, which means you can see three to four falls in a single day without rushing.

If you're coming from Cebu, Biliran is surprisingly doable for a long weekend. You don't need a private tour to enjoy it. Most of the headline waterfalls — Tinago Falls in Caibiran, and the Ulan-Ulan and Recoletos pair in Almeria — are right off the circumferential road, with cemented access roads, short treks, and entrance fees that are still ₱20 per person.

In this guide, I put together the practical version: how to get to Biliran from Cebu via Ormoc or Tacloban, how much everything costs right now, what each waterfall is actually like, and how to add Higatangan Island's famous shifting sandbar without spending a whole day waiting for boats.

## How to Get to Biliran from Cebu (Practical Routes)

There is no direct commercial flight to Biliran, and the direct Cebu to Naval ferry only runs a few times a week now. The two reliable routes Cebuanos use are via Ormoc or via Tacloban.

### Route 1: Cebu → Ormoc → Naval (Fastest for most people)

This is what most of us do. Go to Cebu Pier 1 and take OceanJet to Ormoc City. As of January 2024 to mid-2025, OceanJet runs 4 trips daily: 6:00 AM, 9:30 AM, 1:00 PM, 4:30 PM. Travel time is about 2 hours 40 minutes to 3 hours.

Fares (current range): Tourist Class ₱1,100 to ₱1,430, Business Class ₱1,600 to ₱1,950. Price increased in late 2023, so budget ₱1,500 one-way to be safe. Book on oceanjet.net.

From Ormoc port, walk out to the van terminal (Duptours or Van-Vans). Vans to Naval, Biliran depart hourly from 5:00 AM to 4:00 PM. Fare is ₱200 to ₱250 per head, travel time 2 to 2.5 hours via the Biliran Bridge. Last trip is around 5 PM, so take the morning ferry if you can.

### Route 2: Cebu → Tacloban → Naval (Best if you want cheap flights)

Fly Cebu Pacific or PAL from Mactan Cebu International Airport (CEB) to Tacloban (TAC). Flight time is 45 minutes, fares range ₱800 to ₱2,500 one-way if booked 2-3 weeks ahead. From Tacloban Airport, take a jeep or taxi to Van-Vans or Duptours terminal in downtown Tacloban (Abucay) — about ₱50 by jeep, ₱250 by taxi.

Vans from Tacloban to Naval are ₱200 to ₱220, travel time is about 2.5 to 3 hours. Vans also go from Tacloban to Caibiran (₱180-₱220), useful if you plan to start with Tinago Falls first.

### Once you're on the island: Getting around

Biliran's circumferential road makes navigation easy. Options:

- **Habal-habal (motorcycle):** ₱50 to ₱150 per short hop within a town, ₱300 to ₱800 for a half-day waterfall loop from Naval or Almeria. Always agree on price before.
- **Tricycle:** For groups and luggage, ₱100 to ₱300 per ride depending on distance.
- **Rent a motorbike/scooter:** ₱500 to ₱700 per day in Naval. Ask your hotel, they usually have contacts.
- **Day hire van for 4-6 pax:** ₱1,500 to ₱2,500 per day, driver and gas not always included. The standard island tour rate is ₱1,500 for Ulan-Ulan + Recoletos + Agta Beach.

**Pro tip from Cebu:** If you arrive via Ormoc in the afternoon, stay one night in Naval (GV Hotel Naval starts at ₱970 per night, or Agta Beach Resort in Almeria at ₱1,200+), then start your waterfall chase at 7 AM.

## 1. Tinago Falls, Caibiran — The Most Photogenic

**Best for:** First-timers, families, easy access, swimming
**What to do:** Swim in the turquoise basin, picnic at the eco-park amphitheater, photo at the upper cascades

Tinago Falls is the poster waterfall of Biliran, and for good reason. It's in Barangay Cabibihan, Caibiran — about 30 minutes from Naval and just 1 km off the national highway on a fully cemented road. You don't need to hike far. There's parking, a small amphitheater for picnics, restrooms and shower rooms.

It's a 50-foot drop into a deep, swimmable basin. Water is cold and clear year-round. There are about 50 steps down plus 8 steeper steps to get closer to the falls. There's a ramp for easier access.

**Cost:** Entrance fee is ₱20 per person (it used to be ₱10, now updated). No parking fee reported. Cottage rental is ₱100 to ₱300 if you want shade.

**How to get there:** From Naval, van to Caibiran ₱50 to ₱70. Tell the driver to drop you at Tinago Falls crossing. From there, habal-habal ₱20 to the entrance. If you have a scooter, it's a straight ride on the circumferential road.

**Time needed:** 1 to 1.5 hours.

## 2. Ulan-Ulan Falls, Almeria — The Powerhouse

**Best for:** Adventure swimmers, nature lovers, the classic Biliran shot
**What to do:** Trek 20-30 minutes through forest, swim under the strong curtain falls, feel the "rain" that gives it its name

Ulan-Ulan means "rain" in Waray, and you'll get why when you stand in front of it. This is a 25-meter curtain waterfall in Barangay Sampao, Almeria, about 30 minutes from Almeria town proper. It's considered by locals and guides as the most beautiful waterfall on the island.

Access is a bit more adventurous: from the registration area, you trek 20 to 30 minutes on a marked jungle trail (can be muddy after rain). Wear trekking sandals or shoes with grip. The falls itself is powerful — the basin is deep and the current can be strong after heavy rain, so ask your guide if swimming is safe that day.

**Cost:** Entrance fee ₱20 per person for Ulan-Ulan. That same ticket covers Recoletos if you go the same day — they share a registration point. Guide is optional but recommended for first-timers, tip ₱100 to ₱200.

**How to get there:** Naval to Almeria town via van/jeep ₱30 to ₱50. From Almeria, habal-habal to Brgy. Sampao jump-off ₱100 round trip per person (often ₱150-₱200 for solo). The driver will wait for you.

**Time needed:** 2 to 3 hours including trek.

## 3. Recoletos Falls — The Hidden Sister Falls

**Best for:** Quiet swimming, completing the Almeria loop, fewer people
**What to do:** Continue 10 minutes up from Ulan-Ulan, soak in the calmer natural pool

If you reach Ulan-Ulan, don't skip Recoletos. It's just 10 minutes further up the trail. It's named after the Recollect priests who discovered the area. Smaller and more intimate than Ulan-Ulan — low, wide cascades into a calm basin perfect for just floating.

Because most tour groups stop at Ulan-Ulan, Recoletos is often almost empty. That's the win.

**Cost:** No extra fee if you paid the ₱20 for Ulan-Ulan.

**What to do:** Bring a dry bag for your phone. The pool is great for a longer stay. No big current here.

**Time needed:** Add 30-45 minutes to your Ulan-Ulan visit.

## 4. Higatangan Island Sandbar — Not a Waterfall, But Don't Miss It

**Best for:** Beach day between waterfall days, drone shots, island lunch
**What to do:** Walk the shifting sandbar at low tide, snorkel on the edge, eat seafood in town

Higatangan Island is technically off Biliran's coast, but everyone pairs it with waterfall trips. Its sandbar is long, powdery, and shifts shape depending on season — best at low tide early morning.

**Getting there:** Go to Naval Port. There is a daily public passenger bangka (outrigger boat) to Higatangan that leaves around 11:00 AM (sometimes two trips on busy days). Fare is still very cheap, ₱50 to ₱80 per person each way, travel time 45 minutes to 1 hour.

If you miss the public boat or want flexibility, charter a boat: ₱1,500 to ₱2,500 per boat (good for up to 8-10 people) round trip. Resorts like Agta and islands resorts can arrange it.

There is no entrance fee for the sandbar itself, but some barangay environmental fees of ₱20 to ₱30 may be collected. Cottages on the island are ₱300 to ₱500. Bring your own water and snacks unless you plan to eat at local carinderias.

**Time needed:** Half day (6 AM departure to lunch) or full day if you charter.

- Habal-habal Almeria loop (Ulan+Recoletos): ₱400

- Entrance fees (Tinago ₱20 + Ulan/Recoletos ₱20): ₱40

If you go via Tacloban flight on promo, you can replace the ₱2,860 ferry with ₱1,600 to ₱2,000 round trip flights.

## Best Months to Visit Biliran for Waterfalls

Biliran is Type IV climate — more even rainfall — but the practical window is still the dry season.

**Best overall:** December to May. Seas are calmer for Higatangan, trails are less slippery, and waterfalls are still strong but swimmable. March to May has the sunniest days, which is why most travel guides say March to May is the peak for waterfalls and beaches.

**Shoulder good:** February to April is the sweet spot — waterfalls are powerful from early-year rains, but not brown or raging.

**Avoid if you can:** July to October is typhoon season in Eastern Visayas. Boat trips to Higatangan get canceled often, and the forest trails to Ulan-Ulan become very muddy and leechy. If you must go, check Pagasa and ask your habal driver.

For photos, go early morning (7-9 AM) for Tinago to get soft light. For Ulan-Ulan, mid-morning 9-11 AM lights up the falls without harsh glare.

## Tips Before You Go

1. **Cash is king.** There are ATMs in Naval, but not near the falls. Bring small bills. Most jump-offs don't have GCash signal.
2. **Bring aqua shoes or trekking sandals.** Not flip-flops for Ulan-Ulan — the trail crosses roots and small streams.
3. **Dry bag and rash guard.** Water is really cold, but refreshing. Rash guard helps against the strong spray.
4. **Leave no trace.** There's no big trash collection at the falls. Bring back your plastic.
5. **Start early.** If you want to do Tinago + Ulan-Ulan + Recoletos + Agta Beach in one day, leave Naval by 7 AM. It is doable on a motorbike with a guide.
6. **Phone signal is spotty.** Download offline maps. Ulan-Ulan trail has intermittent signal.
7. **Ask locals about water level.** After heavy rain, Ulan-Ulan can be too strong to swim. A day of sun usually makes it safe again.
8. **Combine with Sambawan Island if you have 3 days.** The Sambawan entry is ₱80 + ₱20 env fee, and you can see it together with Higatangan.

## So, Is Biliran Worth the Trip from Cebu?

Yes — especially if you've already done the usual Cebu falls (Kawasan, Tumalog, Dao) and want something less crowded. Biliran gives you three different waterfall personalities in 24 hours: Tinago for the easy win photo, Ulan-Ulan for power, Recoletos for quiet.

From Cebu, you can leave Saturday morning via OceanJet, sleep in Naval, chase falls Sunday, squeeze in Higatangan sandbar Monday morning, and be back in Cebu Monday night. No need to book a fancy tour — vans, habal-habal, and ₱20 entrance fees make it one of the few waterfall destinations in the Visayas that still feels genuinely local and cheap.

Bring respect for the water, some cash, and a good camera. Biliran will handle the rest.
