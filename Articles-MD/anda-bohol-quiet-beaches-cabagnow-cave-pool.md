# Anda, Bohol: Quiet Beaches and the Cabagnow Cave Pool

Anda sits on the far eastern tip of Bohol, about 100 kilometers from Tagbilaran City. While Panglao gets the resorts and the airport crowd, Anda keeps a different rhythm — a long public shoreline where fishermen pull in bangka at dawn, tricycles pass with rice sacks, and the loudest thing at noon is a coconut vendor calling out. It is not empty, just unhurried.

The draw is twofold. First, the beaches. Quinale Beach is the main public stretch — almost a kilometer of fine white sand that the municipal government manages, so there are no resort fences cutting it up. You get clear, shallow water, casuarina shade, and space to walk without stepping over loungers. Second, the cave pools. Anda sits on limestone, and over 20 cave pools have opened across town. The most famous is Cabagnow Cave Pool, a collapsed doline with brackish blue-green water deep enough to jump into.

If you are in Cebu and want a quieter alternative to Alona or Panglao, Anda works well as a 2-3 day side trip. Here is how to get there from Cebu, what the beaches and cave pools actually cost right now, when to go, and how to plan it without backtracking.

## 1. Getting There from Cebu via Tagbilaran

All trips from Cebu go through Bohol island first. From Mactan-Cebu, you have two gateways: Cebu Pier 1 for ferries, or Mactan-Cebu International Airport (CEB) for flights.

**Ferry Cebu to Tagbilaran — the usual way:** OceanJet, SuperCat and Lite Shipping run fast crafts from Cebu Pier 1 to Tagbilaran Port almost hourly from 5:10 AM to 6:40 PM. Travel time is about 2 hours. Current fares as of 2026 are Tourist / Open Air PHP 800 to PHP 965, Business Class PHP 1,200 to PHP 1,428, plus a terminal fee of PHP 25 to PHP 30 at Cebu and PHP 25 at Tagbilaran. OceanJet raised fares in March 2026, so Tourist at PHP 880 is now common online. Book a day ahead in March-April and Holy Week; other months you can walk in.

**Flight Cebu to Bohol-Panglao (TAG):** Cebu Pacific, PAL and AirAsia fly CEB to TAG in 30-45 minutes, often PHP 1,200 to PHP 2,800 one-way if booked early. You still need land transport from the airport to Anda.

**Tagbilaran Port to Anda town:** Once in Tagbilaran, take a tricycle across to Dao Integrated Bus Terminal — PHP 40 to PHP 50 per person, 10-15 minutes. From Dao, Southern Star Bus Transit runs hourly buses to Anda. Fare is PHP 120 to PHP 170 regular aircon, PHP 98 for non-AC Ceres yellow bus, travel time 2 hours to 2.5 hours, some trips stretch to 3 hours with stops. Vans also leave when full — same fare range, slightly faster but more cramped. Rome2Rio and local guides list the same corridor as 2h to 2h20m for buses.

If you have luggage or land after 6 PM, a private van from Tagbilaran Port to Anda costs PHP 3,999 to PHP 5,500 for up to 12 passengers, 2.5 hours. A direct taxi or Grab from port to Anda is rarely available; most drivers offer charter at PHP 2,500 to PHP 3,500.

**From Jagna:** If you arrive via Jagna port from Camiguin or Cagayan de Oro, Anda is actually closer — about 45 minutes to 1 hour by tricycle or habal-habal, PHP 300 to PHP 500 per ride.

**Best for:** Travelers coming from Cebu who want beach time without Panglao's boat traffic.

**What to do:** Take an early ferry (6:00 AM-8:20 AM) so you reach Anda by lunch. Afternoon buses get slower as they fill with students.

## 2. Quinale Beach and the Other Anda White Sands

Quinale Beach is government-owned, so there is no resort entrance fee. You just walk in. The municipal government collects an environmental fee at the Anda town entrance checkpoint — PHP 10 to PHP 30 per person — keep the receipt because Cabagnow guards will ask for it. There is no separate camping fee if you bring your own tent, though some cottages rent kubos for PHP 1,500 to PHP 2,000 overnight.

The sand is fine and white, with a gentle slope. At low tide, the beach widens to 30-40 meters; at high tide, water comes up near the tree line, especially near the eastern end. Swimming is easy inside the bay, but there are no lifeguards on the open beach, only at the cave pools. Vendors sell grilled isaw, fresh buko for PHP 40 to PHP 60, and you can rent a beachfront cottage table for PHP 300 to PHP 500 per day.

Beyond Quinale, walk 10 minutes to Bitoon White Beach and Talisay Beach — same sand, fewer people, almost no vendors. Bitoon is where locals go on Sundays. If you want a resort-style setup, Anda White Beach Resort and other private pockets charge day-use of PHP 100 to PHP 150 if you are not a guest, usually consumable for food.

Water clarity is best November to May. From June to October, runoff can make the shallows cloudy after rain.

**Best for:** Travelers who want a long, swimmable white beach without entrance gates or Alona-style crowds.

**What to do:** Stay in Anda town proper — near Quinale — so you can swim early morning and late afternoon when the sun is low and tricycle rates are normal. Midday, shift to the cave pools for shade.

## 3. Cabagnow Cave Pool — Fees, Hours, and How to Swim It

Cabagnow is in Barangay Bacong, about 5 minutes by tricycle or habal-habal from Anda town center — PHP 50 to PHP 80 per person one-way, or PHP 150 roundtrip with wait. From the drop-off, walk down concrete steps to a large opening in the limestone. The pool is roughly 8 meters deep at the jump point, about 5 feet in the shallows where you can stand. Water is brackish — mix of fresh and seawater — cool even at noon, with small fish inside.

**Current costs and rules:** Entrance fee PHP 50 per person. Environmental fee PHP 30 per person, waived if you show the same-day Anda environmental receipt paid at town entry. Parking PHP 20 per motorcycle, PHP 40 per car. Life vest rental PHP 50 to PHP 100 if you cannot swim. Guide is not required for Cabagnow but three lifeguards are on duty.

**Hours:** 8:00 AM to 3:30 PM daily for swimmers. On Mondays and Fridays, swimming inside the cave is closed for maintenance and cleaning — you can view but not jump or swim. Locals describe it as the deepest and largest cave pool in Anda.

**How it works:** You exit by metal ladder only. The cliff jump is about 4-5 meters; check depth with guards first, do not dive headfirst. Non-swimmers should wear a life jacket — the deep side drops fast. There is a small coffee stand near the guard house.

If Cabagnow is busy, Combento Cave Pool in Barangay Birhen near Bitoon White Beach is a good second. Entrance PHP 10 to PHP 25, about 6 feet deep, dome-shaped ceiling, much smaller but almost no crowd. Lamanok Island with its pre-colonial caves and bamboo bridge is separate — boat tour PHP 300 to PHP 500 per person, requires Coast Guard clearance if seas are rough.

**Best for:** Swimmers who want a natural cave pool you can actually jump into, without a full caving expedition.

**What to do:** Go at 8:00 AM opening when light shafts come through and before tour vans arrive around 10:30 AM. Bring water shoes — the limestone is sharp.

## 4. Other Things to Do Around Anda

Anda is compact; you can cover it on a rented scooter — PHP 400 to PHP 600 per day, helmet included — or by habal-habal full-day charter PHP 800 to PHP 1,200.

**Can-umantad Falls in Candijay:** About 40 minutes north of Anda, still the tallest waterfall in Bohol at about 60 feet, entrance PHP 30 to PHP 50, guide PHP 200 to PHP 300 per group.

**Cadapdapan Rice Terraces:** On the way to the falls, small but photogenic, no entrance fee, donate PHP 20 at viewpoint.

**Anda Bohol Cave Pool Circuit:** If you like hopping, do Cabagnow first, then Combento, then Tibaw Cave Pool — all within 15 minutes of town. Total spend for three pools: under PHP 150 per person excluding transport.

**Diving and snorkeling:** Anda Reef is less dived than Balicasag. Dive shops in town charge PHP 2,500 to PHP 3,500 per day for two dives including gear. Visibility is best March to May.

## Tips Before You Go

### Time it with the dry season
The best months for Anda are November to May, with December to April offering the calmest seas and clearest cave pool water. November and May are shoulder — fewer crowds, still good sun. March and April are peak in Bohol — rooms fill and bus seats go fast. June to October is rainy; boats to Lamanok get cancelled, and Quinale's shallows turn cloudy after heavy rain.

### Keep your environmental receipt
You will pay the environmental fee once at the town entrance. That same receipt waives the PHP 30 at Cabagnow. Without it, guards charge again. Put it in your dry bag.

### Respect the no-swim days
Cabagnow closes to swimmers Mondays and Fridays. The schedule is enforced by LGU caretakers. Plan your cave pool morning on Tuesday-Thursday or weekends, and use Quinale for swimming on closure days.

### Pack for limestone
Water shoes or sturdy sandals matter — both beach entry and cave pool steps are limestone and barnacle-sharp. A dry bag, rash guard, and small cash in PHP 50 and PHP 100 bills help — most caretakers and tricycles are cash-only.

### Plan your exit tide
If you are heading back to Cebu same day, take the 2:00 PM-3:00 PM bus from Anda to Tagbilaran to catch the 5:40 PM or 6:40 PM OceanJet last trips. Do not cut it close — buses delay 30 minutes during market days.

## So, Is Anda, Bohol Worth the Extra Bus Ride?

If Alona Beach is where Bohol puts its nightlife and dive boats, Anda is where it keeps its quiet. Quinale gives you a free, government-run white beach without gate fees, and Cabagnow gives you a safe, lifeguarded cave pool you can jump into without needing a caving guide. The extra 2.5-hour bus from Tagbilaran filters out most day-trippers, so even in April you can still find sand without speakers.

Come from Cebu early, base one night in Anda town, swim Quinale at dawn, hit Cabagnow right at 8:00 AM opening, then loop Combento or Can-umantad before the afternoon heat. For Cebu-based travelers who have already done Panglao, Anda is the logical next beach — same island, very different pace.
