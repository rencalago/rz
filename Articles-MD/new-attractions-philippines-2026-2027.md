# New Attractions Opening in the Philippines in 2026-2027

The next two years will reshape how travelers move around the Philippines. Cebu is getting a 25,000-seat arena and the Visayas' largest convention center, Caticlan is finally upgrading its gateway to Boracay, Davao is connecting to Samal Island by road for the first time, and Manila is opening a long-awaited maritime museum. If you are planning trips in 2026 and 2027, these openings are worth putting on your calendar now.

## Cebu Leads the Buildout in the South

### SM Seaside Cebu Arena

Cebu officially entered the major-league events map on July 11, 2026, when SM Seaside Cebu Arena opened at South Coast City in SRP. Built on a 1.6-hectare footprint with about 7.4 hectares of gross floor area, it seats over 25,000 and was developed by SM Prime as the first purpose-built indoor arena in Cebu province. The first show was the Cebu leg of BINI's Signals World Tour.

The P7-billion venue changes how travelers plan Cebu. Concerts, PBA games, and international volleyball matches that once skipped Cebu are now booking here. If you are heading to Cebu City for an event, book hotels in SRP, Mambaling, or downtown early, traffic around South Road Properties gets heavy on show nights. The arena is a 20-minute taxi from Cebu City proper and close to SM Seaside City.

### SMX Convention Center Seaside Cebu

Right next to the arena, SMX Convention Center Seaside Cebu is set to open in Q4 2026. SM Hotels and Conventions Corp is investing P3.6 billion to P5.3 billion in the project, with over 21,000 square meters of leasable space across six levels and capacity for around 18,000 guests. It will be the largest MICE venue in Visayas.

For travelers, that means more conventions and trade shows in Cebu in late 2026 and 2027, which also means fuller hotels during peak expo weeks. Check the SMX events calendar when you plan a leisure trip so you avoid the surge pricing, or join it if that is your reason to visit.

## Luzon Gateways and Heritage Upgrades

### Caticlan Airport New Passenger Terminal, Boracay Gateway

San Miguel Corp. is building a brand-new two-story passenger terminal at Godofredo P. Ramos Airport in Caticlan, the closest airport to Boracay. The target completion is Q2 2027. The terminal spans 36,470 square meters, with six passenger boarding bridges and 36 check-in counters, designed to handle up to 7 million passengers a year, more than double current traffic.

Today Caticlan can feel tight, with queues spilling outside during Holy Week and summer. The new terminal will add air-conditioned holding areas and direct jet bridges. Groundbreaking was held in July 2025, and construction started in 2026. If you fly to Boracay in 2027, confirm which terminal your airline uses, as carriers will shift once the new building opens.

### Museo del Galeon, Manila

Inside the historic walled city, Museo del Galeon formally opened its first phase in May 2026. Built as a 60-meter full-scale replica of a 17th-century galleon, it tells the story of the Manila-Acapulco galleon trade. It sits within the growing museum cluster in Intramuros that includes Centro de Turismo and Museo de Intramuros, both built on the former San Ignacio Church site destroyed in 1945.

It is an easy add to a Manila day: Fort Santiago in the morning, Museo del Galeon before lunch, then the National Museum complex across Padre Burgos. Confirm opening hours at the gate, Intramuros museums often adjust for holidays.

### Mt. Samat Flagship Tourism Enterprise Zone Visitors Complex, Bataan

In Pilar, Bataan, TIEZA broke ground on April 9, 2026, for a new Visitors Complex at Mt. Samat National Shrine. Completion is set for June 2027. The complex is part of the Mt. Samat FTEZ and aims to improve visitor flow, add multipurpose halls, assistance centers, and better access to the iconic cross.

Mt. Samat is already a day trip from Manila via NLEX and SCTEX, about 2.5 to 3 hours. Once the complex opens in 2027, expect guided heritage trails and improved facilities for Araw ng Kagitingan events every April. It is a meaningful counterpoint to beach itineraries, especially for travelers interested in World War II history.

### NAIA Terminal 4 and New Manila International Airport in Bulacan

Manila's air system is in transition. At Ninoy Aquino International Airport, MIAA is targeting May 2026 for the completion of a new Terminal 4, which will trigger airline reshuffling across terminals. Check your terminal assignment the day before you fly.

The bigger project, New Manila International Airport in Bulakan, Bulacan, is a 2,500-hectare aerotropolis by San Miguel. Terminal construction is scheduled to start in January 2026, with land development and shoreline piling largely done. The Department of Transportation now points to late 2028 for first runway operations, after earlier 2026 targets. You will not board there in 2026-2027, but construction traffic on NLEX is real, and hotel growth in north Manila is already following the project.

## Mindanao: The Samal Game Changer

### Davao-Samal Bridge (Samal Island-Davao City Connector)

This is the most transformative link in Mindanao tourism. The 3.98-kilometer, four-lane bridge will connect R. Castillo-Daang Maharlika junction in Buhangin, Davao City, to Barangay Limao in Island Garden City of Samal, over Pakiputan Strait. It cuts travel from 30-45 minutes by ferry to about 5 minutes by car.

Originally funded through ODA at around P23 billion, the bridge was targeted for 2027 and as of mid-2026 stands beyond 60% complete, with DPWH noting completion before 2028. For travelers, Samal's 130-plus resorts will suddenly be 15 to 20 minutes from downtown Davao. If you plan Samal in late 2027, factor in ongoing works and possible traffic reroutes on the Davao side. Rental car rules on Samal may also change once private cars can drive over.

### Hann Reserve Public Park, New Clark City

At New Clark City in Capas, Tarlac, Hann Resorts broke ground on a 10-hectare public park as part of the Hann Reserve development. Opening is phased from 2026 to 2030. It adds free recreational space near the athletics stadium and complements the growing mix of sports facilities in the city. Combined with the new SciENSE Museum at Clark Cityfront Mall, which opened with interactive zones on the human body, outer space, and dinosaurs, Clark is positioning itself as an educational family stopover between Manila and northern Luzon.

## How to Plan Around These Openings

**Cebu in 2026-2027:** For concerts at SM Seaside Arena, book hotels 6 to 8 weeks ahead. The area around SRP has new hotels but ride-hailing surges after events. Consider staying in the city and taking a taxi early.

**Boracay in 2027:** With Caticlan capacity jumping to 7 million, airfares may stabilize slightly during peak months. If you love the quieter vibe, travel October to early December before the new terminal brings even more foot traffic.

**Bataan heritage run:** Pair Mt. Samat with Las Casas Filipinas de Acuzar in Bagac or a day in Subic. Road access is good, bring sun protection for the cross viewpoint.

**Davao-Samal:** Once the bridge opens, day trips from Davao City to Samal will be normal. You can base in Davao for malls and food and beach-hop in Samal without ferry schedules. Confirm if your resort requires advance vehicle registration for the bridge.

**Manila museum hopping:** Intramuros is no longer a one-hour stop. With Centro de Turismo, Museo de Intramuros, and Museo del Galeon, you can spend a full day. Wear comfortable shoes, walls are uneven, and water stops are sparse inside.

For travelers from Cebu and Negros Oriental, these new gateways matter. Better Caticlan and Davao links mean shorter domestic hops, and the Cebu arena keeps more big events in the Visayas so you skip Manila transfers. Keep an eye on official pages from DOTr, TIEZA, and SM Prime for final opening dates, soft openings often move by a few weeks.
