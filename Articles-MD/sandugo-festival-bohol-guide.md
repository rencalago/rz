# Sandugo Festival Bohol: Tagbilaran's July Celebration Guide

If you are in Bohol in July, you will feel it before you see the schedule. Drums on CPG Avenue, dancers rehearsing at the pier at night, and the whole of Tagbilaran moving a little faster. That is Sandugo Festival, Bohol's month-long anniversary party that commemorates the island's most famous historical moment and turns it into a present-day street celebration.

This guide covers what Sandugo actually celebrates, when the main events happen, what to expect from the street dancing and grand showdown, and how to plan your trip from Cebu without stress.

## What Is Sandugo and Why It Matters

Sandugo is a Visayan word that means one blood. It refers to the blood compact performed on March 16, 1565 between Bohol chieftain Datu Sikatuna and Spanish explorer Miguel Lopez de Legazpi. According to tradition, the two leaders made a small cut, mixed drops of blood with wine, and drank it to seal friendship and alliance. It is recognized as the first treaty of friendship between Filipinos and Spaniards.

The monument most visitors see is the Sandugo Shrine or Eskulturang Sandugo in the Bo-ol District of Tagbilaran City, with life-size bronze figures of the two leaders. Historians also note the actual compact took place in Hinawanan Bay, Loay, but Tagbilaran became the center of commemoration as the capital city.

The festival itself started in 1989 as part of the national Fiesta Islands promotion. It grew from a small civic program into a full provincial festival. It was paused from 2020 to 2022 during the pandemic and resumed in 2023. In 2025 it returned bigger than ever with 12 contingents, double the previous year.

## Why Sandugo Is Celebrated in July

Although the blood compact happened in March, the festival is held in July for two practical reasons. July 1 is Tagbilaran City Charter Day, and July 22 is Bohol Province Day. The two anniversaries are close together, so the city government and the Bohol Sandugo Foundation, Inc. use the whole month for activities.

You will often see July 22 declared a non-working holiday in the province. Expect Tagbilaran to be busiest during the last week of July, when the street dancing, ritual showdown, and closing events are usually scheduled.

## Main Events to Expect

The schedule changes slightly each year, so confirm the final program on the City Government of Tagbilaran Facebook page before you book. These are the anchors that appear almost every year.

### Tagbilaran Charter Day Opening

July 1 opens with a holy mass, a diana or early morning parade, a motorcade, and a city program at the City Hall Atrium. This is more civic than touristy, but it sets the tone and many displays and food booths start to appear downtown.

### Miss Bohol Sandugo

The beauty pageant is one of the most watched indoor events. It is usually held mid-July at the Bohol Cultural Center or at Island City Mall. In 2025, it drew attention when Jacky Fe Tahakim Melencion from the Badjao community was crowned Miss Bohol, the first from her community to win.

### Sandugo Street Dancing and Festival of Festivals Grand Showdown

This is the main event most travelers time their visit for. Contingents from municipalities across Bohol perform their own local festivals in one grand parade.

The route traditionally starts at the City Pier or Plaza Rizal and moves along CPG Avenue toward the President Carlos P. Garcia Sports Complex, also called the CPG Sports Complex. Street dancing typically starts in the afternoon around 3 pm to 4 pm, followed by the ritual showdown inside the sports complex in the evening.

Costumes are heavy on local materials, from raffia to capiz, and performances tell a town's story. Loboc's winning performance in 2025 showcased the Bolibongkingking Festival, a devotional dance honoring Our Lady of Guadalupe de Extremadura and the town's deliverance from a cholera outbreak in 1843. Other towns bring their own angle, like Loay's heritage dances or Dimiao's cultural motifs.

If you want a good spot, plan to be along CPG Avenue by 2 pm. The sidewalk fills fast, especially near Island City Mall and Alta Citta. Bring a hat, water, and patience, because July is rainy season and the show goes on between showers.

### Reenactment of the Blood Compact

A shorter theatrical reenactment of the Legazpi and Sikatuna meeting is staged near the Sandugo Shrine or as part of the opening week program. It is less crowded than the street dancing and useful if you want photos of the historical context without the huge crowds.

### Trade Fair and Food Expo

For the entire month, the DTI Sandugo Product Showcase and the Sandugo OTOP Trade Expo usually run at the Block, Island City Mall. This is where you will find asin tibuok artisanal salt, peanut kisses, handwoven bags, woodcraft, and local coffee. It is also the most practical place to buy pasalubong without going to multiple towns.

### Tigum Bol-anon sa Tibuok Kalibutan

Tigum Bol-anon, or the gathering of Boholanos worldwide, coincides with Sandugo. Overseas Boholanos come home during this week, so hotels fill quickly and family reunions are everywhere. It explains why accommodation prices rise even before the street dancing weekend.

## How to Experience It Like a Local, Not Just a Spectator

Tagbilaran during Sandugo feels different from the usual tourist loop of Chocolate Hills and Panglao. The city is compact, so you can walk between venues if you stay central.

Arrive early for the afternoon parade, then follow the crowd to the sports complex for the showdown. Tickets for the indoor showdown are often distributed by the city or sold at the gate for a minimal fee. If you do not get inside, you can still hear the music and see the contingents gathering outside.

For nightlife, pop-up food stalls line up near the city pier and the CPG Avenue area in the evening. Local bands and school performances often have free outdoor concerts on the same stage used for the pageant.

If you prefer a quieter historical stop, visit the Sandugo Shrine early morning before the heat builds. The nearby National Museum of Bohol is also worth an hour for context on the pre-colonial and Spanish periods.

## Getting to Tagbilaran from Cebu for Sandugo

Most travelers coming from Cebu take the fast craft, which is the fastest way to be in the city center.

**Fast craft ferry:** OceanJet and SuperCat operate from Pier 1 in Cebu City to Tagbilaran Port. The crossing takes about 1.5 to 2 hours. OceanJet runs the most frequent schedule, roughly from 5:10 am to 5:40 pm daily, with departures almost every hour in peak season. SuperCat is a good backup when OceanJet is full. Both ports are a short tricycle ride to downtown Tagbilaran.

Fare varies by class. Tourist or open-air class is typically around 800 pesos one way, business class around 1200 pesos. Book online at least two days ahead for the last weekend of July, because boats sell out when the Tigum Bol-anon influx peaks.

If you are flying in, you can also fly Cebu to Bohol-Panglao International Airport, then take a bus or taxi 30 to 40 minutes to Tagbilaran. But for Sandugo week, the boat drops you right where the parade starts, so most Cebu-based visitors choose the ferry.

For travelers already based in Bohol, buses from Panglao, Anda, or Tubigon all end at the Tagbilaran Integrated Bus Terminal. From there it is a 10-minute tricycle to CPG Avenue.

## Where to Stay and Practical Tips

Book early. Hotels in Tagbilaran City proper, especially around CPG Avenue and near Island City Mall, are mostly fully booked during the last weekend of July. Panglao is an alternative, but allow travel time back to the city during parade closures.

July is wet season in Bohol. Expect short, strong showers in the afternoon. The parade often continues light rain or shine, so bring a compact raincoat rather than an umbrella that will block others.

Roads close around CPG Avenue and the port area during street dancing. Tricycles will reroute or stop temporarily. If you have a ferry to catch that same afternoon, leave much earlier than usual and check the City Government page for traffic advisories.

Bring cash. Trade fairs and side stalls mostly accept cash, and ATMs along CPG Avenue run out faster on festival day.

Finally, be respectful with photos. Dancers rehearse for months, often after work and school until midnight, and many consider their performance a devotion to their town's patron. Ask before stepping into the street for a close-up.

## Is It Worth Planning Your Trip Around July?

If you want beaches only, July is not peak season. If you want to see how Boholanos celebrate their identity beyond the postcards, Sandugo is the week. It ties together history, town pride, and the current push to preserve Bohol as the Philippines' first UNESCO Global Geopark. You get music, dancing, local food, and a sense of why friendship is still part of Bohol's branding as the Friendship Province.

Time your visit for the street dancing weekend, stay two nights, and keep one day open for Chocolate Hills or Loboc if you want nature in between the festival blocks. That balance works well for most visitors coming from Cebu.
