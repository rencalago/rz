# Parada ng Lechon Balayan: What to Expect at Batangas' Roasted Pig Parade

Every June 24, the streets of Balayan in western Batangas turn into a moving feast. Whole lechon, golden and crisp, ride through town on decorated floats wearing sunglasses, wigs, barong, even superhero capes. Behind them come marching bands, dancers, and crowds armed with tabo, timba, and water guns ready to soak everyone in sight.

That is Parada ng Lechon, Balayan's famous celebration for the feast of St. John the Baptist. If you are planning your first visit, this guide covers where it comes from, what actually happens on the day, and how to enjoy it without frying your phone.

## Why Balayan Celebrates With Lechon

Balayan is one of the oldest towns in Batangas, known for Bagoong Balayan and a strong Catholic tradition. The festival is tied to June 24, the feast day of St. John the Baptist, the town's patron.

The custom started in the Kanluran district, the western poblacion that used to be a working-class neighborhood. During the Spanish and American periods, families who received significant blessings during the past year would parade a lechon to the town plaza as thanksgiving to St. John. For families who could not afford to offer a lechon, watching and being part of the procession was its own form of gratitude.

As more families prospered, more lechon joined the parade each year. What began as a neighborhood thanksgiving became a town-wide attraction. In 1959, elders in Kanluran formed the Hermandad San Juan Bautista, or Brotherhood of St. John the Baptist, to organize the celebration. Under the Hermandad, the one-day parade grew into a fiesta that now runs for about two weeks, with the June 24 parade as the highlight.

In 2025, the town celebrated the 66th edition of the organized Parada. The municipality continues to host it every June 24, and a new law, Republic Act 12249, declares June 24 of every year a special working day in Balayan in celebration of Parada ng Lechon.

## What Happens on June 24

### Morning Mass at Immaculate Conception Church

The day starts with Mass at Balayan Church, the Parish of Immaculate Conception and one of the heritage churches of Batangas. The church stands close to the plaza and serves as the spiritual center of the fiesta. Locals attend early Mass to honor St. John the Baptist before the street events begin. If you want a seat inside, come before 6:00 AM, as regular morning Mass is around that time and the fiesta Mass draws a bigger crowd.

### The Lechon Parade

After Mass, the parade assembles. Expect at least 50 to more than 70 lechon, depending on the year. In 2025, organizers listed 71 participants grouped into three clusters: families, barangays, and local businesses and civic groups.

Each lechon is dressed up. Common themes include:

* Filipino costumes like barong, Filipiniana, and katipunero outfits
* Pop culture characters, from cartoon figures to movie heroes
* Business uniforms and brand mascots
* Funny concepts, like a lechon pharaoh or a pig in shades and wig

The lechon ride on carrozas, pickups, and makeshift floats decorated with banana leaves, flowers, and balloons. Owners and barangay teams walk beside them, dancing and handing out smiles. Judges usually select the best-decorated lechon, and there is also a Lechon Queen chosen at the anniversary ball held in the plaza the night before.

### Basaang Balayan: The Water Dousing

The second icon of the festival is water. Because St. John the Baptist baptized Jesus in the River Jordan, towns that celebrate his feast often practice basaan, or water dousing. In Balayan, everyone gets involved.

Residents stand along the route with hoses, pails, and water guns. Kids giggle and chase friends, teens dance under the spray, and even police officers on duty join in while keeping order. The parade itself gets wet, and spectators should expect to be soaked. This is not a light sprinkle, it is a full town water party.

If you are coming from Manila or Cebu and are not used to San Juan-style basaan festivals, think of the water celebrations in Cebu City or San Juan City, but with the added smell of roasting pork in the air.

### Food, Music, and House-to-House Fiesta

After the formal parade, Balayan does what most Batangas towns do during fiesta: open houses. Many families invite relatives and visitors in for lunch. Lechon served at homes is different from the parade lechon, which are mostly for display before being shared later.

Around the town center, you will find food stalls, local kakanin, lomi, tapang Balayan, and bagoong products. Temporary stages host live bands and dance contests until evening. The atmosphere stays lively well past sunset.

## How to Get to Balayan for Parada ng Lechon

Balayan is in western Batangas, about 2.5 to 3 hours from Metro Manila without traffic, longer on fiesta day.

**From Manila by bus or van:** Buses to Balayan leave from terminals in Cubao, Pasay, and Buendia. Vans and shuttle services also run from Zamora Street behind Kabayan Hotel near Metropoint Mall in Pasay. On June 24, book early trips, ideally before 6:00 AM, to reach town before the parade starts around 9:00 AM.

**By private car:** From SLEX, exit at Santa Rosa, drive through Tagaytay, then follow the road to Nasugbu-Balayan. On fiesta day, roads leading into the poblacion close to through traffic in the morning. Park in designated areas outside the center and walk in. Local traffic enforcers publish rerouting a few days before the event on the Municipality of Balayan Facebook page.

If you are coming from Batangas City or Lemery, jeepneys connect to Balayan throughout the day, but expect delays on June 24 due to street closures.

## Practical Tips for First-Time Visitors

**1. You will get wet. Plan for it.** Bring a change of clothes in a dry bag. Wear quick-dry clothes and slippers or sandals you do not mind getting soaked. Keep at least one set of dry clothes in your car or in your accommodation.

**2. Waterproof your valuables.** Put your phone, cash, and ID inside a ziplock or waterproof pouch before leaving. If you use a camera, bring a rain cover. Many visitors wrap phones in plastic even if they have water resistance.

**3. Arrive early for a good spot.** Locals stake out spots along the main parade route near the plaza and the church by 8:00 AM. The parade usually starts after Mass, around 9:00 AM, but exact timing shifts each year. Check the official Balayan LGU page a week before for the final schedule.

**4. Close car windows early.** Children and teens along the highway splash passing vehicles even before you reach the town proper.

**5. Bring cash and small bills.** Food stalls, water, and tricycle rides are cash-only. ATMs get crowded on fiesta day.

**6. Be respectful about water.** Basaaan is part of devotion to St. John, not a free-for-all. Avoid splashing elders who signal no, people carrying electronics, or workers on duty. And do not get angry when you get splashed, that is the whole point of being there.

**7. Stay overnight if you can.** Balayan has limited hotels, but nearby Lemery and Calatagan offer more options. This lets you avoid the heavy outbound traffic in the afternoon and gives you time to try beach spots on the western coast the next day.

**8. Confirm details close to the date.** The parade route, number of participants, and any rules on basaan are updated yearly by the LGU. The municipal tourism office can confirm road closures and Mass schedules.

## Is Parada ng Lechon Worth the Trip?

If you love Philippine fiestas where faith, food, and community mix without being overly commercial, yes. It is loud, wet, a little chaotic, and very Batangueño. Unlike big city festivals that focus on dance competitions, Balayan keeps the center on a simple thanksgiving custom: a roasted pig carried in gratitude.

It also works well as part of a longer Batangas itinerary. You can pair the June 24 parade with heritage walks in Balayan poblacion, a side trip to Calatagan Lighthouse, beach time in Nasugbu, or coffee stops on the Tagaytay ridge on the way back.

For visitors from Negros Oriental and Cebu, flights to Manila followed by a bus to Batangas make it doable for a long weekend. Just pack for rain, since June is wet season.

If you have been to Balamban's Lechon Festival in Cebu or the Wattah Wattah Festival in San Juan, Balayan will feel familiar but distinct. Here the lechon itself is the star, and the pride of the town is clear in how much effort families put into dressing up their offering.

## Quick Facts

* **When:** June 24 every year, feast of St. John the Baptist
* **Where:** Balayan, Batangas, centered around the poblacion, plaza, and Parroquia dela Inmaculada Concepcion
* **Start of organized celebration:** 1959 with the Hermandad San Juan Bautista
* **What to expect:** Holy Mass, decorated lechon parade, basaan water tradition, live bands, community feasts
* **Holiday status:** Special working day in Balayan under RA 12249 as of 2025

Bring curiosity, a waterproof pouch, and an appetite. Balayan welcomes visitors who come ready to celebrate, get soaked, and give thanks with the locals.
