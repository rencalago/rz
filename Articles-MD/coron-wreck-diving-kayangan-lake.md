# Coron Wreck Diving and Kayangan Lake

Coron in northern Palawan has two things you cannot find together anywhere else in the Philippines: a bay filled with intact WWII Japanese warships and a mountain-fringed lake so clear the Tagbanua community guards it as ancestral domain.

Most visitors from Cebu come for both — two to three days for the wrecks if you dive, plus a full-day island tour that links Kayangan Lake and Twin Lagoon. Everything leaves from Coron Town on Busuanga Island, with Busuanga Airport (USU / Francisco B. Reyes Airport) as the entry point.

Here is how the wreck diving actually works, what Kayangan Lake and Twin Lagoon cost now, how to fly from Cebu or Manila, and when to go for the best visibility and calmest seas.

## 1. WWII Wrecks in Coron Bay — What You Are Diving

On September 24, 1944, aircraft from US Task Force 38 attacked Japanese supply ships hiding in Coron Bay. The raid lasted about 15 minutes and sank 10 to 12 vessels. Today they sit upright or on their sides in 5 to 43 meters, covered in coral and full of fish — one of the largest concentrations of accessible wreck diving in the world.

**Best for:** Certified divers who want history without extreme depth, plus snorkelers who want one shallow wreck. Non-divers can still see Lusong Gunboat by snorkeling.

**Key wrecks to know:**

- **Lusong Gunboat / Lusong Island Gunboat** — 2 to 15m, bow breaks the surface at low tide. Best for snorkeling and introductory dives. Covered in soft coral.
- **East Tangat Gunboat** — 4 to 20m, good for first wreck dives and freediving.
- **Olympia Maru** — 3 to 19m, 122m freighter, holds are penetrable with a guide, usually 15 to 25m visibility on good days.
- **Okikawa Maru** — 10 to 26m, 168m oil tanker, the largest wreck in the bay. Bridge and massive hull intact.
- **Kogyo Maru** — 16 to 34m, freighter with construction materials, bulldozer visible in hold, anti-aircraft guns on deck.
- **Morazan Maru** — 14 to 26m, often confused with Olympia, good for Advanced Open Water.
- **Irako** — 28 to 43m, 147m refrigeration ship, deep and serious. Galley with cooking stoves still recognizable. For experienced divers only.
- **Akitsushima** — 22 to 38m, seaplane tender warship with crane wreckage.
- **Taiei Maru, Kyokuzan Maru, Teru Kaze Maru** — 15 to 30m range, less visited, more intact interiors.

Wrecks are about 30 to 60 minutes by dive banka from Coron Town. Operators require valid certification for dives below 18m and penetration. Three wrecks (Olympia Maru, Okikawa Maru, one small freighter) allow swim-throughs where you are never more than 5 meters from open water; others need proper wreck training.

**What to do:** Book a 2-tank wreck day first, then a separate 3-tank day if you want Irako. Most shops provide nitrox for PHP 200 to PHP 400 extra per tank. If you are not certified, do a Discover Scuba Diving day — you can still dive Olympia Maru and East Tangat with an instructor at 10 to 12m max.

## 2. How Much Wreck Diving Costs

You pay per dive day, not per wreck in most cases.

- **Fun dive 2-tank wreck day:** PHP 2,500 to PHP 4,000 per person including boat, tanks, weights, guide, lunch. Rental gear usually PHP 500 to PHP 800 extra if you do not have your own.
- **Introductory dive (no license):** PHP 3,000 to PHP 4,000 per dive, 1 dive max with instructor.
- **Open Water course:** PHP 15,000 to PHP 19,000 for 3 days in Coron Town, wrecks included on days 2 to 3.
- **Wreck specialization / penetration intro:** PHP 5,000 to PHP 7,000 extra, requires Advanced certification.
- **Reefs and Wrecks snorkel tour (no scuba):** PHP 1,700 to PHP 2,300 joiner per person, includes Lusong Gunboat, Skeleton Wreck, Barracuda Lake viewpoint, beach stops. Mask and snorkel rental PHP 100 to PHP 200 if not included.

Add marine park and island fees on top if snorkeling: Skeleton Wreck PHP 100, Siete Pecados PHP 100 to PHP 150, Lusong area PHP 100.

**Best for:** Divers who want to keep costs predictable — stay in Coron Town and book directly with a shop rather than a resort. Shops cluster near the port and include hotel pickup.

## 3. Kayangan Lake — Fees, Rules, and What It Is Like

**Best for:** First morning on the water, photographers, swimmers who like clear freshwater mixed with brackish.

Kayangan Lake is managed by the Tagbanua people. You climb about 300 concrete steps up and down from the boat landing, then swim in water that is about 70% freshwater, visibility up to 10 meters, with limestone cliffs underwater. Life vests are mandatory, no fins in the lake, no drones without permit, no single-use plastic bottles — guides check bags.

**Current fees (bring cash):**

- **Kayangan Lake:** PHP 300 per person
- **Coron / Calamianes environmental fee:** PHP 200 per person on arrival (airport or port, one-time for your stay, keep receipt)
- **Typical joiner Coron Island Tour A / Ultimate Tour that includes Kayangan:** PHP 1,500 to PHP 2,300 per person including boat, guide, buffet lunch, but often excluding entrance fees. Check inclusion — many operators now collect PHP 300 to PHP 500 extra in cash for entrance fees on the day.
- **Private banka for Kayangan + Twin Lagoon route:** PHP 8,000 to PHP 15,000 total for 6 to 8 pax plus entrance fees, depending on boat size.

Kayangan is busiest 10 AM to 1 PM when all Elnido-Coron ferry groups land. Go with a tour that leaves at 7:30 AM or book a private boat that can be at the dock by 8 AM.

**What to do:** Swim to the far end toward the cave wall, float on your back, then climb the viewpoint for the iconic Kayangan Lake overlook — that famous photo is from the hill, not the water. Do not expect toilets at the lake itself; use them at Banol or Bulog stops.

## 4. Twin Lagoon and the Rest of the Loop

**Best for:** Cooling off after Kayangan, snorkeling in thermoclines where warm and cold water mix.

Twin Lagoon is two lagoons linked by a small crevice under a limestone wall. On low tide you swim through a 2-meter gap; on high tide you climb over a wooden ladder. Inside, water temperature changes sharply because one side is warmer brackish water.

- **Twin Lagoon entrance fee:** PHP 200 per person
- **Barracuda Lake (often on same tour):** PHP 200 to PHP 250 per person — famous for dive thermoclines, freedivers love the 30m deep halocline.
- **Banana Island / Bulog Dos / Banol Beach:** PHP 100 to PHP 200 per beach each if your tour stops there.
- **Coral Garden / Siete Pecados:** PHP 100 to PHP 150 per snorkel site.

Most Ultimate or Super Ultimate Coron Island tours package Kayangan Lake (300) + Twin Lagoon (200) + Barracuda Lake (200) + Banana Island + lunch. Even if the advertised price says inclusive, bring PHP 800 to PHP 1,000 extra per person just in case fees are collected separately — operators do that because Tagbanua fees change and are paid directly to guards.

## 5. How to Get There from Cebu and Manila

Coron Town is on Busuanga Island. You fly into Busuanga Airport (USU).

**Option A: Cebu to Coron direct — fastest if you are in the Visayas.**

**Best for:** Cebu-based travelers who want one flight.

Philippine Airlines and Cebu Pacific fly Cebu (CEB) to Busuanga (USU) nonstop. Flight time is 1 hour 15 minutes to 1 hour 35 minutes. Scheduled morning and early afternoon flights most days.

- **Fare range:** PHP 1,999 all-in on promo to PHP 3,500 to PHP 5,500 regular one-way. Cebu Pacific promo often shows as low as PHP 1,408 base before bags, PAL from PHP 1,999 all-in.
- **Airport to Coron Town:** Shared van shuttle PHP 150 to PHP 350 per person, 30 to 45 minutes. Private van PHP 1,500 to PHP 2,500 total. Tricycle can do it but uncomfortable with dive bags.
- **Total Mactan to hotel time:** About 3 to 4 hours including airport wait.

**Option B: Manila to Busuanga — most flights, usually cheapest.**

**Best for:** Those who can position via Manila, or when Cebu-Coron promo is gone.

PAL, Cebu Pacific, Cebgo, Sunlight Air fly Manila (MNL) to Busuanga (USU) almost hourly daytime. Flight time 1 hour to 1 hour 10 minutes, distance about 283 km.

- **Fare range:** PHP 1,758 to PHP 2,089 all-in on promo one-way, PHP 2,500 to PHP 5,000 regular. Last-minute can hit PHP 6,000+.
- **NAIA to Busuanga onward:** Many same-day connections if you fly Cebu to Manila early morning.
- **Same van transfer to town.**

No need to fly to Puerto Princesa and go overland unless you want to combine El Nido — that adds a 5 to 6-hour van + 4-hour ferry or 1-hour flight El Nido to Coron, which costs more than direct Busuanga flights.

## 6. Costs to Budget in PHP

Bring cash — ATMs in Coron Town have long lines and PHP 250 fee per withdrawal, and island fee collectors do not take GCash.

- **Roundtrip flights Cebu-Busuanga:** PHP 4,000 to PHP 10,000 per person depending on sale
- **Roundtrip flights Manila-Busuanga:** PHP 3,500 to PHP 9,000 per person
- **Hotel:** PHP 1,500 to PHP 3,000 fan / budget, PHP 3,500 to PHP 7,000 mid-range with AC near port, PHP 8,000+ beachfront
- **Joiner island tour Ultimate / Reefs & Wrecks:** PHP 1,700 to PHP 2,300 + PHP 400 to PHP 800 entrance fees if not included
- **Private island tour:** PHP 8,000 to PHP 15,000 boat + fees, split by group
- **2-tank wreck dive day:** PHP 2,500 to PHP 4,000 + gear
- **Meals:** PHP 150 to PHP 350 per local meal, PHP 400 to PHP 700 western
- **Environmental fee:** PHP 200 at airport
- **Motorbike rental in town:** PHP 500 to PHP 700 per day if you want Maquinit Hot Springs or town viewpoints

3 days / 2 nights minimum if you just want Kayangan + Twin Lagoon, 4 to 5 days if you want 2 dive days plus island loop.

## 7. Best Months, Permits and Practical Rules

**Best months for wreck diving:** December to March has best visibility, often 15 to 30 meters on wrecks, calm seas from Amihan (northeast monsoon). October to June is the overall dry window with better conditions than July to September. March to May is hottest, driest, excellent sun for Kayangan photos but more boats on the lake.

**Worst months:** July to September is southwest monsoon Habagat — more rain, lower visibility (5 to 15m), higher chance of tour cancellations. You can still dive, but expect greener water and swell.

**Permits and rules:**

- **No general dive permit needed** — your dive shop handles boat manifest. Bring dive certification card and logbook.
- **Tagbanua ancestral domain rules for Kayangan Lake and Twin Lagoon:** No loudspeakers, no takeaway corals or shells, life vest on in Kayangan, no smoking on island stops, strict no single-use plastic enforcement on boats since 2023. Guards can check coolers.
- **Kayangan Lake opening:** roughly 8 AM to 5 PM, last boat out around 4:30 PM. No overnight on Coron Island itself — you stay on Busuanga.
- **Drone:** Requires permit from Coron LGU and Tagbanua elders if flying over Coron Island. Most day tours prohibit launching from boat.
- **Coron environmental fee ticket:** Keep it. Some checkpoints ask for it at Kayangan dock.
- **Dive safety:** No fly within 18 hours after multiple deep wrecks. Schedule island tours after dive days if you have a morning flight next day.

## Tips Before You Go

### Book wreck days in advance in peak season

December to March dive boats fill 2 to 3 days ahead. Reserve Irako boats first because fewer shops run deep trips. If you want nitrox, say so when booking — not all boats carry it daily.

### Bring reef-safe sunscreen and a rashguard for Kayangan

You climb in full sun and then float. Regular sunscreen is discouraged in the lake. A rashguard covers both sun and mandatory life vest chafing.

### Separate your dry bag for fees

Put PHP 1,000 in small bills (100s) in a ziplock for lake guards — Kayangan 300, Twin Lagoon 200, Barracuda 250, plus beach stops. Guides appreciate exact change and you avoid fumbling on a wet boat.

### Do Kayangan early, wrecks mid-trip

Go to Kayangan on your first non-diving day to get the early light and fewer crowds. Do wreck dives next, then leave a rest day before flying. If you are prone to seasickness, take meds 30 minutes before — Coron Bay can be choppy around noon.

### Do not step into the caves in Kayangan

The far end has a small cave that looks tempting. Swimming into narrow caves without a light and guide is not allowed — the Tagbanua guides will call you back, and it is easy to hit limestone in low visibility.

## So, Worth It From Cebu?

Yes if you count travel time correctly. A direct Cebu to Busuanga flight gets you wreck diving the next morning, and you can finish your Palawan loop in El Nido before flying back to Cebu via Manila or Clark. The key difference from El Nido or Moalboal is intent — you are coming here for history underwater and that one clear lake on top of a cliff, not just beach time.

If you are a diver from Cebu who has done Moalboal and Malapascua, Coron is the logical next step because the wrecks start shallow enough to enjoy without tech training, but still offer deep dives like Irako when you are ready. If you are not diving, budget one joiner day for Kayangan Lake + Twin Lagoon + Barracuda Lake, and add a sunset climb to Mt. Tapyas in town for the bay view. Fly December to March for best visibility, carry cash for Tagbanua fees, and keep November to May in mind for dry seas.
