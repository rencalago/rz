# Philippines Ferry Guide: Main Routes and How to Book

Ferries move most inter-island travel in the Philippines, especially when you want to bring a vehicle, save on airfare, or simply enjoy the sea crossing. If you are planning from Luzon to Visayas or hopping between Visayan islands, four names will keep coming up: 2GO Travel, OceanJet, FastCat, and Montenegro Lines. Here is what each does best, the routes you are most likely to use, and how to book without getting stuck in long terminal lines.

## The Four Major Ferry Operators

### 2GO Travel – the long-haul network
2GO is the only large passenger-cargo operator still running Manila-based overnight routes. Its hubs are Pier 4 at Manila North Harbor and Batangas International Port. A Manila to Cebu sailing takes about 22 to 27 hours depending on the vessel and weather, with around four sailings per week. Other core connections from Manila include Bacolod, Iloilo, Cagayan de Oro, Butuan via Nasipit, and Zamboanga. From Batangas, 2GO also runs to Cebu and Caticlan.

2GO offers economy to suite classes, with options for meals and checked baggage included on many tickets. Official booking is at travel.2go.com.ph, with hotline (02) 8528-7000 and outlets in malls.

### OceanJet – the fast craft specialist
OceanJet runs fast craft between Cebu and Bohol with the highest frequency on that corridor. Both OceanJet and SuperCat leave from Pier 1 in Cebu City to Tagbilaran Port in Bohol, but OceanJet operates roughly 15 to 20 trips daily from early morning to late afternoon, with a crossing of 1.5 to 2 hours. OceanJet also serves routes like Cebu to Siquijor via Tagbilaran, Cebu to Ormoc, Cebu to Camotes, and Iloilo to Bacolod.

Seats are divided into Tourist or Open-Air and Business Class. Because seats sell out on weekends and holidays, booking a day ahead is practical even for this short route.

### FastCat – RoRo that takes passengers and vehicles
FastCat is operated by Archipelago Philippine Ferries Corporation and specializes in Roll-on/Roll-off ferries. Key routes include Batangas to Calapan in Oriental Mindoro, Bulalacao to Caticlan in Aklan for Boracay access, Bacolod to Iloilo, Liloan in Leyte to Lipata in Surigao, Dumaguete in Negros Oriental to Dapitan in Zamboanga del Norte, San Carlos in Negros Occidental to Toledo in Cebu, Cebu Pier 3 to Tubigon in Bohol, and Matnog in Sorsogon to San Isidro in Samar.

FastCat vessels are air-conditioned with lounges and allow you to stay with your vehicle booking. The Dumaguete to Dapitan link is important for Negros Oriental travelers going west to Mindanao, and Toledo to San Carlos connects Cebu west coast to Negros without going through Cebu City.

### Montenegro Lines – Batangas-Mindoro and western Visayas
Montenegro Shipping Lines, commonly called Montenegro Lines, focuses on Batangas to Mindoro with nearly hourly departures on the busiest link. Routes include Batangas to Calapan City, Batangas to Abra de Ilog in Occidental Mindoro, Batangas to Puerto Galera and Odiongan in Romblon. They also operate connections like Roxas in Oriental Mindoro, San Jose and Magsaysay in Occidental Mindoro, and services toward Boracay and Palawan on some seasons.

Batangas to Calapan is a 24-hour service on many days, with travel time of about 2 to 3.5 hours depending on vessel type. Montenegro also opened online booking for four routes in 2024: Batangas to Calapan, Abra de Ilog, Puerto Galera and Odiongan.

## Main Routes You Will Actually Use

### Manila to Cebu and Visayas-Mindanao long haul
If you are coming from Manila, 2GO is the default. Manila to Cebu is the signature route, but if your final destination is Dumaguete, you can take Manila to Cebu then connect to a southbound fast craft or bus-van route, or book Manila to Dumaguete via 2GO TravelLink which combines sea and land transfers through 12Go Asia technology. For travelers based in Cebu, the Manila link is also useful for returning with large baggage or a motorcycle that cannot fly.

### Cebu to Bohol: Tagbilaran and Tubigon
This is the busiest fast craft corridor in the Visayas.
* **Cebu Pier 1 to Tagbilaran:** OceanJet and SuperCat, 1.5 to 2 hours, frequent departures. SuperCat tickets can be booked via its official site or Chelsea Travel app.
* **Cebu Pier 3 to Tubigon:** FastCat and Lite Shipping, about 2 hours, good if you are heading to northern Bohol towns like Calape and Carmen without transiting Tagbilaran.

If you are planning a Cebu-Bohol-Dumaguete loop, you can arrive in Tagbilaran, travel overland, then exit via Tubigon or via Jagna to get back to Camiguin or Leyte routes.

### Batangas to Mindoro: Calapan, Puerto Galera, Abra de Ilog
Batangas Port is the main gateway to Mindoro. Both FastCat and Montenegro run this corridor, with Montenegro offering the most frequent sailings. FastCat Batangas-Calapan is often 1.5 to 2 hours, Montenegro RoRo slightly longer but with more vehicle capacity. For beach trips to Puerto Galera, book Batangas to Balatero Pier in Puerto Galera rather than Calapan, it saves a long van transfer.

### Southern connections: Dumaguete to Dapitan, Iloilo to Bacolod, Toledo to San Carlos
* **Dumaguete to Dapitan:** FastCat, about 4 to 5 hours, links Negros Oriental to Mindanao. Check in at Dumaguete Port well ahead because RoRo loading starts early.
* **Iloilo to Bacolod:** FastCat, OceanJet, Montenegro and Starlite all operate this short strait, around 1 to 2 hours. Good for onward buses to northern Negros or Panay.
* **Toledo to San Carlos:** FastCat, about 2 to 3 hours, connects western Cebu to eastern Negros Occidental. Useful if you are coming from Moalboal or southern Cebu and want to avoid Cebu City traffic.

## How to Book Ferry Tickets in the Philippines

### 1. Book online through official channels
Most operators now sell directly online and this avoids long queues, especially during December, Holy Week, and long weekends.
* **2GO Travel:** travel.2go.com.ph. Choose Manila or Batangas origin, select accommodation class, pay via card, e-wallet or over the counter. E-ticket is sent to email.
* **OceanJet:** oceanjet.net and partner platforms like Klook, PHBus, and 12Go Asia which issue an OceanJet ticket number. You still need to exchange the voucher for a boarding pass at Pier 1.
* **SuperCat:** supercat.ph and Chelsea Travel app.
* **FastCat:** fastcat.com.ph and Triphappy.ph for RoRo with vehicle booking. Vehicle slots need advance booking if it is a long weekend.
* **Montenegro:** montenegrolines.com.ph for Batangas-Calapan, Abra de Ilog, Puerto Galera and Odiongan, plus ticket counters at Batangas Port.

Third-party aggregators like 12Go Asia, BookAway and DirectFerries also list schedules and can be helpful to compare departure times, but always confirm the final sailing time on the operator site.

### 2. Book at the terminal or through accredited agencies
Terminals have ticket booths with posted schedules and fares. For short routes like Cebu to Bohol or Batangas to Calapan, walk-in tickets often work on weekdays, but for vehicles and for Manila long-haul routes, walk-in during peak season is risky. Some towns have accredited travel agencies that sell ferry tickets, particularly in Dumaguete, Cebu City and Batangas.

### 3. What you need before boarding
Bring a valid government ID, your e-ticket or booking reference, and arrive early. Standard advisory is 1 hour for fast craft and 2 to 3 hours for RoRo with vehicles, plus extra during holidays. Terminal fee is usually separate and paid at the port, small amount, and baggage allowance varies: fast craft allows limited hand carry, 2GO allows check-in baggage and charged excess.

## Terminal Tips and Practical Advice

**Know your pier.** In Cebu City alone, Pier 1 is for fast crafts to Bohol, Pier 3 for FastCat to Tubigon and RoRo services, Pier 4 for 2GO to Manila. In Manila, 2GO is at Pier 4, North Harbor. In Batangas, all Mindoro ferries use the Batangas International Port passenger terminal, with separate lanes for RoRo and fast craft.

**Confirm at the gate.** Schedules shift with weather, especially during amihan and habagat seasons and when the Coast Guard issues advisories. If you see a cancellation notice online, head to the port office or call the hotline rather than relying on social media reposts.

**Seats and classes.** For Cebu to Bohol, Open-Air or Tourist is fine for the short crossing, Business Class gives you air-con and reserved seating. For overnight 2GO, Tourist vs Stateroom is a big difference in comfort, meals and privacy, bring earplugs regardless of class.

**Vehicles.** FastCat and Montenegro allow motorcycles, cars and vans, but you need OR/CR copies and a driver license. Vehicle boarding is first-come for placement but reservation secures a slot. Fuel up before Batangas or before Toledo and San Carlos, port fuel is limited.

**For Cebu and Negros Oriental base travelers.** If you are staying in Dumaguete, the most useful ferry tools to bookmark are FastCat Dumaguete-Dapitan for Mindanao connections and the Toledo-San Carlos or Bacolod-Iloilo links if you are doing a Visayas loop. For Siquijor, ferries from Dumaguete are run by OceanJet, Montenegro and other operators via Siquijor Port, and booking early on Fridays is smart as island traffic peaks on weekends.

## Final Checklist Before You Sail

Check the operator site the night before, screenshot your booking reference, bring cash for terminal fees, arrive early for vehicle lanes, and keep your ID ready for coast guard inspection. Ferry travel in the Philippines is straightforward once you match the right operator to your route, and booking online removes the biggest stress point, which is standing in line while seats sell out.

For island-hopping itineraries, combine a 2GO long haul for Manila with fast craft for inter-Visayas hops, you get lower cost, more flexibility, and the chance to see the islands from the water instead of above the clouds.
