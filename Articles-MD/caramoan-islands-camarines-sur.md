# Caramoan Islands, Camarines Sur

Caramoan is not an easy beach trip — that is why it is still pristine. On the northeastern tip of Camarines Sur in Bicol, the Caramoan Peninsula juts into the Pacific and Lagonoy Gulf, hiding limestone islands, secret lagoons, milk-white sandbars, and emerald coves you can only reach by boat. It is the Philippines' best answer to Palawan without the same crowds.

What made it famous internationally is Survivor. Since 2008, multiple international editions — US Survivor, Survivor UK, Survivor France (Koh-Lanta), Israel, Sweden, Bulgaria — have filmed here. Matukad, Lahos, Gota Beach and nearby Malarad were literally tribal council and challenge beaches. The rugged limestone cliffs and isolated coves gave producers the wild, untouched look they wanted.

This guide covers the practical side: how to get to Caramoan from Cebu via Manila and Naga, what island hopping really costs in PHP, what to prioritize on Matukad and Lahos, and when to go so you don't get stranded by rough seas.

## 1. How to Get to Caramoan from Cebu via Manila to Naga, then Van and Boat

There is no direct flight from Cebu to Caramoan. You go Cebu to Manila to Naga, then land and sea to Caramoan. It is a long travel day, plan for 10-14 hours.

**Step 1 — Cebu (CEB) to Manila (MNL):** Daily flights with Cebu Pacific, PAL, AirAsia. Airtime 1h15m-1h30m. Promo fares PHP 1,200-2,500 one-way if booked early, regular PHP 2,800-5,500. Mactan-Cebu Airport terminal fee is included, but bring buffer for delays.

**Step 2 — Manila to Naga (Pili Airport - WNP):** Fly Manila to Naga/Pili with Cebu Pacific or PAL. Flight 55m-1h10m, fare PHP 1,500-4,000 one-way. Alternative if no flights — overnight bus: Philtranco, Penafrancia, Raymond Bus, Isarog Lines from PITX, Pasay or Cubao to Naga, 8-10 hours, PHP 700-1,200 aircon. Some companies run direct Manila to Sabang Port buses (San Jose) 10-12 hours, PHP 1,000-1,400, convenient if you want to skip Naga.

**Step 3 — Naga City to Sabang Port, San Jose:** This is the jump-off. From Naga Airport, take tricycle/taxi to SM City Naga Van Terminal (PHP 200-300). Vans to Sabang run 5am-5pm, every 30-60 mins, PHP 150-200 per person, 1.5-2 hours. You can also take a jeep via Lagonoy if vans are full. Road is good, scenic Bicol countryside. If you arrived on a Manila-Naga flight in the morning, you can still catch a 2pm boat if seas are calm.

**Step 4 — Sabang Port to Guijalo Port, Caramoan:** Passenger boats leave Sabang for Guijalo every hour 6am-11am (main window). Fare PHP 120-150 per person, plus environmental/terminal fee PHP 20-40. Travel 1.5-2.5 hours depending on boat type and swell. This crossing is open sea — can be bumpy. If you miss the morning boats, you can charter a boat PHP 5,000-10,000 split among group, or overnight in San Jose. RO-RO options exist for cargo but passenger outrigger is faster.

**Step 5 — Guijalo Port to Caramoan Centro / Paniman / Bikal:** From Guijalo, habal-habal or tricycle to Caramoan Centro PHP 50-100 per person, 30-45 mins. Most accommodations are around Centro or Paniman. For island hopping, boats leave from Paniman or Bikal Beach, 15-20 mins from Centro by tricycle/habal PHP 100-200.

**Best for:** Travelers who don't mind multi-modal travel; photographers chasing unspoiled islands; Survivor fans wanting to stand on actual Tribal Council beach.

**What to do:** If coming from Cebu, book Cebu-Manila on an early morning flight and Manila-Naga midday to make the last Sabang-Guijalo boat the same day. Otherwise overnight in Naga — try SM Naga area for vans next morning.

## 2. Caramoan Island Hopping: The Survivor Location

Caramoan island hopping is split into two main groupings by local tourism.

**Short Trip / Matukad Group:** Closest to Paniman, 10-20 mins by boat per island. This is where Survivor filmed most challenges. Covers Matukad, Lahos, Cagbalinad, Minalahos, Hunongan Cove.

**Long Trip / Gota and Cotivas Group:** Further, 45 mins-1.5 hours. Includes Gota Beach (Survivor base camp for many seasons), Manlawi Sandbar (floating cottages), Cotivas Island, Sabitang Laya, plus Bag-ing Beach.

You can do both groups in one full day if you start at 6am and charter a private boat (PHP 4,000-6,000 for 10-15 pax boat, whole day). Most visitors do Short Trip Day 1, Long Trip Day 2 for a more relaxed pace.

Boat rental (not per person) is the main cost. Per tourism standards:
- Small boat (6 pax): PHP 3,000-3,500 / day
- Medium (10-12 pax): PHP 4,000-5,000 / day
- Large (15 pax): PHP 5,500-7,000 / day
Includes boatman/guide, life vests. Excludes lunch, environmental and island fees.

**Best for:** Anyone who wants that classic white-sand, limestone-cliff photo without El Nido pricing; Survivor super-fans; snorkelers (Bring your own gear — rental limited).

**What to do:** Gota Beach still has the remains of production sets in some seasons — ask your boatman which cove was used for latest Koh-Lanta. Bring dry bag, island hopping involves wading ashore.

## 3. Matukad Island — Hidden Lagoon and the Legend of the Giant Milkfish

Matukad is the poster island of Caramoan and often the first stop from Paniman.

From the boat you see fine white sand, crystal water, and sharp karst cliffs. What most people miss is the climb. On the right side, there is a short but steep limestone scramble (3-5 minutes, with rope assist). At the top you get a view of both sides of the island, then a descent into a hidden lagoon — brackish, surrounded by karst, with two legendary milkfish (bangus). Locals say they are enchanted guardians — feed them but don't take them. The lagoon is tidal and not for swimming, but the viewpoint is stunning.

Matukad was used for Survivor council meetings. The main beach is powdery but gets crowded after 10am when boats arrive. Go early.

Fees: Matukad entrance PHP 30-50 per person, usually included if you go with a registered boatman. Guide optional but useful for the climb — tip PHP 100-200.

**Best for:** First-time Caramoan visitors, hikers who like a short scramble with payoff, photographers. If you can only see one island, make it this one.

**What to do:** Wear aqua shoes or sturdy sandals for the karst — it is sharp and slippery. Bring water, no vendors on island. Don't chase the milkfish story too literally — respect local taboos.

## 4. Lahos Island and Neighbors — Twin Beaches, Sandbars, and Caves

**Lahos Island** sits 5 minutes from Matukad. It looks like one beach from afar, but at mid to low tide it splits into two white-sand coves connected by shallow water — hence twin beaches. At high tide, one side disappears. This dual character made it a favorite Survivor camp. White sand is extra fine, good for wading, limited shade so bring rash guard. Entrance PHP 30-50.

**Hunongan Cove (Hunongan Lake):** Inside Hunongan Island is a small tidal lake accessed through a cave. Calm kayaking spot, less visited.

**Manlawi Sandbar and Floating Cottages:** Long, creamy sandbar with stilt cottages you can rent for lunch (PHP 300-500 per cottage, plus fee to cook). Vanishes partly at high tide, so timing matters — ask boatman for low tide schedule. Best for drone shots.

**Cotivas Island:** Further out, larger, has a fishing village and long stretch of sand. Sunset side is quieter than Centro islands.

**Cost tip:** Island combo package often bundles fees. If booked DIY, total environmental + per-island fees run PHP 250-400 per person per day. Some tour desks charge flat PHP 300 environmental fee plus island fees.

## 5. Costs, Permits, and What to Budget in PHP (2025-2026)

Caramoan is still affordable compared to Palawan, but bring cash — ATMs in Caramoan Centro are few and often offline. Withdraw in Naga City.

**Typical 3D2N from Manila/Naga budget per person (shared boat of 6-10):**
- Van Naga-Sabang roundtrip: PHP 300-400
- Boat Sabang-Guijalo roundtrip: PHP 240-300
- Guijalo-Centro transfers + Paniman habal-habal: PHP 300-500 total
- Accommodation: Budget fan/AC room PHP 800-1,500/night, mid-range resort PHP 2,500-4,500/night (Gota Village, Tugawe Cove, Rex Tourist Inn are common)
- Island hopping boat rental: PHP 400-700 per person per day if shared (PHP 3,500-5,000 divided)
- Environmental + island entrance + Paniman terminal fees: PHP 300-600 per person total for 2 days
- Food: PHP 300-600 per meal if ordering, seafood paluto cheaper if you buy from market and have accommodation cook (PHP 200-300 service). Many homestays offer boodle fight package PHP 350-500 per head.

**If from Cebu, add:** Cebu-Manila-Cebu flights, Manila-Naga flights/ bus. All-in DIY 3D2N from Cebu: PHP 9,000-15,000 per person on mid-range, sharing boat/hotel. Package 3D2N from Naga (van, boat, hotel, tours, meals) operators offer PHP 4,200-6,500 per person for 4 pax up, 3,500-5,500 all-in from Manila packages (van transfers, no flights) start at PHP 5,500-7,000 per head for group.

**Permits:** No special permit for island hopping beyond barangay environmental fees collected at Paniman/Bikal. Bring ID. If drone flying, ask boatman which islands restrict — Survivor production areas and bird nesting coves sometimes ban drones. No advance booking needed for boats except Holy Week and December peak.

## 6. Best Months to Visit Caramoan

Caramoan faces the Pacific, so it feels typhoon season more than western Bicol.

**Best window: February to May.** Driest, calmest seas, clearest water, reliable Sabang-Guijalo crossing and island hopping. March to May is peak — warmest, best visibility for snorkeling but also crowded around Holy Week (rate hike 10-20%). February offers shoulder-season deal with cooler weather.

**Good but transitional: December to January.** Amihan (northeast monsoon) can still bring swell, but many sunny days. Sea is cooler, crowds lower than summer, but check PAGASA forecast — boat cancellations happen.

**Avoid if you can: June to October.** Habagat + Pacific typhoons, rough seas, Guijalo boats cancelled, island hopping shut for days. If you must, add 1-2 buffer days.

For photographers, April low tide in late morning gives best sandbar exposure (Manlawi, Cotivas). December light is softer for limestone shots.

**Best for:** February-May first-timers; April-May island-hopping guarantee; December for quiet.

## Tips Before You Go

### Bring enough cash and power bank

No 24-hour convenience stores on islands. Bring PHP cash from Naga, dry bag, 20,000mAh power bank, and reef-safe sunscreen. No shops on Matukad/Lahos except during peak when locals sell buko and snacks at markup.

### Book boat early, and respect tide times

Boats leave Paniman/Bikal from 5:30am. If you want Matukad empty, go at 6am. Boatmen know tide chart — Manlawi and Lahos twin beach only work at low-mid tide. Ask for tide printout the day before.

### Wear proper shoes

Limestone cuts feet. Aqua shoes or strappy sandals with grip are required for Matukad lagoon climb. Flip-flops not enough.

### Cash permits, not online permits

There is no online portal for Caramoan. Fees are collected in person at Sabang, Guijalo, and Paniman. Registered boatmen have accreditation IDs — use them for safety and insurance. Avoid random beach offers at night.

### Protect Survivor spots

Survivor production sometimes reserves Gota Beach or islands for weeks when filming. Ask tourism office (at Centro) if any islands are closed. Don't move markers or take rocks.

## So, Is Caramoan Worth the Haul from Cebu?

If you are based in Cebu and have only 2 days, go to Malapascua or Bantayan. If you have 3-4 days and want a place that still feels like Survivor — raw, limestone, sandbar that vanishes, hidden lagoon with a folklore milkfish — Caramoan delivers.

From Cebu, the smart play is an early Cebu to Manila flight, midday Manila to Naga flight, overnight in Naga or push to Sabang if boats still run, then two full days of island hopping before returning the same way. It is a long travel day, but cost-wise it stays under PHP 12k-15k all-in for a shared group, which is less than El Nido private boat charters.

Book February to May, go with a registered boat, bring cash and reef shoes, and start early. Caramoan rewards the early boat.
