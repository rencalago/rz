# Philippine International Hot Air Balloon Fiesta, Clark: Complete Guide

The Philippine International Hot Air Balloon Fiesta is the first thing that comes to mind when travelers picture February in Clark. Before sunrise, the field is already full. Coffee vendors are busy, pilots test their burners, and the first glow of balloons lights up the dark. By 6 a.m., dozens of colorful envelopes rise at once over the wide open grounds of Central Luzon. If you want that experience without the stress, you need to know when to go, where it is now held, and how the day actually works.

This guide covers the history, the current venue in New Clark City, when the fiesta usually happens, what you will see beyond balloons, how tickets work, how to get to Clark from Manila and Pampanga, and practical tips that make the early start worth it.

## A Quick Look: When and Where

The fiesta is known as PIHABF, short for Philippine International Hot Air Balloon Fiesta. It started in 1994 and is branded as A Weekend of Everything That Flies. For many years it was held at Clark Freeport Zone in Pampanga, usually on the second weekend of February, Thursday to Sunday, close to Valentine's Day.

Since 2024, the organizer announced a five-year run at New Clark City in Capas, Tarlac. That is still part of the greater Clark area, about 30 to 40 minutes from Clark International Airport. The 26th edition is scheduled for February 13 to 15, 2026, at New Clark City. Check the official site at philballoonfest.net before you book, because dates can shift with weather and aviation clearances.

## History That Started After Pinatubo

The fiesta did not start as just a pretty show. After Mount Pinatubo erupted in 1991, Clark and nearby communities in Pampanga needed a reason for visitors to return. The idea was pushed by then Tourism Secretary Mina Gabor, British Airways executive John Emery, and Korean balloonist Sung Kee Paik, with pilot Capt. Joy Roa leading the organization in later years. The first edition in 1994 at Omni Aviation Complex in Clark had only 21 pilots from 11 countries and a modest crowd.

It grew into the longest running aviation sports event in Asia. It was paused in 1999 due to funding and air traffic concerns, paused again in 2014 when the Department of Tourism hosted a separate balloon event, canceled in 2019 when government commitments did not arrive in time, moved to San Lazaro Leisure Park in Cavite in 2020, and then went on hiatus from 2021 to 2023 during the pandemic. The return to New Clark City in 2024 brought back the full-scale format and drew around 30,000 visitors over the weekend according to the New Clark City announcement.

## What You Actually See at the Fiesta

It helps to think of the fiesta as a full aviation program, not a single balloon launch.

**The mass dawn launch.** This is the main reason people wake up at 3 a.m. Balloons inflate right after the Philippine flag jump by skydivers, usually around 5:30 a.m. The lift-off window is short because pilots need cool, calm air. Come early or you will miss it. The second flight window is late afternoon, around 4 to 5 p.m., when winds settle again.

**Special shape balloons.** Organizers invite teams from abroad who bring figures that are not your classic teardrop. Past years included a sunflower, a soda bottle, a giant pink elephant named Princess Nelly, and character shapes that kids recognize from far away.

**Night Glow.** Tethered balloons stay on the ground after sunset and glow in rhythm to music. It is paired with fireworks and a drone light show. In 2024 and in the 2026 preview, several hundred drones formed patterns above the field.

**Everything else that flies.** The schedule usually includes skydiving exhibitions, paragliding and paramotor demonstrations, ultralight fly-bys, helicopter demos, radio controlled model aerobatics, kite flying, and search and rescue simulations by the Philippine Air Force. For 2026, the lineup also mentions FPV drone racing, drone soccer, paper plane competitions, and the public debut of a Filipino-built manned drone called Hoverbike Air X1.

**Ground side.** There is a fly market with aviation souvenirs, local crafts, and food stalls. Aviation career seminars run during the day, which is useful if you are a student considering flight school. In the evening, local bands perform on the main stage.

## Tickets and How Much to Budget

Tickets are sold on site and online through HelixPay, SM Tickets, and Klook. Prices change yearly, so use these as reference and confirm on the official ticketing page.

In the 2026 announcement, general admission was listed at around PHP 750 to 770 per person per day. A general admission plus aviation seminar pass was higher, around PHP 1,020. VIP access with premium viewing and priority entry was listed at a much higher rate, over PHP 8,000 per day.

Balloon rides are separate and limited by weather and pilot availability. A free flight was listed at around PHP 25,000 per person for 30 to 60 minutes, with a minimum height requirement of 4 feet. Tethered rides that lift a few meters for photos were listed at around PHP 500 for about 10 minutes, with a 3-foot minimum. These slots fill quickly at the info booth, so book early if you want to fly.

Children usually need tickets too, but organizers often announce discounts for kids and groups close to the event. Bring cash for food, parking, and market stalls even if your entry ticket is digital.

## How to Get to Clark and New Clark City

New Clark City is in Capas, Tarlac. It is not inside Angeles City proper, but it is part of the same Clark corridor and is well signed from SCTEX.

**From Manila by car or rental.** Take NLEX then SCTEX and exit at New Clark City or Clark. Driving time is 1.5 to 2.5 hours without heavy traffic, longer on festival mornings. If you plan to drive, leave before 2 a.m. for the dawn launch or book a hotel nearby the night before.

**From Clark International Airport.** Clark is the closest major airport. From the airport, you can book a private transfer or grab a taxi to New Clark City. Travel is about 30 to 45 minutes.

**By public bus.** Take a bus bound for Capas or Tarlac via MacArthur Highway or via SCTEX. Common drop points are Capas Municipal Hall, the KKK gas station area, or the Bamban Capas cloverleaf. From there, tricycles go directly to the New Clark City grounds. Confirm the tricycle fare before boarding and expect surge pricing on event days.

**Parking and shuttle.** Organizers usually arrange designated parking fields with shuttle jeeps to the entrance. Follow the official traffic advisory on Facebook at Philippine International Hot Air Balloon Fiesta. Shoes will get dusty, so wear closed shoes you do not mind cleaning.

## Where to Stay

If you want to catch both the morning and afternoon flights without long drives, stay in the Clark area. Options cluster around three areas: Angeles City and Clark Freeport for more hotels and restaurants, Capas and Bamban for proximity to New Clark City, and Tarlac City for quieter rates. Park Inn by Radisson Clark, Quest Plus Conference Center Clark, Widus, Hilton Clark Sun Valley Resort, and Microtel Tarlac are frequently mentioned by festival package providers. Festival packages that bundle hotel and tickets often include transfers, which solves the early morning logistics.

## Practical Tips From Regulars

**Arrive twice as early as you think.** The mass launch happens once in the morning. If you arrive at 6 a.m., the balloons may already be up. Aim to be inside the grounds by 4:30 a.m. Gates open earlier.

**Plan for sun and dust.** The field is open with little shade. Bring a cap, sunglasses, sunscreen, a face mask or bandana for dust, and a light jacket for the pre-dawn chill. The ground gets hot by midday.

**Bring a mat or low chair.** You will wait between flight windows. The program runs all day, but there are gaps while winds are checked. A ground sheet makes waiting comfortable.

**Camera settings.** For the morning launch, use a fast shutter and continuous focus because balloons move quickly. For Night Glow, tripod or stable bracing helps. Drone flying by visitors is not allowed during air shows, so check the advisory before bringing your own.

**Power and connectivity.** Signal gets congested with thousands of visitors. Download your ticket QR, hotel confirmations, and maps offline. Bring a power bank.

**Food and water.** Food booths are plentiful but lines get long around noon. Bring a refillable water bottle. Alcohol is usually restricted near the flight line.

**Check the wind call.** Balloon flights depend on wind speed and visibility. The pilot briefing decides if flights push through, delay, or cancel. If morning winds are strong, organizers may try again in the afternoon. Follow the PA announcements rather than leaving right away.

**Kids and seniors.** Strollers work but the terrain is uneven grass and dirt. Seniors should plan for long walks from parking and bring portable shade.

## Why It Is Still Worth Going

Even after the venue change from Clark Freeport to New Clark City, the core remains the same: a rare chance to see close to 100 pilots and aviation teams in one weekend in the Philippines, with a program that mixes international visitors and local aviation schools. It is loud, dusty, early, and crowded, but the moment when dozens of balloons lift together over the Zambales mountain range is difficult to match anywhere else in Luzon.

If you are building a Central Luzon itinerary, pair the fiesta with [Clark Museum and 4D Theater](https://www.rencalago.com/clark-museum-pampanga-guide) for history, a food crawl in Angeles, or a side trip to Capas National Shrine which is less than 5 kilometers from New Clark City. Book tickets early, confirm schedules at the gate, and leave room in your plan for a second morning in case wind cancels the first.
