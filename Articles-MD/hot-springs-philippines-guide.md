# Hot Springs Around the Philippines: Where to Soak

If you are looking for a day trip that does not require a boat or a hike to the summit, hot springs are the easiest win in the Philippines. The country sits on the Pacific Ring of Fire, so geothermal water bubbles up in a lot of places: under Mt. Makiling in Laguna, along the slopes of Mt. Talinis in Negros Oriental, and at the foot of Mt. Hibok-Hibok in Camiguin. Some are full resorts with buffet lunch, some are barangay-run pools with a P50 gate.

This guide groups the best options by region, adds what they are actually like to visit, and includes practical notes you can use before you go.

## Laguna: The Resort Capital

Laguna is the default for anyone based in Manila. The towns of Calamba, Los Baños, and Alaminos sit on a geothermal field fed by Mt. Makiling.

### 1. Pansol, Calamba – Public and Private Pools

**What it is:** A long strip of resorts along the Calamba National Highway, most of them fed by natural hot spring water. You will find everything from ₱110-per-head public pools like Laguna Hot Spring Resort to private villas that rent for ₱17,000 to ₱25,000 for 22 hours for large groups.

**Best for:** Groups, budget day trips, last-minute bookings.

**Access:** About 1 hour 20 minutes from Manila without traffic. Buses from Buendia or Cubao to Calamba, then tricycle to Pansol. Most private resorts have parking for 3 to 6 cars.

**Fees:** Public day resorts usually charge ₱110 to ₱250 entrance. Private resorts quote per villa, not per person. Expect small extra charges for gas stove or videoke.

**Tips:** Ask if the water is natural hot spring or artificially heated. The better places in Pansol run water straight from the Makiling source. Weekdays are quieter and cheaper. Bring cash, most smaller resorts are cash-only.

### 2. Hidden Valley Springs, Alaminos

**What it is:** A 110-acre forest reserve between Mt. Makiling and Mt. Banahaw with six filtered pools of varying heat, small waterfalls, and stone paths. It feels more like a jungle walk than a resort pool.

**Best for:** A nature-focused day tour, couples, photography.

**Access:** 1 hour 45 minutes from Manila. Most visitors book a day tour that includes round-trip van from Metro Manila, which removes the need to navigate to Apasan, Alaminos and then tricycle to the gate.

**Fees:** Day tour typically ₱2,250 to ₱2,500 per adult with buffet lunch and afternoon snack, use of all pools, and a cottage. Kids are usually half price, under 5 free. Overnight rates start around ₱3,500. Prices change seasonally, so confirm at booking.

**Tips:** Wear aqua shoes, rocks are slippery. Lunch is Filipino buffet style and is included, so you do not need to pack food.

### 3. Los Baños and Calauan

**What it is:** Los Baños has smaller academic and municipal springs near UPLB, plus heritage spots like the old baths at the edge of Mt. Makiling. Nearby Calauan hosts Silva Hot Spring, known for clear natural water.

**Best for:** Low-key soaks after a Makiling hike.

## Batangas and Quezon Edges

While Laguna gets the fame, the hot spring line extends south.

**Eagle Point area and Bulaklak sites:** Some resorts along the Tagaytay-Calamba road market as hot springs but use heated water. Check if the source is described as Mt. Makiling spring water.

**Laiya? No.** Laiya in San Juan is beach, not hot spring. For real thermal water near Batangas, stick to the Lipa-Alaminos corridor.

## Negros: Two Islands, Two Styles

### 4. Mambukal Resort, Murcia, Negros Occidental

This is technically Negros Occidental, not Oriental, but it is the most developed hot spring resort in the Visayas and easy to reach from Bacolod.

**What it is:** A provincial government-run mountain resort with seven waterfalls, a boating lagoon, bat sanctuary, and three sulfur hot spring areas. One pool was built in 1927.

**Access:** 30 to 45 minutes from Bacolod City. Take Bacolod-Murcia road, follow signs to Mambukal. Tricycles and habal-habal from Murcia proper.

**Fees:** Park entrance is around ₱50 per person. Hot spring pools have separate fees: regular pool about ₱50 for adults, ₱20 for kids, good for whole day; Japanese Ofuro dipping pools about ₱100. Cottage rental and overnight rooms start around ₱1,500. Bring extra for environmental fees.

**Tips:** Weekends and holidays get packed with local families. Sulfur pools are naturally warm, not scalding. There is also a regular cold pool fed by the falls if you want to alternate.

### 5. Valencia and Dauin, Negros Oriental – Close to Dumaguete

If you are based in Dumaguete or Dauin, you do not need to cross to Occidental.

**Red Rock Hot Spring (Pulang Bato), Valencia:** Located in Barangay Pulangbato/Caidiocan, Valencia, about 20 minutes up from Dumaguete City. The pool is stone-lined, surrounded by coconut trees, and the water picks up a reddish tint from iron and sulfur in the rocks. It pairs naturally with Pulang Bato Falls right nearby. Entrance is typically around ₱50. The area is cooler because Valencia sits at elevation.

**Baslay Hot Spring, Dauin:** A barangay-run cluster of pools in the hills above Dauin, Barangay Baslay, about 2,700 feet above sea level. Local government developed it as a basic resort with warm, highly sulfuric water. Access is habal-habal or tricycle from Dauin proper, the last stretch is steep. It is popular for its view over the coast on the way up. Often free or minimal donation, bring your own food as options are limited at the top.

**Mainit Hot Spring, Bais? No, but nearby:** Locals in Mabinay and Valencia also know small Mainit-style community hot springs. Ask at the Valencia tourism office or your dive resort in Dauin, they track which are open after heavy rains.

This is where Cebu-based travelers benefit. From Cebu, a fast ferry to Dumaguete is about 4 to 5 hours, or a flight to Bacolod then van. If you are doing a Negros loop (Cebu to Dumaguete to Bacolod), plan Mambukal and Valencia on separate days.

## Camiguin: The Island Born of Fire

### 6. Ardent Hot Spring, Mambajao

**What it is:** The island's most popular hot spring, at the foot of Mt. Hibok-Hibok in Barangay Esperanza, Tagdo, about 6 km southwest of Mambajao. Water is heated by the volcano and flows into man-made rock pools. Temperature ranges 33°C to 39°C depending on season and pool level. It is not a boiling spring, more a warm mineral bath.

**Access:** 3.5 km off the main circumferential road. Motors can reach it from Agoho or Yumbing beaches in about 15 minutes. Tricycle tours around the island usually include it.

**Fees:** Entrance is low, usually under ₱50 to ₱100, with small extra for cottage. Check current municipal posting at the gate, rates are set by the local tourism office.

**Note:** Locals will tell you it is not as hot as it was a decade ago, volcanic activity shifts. It is best at night or early morning when air is cooler.

**Nearby alternatives:** Sto. Niño Cold Spring for contrast therapy, and Tangub Hot Spring, a natural sea-level hot spring off the western shore that is only visible at low tide. For Tangub, check tide charts in White Island boat offices.

## Other Worthy Mentions

**Puning Hot Spring, Pampanga:** Near Mt. Pinatubo, accessed via 4x4 ride through lahar fields. Eleven pools plus sand spa and mud pack. Day trips from Angeles City or Clark include lunch. Higher price bracket, usually ₱1,500+ per person with transfer, good if you want an adventure combo.

**Maquinit Hot Spring, Coron, Palawan:** One of the few saltwater hot springs in the world. Mangrove-surrounded pools at the sea edge, best at sunset. Entrance around ₱200 to ₱300. Bring a headlamp for the return.

**Asik-Asik Falls has no hot spring, but Kayud?** Kay's Hot Spring, Puerto Princesa, Palawan, offers alkaline pools just outside town, good if you are not heading to Coron.

**Lakewood?** The hot springs around Laguna Lake are mostly the Pansol resorts already listed.

## What to Bring and What to Know Before You Go

Fevers and geothermal water are not just about temperature.

**1. Bring cash, small bills.** Most barangay-run sites like Baslay, Pulang Bato, and Ardent are still cash-only at the gate, and cottages are paid separately.

**2. Water quality matters.** Sulfur water is good for skin but can sting eyes. Do not soak with open wounds. Rinse off after, especially in pools with high sulfur content.

**3. Timing.** Morning light is better for Hidden Valley and Valencia. Evening is better for Ardent and Maquinit because air temperature drops.

**4. What to pack:**
- Dark swimsuit, sulfur can discolor light fabric
- Aqua shoes or slippers with grip
- Quick-dry towel, the resorts provide cottages but not always towels
- Reusable water bottle, hydration helps in hot pools
- Small plastic bag for wet clothes
- Sun protection for day sites like Puning and Maquinit

**5. Safety.** Limit soaking to 15 to 20 minutes at a time, then cool down. This is especially important for kids, seniors, and anyone with high blood pressure. The hottest pools are usually the topmost ones.

**6. Confirm at the gate or with the tourism office.** Fees, pool closures after heavy rain, and road access to Baslay and Mambukal can change. A quick message to the municipal tourism page or your hotel in Dumaguete, Bacolod, or Mambajao will save a wasted trip.

**7. Etiquette.** Many local pools are family gathering spots. Keep noise low, do not bring speakers, and clean your cottage before leaving.

### How to Choose

- For convenience from Manila: Pansol public pools or Hidden Valley if you want lunch included.
- For Bacolod stopover: Mambukal, combine with waterfalls.
- For Dumaguete and Dauin diving trips: Red Rock and Baslay, easy half-day.
- For island-hopping loop from Cebu: Camiguin Ardent plus cold spring combo.
- For adventure + hot spring: Puning with 4x4.

Hot springs are best when you treat them as part of a place, not just a pool. Pair Hidden Valley with a side trip to Pagsanjan or Nagcarlan, pair Valencia's pools with Pulang Bato Falls and Forest Camp, pair Ardent with a walk through Camiguin's old church ruins. That way, even if the water is only warm instead of hot, the day still works.

Safe soaking, and leave the pool cleaner than you found it.
