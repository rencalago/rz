# Philippines Surf Spots by Season: When and Where to Surf

If you want consistent surf in the Philippines, plan around the wind, not just the calendar. The country faces two different oceans, so when one coast goes flat, the other starts working. This guide breaks down the Amihan and Habagat seasons, the best months for Siargao, La Union, Baler, Zambales and Davao Oriental, and which spots fit beginners, intermediates and advanced surfers.

## How Philippine Surf Actually Works: Amihan vs Habagat

The Philippines has two main monsoon patterns that drive surf.

**Amihan, the northeast monsoon,** runs roughly October to late March. PAGASA marks its onset in late October or early November. Cool, dry air moves from the northeast, sending northeast swells into the west-facing coasts of Luzon and steady offshore winds to the Pacific side. For trip planning, think October to March as the western Luzon season and the clean-wind window for the east.

**Habagat, the southwest monsoon,** runs roughly late May or early June to October. Warm, moist air comes from the southwest, bringing rain to western Luzon and Visayas but also pushing swell and typhoon-generated pulses into the Pacific coast. Habagat season overlaps with typhoon season, which is why the east coast lights up hardest from August to November.

Practical takeaway: west coast surf towns like San Juan, La Union and Liwliwa, Zambales depend on north and northwest windswell in Amihan. East coast breaks like Siargao, Baler and Catanduanes depend on east and northeast groundswell that peaks during Habagat and the transition into Amihan.

## Peak Surf Calendar at a Glance

Use this as a quick filter before you book flights.

* **August to November:** Siargao Cloud 9, Puraran Majestics in Catanduanes, Dahican in Mati, Davao Oriental. This is peak Pacific season.
* **September to March:** Baler, Aurora. Longest window of the east coast, with biggest waves typically December to January.
* **October to March:** La Union and Zambales. Best odds for clean, organized swell in San Juan, Urbiztondo, Monaliza Point and Liwliwa. Mid December to January is often the largest.
* **July to February:** Zambales has a wider shoulder than La Union, with La Union more consistent from October onward.

If you only have one trip in the second half of the year, build it around the east coast. If you have holidays in December or January, the west coast will have more options near Manila.

## East Coast Pacific: Where the Power Is

These spots face the open Pacific. Swell is more powerful, typhoon swells can be sudden, and reef bottoms are common.

### Siargao, Surigao del Norte: September to November

Siargao is the surfing capital for a reason. The island sits close to the Philippine Trench, so it catches open Pacific swell early. Peak months are August to November, with September through November offering the most consistent large swells at Cloud 9. The [Siargao Island International Surfing Cup](https://www.rencalago.com) usually runs in late October, which tells you when organizers expect the most reliable surf.

Cloud 9 itself is a hollow right reef barrel for advanced surfers. Beginners should not paddle there on a big day. Instead, go to Jacking Horse and Cemetery for soft, sandy peaks, or to Daku Island for longer rides. Pacifico and Tuason Point sit in the middle, faster and better for intermediates. General Luna has the schools, rentals and direct flights via Sayak Airport from Manila and Cebu, so logistics are easier than in most Pacific towns.

Confirm local advice: reef is shallow at Cloud 9 on low tide, booties help, and typhoon swells can double in size within hours.

### Baler, Aurora: October to March

Baler is where Philippine surfing started after the filming of Apocalypse Now in 1979. It is also one of the most beginner-friendly Pacific towns because Sabang Beach is a long beach break with forgiving sand bottom.

Season is long, October to March, with best quality often December to January. Summer months April to June bring smaller waves, which many schools use for first-time lessons. For skill levels: Sabang for beginners and intermediates, Cemento Reef for advanced only when a solid offshore typhoon swell lines up. Baler is about 4 to 6 hours from Manila by van or bus, so it works for long weekends. Check the municipal tourism office for surf competition schedules like the Aurora Surfing Cup that can crowd the lineup.

### Puraran (Majestics), Catanduanes

On the remote east coast of Catanduanes, Puraran Bay holds Majestics, a powerful right-hand reef break known for long barrels. Season matches Siargao, August to November, triggered by northeast Pacific swells and typhoon pulses. This is advanced only, with rock and coral entry and strong currents. Access involves a flight to Virac or a ferry from Tabaco, then a land trip to Baras. Limited accommodations mean you should book ahead and bring basic repair kits.

### Dahican Beach, Mati, Davao Oriental: October to March

Dahican is a 7 kilometer beach break on Pujada Bay. It faces east-northeast through Mayo Bay, so it catches E to NE swell from August to November and keeps working through March when Amihan winds go offshore. Local guides often quote October to March as the most consistent window.

Waves here range from gentle rollers to punchier peaks, making it suitable for beginners up to intermediates, with advanced sections on bigger days. From Davao City, it is a 4 to 5 hour drive to Mati City, then 15 minutes to Dahican by tricycle or car. The scene is quiet compared to Luzon, with skimboarding and Amihan sa Dahican festival activities mixed in. For another Mindanao option, see our guide to [Mati and Davao Oriental beaches](https://www.rencalago.com).

## West Coast Luzon: Consistent and Accessible

These breaks work on northwest windswell during Amihan. They are closer to Manila, have more surf schools per kilometer, and are generally sand-bottom or mixed.

### La Union, San Juan and Urbiztondo: October to March

San Juan, La Union is the hub, lined with schools, board rentals, coffee shops and hostels. Locals call it Elyu. Primary season is October to March, with peak surf often December and January when Amihan delivers 1 to 3 meter swells on good days. Monaliza Point and Urbiztondo are rockier point and reef options for intermediates when south swells wrap.

Why beginners like it: waves are manageable, instructors are licensed and plentiful, and you can surf in the morning and be back in Manila by night bus. Mid-week lineups are thinner. Weekend crowds are heavy from November to February. Bring a rash guard, water is warm, and rip currents can form after rain.

If you are traveling from [Cebu to La Union](https://www.rencalago.com), fly to Manila or Clark, then bus north for 5 to 7 hours.

### Zambales, Liwliwa and San Narciso: July to February

Liwliwa in San Felipe and Crystal Beach in San Narciso are the main surf areas. The season is wider than La Union, July through February, with the most reliable stretch September to February and peak surf November to early March. Summer Habagat months can still produce if a southwest pulse aligns.

Liwliwa town itself is a laid-back alternative to San Juan, with art cafes and camping-style stays. Most breaks are beach breaks ideal for beginners to intermediates. The exception is The Peak at Liwliwa, an A-frame beach and reef break that turns hollow on solid northwest swell and is best for advanced surfers. It only appears on bigger winter swells, so ask local lifeguards for a read on the day. Lessons are often found near the lifeguard towers or at long-standing camps in Sitio Liwliwa.

From Manila, San Felipe is 3.5 to 4.5 hours by bus or private car, making it a true weekend option.

## Choosing by Skill Level and Season

Match your level to the swell, not just the destination.

**If you are learning:**
Go in the early part of the local season when swells are smaller. For October to March towns, that means October or late February to March. Good bets: Jacking Horse in Siargao, Sabang Beach in Baler, San Juan in La Union, Liwliwa in Zambales, Dahican in Mati. Take an instructor even if you have surfed before, local knowledge about channels and sea urchins matters.

**If you are intermediate:**
Chase shoulder swells. Tuason Point and Cemetery in Siargao, Monaliza Point in La Union, and Cemento on a small day in Baler let you practice takeoffs on faster waves without committing to a full barreling reef. November in La Union and October in Siargao offer cleaner faces than peak typhoon days.

**If you are advanced:**
Time your trip for the typhoon swell window. Cloud 9 from September to November, Puraran Majestics August to November, Cemento in Baler and The Peak in Zambales from December to February. Bring a step-up board, reef booties and travel insurance that covers surf. Confirm swell charts with local forecasters and respect local priority rules.

## Practical Planning Tips for a Seasonal Surf Trip

1.  **Track two forecasts.** One for swell height and direction, one for wind. East coast wants light southwest offshore wind. West coast wants light northeast offshore.
2.  **Mind typhoon safety.** Pacific swells are often generated by typhoons north of the Philippines. Do not chase a wave during a landfall warning. Waves can double and roads can close. Check PAGASA bulletins daily.
3.  **Pack for both coasts.** If you are hopping from Luzon to Mindanao, bring both a shortboard and a fish or longboard, or plan rentals in each town.
4.  **Book around events.** Siargao International Surfing Cup in late October, Baler competitions in early season and La Union long weekends fill accommodations. Book months ahead.
5.  **Confirm entry logistics at the gate.** Many beaches now collect small environmental fees or require registration at the barangay tourism desk. Fees change. Ask the tourism office for the current rate and for restricted zones for swimming.
6.  **Respect the lineup.** The Philippines has tight-knit surf communities. Queue properly, do not drop in, and support local-owned schools and homestays.

The Philippines can surf almost year-round if you follow the monsoon. Start east for power from August to November, then move west for consistency from October to March. Keep an eye on Amihan and Habagat shifts, pick a break that matches your skill, and build in buffer days for flat spells or too-big days.
