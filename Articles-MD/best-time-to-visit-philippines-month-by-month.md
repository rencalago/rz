# Best Time to Visit the Philippines, Month by Month

For most first-time visitors, the sweet spot is December to March. You get cooler air, calmer seas for island hopping, and the lowest chance of a typhoon disrupting ferries. If you want the absolute best beach conditions and you can handle heat, March to early May is even better for diving and flat seas.

But there is no single best month for the whole country. The Philippines has more than 7,000 islands with four climate types, so one island can be dry while another is soaked. The west side, which includes Palawan, Boracay, Manila, Cebu and Negros, is driest from December to May. The east side that faces the Pacific, like Siargao, Samar and eastern Mindanao, gets more rain during the northeast monsoon from November to February.

This guide breaks it down month by month, so you can pick your timing based on weather, festivals, crowds and budget.

## The Two Seasons Explained

PAGASA, the national weather bureau, officially tracks two monsoons. Amihan, the northeast monsoon, brings cooler and drier air from around late October to early April. Habagat, the southwest monsoon, brings warmer, wetter air from May to early October.

Within the dry stretch, locals further divide it into cool dry from November to February and hot dry from March to May. Average temperatures stay between 25 and 32 degrees Celsius year round, but March to May can push past 34 degrees in Manila and inland areas.

Typhoon season overlaps with Habagat. The Philippines averages about 20 tropical cyclones a year, but most make landfall from July to October. Northern Luzon and Eastern Visayas are hit most often. Southern Palawan and southwestern Mindanao, including areas around Zamboanga, rarely see a direct hit.

## Month by Month: Weather, Festivals and What to Expect

### January: Cool, dry and festival-heavy

January is peak dry season for Cebu, Dumaguete, Palawan and Boracay. Days are sunny with lower humidity, seas are generally calm for transfers to Apo Island or Siquijor.

It is also festival month in the Visayas. Sinulog in Cebu City on the third Sunday honors the Santo Niño with a massive street parade. Ati-Atihan in Kalibo, Aklan is often called the mother of all Philippine festivals with painted faces and tribal costumes. Dinagyang in Iloilo City happens the same week. Book flights and hotels 3 to 6 months ahead if you plan to be in Cebu or Panay this month.

Crowds: Very high. Prices: High.

### February: Best overall beach month

Weather stays cool and dry, with less holiday surge than January. This is arguably the most comfortable month for island hopping around Cebu, Negros Oriental, Bohol and Palawan.

Main festival is Panagbenga Flower Festival in Baguio, with flower floats and street dancing in the second half of the month. It gets crowded in the mountain city, so expect traffic and fully booked hotels that weekend.

Crowds: High but easing after Panagbenga. Prices: High to moderate.

### March: Hot dry season begins, great seas

Temperatures start climbing, but the sea is at its flattest. Dive operations consider this prime time. Tubbataha Reefs Natural Park in Sulu Sea opens to liveaboards from mid-March to mid-June only, and slots sell out early.

In Cebu and Dumaguete, March is excellent for dolphin watching in Bais, snorkeling in Apo Island, and day trips without worrying about afternoon downpours. Water is clear and ferries cancel less.

Crowds: Moderate until Holy Week. Prices: Moderate, with a spike during Holy Week.

### April: Summer vacation and Holy Week

April is the height of the hot dry season. Locals are on school break, so beaches are busy. Holy Week, which moves between late March and mid-April, is a major national holiday. Many businesses close Maundy Thursday and Good Friday, while buses, ports and airports are packed. In 2026, Holy Week falls in early April.

Culturally, Moriones Festival in Marinduque and Lenten rituals in many towns are worth seeing if you are interested in religious traditions. If you travel during Holy Week, reserve transport early and plan for quiet, alcohol-free days in smaller towns on Good Friday.

Crowds: Very high during Holy Week. Prices: Highest after Christmas.

### May: Flower festivals before rains arrive

May is still dry in the west but you will feel scattered thunderstorms by mid-month, especially in Manila and Cebu. It is the last month of the hot dry season.

Do not miss Pahiyas Festival in Lucban, Quezon on May 15, where houses are decorated with kiping, colorful rice wafers, and fresh produce. Flores de Mayo and Santacruzan processions happen across the country.

Crowds: Moderate, dropping after Holy Week. Prices: Shoulder pricing starts.

### June: Start of wet season in the west

PAGASA usually declares the start of Habagat around late May to early June. The western side gets more regular rain, but it is often short afternoon bursts rather than all-day downpours.

Surf season starts to build on the Pacific side. Siargao begins to see more consistent swells, though September is still bigger. If you want to travel in the Visayas, you can still do it. Cebu and Dumaguete will have rainy days, but mornings are often sunny.

Crowds: Low. Prices: Low season begins, good deals.

### July: Rainy and affordable, choose your region

July is deep into the wet season for Luzon and the western Visayas. Manila, Zambales and Palawan can be wet and humid. Floods and ferry cancellations are more common.

For travelers who want lower prices and fewer tourists, this is a workaround month. Pick areas that stay drier in Habagat, like Cagayan de Oro, Davao, or parts of southern Palawan. If you are in Dumaguete, diving in Dauin remains possible because the area sits in a rain shadow and the reef is close to shore.

Crowds: Low. Prices: Lowest.

### August: Kadayawan and fewer tourists

Similar weather to July, with the added risk of typhoons passing north of Luzon. If a storm is active, some island transfers in northern areas pause.

Kadayawan Festival in Davao City usually runs in the third week of August and celebrates the harvest of durian, fruits and flowers. It is a great alternative to the January festivals if you want culture without peak-season prices in Mindanao.

Crowds: Low. Prices: Low.

### September: Peak of typhoon season in the north

September is statistically the most active month for typhoons in the Luzon Strait. Avoid planning tight inter-island hops in the north this month unless you have flexible days.

In the Bicol region, Peñafrancia Festival in Naga City brings a colorful fluvial procession. In Bohol and Negros, seas can be choppier, so check wind forecasts for small boat trips.

Crowds: Very low across beach resorts. Prices: Cheapest, but factor in weather risk.

### October: Transition month and MassKara

By early October, PAGASA typically declares the end of Habagat. Rains start easing, and Amihan begins to return late in the month. Weather is still mixed, with rain in the west and improving conditions in the east.

This is MassKara Festival month in Bacolod, Negros Occidental, just a ferry away from Dumaguete. The smiling masks and street parties usually happen in mid to late October. It is one of the most photogenic festivals and hotel prices in Bacolod spike that weekend, so book ahead.

Crowds: Low to moderate. Prices: Still low, rising in Bacolod for MassKara.

### November: Amihan returns, good for island hopping again

November marks the real start of the dry and cooler season for the western Philippines. Seas calm down, visibility improves for snorkeling, and beach towns reopen full schedules.

Surf season peaks in Siargao for Cloud 9, and Siargao International Surfing Cup events often fall in this window. However, eastern Visayas can still get rain from the tail end of the northeast monsoon. For Cebu to Dumaguete routes, November is a reliable month again.

Crowds: Moderate, building toward holidays. Prices: Shoulder to high.

### December: Christmas season and dry days

December brings consistent sunshine to Palawan, Boracay, Cebu and Negros, and the country enters its famous long Christmas celebration. Simbang Gabi dawn masses run from December 16 to 24. In San Fernando, Pampanga, the Giant Lantern Festival with massive illuminated parols lights up the night, usually on the second Saturday of December.

This is also the busiest travel month for Filipinos returning home. Expect airports and ferries to be crowded from December 20 onward. If you want Christmas atmosphere plus beach time, come early December.

Crowds: Very high from third week. Prices: Highest.

## Regional Differences That Matter

If your entire trip is in Cebu, Bohol and Dumaguete, your calendar is simpler. December to April gives you the driest days, calmer 8 a.m. boat rides to Apo Island, and reliable early morning ferries to Siquijor. May to October still works because the Central Visayas does not get as much rain as Manila, but add a buffer day for rain.

If you plan to split Palawan and Siargao in one trip, you cannot optimize both at once. Palawan is best December to May, Siargao for surfing is best September to November. Most travelers who want to do both pick March to May for good general weather and compromise on surf size.

Mindanao is diverse. Davao and northern Mindanao are less affected by typhoons and can be visited even in July to August, while eastern Mindanao towns like Bislig get heavy rain during Amihan.

## Crowds and Budget at a Glance

Peak and most expensive: Late December to early January Christmas and New Year, Holy Week around March to April, and third week of January for Sinulog in Cebu. Hotels can quadruple and ferries sell out. Book 3 to 6 months ahead.

Shoulder and best value: February to early March, late April to May, and November. You still get dry weather with fewer local tourists.

Low season and cheapest: June to September. You will find the best hotel promos and fewer people on beaches, but you need a flexible itinerary. Confirm boat schedules at the port or with your resort, and build in an extra day between transfers.

## Practical Tips to Pick Your Month

Choose January to February if festivals and cool dry weather are your priority, and you do not mind crowds.

Choose March to May if diving, flat seas and maximum sun are your priority, and you can handle heat.

Choose June to August if budget and empty beaches matter more than perfect sun, or if you are surfing the Pacific side.

Choose October to December if you want to see MassKara, lanterns and Christmas traditions plus the return of dry season.

Whatever month you pick, check PAGASA bulletins for active tropical cyclones, and for inter-island trips keep your schedule loose. For popular festivals, reserve your accommodation first before flights, since guesthouses are limited in festival towns.
