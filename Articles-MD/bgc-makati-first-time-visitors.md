# BGC and Makati for First-Time Visitors

If you are flying in from Cebu and want a clean, walkable introduction to Metro Manila, start with Bonifacio Global City (BGC) in Taguig and Makati CBD. They are 4 to 6 kilometers apart, both built for pedestrians by Manila standards, with wide sidewalks, English-friendly menus, 24-hour cafés, and easy Grab access.

BGC feels new — high-rise condos, street art, Bonifacio High Street, SM Aura, Market! Market!, and the Mind Museum. Makati CBD feels established — Ayala Triangle Gardens, Greenbelt and Glorietta malls, Salcedo and Legazpi weekend markets, Ayala Museum, and Poblacion just on the edge for nightlife. Together they cover shopping, museums, parks, and food without the jeepney-and-MRT learning curve of older districts.

This guide is written for first-timers coming from Cebu via Manila NAIA: how the flight works, exact transport options from NAIA to BGC and Makati with current PHP costs, what to do in each area, where to eat, safety, best months, and how much to budget per day.

## 1. How to Get There from Cebu via Manila NAIA

**The flight — Cebu (CEB) to Manila (MNL):** Cebu Pacific, Philippine Airlines, and AirAsia Philippines fly Mactan-Cebu International Airport to Ninoy Aquino International Airport every 30 minutes from early morning to late night. Flight time is 1 hour 35 minutes to 1 hour 45 minutes. One-way fares are PHP 1,200 to PHP 2,500 on promo or off-peak, PHP 2,500 to PHP 6,000 regular, and PHP 6,000 to PHP 9,000 on holidays or last-minute. Add PHP 200 to PHP 600 for checked baggage on low-cost carriers. All four NAIA terminals can receive Cebu flights — check your terminal on the booking.

**Once you land at NAIA — how long will it take to exit?** For domestic arrivals, plan 20 to 45 minutes from gate to arrivals hall to get bags. NAIA has free WiFi — use it to book your Grab while still inside.

**Four ways from NAIA to BGC and Makati, with current costs:**

1. **Grab Car (most convenient for first-timers):** Book in the app, choose Cash or GCash, meet at the designated Grab pickup zone per terminal. NAIA to BGC: PHP 250 to PHP 450 off-peak, PHP 400 to PHP 600 in rush hour (5 PM to 8 PM), 25 to 50 minutes without heavy traffic, 60 to 90 minutes in rush hour. NAIA to Makati CBD (Ayala Ave / Greenbelt): PHP 200 to PHP 350 off-peak, PHP 300 to PHP 500 rush hour, 15 to 35 minutes off-peak, 40 to 75 minutes rush hour. Tip: Have your hotel or mall name ready — e.g., "Seda BGC, 30th St" or "Greenbelt 3, Makati".

2. **Yellow Airport Metered Taxi (coupon taxi stand):** Available at arrivals. Tell the dispatcher your exact address. Flag fall PHP 40 to PHP 48, NAIA to Makati around PHP 350 to PHP 450 flat coupon, NAIA to BGC around PHP 400 to PHP 550 flat. Ask for a receipt. Avoid white taxis offering a fixed price without meter — they overcharge.

3. **UBE Express P2P Bus (cheapest aircon direct):** UBE Express NAIA routes run from all terminals to Glorietta / Makati and to Market! Market! / BGC. Fare PHP 150 to PHP 200 per person. Travel time 45 minutes to 1.5 hours depending on traffic. Runs roughly 5 AM to 10 PM, every 30 to 60 minutes. Pay cash to the conductor. Good if you have one backpack and no rush.

4. **Regular city taxi + BGC Bus combo (budget + local experience):** Take a metered taxi to One Ayala Terminal or Market! Market! (PHP 150 to PHP 250), then ride the BGC Bus. BGC Bus fare is PHP 15 to PHP 40 per ride depending on route, with cards sold at terminals. This is only worth it if you want to see the city slowly — Grab is simpler first time.

For Cebu travelers doing a weekend: fly Friday early morning Cebu to Manila, grab to BGC, stay 2 nights, return Sunday evening Manila to Cebu. No ferry needed — ferry Manila to Cebu is 22 hours and PHP 1,200 to PHP 2,300, not practical for a city break.

**Best for:** First-timers who want the least hassle should use Grab off-peak. If you arrive 5 PM to 8 PM on a Friday, expect surge pricing — wait 15 minutes airside or take UBE Express.

## 2. Bonifacio Global City (BGC) — What to Do

BGC is a former military camp turned master-planned city. Streets are numbered, sidewalks are wide, and you can walk at night.

**What to do:**

- **Bonifacio High Street and Central Square:** Free, open 10 AM to 10 PM shops, but the walk itself is the attraction. Morning walk 6 AM to 8 AM is quiet with joggers. Street art walls along 28th, murals rotate. Cafés every 50 meters.
- **The Mind Museum:** At JY Campos Park. Open Wed to Sun 9 AM to 6 PM, closed Mon-Tue. Standard admission PHP 750 per person for 3 hours, free for kids under 2 feet. Discounted rates around PHP 190 to PHP 625 for school promos or Klook pre-book. Book online — walk-ins queue. Five galleries: Atom, Life, Earth, Universe, Technology.
- **Manila American Cemetery and Memorial:** Just outside BGC, free entry, quiet, open 8 AM to 5 PM. Best early morning before heat.
- **SM Aura Premier, Market! Market!, Uptown Mall, Mitsukoshi BGC:** All within BGC Bus ride. No entrance fees. Supermarkets for water and snacks.
- **BGC Arts Center, Burgos Circle, Track 30th:** Free to walk, ticketed shows at Arts Center from PHP 300 to PHP 1,500.
- **BGC Bus loop:** If tired, ride the full loop for PHP 15 to PHP 40 to orient yourself.

**Where to eat in BGC:**

- **High Street & Central Square:** Wildflour Café, Wrong Ramen, 8 Cuts Burgers, Frank & Dean. Average PHP 350 to PHP 650 per main, PHP 900 to PHP 1,200 with coffee.
- **The Alley at BGC's C1 Park area:** Hidden alleys with Japanese, Thai, Filipino craft beer — Chef's kiss for diversity.
- **Market! Market! Food Court & Serendra:** More local — PHP 150 to PHP 300 per meal (tapsilog, lechon manok).
- **Upmarket:** Mecha Uma, The Test Kitchen, Gallery by Chele — PHP 1,500 to PHP 3,000 per person tasting.

**Best for:** First-timers who like walkable, Instagrammable, family-friendly Manila. Safe at night compared to other districts, best for 1 to 2 days as a base.

## 3. Makati CBD — What to Do

Makati CBD is the old financial district built around Ayala Avenue. Less flashy than BGC, more green spaces and weekend markets.

**What to do:**

- **Ayala Triangle Gardens:** Free park in the middle of Paseo de Roxas, Ayala Ave, Makati Ave. Open 6 AM to 10 PM. Great for morning jog or free Christmas lights show mid-November to early January. Surrounded by fast food and coffee.
- **Ayala Museum:** At Greenbelt, entrance PHP 300 to PHP 650 for adults depending on exhibit, kids discounted, free on Sundays for some promos. Covers Philippine gold, dioramas of history, contemporary art. Aircon break from heat, 2 hours enough.
- **Greenbelt & Glorietta Malls:** Connected by covered walkways. No fees. Greenbelt has the chapel, Glorietta has more shops. Landmark Food Center inside Landmark Makati — PHP 120 to PHP 250 meals.
- **Salcedo Saturday Market & Legazpi Sunday Market:** Salcedo at Jaime Velasquez Park, Saturdays 7 AM to 2 PM. Legazpi at Legazpi Park, Sundays 7 AM to 2 PM. Safe, yuppie-style farmer's market. Budget PHP 100 to PHP 300 per dish — try fresh lumpia, bagnet, paella, vegan bowls, cold brew. Bring cash and eco-bag.
- **Poblacion (edge of Makati):** 10 minutes from CBD — street food, Agimat, Alamat craft cocktails, Z Hostel rooftop. More local nightlife but still walkable early evening.
- **Ayala-Buendia to Jupiter street art walk:** Small but free.

**Where to eat in Makati:**

- **Budget pro lunch hubs:** The Grid Food Market at Power Plant (just outside CBD but BGC Bus accessible), Foodie hubs at RCBC Plaza / V Corporate Centre / The Columns — meals PHP 150 to PHP 300.
- **Filipino comfort:** Ayala Triangle has Fely J's (PHP 300 to PHP 600 mains), in Greenbelt: Mesa, Manam.
- **International splurge:** La Cabrera (Argentine steak, PHP 800 to PHP 1,800 mains), Mendokoro Ramenba on Makati Ave (PHP 350 to PHP 550 ramen), Sala by Chef Colin McKay — fine dining PHP 1,200 to PHP 2,500.
- **Poblacion late:** Hummus Elijah, El Chupacabra, Tambai.

**Best for:** First-timers who want culture, weekend markets, and an older Manila feel without leaving the CBD safety net. Best for 1 day plus a market weekend.

## 4. Transport Inside BGC and Makati

You do not need to rent a car.

- **Walking:** BGC blocks are 300 to 500 meters, Makati CBD 400 to 600 meters. Sidewalks are lit. Use pedestrian crossings — cars rarely yield fully.
- **BGC Bus:** Three main routes — Central, East, West. PHP 15 to PHP 40. Tap with card at terminals near Bonifacio High Street, Market! Market!, Ayala MRT. Runs 6 AM to 10 PM, more frequent 7 AM to 9 PM. Look for signs at BGC Bus stations.
- **Grab / taxi meter:** Inside BGC to Makati CBD: PHP 120 to PHP 230 off-peak, PHP 250 to PHP 350 rush hour, 15 to 30 minutes off-peak, 40 to 70 minutes rush. Short hops inside BGC/Makati: PHP 80 to PHP 150.
- **MRT-3 for Makati:** Ayala MRT station on EDSA connects to Glorietta via pedestrian bridge. MRT fare PHP 16 to PHP 30, but crowded 7 AM to 9 AM and 5 PM to 8 PM. Not recommended with luggage.
- **Jeepney (Makati only, for experience):** PHP 13 to PHP 25 per ride, but confusing for first-timers. Use only if with local.

Budget for transport: PHP 300 to PHP 600 per day if using Grab twice plus walking.

## 5. Costs, Budget, Safety, Permits/Fees

**No permits needed** for BGC and Makati public areas. Only ticketed attractions charge.

- **Flights Cebu to Manila one-way:** PHP 1,200 to PHP 6,000.
- **NAIA to BGC/Makati Grab:** PHP 200 to PHP 600 per ride.
- **Accommodation:** BGC hotels PHP 2,500 (3-star like Seda / F1 / Ascott) to PHP 6,000 to PHP 12,000 (Grand Hyatt, Shangri-La The Fort). Budget condos via Airbnb PHP 1,800 to PHP 3,500 per night. Makati similar: Holiday Inn, Citadines PHP 2,800 to PHP 5,500.
- **Food per day:** PHP 600 to PHP 1,000 budget (food courts, markets), PHP 1,200 to PHP 2,000 mid-range (café lunch + casual dinner), PHP 2,500+ if fine dining.
- **Attractions:** Mind Museum PHP 750, Ayala Museum PHP 300 to PHP 650, BGC Arts Center shows PHP 300 to PHP 1,500. Parks and markets free.
- **Total realistic first-timer daily budget (excluding Manila flight):** PHP 2,000 to PHP 3,500 budget backpacker (hostel, market food, bus), PHP 4,000 to PHP 7,000 mid-range comfortable (hotel sharing, 2 Grab rides, museum + dinner).

**Safety:** Both districts have private security and are considered safer than most of Metro Manila for tourists.

- Walk on main avenues after dark — High Street, 7th Ave, Ayala Ave, Greenbelt — avoid dark vacant lots near Kalayaan and J.P. Rizal side late night.
- Use Grab at night, not hailing taxis on EDSA.
- Keep phone in front pocket in markets — markets are safe but crowded, watch for pickpockets same as anywhere.
- Scam check: Do not accept help from strangers offering cheaper taxi at arrivals — use the official Grab zone or Yellow Taxi stand.
- Water: Buy bottled — PHP 20 to PHP 40 per bottle.

**Best months:** Manila is year-round but best November to April — dry season, Amihan. December to February coolest (24 to 31°C), least rain, perfect for walking BGC to Makati. March to May hottest (33 to 36°C) — do parks early morning. June to October wet season — afternoon downpours 3 PM to 6 PM, more traffic, still doable with malls. For weekend markets: Salcedo and Legazpi run year-round except heavy typhoon warnings.

## Tips Before You Go

### Book Grab while still on NAIA WiFi

NAIA's pickup zones are far from exits. Connect to free WiFi before you leave, pin your exact destination with building name, and choose Cash if GCash fails. Save a screenshot of the plate number — signal drops in arrival bays.

### Stay in one district, play in both

If first time, base in BGC for walkability and newer hotels, then day-trip to Makati via BGC Bus + Grab — 20 minutes off-peak. Switching hotels same day wastes time in Manila traffic. Two nights BGC + one night Makati is enough for a first taste.

### Bring cash small bills for markets

Salcedo and Legazpi vendors prefer cash PHP 50, PHP 100. GCash Pay accepted at some stalls but cash faster. ATMs in BGC High Street and Greenbelt charge PHP 250 per foreign withdrawal — bring PHP from Cebu.

### Use BGC Bus for orientation but not with large luggage

BGC Bus is crowded 8 AM and 5 PM. No luggage rack. Use Grab if you have medium suitcase. Inside BGC, walking is often faster than waiting for bus during rush hour.

### Dress code: Casual but not beachwear

BGC and Makati malls deny entry in slippers and sando for some upscale restaurants. Shirt, closed shoes or neat sandals, shorts are fine. For Ayala Museum, casual smart ok.

### Download offline maps

Google Maps works but signal drops in Greenbelt 3 to Glorieta underpass. Download BGC + Makati offline map. Mark your hotel, Market! Market!, One Ayala, Ayala Triangle.

## So, BGC or Makati for Your First Time?

If this is your first time in Manila from Cebu and you want easy, clean, and safe — stay in BGC.

You get Bonifacio High Street for free evening walks, the Mind Museum for a half-day aircon activity, art walls, and Market! Market! for budget eats. Then take a Saturday Grab to Salcedo Market in Makati (7 AM to 2 PM, PHP 150 to PHP 350 Grab from BGC), walk Ayala Triangle Gardens, visit Ayala Museum, have lunch at Landmark Food Center, and be back in BGC by 4 PM before rush hour.

Plan PHP 1,200 to PHP 2,500 for Cebu-Manila flight one-way, PHP 500 to PHP 1,200 for NAIA transfers roundtrip, PHP 2,500 to PHP 5,500 per night for a decent hotel, and PHP 800 to PHP 1,500 per day for food and fun. November to February gives the best walking weather.

You do not need permits — just show up, walk, eat, and Grab when tired. That is the BGC and Makati advantage for first-timers.
