# Ati-Atihan and Dinagyang 2027 (January)

January is festival month in Western Visayas. Two of the biggest — Ati-Atihan in Kalibo, Aklan and Dinagyang in Iloilo City — both honor the Santo Niño and happen within days of each other. Ati-Atihan is called the Mother of All Philippine Festivals, an open street party. Dinagyang is a choreographed tribes competition with judging stages.

In 2027, preliminary Ati-Atihan activities run January 7 to 17, with main days around January 16 to 19 depending on final church calendar, while Dinagyang's main weekend is expected on the fourth Sunday of January — January 24, 2027 — with Kasadyahan and Tribes Competition on January 23 to 24. Dates are finalized by the LGUs and church in November to December 2026, so confirm before booking.

Here is how the two festivals differ, where to base yourself, how to get there from Cebu, and what to budget.

## 1. Ati-Atihan Festival 2027 — Kalibo, Aklan

**What it is:** Street dancing where anyone can join. Participants paint faces black, wear tribal costumes, and dance to drums along Kalibo streets for hours. It is religious at its core — devotion to Santo Niño — but feels like a city-wide party.

**2027 schedule (based on early announcements):** Opening Salvo in early November 2026 (ceremonial launch and Binibini ng Kalibo Ati-Atihan presentation), then main festival week January 7 to 17, 2027. Peak days are usually Friday to Sunday of the third week — expect January 16 to 17 for street dancing and January 17 to 19 for religious events. Final dates overlap with church calendar and are confirmed by Kalibo LGU.

**Where it happens:** Pastrana Park, Martelino Street, and the streets around Kalibo Cathedral. Pastrana Park was rehabilitated and is now the central stage for opening salvo.

**What to see:** Sadsad street dancing (anyone can join), tribal groups, Higante puppets, and the Santo Niño procession.

**Best for:** Travelers who want an immersive, participatory festival rather than a seated show.

**What to do:** Wear clothes you do not mind getting painted or wet, bring a small waterproof bag for phone and cash, and join a sadsad group in the afternoon — mornings are for watching, afternoons are for dancing.

## 2. Dinagyang Festival 2027 — Iloilo City

**What it is:** A tribes competition. Costumed tribes perform choreographed stories honoring Santo Niño and Panay's history, judged at multiple stages. It is more organized than Ati-Atihan, with ticketed grandstands.

**2027 schedule (based on tradition and early organizer notes):** Dinagyang is held on the fourth Sunday of January, right after Sinulog in Cebu and Ati-Atihan in Kalibo. For 2027, that Sunday is January 24. Kasadyahan sa Kabanwahanan (provincial festivals showcase) and Tribes Competition are usually Saturday and Sunday — January 23 to 24, 2027. Organizers in 2027 plan a return to City Proper routes with judging areas at Iloilo Freedom Grandstand, Provincial Capitol, Quezon-Ledesma, Mabini-de Leon, and Iznart Street, plus new stage roofs and online ticketing to fix 2026 viewing complaints.

**Where it happens:** Downtown Iloilo City, Freedom Grandstand along Iloilo River, and surrounding streets.

**What to see:** Kasadyahan (showcase of Iloilo province festivals), Dinagyang Tribes Competition (Ati tribes with elaborate floats), and the fluvial procession of Santo Niño on Iloilo River.

**Best for:** Travelers who want a seated, photogenic competition with clearer viewing and less street chaos.

**What to do:** Buy grandstand tickets early — online queue opens weeks before. Choose Freedom Grandstand for the full production, Capitol for less crowded viewing. Bring a hat and water — performances run under midday sun.

## 3. How They Differ — Which Should You Choose?

- **Participation:** Ati-Atihan — anyone can join street sadsad. Dinagyang — audience watches judged tribes from stands.
- **Vibe:** Ati-Atihan — raw, loud, street-party, face paint. Dinagyang — choreographed, theatrical, competition scoring.
- **Crowd and safety:** Both draw hundreds of thousands. Ati-Atihan crowds are spread across streets, Dinagyang crowds concentrate at grandstands. Both have heavy police deployment — 1,700+ personnel in Kalibo for 2027.
- **Religious core:** Both honor Santo Niño, both have masses and processions, but Ati-Atihan's religious events are more intertwined with street dancing.
- **If you can do both:** Many travelers do Ati-Atihan January 16–17 in Kalibo, then take a van or bus Kalibo to Iloilo (4–5 hours) or fly Kalibo to Iloilo via Manila/Cebu, then catch Dinagyang January 23–24 in Iloilo. That gives you both styles in one trip.

## 4. Getting There from Cebu

**Cebu to Kalibo for Ati-Atihan:**
- **Direct flight CEB to KLO:** Cebu Pacific, Philippine Airlines, and AirAsia fly CEB to Kalibo International Airport, about 1 hour. Fares PHP 1,200 to PHP 4,000 one-way. Kalibo Airport to Kalibo town is 10 to 15 minutes by tricycle PHP 50 to PHP 100.
- **Via Caticlan + van:** Fly CEB to Caticlan (MPH), then van Caticlan to Kalibo 1.5 to 2 hours PHP 200 to PHP 500. Useful if Kalibo direct flights are full.
- **Ferry Cebu to Caticlan/Kalibo:** Cokaliong and Trans-Asia overnight ferries, 11–13 hours, then van.

**Cebu to Iloilo for Dinagyang:**
- **Direct flight CEB to ILO:** Multiple daily flights, 1 hour, promo PHP 1,000 to PHP 2,500. Airport to Iloilo City Proper 30–45 minutes PHP 100–150 shared van or PHP 400–700 Grab.
- **Ferry Cebu to Iloilo:** Overnight ferries 11–13 hours, PHP 800–2,500.

**Kalibo to Iloilo between festivals:** Van or bus 4–5 hours PHP 400–700, or fly via Cebu/Manila if you want faster.

## 5. Costs, Where to Stay, and Practical Tips

**Accommodation:** Book 3–4 weeks ahead for peak festival weekends. Kalibo hotels range PHP 1,500–5,000 per night, Iloilo City PHP 2,000–6,000. Many require 2–3 night minimum during festival. Stay near Pastrana Park in Kalibo or near Freedom Grandstand/Atria in Iloilo for walking access.

**Food and water:** Street food stalls are everywhere — chicken inasal, batchoy, and BBQ. Drink bottled water, not tap. Bring a reusable bottle to refill at hotel.

**What to bring:** Hat, sunglasses, reef-safe sunscreen, light rain poncho (January can still have showers), small waterproof bag for phone and cash, earplugs if you stay near drum routes, and clothes you do not mind getting dirty.

**Safety:** Both festivals have heavy police presence. Keep phone and wallet in front pockets, avoid large backpacks in dense crowds, and agree on a meeting point if you split from friends — mobile signal drops during peak hours. Do not bring valuables to street sadsad.

**Tickets:** Ati-Atihan has no ticket for street dancing — free to watch and join. Dinagyang grandstand tickets cost PHP 500 to PHP 1,500 depending on location and day — buy via official Iloilo Festivals Foundation online queue, not scalpers.

## So, Ati-Atihan or Dinagyang for 2027?

Choose Ati-Atihan in Kalibo if you want to be in the dance, painted face, drums all day, and the title of Mother of All Festivals. Choose Dinagyang in Iloilo if you want choreographed performances, clearer viewing from a grandstand, and a city that also offers La Paz batchoy and Guimaras day trips.

If you have 10 days in January, do both: Ati-Atihan January 16–17, travel to Iloilo January 18–19, Dinagyang January 23–24. Fly Cebu to Kalibo and Iloilo to Cebu to avoid backtracking.
