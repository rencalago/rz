# Summer in the Philippines: Top Destinations for March to May

March to May is the Philippines' official hot and dry season, the window most Filipinos call summer vacation. School is out, seas are calmest in the west, and island tours run with almost no rain interruptions. The trade-off is heat in the lowlands that can reach 34 to 36°C, and crowds in famous spots, especially around Holy Week and Labor Day.

If you want to make that season work for you, decide early which kind of heat you prefer. The Philippines gives you two real options: lean into it on a beach where the sea breeze does the cooling, or escape it in the highlands where pine trees and elevation drop temperatures by 8 to 12 degrees.

Here is where to go, what each place is best for in March to May, and how to handle crowds.

## The Beach Route: Calm Seas and Long Days

### 1. El Nido, Palawan

El Nido sits on the western side of Palawan where the northeast monsoon has faded by March. That means flat water for island hopping in Bacuit Bay, good visibility for snorkeling around Shimizu and Seven Commandos, and sunset flights that rarely divert. Direct flights from Manila on AirSWIFT land in Lio, or you can fly to Puerto Princesa and take the 5 to 6 hour van north.

El Nido works best if you book tours A and C in advance for weekdays rather than weekends. Local operators limit boats per lagoon, and Big Lagoon permits sell out during Holy Week. Stay in Lio or Corong-corong if you want quieter nights than El Nido Town.

### 2. Boracay, Aklan

Boracay is at its driest from December to May, with March and April offering the most reliable sunshine and the gentle easterly wind that makes White Beach swimmable. The island has a daily entry cap and strict activity zones since its 2018 rehabilitation, so you need to pre-book accommodation with a DOT-accredited resort to get your QR code.

Crowd management in Boracay is about timing. Late March to early April before Holy Week is busy but manageable. If you are coming during Holy Week, book boats and e-trikes early and expect a no-party curfew on the beach itself.

### 3. Panglao and Balicasag, Bohol

Bohol leans into summer. The Chocolate Hills turn brown exactly in March to May, which is the point of seeing them. Panglao Island has white-sand beaches without the Boracay density, and water between Panglao and Balicasag stays calm enough for snorkeling and diving.

Panglao Airport has direct flights from Manila, Cebu, and Davao. Base yourself in Alona if you want restaurants and dive shops in walking distance, or Danao Beach or Doljo if you want a longer beach walk with fewer vendors.

### 4. Siargao, Surigao del Norte

Siargao flips common wisdom. Surf season peaks August to November, but March to May is the best window for non-surfers. The island is dry, sunny, and ideal for Magpupungko Rock Pools, Sugba Lagoon, and cycling between General Luna and Pacifico. Cloud 9 still has small waves for beginners, without the lineup stress of peak surf months.

Flights go to IAO via Cebu or Manila. The island has fully rebuilt since Typhoon Odette, with power and resorts stable, but reserve transfers in advance as roads get dusty and traffic spikes in the afternoons around General Luna.

### 5. Camiguin and Bantayan: The Quieter Choices

If popular beaches feel too packed, look at Camiguin in Northern Mindanao and Bantayan Island in Northern Cebu.

Camiguin is a small volcanic island you can loop in a day by motorbike. White Island sandbar, Ardent Hot Springs, and cold springs balance out in a single loop. The best months are April and May when sea crossings from Balingoan are calmer.

Bantayan Island has Bakhaw Beach and Kota Beach Park with wide, shallow water perfect for families. It is less developed than Boracay, so you get long beach fronts without resorts blocking access. Take the bus to Hagnaya Port and then the 1-hour ferry. Summer brings reliable bangka schedules, unlike July to September.

## The Cool Route: Mountain Towns When Lowlands Hit 34°C

### 6. Baguio, Benguet

Baguio is called the Summer Capital for a reason. At 1,400 meters, daytime temperatures hover around 20 to 26°C while Manila hits mid-30s. March is especially festive because the Panagbenga Flower Festival usually extends into early March with street dancing and float parades along Session Road.

Baguio's summer downside is traffic. The city sees heavy volume from February to May. Stay upcountry in Pinsao or near Camp John Hay if you want quieter mornings, and walk or book local jeepneys for Mines View and Botanical Garden.

### 7. Sagada, Mountain Province

Sagada sits about 1,500 meters high, 5 hours from Baguio via Halsema Highway. Nights can drop to 12 to 16°C even in April, and Sumaguing Cave stays cold enough for a real cool-down swim.

Sagada caps visitor entries for caves and hanging coffins through its local tourism office, and guides are mandatory. Register at the Municipal Tourism Office upon arrival, and book a guide the night before if you plan for sunrise at Kiltepan. Holy Week and long weekends fill homestays fast, so reserve at least two weeks ahead.

### 8. Tagaytay, Cavite

Tagaytay works as a quick escape, 1 to 2 hours from Manila depending on traffic. At 600 meters, it overlooks Taal Volcano and Taal Lake with constant wind. March to May mornings are clear, giving you the full view of the volcano before afternoon clouds move in.

Pick a weekday for the best experience. Tagaytay's ridge restaurants get long queues on weekends, and the Santa Rosa to Tagaytay road jams up by 10 a.m. on Saturdays.

### 9. Canlaon, Negros Oriental and Bukidnon

Two locals-favorite cool spots rarely in Manila-centric lists. Canlaon City sits at the foot of Mount Kanlaon, with natural springs and cooler highland farms. Bukidnon's Dahilayan and Lantapan areas offer pine forests and highland resorts similar to Baguio but with far fewer visitors.

Agoda and DOT have previously highlighted Canlaon and Tanay, Rizal as alternatives to Baguio for travelers who want cooler weather without the Baguio crowd build-up. In Canlaon, coordinate with the City Tourism Office for treks and hot springs access.

## How to Plan Around Summer Crowds and Heat

**Book Holy Week and Labor Day separately.** The busiest dates every year are Palm Sunday to Easter Sunday and April 30 to May 2. If you travel then, expect double van and boat fares, limited last-minute rooms, and mandatory online booking for island tours.

**Use the dry window wisely.** December to May is dry in western Philippines, but March to May is the only period when both east and west are reasonably dry. Siargao is an exception: March to May is drier on the east coast compared to December to February, which is why many domestic families prefer it for summer.

**Protect the midday.** Island tours default to 9 a.m. to 4 p.m. Bring rash guards, refillable water, and reef-safe sunscreen. In El Nido and Boracay, operators often allow early 6 a.m. starts on private boats if you ask a day ahead.

**Festivals add crowds and color.** February has Panagbenga in Baguio, Holy Week has Moriones in Marinduque, and May 15 has Pahiyas in Lucban, Quezon. If you want the festival, book two months ahead. If you want quiet, avoid those towns on those dates.

**Check sea and activity status.** Even in summer, coast guard advisories can suspend boat tours after a sudden low-pressure area forms. Ask your resort or tourism office for the morning sea condition bulletin rather than relying only on weather apps.

Summer in the Philippines does not have to mean overheating or standing in lines. Pick one beach base and one mountain base, stay longer in each, and travel between them on weekdays. That split gives you both the classic sunny island photos and a real chance to sleep without air conditioning.
