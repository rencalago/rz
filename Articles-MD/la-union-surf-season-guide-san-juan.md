# La Union Surf Season Guide: San Juan Spots, Lessons, Costs and Tips

San Juan in La Union is called the Surfing Capital of the North for a simple reason: it gets consistent, rideable waves for half the year and has turned that consistency into a full surf town. Urbiztondo Beach is a long, dark-sand strip lined with surf schools, board rentals, cafes, and hostels. You can walk from your room to the water in five minutes and find an instructor before you finish your coffee.

The surf season runs from October to March, shaped by two swells. The South Swell from July to October is less organized and more for those already surfing. The North Swell from November to March brings cleaner, more consistent sets that beginners can actually learn on. Most first-timers aim for November to February when the waves are reliable but manageable.

This guide covers what to expect during surf season in San Juan, where the main spots are, how much lessons and rentals cost in 2025, where to stay from budget to beachfront, how to get there from Cebu via Manila, and what to do for food and nightlife when you're out of the water.

## 1. When is Surf Season in La Union? Best Months Explained

Surf season in La Union officially runs October to March, with peak conditions from November to February.

**October - transition:** Crowds start to arrive, winds shift to northeast. Waves start building but can be inconsistent. Good for avoiding peak crowds if you have some experience.

**November to January - peak:** This is the core season. The northeast monsoon (amihan) delivers steady 3-6 ft sets to San Juan and Bacnotan. San Juan gets the most learners because the waves break softer over sand. Expect weekends and holidays to be packed. Competitions often happen in this window.

**February to March - late season, beginner friendly:** Still solid surf but often smaller and cleaner in the morning. Less crowded than December. Many schools say this is the best window for complete beginners because conditions are less intimidating.

**April to September - off-season:** Not flat, but waves are smaller, more wind-affected, and less predictable. Some surf still happens during the southwest monsoon, especially July to September in the south swell spots.

**Best for:** First-time lessons - December to February for consistent small-to-medium waves; experienced surfers chasing bigger days - November to January.

**What to do:** Book ahead if you're going December, Holy Week, or long weekends. Mornings are generally best before the onshore wind picks up. Check with your hostel or instructor the night before for tide timing.

## 2. San Juan Surf Spots: Where to Surf

Most people surf Urbiztondo, but San Juan has a few distinct breaks within a 20-minute tricycle ride.

**Urbiztondo Beach (San Juan town proper):** The main beach break. Sandy bottom, wide, forgiving. This is where 90% of schools operate. Waves are typically 1-3 ft for beginners on the inside, larger outside. Best for learning, longboarding, and first green waves.

**Monica Pier / Monaliza Point (Barangay Urbiztondo):** At the south end of the bay near the old pier. Slightly more powerful, can get hollow on good swells. More locals and intermediate surfers. Watch for rocks near the pier foundations at low tide.

**Bacnotan (further south, 15-20 mins from San Juan):** More open swell, less crowded. Known for slightly bigger sets. Some instructors bring intermediates here when Urbiztondo is too small or crowded.

**San Fernando Bay:** Inconsistent but an alternative when San Juan is flat.

**Best for:** Absolute beginners - inside of Urbiztondo; improving surfers - outside Urbiztondo and Monaliza; escaping crowds - Bacnotan on weekdays.

**What to do:** Ask your instructor where they plan to take you based on the swell that day. Urbiztondo is the default, but on bigger days beginners may be moved to a more sheltered section.

## 3. Beginner Lessons and Board Rentals: What They Cost

San Juan is one of the cheapest places in the Philippines to learn to surf. Prices are fairly standardized along Urbiztondo Beach.

Surf lesson with instructor, 1 hour, including board: PHP 400 per hour. This is the standard rate posted by most schools including La Union Surf School, San Juan Surf School, Sebay Surf School. The instructor stays with you in the water and pushes you into waves.

Surfboard rental only, 1 hour: PHP 200. Half-day rental is usually PHP 500 and full-day PHP 800, though some shops charge PHP 600 half-day depending on board quality.

Surf guide or advanced coaching, 1 hour: PHP 500. This is for surfers who can already paddle out but want line-up guidance.

A typical beginner does 2 hours in a morning, then rests. Expect to pay around PHP 800 for instruction plus board for two hours, plus maybe another PHP 200 if you rent extra time to practice alone.

**Best for:** First-timers budgeting for 2-3 sessions; travelers bringing their own rash guard and reef booties to avoid rental fees.

**What to do:** Confirm total time includes briefing on land. Time your lesson with your instructor at the start. Bring or buy a rash guard - Urbiztondo's sun is strong even in amihan season. Tip your instructor if you got up - PHP 50-100 is common and appreciated.

## 4. Where to Stay in San Juan During Surf Season

Prices jump during October to March, especially December to February and weekends. Book at least 2-3 weeks ahead for beachfront, earlier if December.

**Budget - Hostels and guesthouses - PHP 800 to 1,800 per night:** Flotsam and Jetsam Artist Beach Hostel, Circle Hostel La Union, and many Urbiztondo homestays. Dorm beds from around PHP 800-1,200, private rooms from PHP 1,500. Walking distance to rentals and cafes. Good for solo travelers.

**Mid-range - Surf resorts and boutique hotels - PHP 2,500 to 5,000 per night:** San Juan Surf Resort, Kahuna Beach Resort and Spa, and resorts along Urbiztondo Highway. San Juan Surf Resort has been operating since 1992 and is right across the road from the beach with rooms around PHP 3,500-4,500 in peak. Expect small pools, restaurant, board storage.

**Higher-end - Beachfront villas and design hotels - PHP 6,000 to 15,000+:** The Modernist boutique hotels north of Urbiztondo, Awesome Hotel, and private villas in Ili Norte. Many are PHP 8,000-12,000 in peak season for 2 pax. Villas like Alesea and Oeste can go much higher but split well with groups.

**Best for:** Surfers who want to be in the water at 6am - stay in Urbiztondo proper; quieter nights - Ili Norte or Bacnotan side where resorts are more spread out.

**What to do:** If you are sensitive to noise, avoid rooms directly above beach bars on Urbiztondo strip - nightlife goes late on weekends in season. Ask if the resort has board storage and outdoor showers.

## 5. How to Get to San Juan La Union from Cebu

There is no direct flight from Cebu to La Union. San Fernando Airport is not for commercial flights. The usual route from Cebu is fly to Manila, then bus north.

**Step 1: Cebu to Manila - by air.** Fly from Mactan-Cebu International Airport (CEB) to Ninoy Aquino International Airport (MNL) or Clark (CRK). Cebu Pacific, Philippine Airlines, and AirAsia fly this route 15+ times daily. Flight time about 1 hour 30 minutes. One-way fares range PHP 1,200 to 3,500 depending on how early you book. Morning flights give you time to catch an evening bus.

**Step 2: Manila to San Juan La Union - by bus.** From Manila, Partas and Genesis are the main operators to La Union. Main terminals are Partas Pasay and Partas Cubao, and Genesis Joy Bus terminals in Pasay and Cubao.

Travel time is 6.5 to 8 hours depending on traffic and stops. Buses run every 2-4 hours through the day and night.

Fares as of 2025-2026 posted schedules: Pasay to San Juan La Union PHP 644 to PHP 783 for regular aircon, Cubao to San Juan around PHP 783. Premium Joy Bus / deluxe can cost PHP 900-1,200. Senior, student, and PWD discounts apply.

Route: NAIA to Pasay Partas terminal is about 20-40 minutes by Grab or taxi. Avoid rush hour if possible. Many travelers take a late afternoon flight from Cebu, stay overnight near Pasay, then take a 9pm to midnight bus to arrive in San Juan by 4-7am.

Alternative: Fly Cebu to Clark, then bus from Dau or Mabalacat to La Union - a bit shorter land trip (4.5-6 hours from Clark area) but fewer flight options.

**Best for:** Fastest same-day connection - early morning Cebu-Manila flight + Joy Bus direct to San Juan; budget option - regular aircon night bus from Pasay.

**What to do:** Book your Manila-La Union bus online via Partas website or Pamasahe.com if traveling on a weekend or holiday. Keep your surfboard in the bus luggage compartment - wrap it or ask the conductor for space.

## 6. Food and Nightlife in San Juan

San Juan has changed from a quiet surf village to a food and cafe strip. Most places are along the National Highway and beach access roads in Urbiztondo.

**Food:** Halo-Halo de Iloko in San Fernando is a local institution for halo-halo since the 1960s, about 15 minutes by tricycle. Along Urbiztondo: El Union for coffee and pastries (arrive early, lines are long in peak), Surf Shack for pizza and burgers, Urbiztondo Grill for grilled seafood and inihaw. Tagpuan, Kabsat, and Mad Monkeys are popular for all-day brunch and rice meals from PHP 200-400. For budget eats, local carinderias near San Juan Public Market serve bagnet, longganisa, and Ilocano pinakbet for under PHP 150.

**Nightlife:** Nightlife is centered on the Urbiztondo beach bars, not San Fernando city. Flotsam and Jetsam hosts acoustic sets and DJ nights from Thursday to Saturday. Other spots along the strip have live bands during season, especially December-February. Most bars close around 12am to 2am, stricter now than pre-pandemic.

**Costs:** Expect cafe meals PHP 250-450, cocktails PHP 180-300, local beer PHP 90-150. Tricycle around San Juan town proper PHP 30-50 per person, or PHP 150-250 for a special trip to San Fernando.

**Best for:** Sunset food trip - start with coffee at El Union, surf until 5:30pm sunset, dinner at Urbiztondo Grill; night out - Flotsam and nearby beach bars on weekends.

**What to do:** Bring cash - some smaller eateries and trikes don't accept GCash or cards reliably, though larger cafes do. Respect local guidelines: San Juan has a beach curfew enforcement and ordinances against loud music past certain hours. Check with your accommodation.

## Tips Before You Go

**1. Book lessons early morning.** The best learning window in San Juan is 6am-10am before the wind gets onshore. You get cleaner faces and fewer learners in the lineup. Afternoon is still surfable but choppier.

**2. Check conditions, not just forecasts.** Amihan can bring strong offshore winds in the morning that drop. Ask your instructor about tide. High tide in Urbiztondo can be shore-breaky; mid-tide rising is often ideal for beginners.

**3. Protect yourself from sun and surf.** Water temperature is warm (27-29C) so boardshorts and rash guard are enough. Wear reef booties only if going to Monaliza or rocky parts - most beginners don't need them on Urbiztondo sand. Use reef-safe sunscreen.

**4. Budget realistically.** A 3-day weekend from Cebu: roundtrip flights Cebu-Manila around PHP 2,500-6,000 if booked early, bus roundtrip PHP 1,300-1,600, accommodation PHP 3,000-10,000 for 3 nights, lessons PHP 800-1,600 per day, food PHP 600-1,200 per day. Total around PHP 8,500 solo on a shoestring to PHP 18,000+ mid-range, excluding shopping and nightlife.

**5. Respect the lineup.** San Juan gets crowded in peak. Don't drop in, don't litter, don't learn in the main peak where locals are. Your instructor will guide you to beginner zones.

**6. For Cebu-based travelers:** Consider combining La Union with Baguio - buses between San Juan and Baguio are 2-3 hours. Many travelers from Manila do La Union as a long weekend, then take the bus back via Baguio if they have extra days.

## So, Is La Union Worth It for Surf Season?

If you want to learn to surf without flying to Siargao or Bali, La Union makes sense. The season is long (October to March), lessons are cheap at PHP 400 an hour with board, the beach is set up for beginners, and the infrastructure - hostels, coffee shops, bus links - is built for weekend surfers. You trade Siargao's turquoise clarity for darker Ilocos sand, stronger crowds on weekends, and a road trip vibe rather than island vibe.

For Cebuanos, it's not the closest option - Siargao from Cebu is still easier logistically - but it offers a different kind of surf town experience and cooler weather from November to February. Try it in late January or early February when crowds ease, waves are still consistent, and early morning sessions feel glassy. Two mornings of lessons and you'll know if you want to keep going.
