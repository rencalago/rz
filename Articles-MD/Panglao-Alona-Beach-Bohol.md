# Panglao and Alona Beach, Bohol

Panglao Island is where Bohol keeps most of its beach infrastructure. It is connected to Tagbilaran by two bridges, small enough to cross in under an hour, and dense enough that almost everything you need for a beach trip is within walking distance once you reach Alona Beach in Barangay Tawala.

Alona itself is not a long, empty shoreline. It is a short, curved white-sand cove lined with dive shops, resorts, bars, and boat outriggers. The appeal is practical: you can step off your resort, book a boat to Balicasag in the morning, swim with sardines at Napaling in the afternoon, and be back for fire dancers on the sand by 7 PM. That concentration is why first-time visitors base themselves here even when they plan to see the rest of Bohol.

Here is how to get to Panglao from Cebu, what to do around Alona Beach, how much it actually costs right now, and how to avoid the common timing mistakes.

## 1. Getting There from Cebu: Ferry vs Flight

Most travelers from Cebu take the fast craft, not a plane.

**Ferry Cebu to Tagbilaran:** OceanJet, SuperCat and Lite Shipping run from Cebu Pier 1 or Pier 4 to Tagbilaran Port. The crossing takes about 2 hours. Economy tickets start around PHP 500 to PHP 800, business class PHP 1,000 to PHP 1,600, plus a PHP 25 to PHP 30 terminal fee at Cebu and PHP 25 at Tagbilaran. Boats leave hourly from early morning.

**Tagbilaran to Alona Beach:** From the port, walk across the road and take a tricycle to Dao Integrated Bus Terminal — PHP 40 to PHP 50 per person, 10 to 15 minutes. Then take a Panglao-bound Southern Star bus or jeepney to Alona — PHP 70 to PHP 100, 45 to 60 minutes, every 15 to 20 minutes until around 9 PM. If you have heavy luggage, negotiate a direct tricycle from port to Alona for PHP 250 to PHP 400, or a taxi/Grab for PHP 440 to PHP 550, 20 to 30 minutes.

**Flight option:** Cebu Pacific, Philippine Airlines and AirAsia fly from Mactan-Cebu International Airport (CEB) to Bohol-Panglao International Airport (TAG) in under an hour. The airport sits on Panglao Island itself. From arrivals, a Southern Star bus to Alona is PHP 50 to PHP 100 (40 to 50 minutes), a shared van PHP 100 per person (20 to 30 minutes), and a tricycle or Grab PHP 110 to PHP 150 per vehicle (15 to 20 minutes). Flying makes sense if you are connecting from an international flight or you want to skip the ferry entirely.

**Practical tip:** If you land or dock after 6 PM, pre-book a Grab or ask your hotel for a transfer. Buses still run but become less frequent, and tricycle touts inflate prices at night.

## 2. Alona Beach Itself — What to Expect

The sand is fine and noticeably white, but the usable beach width changes with tide. At high tide, water pushes close to resort fronts and loungers disappear. Early morning and late afternoon are the most comfortable for walking and swimming.

Swim inside the flagged areas. Boat lanes run close to shore, and outriggers come and go throughout the day. There is no general entrance fee to walk on Alona Beach. Resorts may charge non-guests for chairs or shade, usually if you are not ordering food or drinks.

Nightlife starts around 7 PM. Bars move tables onto the sand, live acoustic sets begin, and fire dancers perform along the shoreline. Compared to Boracay White Beach, Alona is smaller and less polished, but also less overwhelming and cheaper. You can hold a conversation near the performance — something that is hard to do on Boracay's peak nights.

**Best for:** Travelers who want a social, walkable base with dive shops, tour desks, and food options steps away.

**What to do:** Choose a resort slightly set back from the beach if you want quiet sleep — rooms 50 to 100 meters inland cost significantly less than beachfront and spare you the bass from beach bars.

## 3. Island Hopping: Balicasag and Virgin Island

This is the reason most people stay on Panglao.

Boats leave Alona between 6:00 and 6:30 AM. A standard joiner tour covers dolphin watching in the channel around 6:30 AM, snorkeling at Balicasag Reef around 8:00 AM, and the Virgin Island sandbar around 11:00 AM. Private boats can adjust the order based on tide.

**Balicasag Island:** About 40 minutes by bangka from Alona. The island has two main snorkel areas — a shallow coral garden and a drop-off wall. Turtles pass through regularly in the shallows. To protect the reef, you transfer to a small paddle boat with a local guide for PHP 250 to PHP 300 environmental fee. Balicasag has a daily cap of around 150 divers, so early boats get better spots. Visibility is best from November to May.

**Virgin Island (Pungtud):** A sandbar that is widest at low tide, with vendors selling sea urchin for PHP 50 to PHP 100 and grilled seafood for PHP 200 to PHP 500. There is a PHP 100 landing fee in some seasons. It gets crowded by late morning — earlier is better.

**Costs:** Joiner tours PHP 800 to PHP 1,500 per person including boat and snorkel gear, private boat PHP 3,000 to PHP 5,000 per group. Book the evening before rather than hoping for a dawn walk-up, especially during Holy Week and March to April.

**Best for:** First-time Bohol visitors, snorkellers, and divers who want turtles and reef without an overnight island stay.

## 4. Napaling Sardine Run — The Underrated Half-Day

A short tricycle ride from Alona, Napaling Reef hosts a massive, swirling school of sardines just meters from shore. You do not need scuba gear — free-diving with a mask is enough.

Tricycle roundtrip from Alona is around PHP 300. At the site, you pay stacked small fees: roughly PHP 100 entrance, PHP 100 environmental, PHP 100 guide — PHP 300 total. The sardines stay close even in shallow water, and the show is immediate.

**Best for:** Budget travelers and underwater photographers who want a Moalboal-style sardine run without leaving Bohol.

**What to do:** Bring your own mask if you have one. Rental masks often fog and leak.

## 5. Using Alona as a Base for Bohol Countryside

Alona works well for the classic mainland loop if you do it on a separate day from island hopping.

The efficient sequence is: Hinagdanan Cave near Dauis, then the Philippine Tarsier Sanctuary in Corella (PHP 80 to PHP 150, quiet guided walk required), the Bilar Man-Made Mahogany Forest, Loboc River Cruise with buffet lunch (PHP 850 to PHP 1,200), and the Chocolate Hills Complex in Carmen (PHP 150 for non-Bohol residents, 214 steps to the view deck).

A private van for 8 hours costs PHP 2,500 to PHP 3,500 per vehicle. Scooter rental is PHP 350 to PHP 500 per day if you are confident on provincial roads. Do not combine this loop with island hopping on the same day — both deserve a full day.

## Tips Before You Go

### Choose your beach wisely

If you want quiet sand, walk or ride 10 minutes to Dumaluan Beach or Libaong Beach. Same island, far fewer boats, wider shoreline at high tide. Stay in Alona for logistics, swim elsewhere for calm.

### Time your island hopping with tides

Check low tide for Virgin Island — the sandbar is largest then. For Balicasag, aim for early morning when seas are flat and fewer boats have stirred up sand.

### Bring cash in small bills

Many guesthouses, tricycles, and small tour desks are cash-only. PHP 1,000 bills and smaller are easier to break than larger notes.

### Book ahead in peak weeks

March, April, and Holy Week fill accommodations two to three weeks out, and beachfront prices spike. November to January gives you dry weather with fewer crowds and lower rates.

### Protect the reef

Wear a rash guard instead of heavy sunscreen, keep fins off coral, and do not touch turtles. Guides at Balicasag enforce distance — follow their lead and you help keep the daily cap sustainable.

## So, Is Panglao and Alona Beach Worth It?

For active travelers who want reliable boat access, decent nightlife, and a central place to launch both reef trips and countryside tours, Panglao and Alona Beach deliver. The beach strip itself is not the quietest in the Visayas, but it is arguably the most convenient base in Bohol.

Stay in Alona for the logistics, island hop to Balicasag early, fit in Napaling for the sardines, and use a second day for the Chocolate Hills and tarsiers. That two-day split gives you both the marine and mainland sides of Bohol without rushing through either.
