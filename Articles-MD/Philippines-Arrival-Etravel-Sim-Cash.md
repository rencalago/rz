# Arrival Basics: eTravel, SIM Cards, Cash vs Cards

First time in the Philippines, the arrival process has three steps that trip up travelers: eTravel registration before boarding, SIM card registration at the airport, and figuring out cash versus card before you leave Manila or Cebu for smaller islands.

Get these right and your first day is smooth. Miss them and you queue at immigration, wait for your SIM to activate, or hunt for an ATM that is offline. This guide covers what is mandatory right now, what costs what, and what to bring.

## 1. eTravel — Mandatory for All Inbound Travelers

The Philippines eTravel system is mandatory for all travelers entering and departing the Philippines, regardless of nationality or visa status. It replaced the paper arrival card and One Health Pass and is enforced at check-in and at immigration.

**What it is:** A free online registration at etravel.gov.ph. You create a record with flight details, passport, and address in the Philippines, then receive a QR code.

**When to do it:** Within 72 hours before departure. Airlines may deny boarding if you do not have it. Immigration officers also ask for it on arrival.

**Cost:** Free. The official site is etravel.gov.ph. Do not pay third-party sites that charge for registration — there is no legitimate fee.

**Who needs it:** Everyone — Filipino citizens, foreign tourists, visa holders, even children. There is no exemption.

**What you need:** Passport details, flight number, address of your first night (hotel name is enough), and contact details.

**Best for:** All arrivals. Print or screenshot the QR code, but also keep it on your phone — you will be asked to show it at boarding and at immigration.

**What to do:** Register early, double-check that your flight number matches your boarding pass, and keep your phone charged for the QR scan. If you transit through the Philippines and do not enter, you generally still need it for the entry leg.

**Location:** Done online before travel. No registration desk at the airport can replace pre-registration without delay.

## 2. SIM Cards and eSIMs — Registration Required, 30-Day Tourist Limit

The SIM Registration Act (Republic Act 11934) requires registration of all SIMs, including tourist and eSIMs bought in the Philippines. A tourist SIM bought at the airport will not activate until you register it.

**Where to buy:** Globe and Smart kiosks at NAIA Terminals 1, 2, 3, Mactan-Cebu Airport, Clark, and other international gateways. Globe tends to handle tourists most smoothly at NAIA Terminal 3. Avoid convenience stores that sell SIMs without registration help — you will have to register yourself online.

**What you need to register:** Passport, proof of address in the Philippines (hotel name and address is accepted), return ticket or onward ticket, and a live selfie. Registration portals: Globe at new.globe.com.ph/simreg, Smart at smart.com.ph/simreg or simreg.smart.com.ph. Staff at airport kiosks usually walk you through it — allow 15 to 30 minutes.

**Validity:** Tourist SIMs registered under a foreign passport are valid for only 30 days and are automatically deactivated after expiry. If you stay longer than 30 days or have a visa extension, you need to visit a Globe or Smart store to request an extension with proof.

**Coverage and choice:**
- **Globe:** Widest nationwide coverage, best default for most tourists, especially outside big cities.
- **Smart:** Stronger 5G speed in major cities, good second option.
- **DITO:** Fastest where it has coverage and cheapest data, but limited reach in rural areas — better as a backup SIM.

**eSIM option:** Globe and Smart both offer eSIMs for tourists. You can get a QR code and activate before landing, which saves time. The same registration rules and 30-day limit apply to eSIMs bought locally. A foreign travel eSIM that roams does not need Philippine registration, but costs more for data.

**Costs:** Tourist SIM with initial data pack PHP 300 to PHP 700 depending on data. Top-up promos like Globe Go or Smart Giga are PHP 100 to PHP 500 for 7 to 30 days. Airport prices are slightly higher than mall prices but include registration help.

**Best for:** All tourists who want local data and Grab. Buy at the airport if you need data immediately for transport.

**What to do:** Bring an unlocked phone. Buy from a carrier kiosk, not a random reseller. Register on the spot and test data before leaving the airport. Keep your home SIM if your phone supports dual SIM — you can keep your home number active while using Philippine data.

## 3. Cash vs Cards — Cash-Heavy Outside Manila and Cebu

The Philippines is still cash-heavy for tourists. Cards work in Manila malls, larger hotels, and chain restaurants, but islands and small towns are often cash-only or add a surcharge for cards.

**Currency:** Philippine peso (PHP). Break large bills at airports, department stores, or hotels. Carry small bills — PHP 20, 50, 100 — for tricycles, market stalls, and sari-sari stores.

**Cards:** Visa and Mastercard are widely accepted in urban areas, hotels, and larger restaurants. American Express less so. Always choose to pay in PHP, not your home currency, to avoid dynamic currency conversion fees. Some establishments add 3 to 5 percent for card payments.

**Cash needed for:** Tricycles, jeepneys, local markets, small guesthouses, island boat transfers, environmental fees, entrance fees, and most tour desks outside Manila and Cebu. Even in Boracay and Siargao, many tour boats are cash-only.

**ATMs:**
- **Where:** BDO has the largest network, followed by BPI, Metrobank, and BPN. Airports have several machines, but queues can be long.
- **Fees:** Most ATMs charge PHP 250 per foreign card withdrawal, standard across banks. Some HSBC and Citibank ATMs allow higher limits — up to PHP 20,000 to PHP 40,000 per withdrawal — versus the usual PHP 10,000 cap at many machines.
- **Tips:** Tell your bank you are traveling to the Philippines before departure. Avoid Sundays when some ATMs run out of cash and are not restocked until Monday. Withdraw the maximum you need at once to reduce per-transaction fees. ATMs on small islands are scarce and often offline — withdraw cash in Manila, Cebu, or Iloilo before heading to islands like Malapascua, Siargao, or Apo Island.

**Money changers:** Rates are usually better in Manila than on islands. Change some USD at the airport for immediate needs, but larger amounts get better rates at money changers in Ermita, Makati, or Cebu City. Keep receipts — some changers require them for re-exchange.

**Best for:** Budgeting for both. Bring a debit card for ATM withdrawals and a credit card for hotels, plus some USD cash as backup.

**What to do:** Withdraw enough cash for 2 to 3 days before going to an island. Keep cash in two places — day money in a wallet, reserve in a hotel safe or money belt. Break PHP 1,000 bills whenever you can — small vendors often cannot break them.

## 4. Quick Arrival Checklist Before You Fly

- **72 hours before:** Register eTravel at etravel.gov.ph and save the QR code.
- **Phone:** Unlock your phone, bring a SIM pin, and decide Globe vs Smart. If your phone supports eSIM, consider getting a local eSIM QR code in advance.
- **Cash:** Bring some USD and at least one Visa/Mastercard debit card. Tell your bank you are traveling. Plan to withdraw PHP in Manila or Cebu before going to islands.
- **Documents:** Passport with at least 6 months validity, return ticket (required for SIM registration and sometimes for immigration), hotel address for eTravel and SIM forms.
- **Apps:** Install Grab, GCash (useful but needs local SIM verification), and your airline app. Offline maps help when data is spotty.

## Tips Before You Arrive

### Do eTravel early, not at the airport

Airlines check eTravel at check-in abroad. Doing it at the airport with weak Wi-Fi adds stress. The site is free — if a site asks for payment, you are on a scam clone.

### Buy your SIM at a carrier kiosk

The kiosk staff register you on the spot and test activation. Convenience store SIMs are cheaper but you have to register yourself, and airport Wi-Fi can be slow.

### Assume cash-first outside big cities

Even in tourist hubs like El Nido, Panglao, and Siargao, many boat operators and guesthouses are cash-only. Have PHP ready before you board a boat.

### Keep small bills

Tricycle drivers and market vendors rarely have change for PHP 1,000. Break bills at 7-Eleven or department stores.

## So, What Should You Prepare First?

Do eTravel first — it is mandatory and free. Then plan your SIM — Globe for widest coverage, Smart for city speed, and budget 30 minutes at the airport for registration. Then sort cash — withdraw enough PHP in Manila or Cebu to cover islands, expect PHP 250 ATM fee per withdrawal, and carry small bills for daily expenses.

With those three done, your arrival is handled and you can focus on the actual trip — whether that is Apo Island turtles, Siargao surf, or an Iloilo food crawl.
