# Lake Sebu: T'boli Culture, Seven Falls and the Zipline Over the Falls

Lake Sebu is a highland town in South Cotabato, Mindanao, built around three crater lakes and home to the T'boli people. The main lake — Lake Sebu itself — is 354 hectares of calm water that turns pink at dawn when thousands of lotus flowers open. At midday the draw shifts to the Seven Falls in Barangay Lake Lahit and the zipline that flies over them, billed as one of the highest in Southeast Asia at around 180 meters above ground.

The town sits at about 700 meters above sea level, cool compared to General Santos City down the hill. Mornings start with mist over the water and the low sound of T'boli brass gongs. Afternoons are for waterfalls, t'nalak weaving houses, and tilapia straight from the lake to the grill.

For travelers coming from Cebu, Lake Sebu is not a day trip, but it is a direct flight to General Santos plus a 2 to 3-hour land transfer. Here is how the pieces fit — culture, lakes, falls, costs now, and when to plan it.

## 1. The Lakes — Lotus, Owong Canoes and Tilapia

Lake Sebu municipality has three lakes: Lake Sebu (the biggest, where the town center sits), Lake Seloton (the deepest), and Lake Lahit. Most visitors stay near Lake Sebu.

**What to do:** Book a dawn boat ride, 5:30 AM to 7:30 AM, when lotus (Nelumbo nucifera) are fully open. The local dugout canoe is called owong, paddled standing up by a T'boli boatman. Many resorts offer costumes for photos — you can wear a T'boli embroidered top while floating through the lotus garden. The best light is before 8 AM; after that the flowers start to close and tourist boats increase.

**Costs right now:** Motorized boat with lotus tour PHP 300 to PHP 500 per boat for 30 to 45 minutes, good for 2 to 3 persons. Paddled owong PHP 200 to PHP 350. Habal-habal around town PHP 50 to PHP 100 per short ride.

**Tilapia:** Lake Sebu produces most of South Cotabato's tilapia. Grilled tilapia at Punta Isla Lake Resort and lakeside eateries costs PHP 180 to PHP 280 per order, often with rice and ensalada.

**Best for:** Photographers and slow mornings. Come for the lotus, not just the falls.

## 2. T'boli Culture — Dreamweavers and T'nalak

The T'boli are the main Indigenous group here, along with Ubo, Manobo and Tiruray communities. Two things to understand before you go.

**T'nalak weaving:** T'nalak is cloth woven from abaca fiber, dyed with natural pigments and patterned with designs that weavers say come from dreams through Fu Dalu, the T'boli god of abaca. National Living Treasure Lang Dulay (1928-2015) from Lake Sebu was recognized in 1998 for keeping over a hundred dream patterns alive, weaving without being able to read or write. Her former weaving center in Sitio Tukolefa, Lamdalag still teaches. Today three other GAMABA awardees are tied to Lake Sebu — Rosie Sula (T'boli chanting), Barbara Ofong and Bundos Fara (t'nalak).

**Where to see it:** Lang Dulay Weaving Center / Manlilikha ng Bayan Center (free, donations appreciated, t'nalak panels from PHP 2,000 to PHP 15,000 depending on size and complexity), Lake Sebu T'boli Museum (PHP 20 entrance), and small family houses around Lake Seloton where you can watch abaca stripping and dyeing. Ask permission before taking close-ups of weavers.

**Music and dress:** Instruments include hegalong (two-string lute) and kulintang brass gongs. You will be offered to rent T'boli attire at Seven Falls for PHP 50 to PHP 100 for photos — if you bring the costume on the lotus boat, ask the weavers first.

**Best for:** Cultural travelers who want context beyond a photo.

## 3. Seven Falls — From Hikong Alo to Hikong Tonok

Seven Falls are fed by Lake Sebu and Lake Seloton and drop in a chain through Barangay Lake Seloton. Only Falls 1 and 2 are fully developed for visitors; reaching Falls 3 to 7 requires a guide and a half-day trek.

- Falls 1: Hikong Alo (45 feet) — the postcard fall with viewing deck and costume rental area.
- Falls 2: Hikong Bente (70 feet) — just below Falls 1, reached by 100+ concrete steps down.
- Falls 3: Hikong B’Lebel, Falls 4 Hikong lowig, Falls 5 Hikong K’Fo-I, Falls 6 Hikong Luba, Falls 7 Hikong Tonok (highest).

**What to do:** If you only have an hour, do Falls 1 and 2. Wear shoes with grip; the steps get slippery. For the full Seven Falls trek, hire a local guide at the tourism desk for PHP 500 to PHP 700 per day and start by 8 AM. Guide is optional for Falls 1-2 but required for Falls 3-7.

**Best for:** First half for everyone, second half for hikers.

## 4. The Zipline — Highest in Southeast Asia Claim

This is why many Cebuanos come. The Lake Sebu Seven Falls Zipline crosses the gorge above Falls 1 and 2.

Two lines operate in tandem: The first line is about 700 meters long at roughly 180 meters (600 feet) high, the second return line is shorter, around 400 meters. The thrill is the open view — rainbows appear in the falls spray on sunny mornings between 9 AM and 11 AM.

**Current fees (2025-2026 season):**
- Seven Falls Eco Park entrance / environmental fee: PHP 20 to PHP 30 per person (PHP 10 for students, PHP 5 for kids).
- Zipline: PHP 250 per person weekdays, PHP 300 per person weekends and holidays. Some operators quote PHP 350 when both lines plus photo package are bundled, but base ride remains PHP 250-300.
- Guide tip for falls descent if doing after zipline: PHP 50 to PHP 100.
- Photo prints or SD card from zipline staff: PHP 100 to PHP 200 extra.

Hours are usually 8 AM to 5 PM, with last zipline at 4:30 PM. Close on heavy rain.

**Safety note:** Helmet and harness are provided. Weight limits are commonly 35 kg to 120 kg. If you have fear of heights, you can still view Falls 1 and 2 from decks without riding.

**Best for:** Adventure and views in one ride. Do Falls 1 morning, zipline around 10 AM when light is good.

## 5. How to Get to Lake Sebu from Cebu via General Santos or Cotabato

There is no direct land or boat route. Fly.

**Option A — Cebu to General Santos City (recommended):**
- Flights: Cebu Pacific Air 5J251, 5J249 and Philippine Airlines PR1357 fly nonstop Mactan-Cebu (CEB) to General Santos (GES). Flight time 1 hour 15 minutes to 1 hour 30 minutes. One-way fare PHP 1,500 promo to PHP 3,500 to PHP 5,000 regular. Cebu Pacific flies twice daily most days, PAL one daily.
- GES Airport to GenSan City Bulaong Terminal: Tricycle or multicab PHP 100 to PHP 200, 15 to 20 mins.
- GenSan to Lake Sebu town: AC van from Bulaong or KCC Terminal direct to Lake Sebu, PHP 150 to PHP 220, 1.5 to 2 hours. Last vans around 4 PM to 5 PM. Alternative: GenSan to Surallah (PHP 120 to PHP 180, 1 to 1.5 hours), then Surallah to Lake Sebu van or habal-habal PHP 50 to PHP 80, 30 to 45 mins.
- Total Cebu airport to Lake Sebu: 5 to 7 hours including waiting time.

**Option B — Cebu to Cotabato City (Awang) then to Lake Sebu:**
- Cebu to Cotabato via Cebu Pacific (limited) or via Davao. Less frequent than GenSan but usable if you combine with Maguindanao tour. Cotabato to Isulan to Surallah to Lake Sebu is 4 to 5 hours by van, PHP 350 to PHP 500 total.
- For most Cebu travelers, GenSan is faster and has more van options.

**From Davao if you detour:** Davao Ecoland Terminal to Marbel (Koronadal) PHP 300 to PHP 450, 3 to 4 hours, then Marbel to Lake Sebu.

**Getting around Lake Sebu:** Rent a habal-habal for PHP 500 to PHP 800 per day for falls, lake resorts and weaving centers. Tricycle within poblacion PHP 20 to PHP 40.

- Mactan to GenSan flight one-way: PHP 1,500 – PHP 5,000.
- GenSan City to Lake Sebu van: PHP 150 – PHP 220.
- Motorcycle day rental: PHP 500 – PHP 800.
- Accommodation: Homestays PHP 800 – PHP 1,500, mid-range lake resorts like Punta Isla Lake Resort, Mountain Lake Eco Resort, Merl Garden Lake Resort PHP 2,000 – PHP 4,500 per night, often with lake view.
- Seven Falls entrance: PHP 20 – PHP 30.
- Zipline weekdays PHP 250, weekends PHP 300.
- Lake boat/lotus tour PHP 200 – PHP 500 per boat.
- T'boli Museum PHP 20, Dreamweavers Hill / lang Dulay Center donation PHP 50 – PHP 100.
- Meals: Tilapia meals PHP 180 – PHP 280, rice coffee PHP 40 – PHP 60.
- Full-day guide for Seven Falls trek PHP 500 – PHP 700.
- Bring cash — ATMs are scarce in Lake Sebu poblacion and many resorts are cash-only.

## 7. Best Months to Visit Lake Sebu

Lake Sebu is cool year-round, 20°C to 27°C, cooler at night. It has no typhoon-belt extremes but rain affects falls clarity and zipline operations.

**Best overall: November to May (dry season).** February to April has clearest skies and strongest lotus bloom. Morning fog over the lake is most photogenic then. December to February is coolest, good for hiking.

**Peak local travel:** February, July and December get more Philippine tourists because of school breaks and festivals. Helobung Festival celebrating T'boli culture is usually second week of November with horse fight, brass playing and T'nalak parade.

**Wettest: June to October.** May is often reported as wettest month locally. Trails to Falls 3-7 get muddy and zipline may pause on rain. Lake is still visitable but expect afternoon showers and reduced visibility.

For Cebuano weekenders, target late January to early May for best mix of lotus flowers, dry steps at Seven Falls and reliable zipline operations. Wake before sunrise for lotus, keep mid-morning for zipline when spray makes rainbows.

## Tips Before You Go

### Go at dawn for lotus, mid-morning for zipline

Lotus open 5:30 to 9 AM. Zipline is better 9 to 11 AM when sun lights the falls. Split them across two mornings if you can stay one night.

### Bring layers and grip

Lake Sebu is 700 MASL — evenings drop to 20°C. Bring light jacket. Falls steps are wet and mossy; use trail shoes, not flip-flops.

### Respect T'boli protocols

Ask before photographing weavers or entering weaving houses. Abstain from touching unfinished t'nalak on the loom. In the museum and GAMABA houses, do not wear shoes on raised floors if asked.

### Stay overnight

The last vans to GenSan leave mid-afternoon. One night lets you do lotus sunrise, falls and zipline without rushing. Book lakeside resorts early in February to April and November festival.

### Download offline maps

Signal is spotty beyond poblacion. Download Google offline for South Cotabato or take a local driver who knows the Surallah and Lamdalag roads.

## So, Is Lake Sebu Worth the Trip from Cebu?

If you want beaches, Negros and Bohol are closer. If you want highland lakes, living textile tradition and a zipline that actually flies over waterfalls instead of trees, Lake Sebu earns its plane ride.

Fly Cebu to General Santos in the morning, catch a van by noon, be on the lake by sunset with grilled tilapia. Next day, do lotus at 5:30 AM, T'boli weaving houses at 8 AM, Falls 1 and 2 by 9:30 AM and the 700-meter zipline before lunch. Budget PHP 4,000 to PHP 6,000 per person for 2 days / 1 night excluding flights if sharing boat and room, PHP 8,000 to PHP 12,000 with mid-range resort.

For a Cebu-based itinerary, pair it with GenSan tuna market or a night in Koronadal. The altitude, the lotus and the dreamweavers make it feel different from anything in the Visayas.
