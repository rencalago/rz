# Anilao Diving, Batangas

Anilao is a cluster of fishing villages along the coast of Mabini, Batangas, about 120 kilometers south of Manila. It is not a white-sand island with beach bars. It is a hillside coastline facing Balayan Bay and Batangas Bay, lined with dive resorts, small ports, and banca boats. Since the 1960s it has been called the birthplace of Philippine diving, and it still shapes how people learn to dive in Luzon.

What keeps divers coming back is not big pelagics, but small life. Anilao is considered one of the best muck and macro destinations in the world. On a sandy slope that looks empty at first, you can spend an hour watching a flamboyant cuttlefish change color, a mimic octmodel, nudibranchs the size of a grain of rice, frogfish hiding in sponges, seahorses holding on to a sea fan. It is slow, quiet diving, best with a good guide and a magnifying glass.

Here is a practical guide to Anilao diving — when to go, how to get there from Manila and from Cebu via Manila, what muck diving really means, where to stay, what it costs now, and what to pack.

## 1. Why Anilao Is Famous for Muck and Macro Diving

Muck diving means diving over sand, silt, rubble or seagrass in shallow water, looking for cryptic critters rather than big reef scenes. Anilao has more than 40 named macro sites within 15 minutes of most resorts, with depths from 3 meters to 25 meters. That makes it ideal for photographers and for training eyes.

Typical sightings include: flamboyant cuttlefish, wonderpus and mimic octmodel, blue-ringed octmodel, hairy frogfish and painted frogfish, pygmy seahorses on gorgonians, bobtail squid at night, nudibranchs — especially chromodorids and flabellinas — seahorses, pipefish, skeleton shrimp, and for night divers, bobbit worms, stargazers, Ambon scorpionfish, and decorator crabs. On night dives you will also see hunting morays and hunting lionfish under your torch.

For something different, many Anilao operators run blackwater dives. You float in the dark over deep water — Balayan Bay drops to hundreds of meters — tethered to a lit downline. Plankton rises and pelagic larvae appear: larval octmodel, paper nautiluses, juvenile frogfish riding jellyfish, and the occasional blanket octmodel. Blackwater is best around new moon, November to May, and particularly January to March when water is slightly cooler and critter density is higher.

**Best for:** Underwater photographers, macro lovers, advanced beginners who want to improve buoyancy and spotting skills. Open Water certification is enough for most sites, which sit at 8 to 18 meters.

**What to do:** Ask for a dedicated spotter guide if you are into macro. A 1:2 or private guide ratio makes a huge difference. Tell your guide what you hope to see — for example, rhinopias or seahorse — so they can choose sites like Secret Bay, Basura, or Twin Rocks accordingly.

**Location:** Mabini Peninsula, Batangas. Most dive sites are between Anilao Pier, Ligpo Island, and Sombrero Island. Boat rides are 5 to 15 minutes.

## 2. Dive Resorts — Where to Stay in Anilao

Anilao is not a backpacker strip where you walk in and pick a boat. Most resorts are dive resorts with their own boats, compressors, rental gear, and resident divemasters. Rates are usually per person per night with full board, because restaurants outside resorts are limited.

**Categories and typical rates:**

- **Budget guesthouse / small resort:** PHP 1,800 to PHP 3,200 per night for a room with fan or basic aircon, without diving. Examples: Blue Ribbon Dive Resort, Anilao Beach Club. Diving is charged à la carte at PHP 1,500 to PHP 1,800 per boat dive.
- **Mid-range dive resort:** PHP 3,500 to PHP 6,500 per person per night full board + 2 to 3 boat dives. Examples: Buceo Anilao Beach and Dive Resort, Vivere Azure (boutique), Aiyanar Beach and Dive Resort, Crystal Blue Resort. This is the most common package for weekend divers from Manila.
- **High-end photo-focused resort:** PHP 8,500 to PHP 14,000 per person per night full board with guided diving, camera room, rental lights, private guide option. Examples: Solitude Acacia Resort, Crystal Blue Resort, Anilao Photo Hotel. These cater to underwater photographers with rinse tanks, air-gun stations, and critique rooms.
- **Day trip option from Manila:** Some Manila-based dive centers run day trips Saturday or Sunday — van transfer, 2 to 3 dives, lunch, guide. Price is PHP 6,000 to PHP 9,000 per person all in.

Gear rental if not included: BCD PHP 300–400 per day, regulator PHP 300–400, 3mm wetsuit PHP 300, fins and mask PHP 200–300, torch PHP 300 for night dive. Dive computer rental PHP 500–700 if you do not own one.

**Best for:** Stay at least 2 nights / 2 dive days if you come from Manila. One dive day gives you only 3 dives and little time to learn the macro search. Three nights is ideal for a photo workshop or if you plan a blackwater dive.

**What to do:** Book directly with the resort rather than through a generic booking app if you want diving included. Confirm if boat fee, tank, weights, guide, and dive permit are included. For photographers, ask about camera room access and number of divers per boat.

## 3. How Much Does Anilao Diving Cost — Fees, Tanks, Boats

Anilao dives are relatively cheap compared to boat diving elsewhere because sites are close.

**Typical per-dive costing that resorts build into packages:**
- Tank + air fill: PHP 150 to PHP 250
- Divemaster/guide fee: PHP 500 to PHP 700 per dive per small group, or shared among 4 to 5 divers
- Boat / banca fee: PHP 1,500 for nearby sites (Secret Bay, Twin Rocks, Cathedral) for up to 4 to 5 divers sharing, PHP 3,000 to PHP 3,500 for farther sites like Sombrero, Kirby's Rock, Beatrice Rock
- Average all-in fun dive price if you pay à la carte: PHP 1,200 to PHP 1,800 per dive with your own gear, slightly more for night and blackwater

**Environmental and conservation fees:**
- Mabini/Anilao Marine Protected Area diving fee: PHP 200 per diver per day is commonly collected now, up from PHP 100 a few years ago. Some resorts include it, some collect on the boat.
- Tingloy local environmental fee if you cross to La Estrella or Masasa side: PHP 100 to PHP 155 per person at the port counter
- Blackwater surcharge: PHP 800 to PHP 1,500 extra per night because of lights, longer boat time, deeper water
- Freediving or snorkeling Giant Clam Sanctuary: PHP 100 conservation fee + boat PHP 500 per boat

Most dive resorts require full payment in cash or bank transfer for diving. GCash is accepted at larger resorts, but bring cash — there is no reliable ATM past Batangas City Grand Terminal. Withdraw before the jeepney.

## 4. How to Get to Anilao from Manila

Anilao is about 2.5 to 4 hours from Manila depending on traffic, route, and whether you have a private car.

**By public bus + jeepney (cheapest):**
1. From Buendia / LRT Buendia terminal in Pasay, or Cubao terminal, or PITX in Parañaque, take a JAM Liner, JAC Liner, ALPS, or Ceres Transportation bus bound for Batangas City Grand Terminal / Batangas Pier. Fare PHP 180 to PHP 300, aircon. Travel time 2 to 3 hours. Buses leave every 20 to 30 minutes from early morning.
2. At Batangas Grand Terminal, ride the jeepney marked “Mabini / Anilao” to Mabini Multipurpose Port, also called Anilao Port. Fare PHP 40 to PHP 70, about 40 minutes.
3. From Anilao Port, hire a tricycle to your resort — PHP 60 to PHP 150 depending on how far along the Mabini coast your resort is. Tell the driver the resort name, for example Planet Dive, Buceo, or Crystal Blue.

Total public transport cost one-way: about PHP 280 to PHP 450 per person.

**By private car / Grab from Manila:**
Take SLEX to STAR Tollway to Batangas City exit, then to Mabini. Toll PHP 300 to PHP 430 one-way (SLEX + STAR), fuel about PHP 400 to PHP 700. Parking is inside resorts. If you book a van transfer through a dive shop, it is PHP 2,500 to PHP 5,000 per van (4 to 6 pax) one-way from Manila, often shared among divers.

**By resort transfer:**
Many mid-range and high-end resorts offer Manila-Anilao-Manila transfers on weekends if you book a package. Ask at least 3 days ahead. It is the least stressful option for first-timers.

**Tip:** Leave Manila before 6:00 AM on weekends to avoid Batangas traffic. On the way back, Sunday afternoon traffic northbound on SLEX can be heavy — plan a late lunch in Batangas City or Lipa to let it clear.

## 5. How to Get to Anilao from Cebu via Manila

There is no direct Cebu-Anilao ferry or flight. You go Cebu to Manila, then Manila to Anilao.

**Step 1: Cebu to Manila:**
- **Flight:** Fastest. Cebu Mactan (CEB) to NAIA or Clark. 1 hour 20 minutes airtime, plus airport time. Fares PHP 1,200 to PHP 3,500 one-way if booked early. Cebu Pacific and Philippine Airlines have many daily flights.
- **Ferry:** 2GO Travel overnight ferry Cebu to Manila Port, about 22 to 24 hours, PHP 900 to PHP 2,800 depending on class. Only if you like long sea journeys.

**Step 2: Manila to Anilao** as above — bus to Batangas Grand Terminal then jeepney. If you arrive at NAIA evening, it is better to stay one night in Manila near Pasay / Buendia terminal and go to Anilao the next morning, rather than trying to catch a night bus after a flight.

**If you compare:** A typical Cebu-based diver flying out early morning can be in Anilao by lunch or early afternoon if they pre-arrange a van pickup at the bus terminal or airport. Many Cebu dive clubs do weekend Manila-Anilao runs once or twice a year as a group.

**Sample budget from Cebu, 3 days 2 nights:**
- Flight return Cebu-Manila: PHP 2,500 to PHP 5,000
- Bus jeep tricycle return Manila-Anilao: PHP 600 to PHP 900
- Accommodation full board 2 nights + 6 to 7 dives + fees: PHP 9,000 to PHP 16,000
- Total without gear rental: PHP 12,000 to PHP 22,000

## 6. Best Months to Dive Anilao and Water Conditions

Anilao is diveable year-round, but conditions change.

- **November to May — dry season, best overall:** Calmer seas, visibility 15 to 25 meters, sometimes 30 meters at offshore pinnacles like Kirby's or Beatrice. December to April has the most stable topside weather. This is peak season — resorts are full on weekends, book at least 2 weeks ahead.
- **December to March — best for macro and blackwater:** Water slightly cooler, 26 to 28°C, which brings out more critters. New moon nights are especially good for blackwater. Photographers prefer these months.
- **April to May — warmest water, most crowded:** Water 29 to 30°C, flat seas, but more day-trippers from Manila on long weekends. Visibility still good, but plankton can increase slightly offshore.
- **June to October — rainy / typhoon season, quiet season:** Fewer divers, lower resort rates, but seas can be rough and some boats cancel. If a typhoon signal is up in Batangas, trips are suspended. If you go, weekdays are better than weekends, and have a buffer day. Some resorts close for maintenance in August or September.

Surface interval: Expect sun and humidity. Inland Mabini can be hot. Bring quick-dry cover-up for boat rides.

## 7. What to Bring and Practical Details

Anilao diving is shore and small-boat based, not liveaboard. Boats are small bancas — you enter from the beach, sit on the side, backroll.

**Essentials:**
- Certification card and logbook — most shops ask
- Dive computer if you own one; otherwise rent
- 1.5mm to 3mm wetsuit — 3mm is comfortable for night and blackwater, especially November to March. Some locals dive rashguard only in May, but you will get cold on a 70-minute macro dive
- Dive torch and focus light for night dives and macro photography — even for day dives a small torch helps look under rocks
- Reef-safe sunscreen, hat, dry bag, cash in small bills (PHP 100s and 50s), reusable water bottle
- Motion sickness meds if you are sensitive — boats are small and can rock while you wait for other divers
- Underwater camera / macro lens if you have it — add a magnifying glass attachment for spotting

**What not to bring:**
- Big hard suitcases — tricycles and bancas have limited space. Use a backpack and a small mesh bag.
- Single-use plastics — most dive resorts have refill stations.
- Flash photography without asking — some critters, especially seahorses, are sensitive. Follow guide advice and do not use strong focusing lights directly on eyes for long.

**Safety reminders:** Dive sites are inside marine protected areas. Do not touch, collect, or move critters for a photo. Do not kneel on coral or stir silt when another diver is photographing nearby. Keep a slow pace — macro diving is about buoyancy just above the sand, not finning hard.

## Tips Before You Go to Anilao Diving

### Book your resort and dives before the weekend
Anilao runs full on Friday to Sunday from November to May. Walk-ins often miss a boat or get a room far from the dive shop. Message the dive center with your certification level, last dive date, and whether you need rental gear.

### Ask for a macro guide, not a general tour guide
Anilao guides specialize. Some are excellent at finding pygmy seahorses or frogfish. If you are there for macro, say so explicitly and ask for a private or 2:1 guide if budget allows — typically PHP 800 to PHP 1,200 extra per day but worth it for photographers.

### Log night and blackwater after checking conditions
Blackwater needs calm seas, no strong moon, and a good operator with proper lights and safety line. Confirm the night before. If you have not dived in 6 months, do a day check dive first. For blackwater you should have Advanced Open Water and good buoyancy because there is no bottom reference.

### Bring cash and withdraw early
There is no reliable ATM in Anilao proper. Bring enough cash for environmental fees, tricycles, tips for boat crew (PHP 100 to PHP 200 per day is customary), and emergency. Withdraw at Batangas Grand Terminal or in Batangas City before the jeepney.

### Do not rush Secret Bay and Basura
Two of the most famous muck sites — Secret Bay (also called Secret Bay) and Basura (which literally means trash) — look disappointing at first: sand, bottles, tires. The magic is in the patience. Stay still, look for movement, and let the guide point with a pointer, not your hands.

## So, Is Anilao Worth It for a Cebu-Based Diver?

If you are based in Cebu and used to reef walls, turtles, and sardine runs, Anilao feels different. It is slower, more focused, and less about big scenery and more about tiny behavior — how a hairy frogfish walks, how a mimic octmodel changes texture, how a nudibranch eats hydroids as small as eyelashes.

For macro training, it is hard to beat. You can get more quality critter encounters in one weekend in Anilao than in many months of reef diving elsewhere, because sites are close, guides know exactly where a resident seahorse lives, and boats do not waste time traveling.

If you only have a weekend coming from Cebu, fly to Manila early, add one transit buffer night, and give Anilao two full dive days. If you cannot add that buffer, stick to Moalboal, Dauin, or Malapascua for macro closer to home. But if you are building a Philippines macro circuit — Anilao, Dauin/Apo Island, Malapascua, Romblon — Anilao is the logical first stop after Manila.
