# Bucas Grande and Sohoton Cove, Surigao del Norte

Bucas Grande is the big island next to Siargao that most people skip — until they see photos of Sohoton Cove. It is part of the municipality of Socorro in Surigao del Norte, a maze of limestone karsts, hidden lagoons, sea caves, and one famous lagoon filled with thousands of stingless jellyfish.

Inside the island sits Sohoton Cove National Park, a protected seascape covering 70+ islets, caves, and channels. The main draw is the Tojoman Lagoon or Jellyfish Sanctuary, where you can swim among brown spotted jellyfish that do not sting, plus Hagukan Cave with water that glows blue, and Magkukuob Cave where you exit with a cliff jump into the lagoon.

From Cebu, you can reach Bucas Grande two ways: fly or ferry to Surigao City and take a boat via Hayanggabon or Socorro Port, or fly to Siargao and do a full-day boat tour from General Luna or Dapa. Here is what it costs now, when to go, and what to expect inside the cove.

<br>
## 1. Bucas Grande Island and Sohoton Cove National Park — What It Is

**Location:** Socorro, Bucas Grande Island, Surigao del Norte, Caraga Region, Mindanao. Just south of Siargao Island, about 2 hours by boat from General Luna.

**Why it is protected:** Bucas Grande is a National Park managed by DENR. You cannot enter Sohoton Cove without registering at the Sohoton Cove Tourist Registration Center, attending the short orientation, wearing a life vest, and going with an accredited guide in a small paddle boat. No large motorboats inside the narrow channels.

**What you see on a standard tour:** The tour usually covers 4 to 5 stops, depending on tide:

- **Sohoton Cove Lagoon entrance** — limestone cliffs, mangroves, narrow channel only passable at low enough tide.
- **Hagukan Cave** — partly submerged cave, best at midday when sun enters the water and the surface glows turquoise. Swim inside required, water shoes recommended.
- **Magkukuob Cave** — low ceiling, then a small cliff jump platform to exit. Helmets provided.
- **Crystal Cave and Bolitas Cave** — stalactites, limited lighting.
- **Tojoman Lagoon / Jellyfish Sanctuary** — the stingless jellyfish lake. You transfer to a small paddle boat rowed by a local paddler.

**Best for:** Travelers who want caves, lagoons, and snorkeling without the surf crowds of Siargao, and photographers.

**What to do:** Book a guided eco-tour, not DIY island hopping. Listen to the tide briefing — Hagukan is inaccessible at high tide. Bring a waterproof phone pouch.

<br>
## 2. Stingless Jellyfish in Sohoton — When to See Them

The jellyfish are golden-brown spotted jellyfish (Mastigias). They have lost their sting over generations in this enclosed lagoon. You can swim with them, touch gently, but do not lift them out of the water.

**Season:** Numbers are highest during dry and warm months. Most guides say March to June is peak, with April to June giving the densest sightings. Local reports also mention good sightings July to August in some years, but March to May is most consistent. In the rainy months (September to February), you still see clear Moon Jellyfish — their sting is very mild, but numbers of the brown spotted ones drop.

**Rules inside Tojoman:**

- No sunscreen or lotion before swimming — it kills the jellies. Apply at least an hour before or cover with rash guard.
- No standing on corals, no fins kicking up silt.
- Paddler fee is separate: around PHP 100 per person for the small boat.
- Do not use flash photography inside caves.

**Best for:** Families and calm-water swimmers — lagoon is very sheltered.

**What to do:** Wear a rash guard and aqua shoes. Go mid-morning when light penetrates. If you visit off-peak, ask at registration if jellyfish are present that day — they move.

<br>
## 3. Hagukan Cave and the Other Caves

Hagukan Cave is the most famous cave in Sohoton. You swim through a narrow entrance about a meter high (helmet and headlamp provided), then the water inside glows luminous blue when sunlight hits. The glow is strongest around 10 AM to 2 PM on sunny days.

Magkukuob Cave is connected — you wade inside, see formations, then climb a small limestone step and jump about 8 to 10 feet back into the lagoon. You can opt to wade back instead of jumping. Crystal Cave has more stalactites but is drier.

**Fees inside the park (current rates as of 2025, confirm on site):**

- **Entrance fee:** PHP 100 per person
- **Sohoton Cove Eco-Tourism Tour Fee:** PHP 800 per boat (max 8 persons per banca)
- **Pump boat for Sohoton tour inside park:** PHP 500 per group (max 6)
- **Boat docking fee:** PHP 100 per boat
- **Guide fee:** PHP 165 per guide (two guides per boat in some tours)
- **Life vest rental:** PHP 40 per person if not provided
- **Tojoman paddle boat to jellyfish sanctuary:** PHP 100 per person
- **Kayak rental (Blue Lagoon area):** PHP 200 to PHP 300 per hour

Budget PHP 550 to PHP 750 per person all-in if you are in a group of 4 to 6 sharing a boat from Socorro, excluding transfer from Siargao or Surigao City.

## 4. How to Get to Bucas Grande and Sohoton Cove from Cebu

### Option A: Cebu via Surigao City (Recommended for direct Bucas Grande access)

This is cheaper if you do not plan to stay in Siargao.

**Cebu to Surigao City:**

- **Flight:** Mactan-Cebu (CEB) to Surigao Airport (SUG) — CebGo DG6901, about 55 minutes daily. Fare PHP 1,800 to PHP 4,500 one way depending on promo. From Surigao City airport, tricycle to city proper or to Surigao Ferry Terminal / bus terminal.
- **Ferry:** Cebu Pier 3 to Surigao City Port — Starlite Ferries, Cokaliong, Oceanjet night ferry. Departure usually 7 PM to 10 PM, arrival 5 to 7 AM next day, 8.5 to 10 hours. Fare PHP 1,200 (economy/tourist bed bunks) to PHP 1,560 to PHP 2,500 (cabin). Plus terminal fee PHP 25 to PHP 50.

**Surigao City to Bucas Grande:**

- Go to **Hayanggabon Port in Claver** — van from Surigao City Integrated Terminal, PHP 120 to PHP 200, 1 to 1.5 hours. This is the main jump-off to Sohoton. Or go to Surigao City Port for boats to Socorro.
- At Hayanggabon: private boat hire standard rate PHP 3,500 roundtrip (good for up to 6 to 8). At **Socorro Port** itself (town of Socorro on Bucas Grande), rate is cheaper — PHP 2,500 roundtrip. The Socorro route requires first taking a passenger ferry Surigao City to Socorro (PHP 150 to PHP 200, 1.5 to 2 hours, daily 6 AM, 12:30 PM trips) then hiring a local boat PHP 1,200 to PHP 1,500 to Sohoton.
- If you already landed in Socorro, island-hopping rates start at PHP 2,000.

**Best for:** Travelers who want to overnight in Bucas Grande. There are homestays and resorts in Socorro and Sohoton.

### Option B: Cebu via Siargao (Best if you base in General Luna)

Most Cebu-based visitors use Siargao as base.

**Cebu to Siargao:**

- **Flight:** CEB to Siargao Airport (IAO) in Del Carmen — 55 minutes to 1 hour 5 minutes direct, Cebu Pacific/CebGo, Philippine Airlines, Sunlight Air, 1 to 2 flights per day. Promo fares PHP 1,500 to PHP 2,500, regular PHP 3,000 to PHP 5,500.
- **Ferry:** Cebu to Surigao City as above (PHP 1,200+) then Surigao to Dapa Port fast craft PHP 400 to PHP 800, 2 to 3.5 hours, multiple trips 5:30 AM, 7 AM, 11 AM, 1 PM. Total 11 to 13 hours.

**Siargao to Bucas Grande / Sohoton:**

- Book a **shared or private day tour** from General Luna or Dapa. Local motorboat transfer from General Luna to Bucas Grande is PHP 3,500 roundtrip for 1 to 4 persons boat, covering transfer only — not the Sohoton environmental fees inside. Organized private tours that include all entrance fees, guides, lunch, and boat transfers are priced USD 250 to USD 350 per boat overseas online, but if you book locally in General Luna tour shops, day tour is often PHP 2,000 to PHP 2,800 per person for joiners (minimum 5 to 6 pax) or PHP 10,000 to PHP 15,000 private boat all-in.
- Travel time: 1 to 2 hours each way by boat depending on sea.
- Tour start: 6 AM to 8 AM, return 3 PM to 4 PM.

**Which option from Cebu?** Via Surigao City is cheaper and faster if your destination is only Bucas Grande. Via Siargao is better if you also want to surf, stay in General Luna, and do Sohoton as a day trip.

## 5. Best Months, Sea Conditions, and Permits

**Best months:** March to June / dry season. Calm seas, sun for Hagukan glow, peak jellyfish numbers. Late February and early July also good. April to June is ideal.

**Worst months:** November to February (Amihan / northeast monsoon). Rough seas can cancel boats from Siargao, caves may be closed due to high tide or swells. Surigao del Norte also gets more rain December to January.

**Permits:** No separate DENR permit needed for day tourists — registration at Sohoton Cove Center covers it. Overnight camping or drone use needs prior clearance from Socorro LGU tourism office.

**Safety checks:**

- Tours are tide-dependent. Full moon and new moon bring higher tides — Hagukan may be closed.
- DENR enforces a no-plastic single-use policy inside the park.
- Bring cash — no card payments at registration.

## 6. What to Bring and How Much to Budget

**What to bring:** Rash guard, aqua shoes with grip, dry bag (10L), waterproof action cam, 1L water, snacks, reef-safe sunscreen applied early, insect repellent after the lagoon.

**Sample budget (per person, 2025 prices):**

- Via Surigao City DIY, group of 5:
  - Surigao boat + guides + entrance + jellyfish paddle: PHP 650 to PHP 800 each
  - Van Surigao to Hayanggabon: PHP 120 each
  - Ferry Cebu-Surigao: PHP 1,200 each one way
  - Overnight in Socorro: PHP 800 to PHP 2,000 per night

- Via Siargao day tour from General Luna:
  - Joiner tour: PHP 2,200 to PHP 2,800 all-in with lunch
  - Private boat: PHP 3,500 transport + PHP 800 eco fee + PHP 500 pump boat + PHP 100/pax + guides = PHP 6,000 to PHP 8,000 per boat total

**Overnight options:** Socorro has guesthouses, Sohoton area has eco-lodges (Sohoton Cove Camp, Club Tara Resort). Pre-book during Holy Week and peak summer.

## Tips Before You Go

### Check jellyfish and tide status a day before

Call Socorro Tourism or your boatman — jellyfish numbers and Hagukan access depend on tide tables. If high tide at noon, request an early start.

### Do not bring your own kayak into Tojoman

Only accredited paddle boats are allowed in the jellyfish lagoon to protect the animals. Kayaks are for Blue Lagoon only.

### Pack light if coming from Siargao

The Siargao-Bucas Grande crossing can be wet. Waterproof your bag, wear water shoes, not slippers.

### Leave early from Cebu if doing a day trip via Surigao

Catch the overnight ferry from Cebu, arrive Surigao early, catch Hayanggabon boat by 8 AM to maximize sunlight in Hagukan Cave.

### Stay a night if you can

Day trips feel rushed. Overnighting lets you see Tiktikan Lake, hidden beaches, and do Hagukan when crowds are gone.

<br>
## So, Is Bucas Grande Worth It from Cebu?

Yes — if you like quiet lagoons more than party beaches. Sohoton Cove is more contained and easier than Siargao's busy Magpupungko during peak months, and the stingless jellyfish experience is something you cannot get in Cebu.

For a first visit from Cebu, the most practical setup is fly Cebu to Siargao for 3 to 4 days and add Sohoton as a full-day tour on your second day, when you are adjusted to boat time. If you are on a tighter budget or want to avoid Siargao crowds, go Cebu to Surigao City via overnight ferry, overnight in Socorro, and explore Bucas Grande at your own pace for two days.

Bring cash, check tides, skip lotion before the jellyfish swim, and keep voice low inside Hagukan — the glow is better when it is quiet.
