# Malapascua and Thresher Sharks

Malapascua is a small island off the northern tip of Cebu, part of Daanbantayan municipality. It has white-sand beaches and a quiet village feel, but most people go for one reason: it is the only place in the world where you can see pelagic thresher sharks almost daily, year-round, at a shallow cleaning station.

The sharks come in at dawn to have parasites removed by small wrasse and cleaner fish. They are shy, long-tailed, and usually stay a few meters above the reef. The dive is early, cold, and dark at first, then unexpectedly calm as the first shark appears out of the blue.

Here is how the thresher shark dives work now, how to get to Malapascua from Cebu, what it costs, and how to make your trip smoother.

## 1. Monad Shoal vs Kimud Shoal — Where the Sharks Are Now

For years, every map pointed to Monad Shoal, a submerged plateau 30 minutes by boat from Malapascua. The top sits around 20 to 24 meters, with cleaning stations at 20 to 28 meters. Dawn dives left around 4:30 to 5:30 AM to be on site by first light.

In the last few years, tiger shark presence around Monad pushed threshers to move. Today most operators report more reliable sightings at Kimud Shoal, a deeper shoal further offshore. Kimud is shallower on top for the sharks — encounters are often at 14 to 18 meters, sometimes later into the morning — which can be easier for divers. Some shops now call it Kimud Shoal for threshers and keep Monad for other marine life.

Both sites are treated as protected. No touching, no chasing, no bright lights aimed directly at cleaning stations. Guides brief you to kneel or hover low behind the reef edge and wait.

**Best for:** Certified divers with at least Advanced Open Water. The depth is around 20 to 30 meters, and you need good buoyancy to stay still in a light current.

**What to do:** Book at least two mornings. Operators report 80 to 90 percent sighting rates on dawn dives in peak season, but one extra morning gives you a buffer for weather or shy sharks. Some shops offer a free extra dive if you miss the sharks.

**Location:** Monad Shoal 7 to 8 km east of Malapascua; Kimud Shoal further northeast, about 45 to 60 minutes by boat.

## 2. How to Get to Malapascua from Cebu City

The island is not connected by a bridge. You go by bus or van to the far north, then by boat.

**Cebu City to Maya Port, Daanbantayan:** Take a bus from Cebu North Bus Terminal. Ceres buses and vans leave every 30 minutes from around 1:00 AM to 10:00 PM. The ride takes 4 to 5 hours and costs PHP 200 to PHP 350 depending on aircon and van vs bus. Tell the conductor Maya Port or Maya Roro Port. Vans are faster but more cramped.

**Maya Port to Malapascua:** Public boats run from around 6:30 AM to 5:30 PM, every 30 minutes or when full. The crossing takes 30 to 45 minutes. Fare is PHP 100 to PHP 200 per person, plus PHP 50 to PHP 75 environmental fee for visitors. At low tide you may transfer to a small paddle boat for the last few meters. No public boats after 5:30 PM — if you arrive late, you will need a private charter for PHP 1,500 to PHP 3,000 per boat, or stay overnight in Maya.

**Total door-to-door:** About 6 to 7 hours from a Cebu City hotel to a Malapascua resort if you time it well. Plan an overnight in Cebu City before or after, rather than trying to connect a flight and a boat on the same day.

**Practical tip:** Leave your car or large luggage in Cebu City if possible. Parking near Maya Port is limited to private lots, and boats have limited space for big bags.

## 3. Dive Costs, Gear, and What to Expect on Thresher Day

You need to be on Malapascua the night before your thresher dive. Shops will not take same-day arrivals for the dawn trip because of timing.

**Typical pricing:**
- Fun dive Monad / Kimud Shoal: USD 35 to USD 50 per dive, or PHP 1,800 to PHP 2,800.
- Gato Island (2 dives, sea snakes and reef): USD 80 for two dives.
- Marine park fee: PHP 150 per day.
- Monad/Kimud shoal fee: PHP 50 per dive at some shops.
- Gear rental: Often included in fun dive price if you book a package; otherwise PHP 500 to PHP 800 per day for full set.

**The morning:** Wake-up around 4:00 AM, coffee at the shop, boat leaves around 4:30 to 5:00 AM. You descend in dim light, settle behind the cleaning station at about 20 meters, and wait. The water is cooler at depth — a 3mm wetsuit is comfortable for most. Bottom time is around 45 to 60 minutes, with a slow ascent and safety stop. Back at the island by 7:30 to 8:30 AM for breakfast.

**Skill level:** Advanced Open Water is recommended because of depth and early start. Some shops allow experienced Open Water divers to 18 meters at Kimud, but you will be limited on how close you can get to the main cleaning stations.

**Best for:** Divers who are comfortable with early mornings, deep water, and waiting quietly. This is not a fast drift or a colorful wall — it is patience.

## 4. Other Dive and Snorkel Spots Around Malapascua

While threshers are the headline, the island has other solid sites for a second dive day.

- **Gato Island:** A small protected island and sea snake sanctuary, 45 minutes by boat. Caves, white-tip reef sharks, seahorses, and cuttlefish. Often done as two dives.
- **Kalanggaman Island:** A long sandbar famous for photos. Good for beach time and snorkeling, but better as a day trip combined with diving rather than a snorkel-only destination from Malapascua.
- **Dakit-Dakit, Coral Garden, Japanese Wreck:** Shallow sites 5 to 10 meters for beginners, free divers, and underwater photography.
- **Bantigue Cove and Lighthouse:** Easy shore snorkeling at high tide, with small coral heads.

For non-divers, free diving schools on the island offer courses and guided sessions to see baby blacktip sharks and shallow reefs without tanks.

## 5. Where to Stay and How Long to Stay

Stay at least 2 to 3 nights. One night gives you only one shot at threshers, and weather can cancel boats. Two to three nights gives you two dawn attempts plus a day for Gato or Kalanggaman.

Accommodation ranges from simple guesthouses PHP 1,000 to PHP 2,000 per night to dive resorts PHP 3,500 to PHP 8,000 per night with aircon and breakfast. Most dive resorts are along Bounty Beach on the south side, where boats load. Book ahead for weekends and from November to May.

There is no ATM that is consistently reliable on the island — bring cash. Power can be intermittent; many resorts run generators at night.

## Tips Before You Dive Malapascua Thresher Sharks

### Book your dive shop before you arrive

Shops limit boats at Kimud/Monad to avoid crowding cleaning stations. Walk-ins during peak season often miss the early boat. Message your shop with your certification card and last dive date.

### Bring proof of certification and a logbook

Guides check. If you have not dived in more than a year, expect a paid refresher in shallow water first.

### Use a dive computer and torch, but no flash

A small torch helps on descent before sunrise, but turn it off at the cleaning station. Flash photography disturbs threshers — most shops ban strobes at the shark stations.

### Protect your ears and core temperature

Descent is quick and water is cooler at 20+ meters. Equalize early and often. A hood or vest helps if you get cold easily, especially for two dives in a row.

### Respect the cleaning station

Do not swim over the cleaning area, kneel on coral, or chase a shark for a photo. The sharks stay longer when divers stay still and low.

## So, Is Malapascua Worth the Long Trip from Cebu?

If you have dived before and want a shark encounter that is rare elsewhere, Malapascua is worth the 6-hour journey. The thresher dive is not about colorful reef — it is about a quiet, early morning meeting with a pelagic shark that rarely comes this shallow anywhere else.

For non-divers, Malapascua still works as a low-key beach island, but you will get more value if you try an introductory dive or free diving course. Otherwise, islands closer to Cebu like Moalboal or Apo Island offer easier snorkeling with less travel.
