# Hundred Islands, Pangasinan: Complete Guide to Alaminos, Boat Tours and Island Hopping

Hundred Islands National Park in Alaminos City, Pangasinan, is one of the oldest national parks in the Philippines. It covers 1,844 hectares in Lingayen Gulf and has 123 islands at high tide and 124 at low tide, scattered as limestone karsts with thin vegetation. Only a handful are developed for tourism, which keeps the actual island-hopping manageable even if the total number sounds overwhelming.

The park is run through the Lucap Wharf in Barangay Lucap, the main jump-off. Everything — registration, boat assignment, guide assignment, life vest rental — happens there. The system is more organized than most island-hopping spots in Luzon, with set boat sizes, set hours (6:00 AM to 5:30 PM), and a deposit-based trash system. If you have been to island hopping in Cebu or Bohol, expect more rules here, but also clearer fees.

For travelers coming from Cebu, this is a long-haul trip worth doing as part of a Northern Luzon loop. You will fly to Manila first, then take a bus north to Pangasinan. It is not a quick side trip, but an overnight in Alaminos or Lucap makes it a relaxed two-day escape with snorkeling, viewpoints, and caves without needing a private yacht.

## Alaminos City: Gateway to Hundred Islands

Alaminos is a component city in western Pangasinan, facing Lingayen Gulf. It is where you stay, eat, and arrange your trip. The city itself is low-key, but Lucap, its coastal barangay, has most of the tourism infrastructure — hotels, inns, seafood restaurants, tricycle terminal, and the Hundred Islands Welcome Center at Don Gonzalo Montemayor Wharf.

**Best for:** Base for your island-hopping day, especially if you arrive the night before.

**What to do:** Check in, register online via the Hundred Islands National Park Management System (HINPMS) if you want to save time, and buy dry snacks and water. Try Pangasinan bangus (milkfish) and bagoong at eateries near the wharf. Ask your accommodation if they offer tricycle transfers to Lucap Wharf in the morning.

**Location:** Alaminos City, Pangasinan, about 250 km north of Manila. Trike from city proper to Lucap Wharf: 15-20 minutes, around ₱50-₱100 per trike depending on load.

## How to Get There from Cebu via Manila

There is no direct flight from Cebu to Alaminos. The standard route is Cebu → Manila → Alaminos by bus.

**Step 1: Cebu to Manila (NAIA).** Fly Mactan-Cebu (CEB) to NAIA (MNL). Airlines: Cebu Pacific, Philippine Airlines, Philippines AirAsia. Flight time 1h 30m to 1h 50m. Book early morning arrival if you want to catch a bus the same day, but staying overnight in Manila is less stressful.

**Step 2: Manila to Alaminos City by bus.** From Cubao, Pasay, or Trinoma, take Victory Liner, Five Star Bus, Dagupan Bus Co., or Philippine Rabbit bound for Alaminos. Travel time is 5 to 7 hours depending on traffic and route (via NLEX-SCTEX-Tarlac-Camiling-Alaminos). Buses depart almost hourly from 3:00 AM onwards.

**Bus fare (2026):** ₱566 to ₱650 for air-conditioned bus from Cubao/Pasay to Alaminos. Check Victory Liner and Five Star official pages for real-time schedule. From NAIA, take a Grab or taxi to Cubao or Pasay bus terminal (45-90 mins depending on Metro Manila traffic).

**Step 3: Alaminos terminal to Lucap Wharf.** Tricycle to Welcome Center, 20-30 minutes.

**By private car from Manila:** NLEX → SCTEX → exit Luisita → Camiling → San Clemente → Mangatarem → Aguilar → Bugallon → Labrador → Sual → Alaminos. Driving time 5-6 hours without long stops.

For Cebuanos, the best setup is: Day 1 fly Cebu-Manila late morning, bus to Alaminos afternoon, check in Lucap. Day 2 full island-hopping day. Day 3 return.

## Boat Tours: Fees and How It Works

All boats are motorized bancas regulated by the City Tourism Office. You cannot bring your own boat. Fees are fixed and posted at Lucap.

### Registration fees (per person, 2026)

- Day tour registration: **₱120** (breakdown: ₱80 environmental fee + ₱30 IPAF/entrance + ₱10 emergency/insurance)
- Overnight registration: **₱200** per person
- Children 5 years old and below: FREE
- Senior citizens, PWDs: 20% discount on environmental fee with valid ID
- Pangasinan residents get discount on environmental component — bring ID

You also pay a refundable **Basura Mo, Iuwi Mo deposit: ₱200 per group**. You are given trash bags. Bring your sorted trash back to the Tourism Office after the tour to get the ₱200 back.

Online registration via thehundredislands.ph (HINPMS) is active since Feb 2026 and recommended during weekends.

### Boat rental fees (2026, per boat)

- **Small (1-5 pax):** ₱1,400 for 3-island tour / ₱1,600 one-day (all-day) / ₱3,000 two-day
- **Medium (6-10 pax):** ₱1,800 / ₱2,000 / ₱3,800
- **Large (11-15 pax):** ₱2,000 / ₱2,400 / ₱4,500
- **De Luxe (16-20 pax):** ₱2,500 / ₱2,800 / ₱5,500

Most visitors book the one-day tour. The 3-island tour is limited to Governor's, Quezon, and Children's only with about 20 minutes per island unless you arrange to stay longer on one and be picked up later.

### Other mandatory and optional fees

- **Tour guide:** Required. ₱1,000 per day for Hundred Islands guide / ₱2,000 two-day; specialist guide (cave, birdwatching) ₱1,500/day; dive guide ₱1,500/day. One guide per boat.
- **Life vest:** Mandatory for boat transfers and deep water swimming. Rental ₱50 per vest.
- **Snorkel set:** ₱150 per set at Lucap wharf.
- **Kayak:** ₱250/hour for 2 pax.
- Boats operate 6:00 AM to 5:30 PM only. Plan to be back by 5:00 PM.

**Sample cost for 5 pax, one-day tour:** Registration ₱120 x 5 = ₱600; boat ₱1,600; guide ₱1,000; life vests ₱50 x5 = ₱250; snorkel ₱150 x2 = ₱300; deposit ₱200 (refundable). Total group around ₱3,750 split 5 ways = ~₱750 each before food and transport.

## Islands to Visit: Governor's, Quezon, Children's and More

Only five islands are developed for tourism: Governor's, Quezon, Children's, Marcos and Lopez. The rest are limestone outcrops you view from the boat, including Turtle Island and Crocodile Island.

### 1. Governor's Island — The Viewpoint

Governor's is the largest developed island. It has the 180+ step climb to the viewing deck, the Pinoy Big Brother (PBB) house from the show, camping spots, and the longest zipline in the park: 546 meters to Virgin Island at ₱500 per jump.

**Best for:** First-time visitors who want the classic panoramic photo of Hundred Islands.

**What to do:** Climb to the viewpoint early when it is cooler. Bring water. Take photos from both sides of the deck. If you are camping, Governor's has designated pitching areas (₱200 pitching fee, tent rental ₱500-₱1,000).

**Tip:** The steps are exposed. Wear proper footwear, not just slippers.

### 2. Quezon Island — The Activity Hub

Quezon Island is the most commercial. It has tables (₱200-₱300), nipa huts (₱350-₱500 for half day), a giant clam sanctuary with over 5,000 giant clams seeded by UP Marine Science Institute, snorkeling areas, helmet diving (₱400/15 mins), jet ski rental, and a short 120m zipline (₱100).

**Best for:** Groups who want to stay longer, eat together, snorkel or try helmet diving.

**What to do:** Snorkel near the coral and giant clams rather than right off the beach where it is crowded. Arrange a boodle fight lunch with your guide if you want to eat on the island (order ahead). You can also rent a nipa hut for your group.

**Tip:** Quezon can feel crowded on holidays. Go early or tell your boatman you want to eat elsewhere.

### 3. Children's Island — Family-Friendly Shallow Water

Children's Island has a wide, shallow sandbar, calm water, tables, and nearby Cuenco Cave. The water stays knee- to waist-deep for a long stretch, which is why families prefer it.

**Best for:** Families with kids, non-swimmers, and anyone who wants calmer water.

**What to do:** Swim in the designated area, visit Cuenco Cave from the water side, and bring floaters for kids (life vests mandatory). Tent space also available (₱200).

### 4. Marcos Island — Cliff Jump and Caves

Marcos Island is not always in the 3-island tour but is included in the all-day tour if you ask. Its highlight is Imelda Cave and the 10- to 12-foot cliff jumping point into deep water.

**Best for:** Barkadas and adventure seekers.

**What to do:** Wear life vest to approach the cave, but remove it before jumping as instructed — inflated vests can cause injury on impact. Jumping is optional; you can watch from the ledge.

### 5. Lopez Island — Second Zipline

Lopez Island has its own 345m zipline (₱350) and quieter beach. Guides often suggest it if Governor's zipline has a queue.

## Snorkeling in Hundred Islands

Snorkeling is best around Quezon Island coral garden and between Lopez and Quezon islands where it is less crowded. Visibility is decent November to May, best February to April. Gear rents at Lucap for ₱150 per set (mask, snorkel).

**What to bring:** Rash guard, reef-safe sunscreen, dry bag. Do not step on corals or touch giant clams. The park enforces no fish feeding and no collection.

If you want better marine life than the main beach, ask your boatman to drift in open water with a guide. They know where re-seeded clams are and where small reef fish congregate.

## Best Months to Visit

**Best:** December to April (dry season). February to early April has the calmest seas, clearer water, and least rain. Best for drone shots, viewpoint climbs, and snorkeling.

**Good but watch rain:** Late November and May can still be good, but afternoon rain increases.

**Avoid or be cautious:** June to October (habagat/wet season) brings rougher seas, occasional boat cancellations, and murkier water. Holy Week and long weekends are extremely crowded at Lucap — arrive by 6:00-7:00 AM and pre-register online if you go then.

Boat tours are daily but may be suspended when PAGASA issues gale warnings.

## Tips Before You Go

### Book and arrive early

Boats are dispatched on a first-come, first-served basis at Lucap. On weekends, lines start before 7:00 AM. If you can, stay in Lucap the night before and be at the Welcome Center by 6:30 AM.

### Bring cash

Card and GCash signals are unreliable at the wharf and on islands. Bring small bills for table fees, tent fees, snorkel rental, and island food.

### Pack smart

Pack reef-safe sunscreen, extra water, dry bag, power bank, hat, and water shoes for rocky areas. If you plan to snorkel, bring your own mask if you are particular about fit and hygiene.

### Respect the park rules

File the passenger manifest, wear life vest during transits, keep noise down, and bring back your trash to claim your ₱200 deposit. There is also a gift certificate promo historically given to compliant groups — ask the staff if still active.

### Manage expectations

The developed islands have concrete huts, tables, and food stalls. It is not untouched wilderness — it is a managed national park close to Manila with 380,000+ visitors a year. If you want quiet, go midweek and ask your boatman to skip Quezon at peak lunch hour.

### For Cebu-based travelers

Treat this as a 2D1N or 3D2N add-on to a Manila trip, not a day trip from Cebu. Combine it with Bolinao's Patar Beach or a quick stop in Dagupan for bangus tasting if you have an extra day.

## So, Is Hundred Islands Worth It from Cebu?

If you like island-hopping with structure — viewpoints you can climb, caves you can swim into, giant clams you can actually see, and boat fees that are posted and fixed — Hundred Islands is worth the travel from Cebu via Manila. Stay overnight in Lucap, book the one-day boat instead of just three islands, target Governor's for views, Quezon for snorkeling and giant clams, Children's for calm swimming, and Marcos if your group wants a cliff jump.

Go between February and April for the best balance of clear skies, calm water, and manageable crowds. Split the boat with four to five friends, bring cash and your own snacks, and be back at Lucap before 5:30 PM for your trash refund.
