Coron Island Hopping Tours 2026: Which One to Book

Ren Calago 29 October 20260

Ask three agencies in Coron town for "Tour A" and you may get three different boats. Some shops use letters, others use names like Ultimate or Island Escapade, and the same stops get mixed and matched under different labels. The way to book well is to ignore the label, read the stop list, and match it to what you want from the day.


For a first visit with one day on the water, book an Ultimate tour. It covers Kayangan Lake, Twin Lagoon and the Skeleton Wreck in a single trip around Coron Island. With a second day, take a beach tour south to Malcapuya and the Bulog Dos sandbar. A third day suits a snorkelling trip to the reefs and wrecks, or the long ride north to Calauit Safari Park.


This guide sorts the common Coron tours by what they cover, what they cost in 2026 and who each one suits, followed by a simple plan for two to four days. Agencies set their own rates and change routes with the tide and weather, so confirm the stop list and the fees the evening before you go.




Coron Island Tours vs Outer Island Tours


Nearly every Coron tour falls into one of two groups. The first stays around Coron Island itself, a short boat ride from the town pier. These trips visit the lakes and lagoons inside the limestone, plus a wreck or coral garden on the way. Boat rides are short, so you spend most of the day in the water.


The second group heads out to the smaller islands south and west of Busuanga, where the draw is white sand rather than cliffs. The ride to the first stop can take an hour and a half or more, and the open water there gets choppier than the sheltered bay. Keep that in mind if anyone in your group gets seasick.


Shared boats leave the municipal pier between 8:00 and 9:00 in the morning and return in the late afternoon. Joiner rates usually include the boat, a guide, lunch and drinking water. Whether the entrance fees are included depends on the agency, so ask before paying.



The Main Coron Island Hopping Tours


Ultimate tour


Stops: Kayangan Lake, Twin Lagoon, Skeleton Wreck, a coral garden, Siete Pecados and a beach for lunch, usually CYC or Atwayan. Joiner rate: around ₱1,700 to ₱2,300, depending on whether fees are included.


This is the default choice for a single day because it packs Coron's best-known sights into one trip. Kayangan has a stair climb to the viewpoint before you swim, Twin Lagoon is entered by swimming under a rock wall or climbing a ladder, and the Skeleton Wreck lies shallow enough to see from the surface. The downside is crowds. Most boats reach Kayangan in the late morning, so it can feel busy.


Super Ultimate tour


Stops: everything on the Ultimate route plus Barracuda Lake. Joiner rate: around ₱1,900 to ₱2,500.


Barracuda Lake is known for its thermocline, a layer of warm water a few metres down that you can feel as you dive. Adding it makes for a long day with little time at each stop. Choose this if you only have one day and want both lakes. With two days, splitting the stops over two tours is more relaxed.


Island Escapade or beach tour


Stops: Malcapuya Island, the Bulog Dos sandbar and Banana Island. Joiner rate: around ₱1,700 to ₱2,000.


This is the white-sand tour. Malcapuya has a long, soft beach with a small hill behind it, Bulog Dos is a sandbar that appears and shrinks with the tide, and Banana Island is a quiet stop for swimming and snorkelling. Each island collects its own fee, usually ₱200 to ₱300 per person. The boat ride is long, so this tour is the most likely to be cancelled when the wind picks up.


Reefs and Wrecks tour


Stops: Lusong Gunboat, Lusong Coral Garden and the East Tangat Wreck, sometimes with a beach stop. Joiner rate: around ₱1,500 to ₱1,800.


A snorkeller's day. The Lusong Gunboat sits so shallow that parts of it break the surface at low tide, and the coral garden next to it is one of the best reef stops close to town. You do not need to dive to enjoy it, but you do need to be comfortable in open water with a mask and fins.


Calauit Safari Park


Joiner rate: around ₱2,500 to ₱3,000 for the full day, including the van and boat transfer.


Calauit is on an island off the northwest tip of Busuanga, where giraffes, zebras and native animals such as the Calamian deer roam in a protected park. Getting there takes a long van ride and a short boat crossing, so plan for an early start and a full day. It is the best choice for families with children, or for a day when the sea is too rough for the island tours.



Fees to Carry in Cash


Most of these stops are run by local communities or the Tagbanua, who manage much of Coron Island, and their fees are collected at the landing. Even when an agency says fees are included, bring small bills.


  • Kayangan Lake: ₱300
  • Twin Lagoon: about ₱200
  • Barracuda Lake: ₱200 to ₱250
  • Skeleton Wreck and coral gardens: about ₱150 each
  • Siete Pecados: about ₱100
  • Malcapuya, Bulog Dos and Banana Island: ₱200 to ₱300 each
  • Snorkel set rental, if not included: about ₱150

Plan on ₱800 to ₱1,000 per person in cash for a lagoon day and a little less for the reef tour. ATMs in town are few and often have queues, so withdraw before your first tour.



Joiner Boat or Private Boat?


A joiner boat is the cheaper choice for one or two people, and it is a sociable day. A private boat makes sense for groups of four or more. Agency quotes for a private Coron Island trip usually run from about ₱3,500 to ₱9,500, depending on the boat size and route, plus entrance fees, lunch and a guide.


The real reason to go private is timing. Kayangan Lake opens early in the morning, and a boat at the landing before the joiner tours arrive gets a calmer climb and an emptier lake. A private boat also lets you combine stops from two tours, such as Kayangan in the morning and the Lusong reefs after lunch.



A Plan for Two to Four Days


Two days: the Ultimate tour first, then either the beach tour or Reefs and Wrecks, depending on whether you prefer sand or snorkelling.


Three days: add the other of those two. Put the beach tour on the day with the calmest forecast, since it covers the most open water.


Four days: add Calauit, or spend the day on Busuanga itself with a soak at the Maquinit hot springs and the climb up Mount Tapyas for the sunset view over town. Divers can swap any of these days for a wreck dive. Our guide to Coron wreck diving and Kayangan Lake covers the dive sites, costs and lake rules in more detail.



Weather and When to Book


December to April brings the calmest sea and the most reliable tours, with December the busiest month. From June the southwest monsoon brings afternoon storms and rougher water, and August is usually the hardest month for boat trips. When the coast guard raises a storm warning, every tour boat stays at the pier.


Book your most important tour for the first full day, so a cancellation still leaves time to try again. In peak months, reserve a day ahead through your hotel or an agency in town. In the quieter months, booking the evening before is usually fine.



Choosing Your First Coron Tour


If you see only one thing in Coron, make it the lakes and lagoons on the Ultimate tour. Add the beach tour for white sand, Reefs and Wrecks for snorkelling, and Calauit for a different kind of day. Whatever the agency calls its tours, check the stop list, ask what the price includes, and carry cash for the landings.
