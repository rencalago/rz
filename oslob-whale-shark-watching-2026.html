Oslob Whale Shark Watching in 2026: What Visitors Should Know

Oslob is the town most first-time visitors to Cebu hear about when they search for whale sharks. The sharks are real, and the sightings are often close. The way the tour is run, however, has been argued over for years, and the rules that were meant to change it have been proposed, reported and put on hold. A plan based on an older article may not match what you find at the beach.


This guide sets out what is known about the visitor cap and fees, what is still unclear, and how to decide whether the visit suits you. Figures for fees and visitor numbers change, so treat them as background and check the latest position with the Oslob tourism office or your operator before you travel.


The town sits on the southern coast of Cebu, and the shark-watching takes place at Barangay Tan-awan, a short ride from the town centre. Sightings depend on the sharks, the weather and the water, and no visit is guaranteed. Keep that in mind before you plan a full day around one swim.



What the Experience Looks Like


Most visitors enter the water from the shore at Tan-awan, put on a life vest and join a guide near the spot where the sharks gather. The groups are managed in turns, so the wait can be long on busy mornings. Once you are in the water, you swim alongside the animal for as long as the guide allows, and the water is deep enough that you should be comfortable swimming on your own.


The sharks in Oslob have been fed by the tour operators for many years, and feeding is the part of the experience that draws the most criticism. Marine researchers have raised concerns that regular feeding changes how the sharks move and can make them come to the same spot whether or not food is there. Locals who depend on the tour argue that it gives fishers an income and a reason to protect the animals. Both points are made in good faith, and you can weigh them yourself.


The Daily Cap and Why It Keeps Changing


The number of visitors allowed at Tan-awan each day has been the main issue. Earlier reports describe an agreed cap of 800 visitors a day, down from 1,000, with 600 places for tour groups and 200 for local government units. Those reports were written when the limit was still being finalised, so the figures show what was proposed, not what is enforced today.


More recent coverage describes a regulated policy with the same 800 figure. The Oslob mayor and local operators said it favoured city-based tour guides and hurt the local people who run the boats and the beach. Cebu's Vice Governor then ordered that the existing arrangements, the status quo, be kept while the parties talk. We could not find a current official notice that sets out which rules apply in 2026, so do not rely on any cap number here. Ask the tourism office on arrival what is in force that day.


Fees, Time in the Water and Receipts


Entrance and guide fees at Tan-awan have been reported at different levels over the years, and the most recent figures we found were not dated clearly. Earlier coverage also said the town and the local association wanted to raise visitor fees, and that fewer visitors could mean more time in the water, possibly up to an hour instead of 30 minutes. We could not confirm either change as having taken effect.


Budget as if the fees have gone up since the last price you read. Bring cash in small notes, and ask for a receipt for every payment. If the price you are quoted does not match what you read online, ask the office to show you the posted rate before you pay. Staff who are open about fees are a good sign.


Getting to Oslob from Cebu City


Oslob is often quoted at about three hours by road from Cebu City, though traffic and the stops along the way can stretch the trip. Buses and vans leave from the Cebu South Bus Terminal, and the drop-off is in the town rather than at the beach. From the town, a tricycle or habal-habal takes you to Tan-awan. Schedules change, so confirm the last trip back at the terminal before you set out, since the return can be harder to find in the late afternoon.


If you are already on the south coast, the stops are worth combining. Tumalog Falls is on the road into the town, and Sumilon Island, a marine sanctuary, is reached by boat from Oslob. Both are easier to visit in the morning, so plan the shark swim first and the rest after lunch. Our south Cebu waterfall stops guide covers the wider route if you have more than one day in the area.


How to Behave Around the Sharks


Keep your distance from the animal, do not touch it, and do not swim after it or block its path. Follow the guide's instructions even when they feel slow, since the sharks respond to people moving around them. Avoid using flash photography in the water, and keep your fins and legs away from the animal. These are simple habits, and they matter more than any rule printed on a sign.


If you are uneasy about feeding, ask the guide whether the animal is being fed before you enter the water. You are not required to join a session that you are not comfortable with, and you can leave at any time. Choosing to skip the swim is a fair decision, and it does not make the rest of your trip less worthwhile.


Who Should Think Twice


The water at Tan-awan is deep, and the sharks are large. Anyone who is not confident in open water should think carefully before booking, even with a life vest. Children should be supervised at all times, and people with heart or breathing conditions should check with a doctor first. The sea can also be rough on some days, and the guides may cancel or shorten a session if conditions change.


Crowds are the other consideration. Late morning and weekends are the busiest times, so arriving early gives you a calmer start and a better chance of a shorter wait. If you dislike crowded beaches, a mid-week visit in the early morning is the least hectic option.


When to Go


The dry months, generally November to May, are usually calmer for boats and easier for visibility. The rainy months can bring rough seas and murky water, which makes sightings less predictable and the swim less pleasant. Check the weather on the day you plan to go, and keep a spare day in your schedule if the trip is part of a short stay.


If Oslob Is Not Right for You


If the rules remain unsettled and the feeding bothers you, consider a different place to see whale sharks. Donsol, in Sorsogon, is the other Philippine site most often recommended, though it is a long way from Cebu and needs a planned trip. If you want to stay in Cebu, our three-day Cebu itinerary shows how a south trip fits alongside the city, and the Kawasan Falls canyoneering guide covers another popular day out from Moalboal.


Whatever you decide, verify the latest rules and prices on the day. A few minutes of questions at the tourism office or with your operator will tell you more than any article can, including this one.
