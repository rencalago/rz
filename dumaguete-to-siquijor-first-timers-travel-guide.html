Dumaguete to Siquijor: A First-Timer's Travel Guide

Ren Calago 11 October 2026

One of the advantages of visiting Dumaguete is having another island within reach. A ferry ride across the water takes you to Siquijor, a destination known for its waterfalls, beaches, old churches, forest scenery and relaxed island atmosphere.


If it's your first time making the trip, the key is planning beyond the ferry ticket. You also need to consider which port you'll arrive at, how you'll get around, which attractions make sense to combine and whether a day trip is enough. Here's a practical guide to help you plan your first Siquijor adventure from Dumaguete.


1. Plan Your Ferry Trip Before Anything Else


The first step is checking the ferry schedule from Dumaguete to Siquijor. Several shipping operators serve the route, and their crossing times, departure points, fares and vessel types can differ.


The Philippine Ports Authority's Port of Dumaguete schedule lists services to Siquijor, including departures operated by OceanJet, Montenegro Shipping Lines, Aleson Shipping and other companies. However, schedules can change without prior notice, so use the official port schedule and the relevant operator to confirm the latest departure time before traveling.


Check whether your ticket is for Siquijor Port or Larena Port. These are different arrival points, and your choice can affect the time and cost of reaching your accommodation or first destination. Don't assume every ferry arrives at the same place.


Book ahead when possible, particularly on weekends, public holidays and busy travel periods. Arrive at the terminal early enough to complete ticketing and boarding procedures, and keep your ticket and valid identification accessible.


Check the return ferry schedule at the same time. This is especially important if you're planning to return to Dumaguete that evening. Weather and sea conditions can affect sailings, so leave some flexibility in your travel plans.


2. Decide Whether to Visit for a Day or Stay Overnight


A day trip can work if you have only a few hours available and choose attractions that are reasonably close together. However, it can feel rushed when you factor in ferry check-in, the crossing, transport around the island and the return journey.


For a more relaxed first visit, consider staying one or two nights. This gives you more time to explore different parts of the island without spending the entire day watching the clock.


An overnight stay is particularly useful if you want to visit Cambugahay Falls, explore Lazi's heritage sites, enjoy a beach sunset and still have time for a leisurely meal. These experiences are more enjoyable when you don't have to fit everything into a narrow window before the last ferry.


Your arrival port, accommodation location and preferred attractions should determine your itinerary. Choose your lodging first, then group nearby destinations together to avoid unnecessary backtracking.


3. Visit Cambugahay Falls in Lazi


Cambugahay Falls is one of Siquijor's best-known natural attractions. Located near Lazi, it features several levels of cascading water and natural pools surrounded by tropical greenery.


Visitors often come to swim, take photographs or enjoy the rope swings and bamboo rafts available on-site when operating. The falls are popular, so arriving earlier in the day may help you enjoy a less busy atmosphere.


Be prepared for stairs and uneven surfaces on the way down to the pools. Wear footwear with a good grip, keep your belongings secure and follow the instructions of local staff. If the weather has been poor, check whether access is safe before traveling there.


Conditions and site arrangements can change. In early October 2026, Siquijor Tourism announced that Cambugahay Falls had resumed operations after a temporary closure. Before your visit, check for new advisories, current fees and any rules that may affect swimming or other activities.


4. Explore Lazi's Heritage and the Enchanted Balete Tree


Siquijor isn't just about beaches and waterfalls. Its cultural and historical sites are worth making room for, particularly if you want a more varied itinerary.


In Lazi, visitors can explore the historic San Isidro Labrador Church and its convent. The church complex is an important part of the island's heritage and offers a different perspective from the natural attractions that usually dominate travel photographs.


Another familiar stop is the Old Enchanted Balete Tree, a large, old tree associated with local stories and traditions. A spring and fish-spa experience can be found around its base. Treat the place respectfully and follow posted guidance on activities and fees.


These stops can often be combined with a trip to Cambugahay Falls, depending on your route and transport. Check opening times and allow enough time at each place instead of treating every stop as a quick photo opportunity.


5. Make Time for the Beaches


If you prefer a slower afternoon, add a beach stop to your itinerary. Paliton Beach is known for its white sand, coconut trees and sunset scenery. It can be a lovely place to wind down after a day of sightseeing, although conditions and crowds vary.


Salagdoong Beach in Maria is another well-known coastal destination, recognized for its rocky coves and cliff-jumping platforms. Don't assume that jumping is always permitted. Follow current safety rules, check the water conditions and obey any closure or access notices.


You don't have to visit every beach in one trip. Choose one that fits your route and leave enough time to enjoy it. If you are planning to watch the sunset, consider your distance from the port or accommodation before deciding how late to stay.


6. Choose How You'll Get Around the Island


Transportation is one of the most important parts of planning a Siquijor visit. Attractions are spread across different municipalities, and reaching one stop doesn't automatically mean the next one is nearby.


Depending on your budget and comfort level, options may include renting a motorcycle, arranging a tricycle or private driver, or using available local transport. Confirm the fare and route before starting a journey, particularly if you're traveling to several destinations in one day.


Motorcycle rental can offer flexibility, but only ride if you're legally permitted and experienced enough to handle the roads. Wear a helmet, check the vehicle's condition and avoid riding after drinking. If you're unfamiliar with the island's roads, hiring a driver may be the more comfortable choice.


Before booking a ride, share your intended stops and ask whether waiting time, fuel and return transport are included. A clear arrangement helps you avoid unexpected costs and last-minute transportation problems.


7. Prepare a Realistic Budget


Your total budget depends on the ferry operator, accommodation, transport, meals and activities. A simple day trip will usually cost less than staying overnight, but transportation around the island can add up if attractions are far apart.


Before leaving Dumaguete, make a short list of expected expenses: round-trip ferry tickets, terminal charges, local transport, entrance fees, meals and a contingency amount. If you plan to rent a motorcycle or book a private driver, confirm the rate and what's included.


Avoid relying on old online prices without checking them. Ferry fares, site fees and tour arrangements can change, and different operators may charge different amounts. Bring some cash for small establishments and local transport, while keeping your belongings secure.


A Simple Two-Day Siquijor Itinerary


If you have two days, use the first day to explore the Lazi area. After arriving and checking in, visit Cambugahay Falls if access and timing allow, then explore nearby heritage sites. Finish the day with a relaxed dinner or a sunset stop that fits your accommodation's location.


On the second day, choose a beach or another attraction on the island. Paliton Beach can suit travelers who want coastal scenery, while Salagdoong may appeal to those exploring the eastern side. Check travel times and your return ferry before committing to distant stops.


This is a flexible outline, not a fixed schedule. The best order depends on where you stay, which port you use and the ferry times available for your dates. Avoid trying to circle the entire island if doing so would leave you rushing through every destination.


Before You Leave Dumaguete


Confirm both ferry journeys, verify the arrival port and book your accommodation if you're staying overnight. Check the weather, site advisories and local transport arrangements. Keep a little flexibility in your plans in case a sailing or attraction schedule changes.


Most importantly, respect the island's communities and natural environment. Follow instructions at waterfalls and beaches, avoid leaving rubbish, and ask permission before photographing people. Small actions help make these destinations welcoming for future visitors.


Make Siquijor More Than a Quick Stop


Traveling from Dumaguete to Siquijor can be a straightforward island escape when you plan around the ferry and keep your itinerary realistic. The island offers a mix of natural scenery, heritage, beaches and local experiences that deserves more than a hurried checklist.


For first-time visitors, the best approach is simple: confirm the ferry, choose a convenient place to stay, group nearby attractions together and leave room for unexpected discoveries. With a little preparation, the crossing from Dumaguete can be the beginning of a memorable Siquijor trip.
