Samar Caves: Sohoton and Calbiga Costs, Routes and Seasons

Ren Calago 21 October 2026

Samar's two best-known cave trips share an island and little else. Sohoton, in Basey, is a half-day of river, kayaks and a lit cave that a first-timer can handle. Langun-Gobingob, in Calbiga, is a full day underground with mud, ropes and a 40-metre wall. Choose the wrong one and you either waste a flight or end up out of your depth.


This guide sets the two side by side on difficulty, cost and logistics, shows how to reach them from Cebu, and explains why the "dry season" advice repeated online needs a closer look. The east of Samar has no true dry season, and that changes when you should book.


Both caves sit inside the Samar Island Natural Park, so guides and permits are part of the price rather than an optional extra. Sohoton Cove in Bucas Grande, which gets a mention in our Siargao guide, is a different place entirely.




Sohoton: The Easy One


What the trip involves


The name comes from a Waray word meaning "to pass through", which suits the narrow gaps between chambers. The park covers about 840 hectares and was first protected in 1935. You register at the Basey Tourism Office near the town plaza, which arranges the boat. A guide is compulsory inside the cave, and only the guide may touch the formation that gives out musical tones.


A boat of roughly 30 minutes takes you up the river to Panhulugan Cave, which has two chambers of stalactites, stalagmites and bats. Kayaks then carry you on to the Natural Bridge, a limestone arch with the river running beneath it and a pool on the far side. Life vests are required on the water. Local tradition and archaeology add some weight to the visit, because the caves were burial grounds in the Stone and Iron Ages and later hideouts for fighters in the revolutionary wars.


What it costs


The posted charges are PHP 20 at the gate for Filipinos and PHP 200 for foreign visitors, PHP 500 for the return boat (up to five people), PHP 300 for the cave guide and PHP 300 for cave lighting. Kayak rental is PHP 50 and a kayak guide PHP 150. The boat, guide and lighting add up to PHP 1,100 per group, so a full boat of five makes the trip cheap per head. These rates were listed in April 2025, so confirm the total with the tourism office before you board.


Wear shoes that can get wet and keep your phone in a dry bag. The cave suits beginners, but the kayak leg means you will get splashed.


Calbiga's Langun-Gobingob: The Serious One


What a day tour covers


Langun-Gobingob is a protected cave system of 12 chambers stretching about seven kilometres under a 2,968-hectare protected area. It is not a walk-in attraction. The operator that runs the standard trips, Trexplore, is based in Catbalogan and handles the permits, gear and guides.


Its one-day Discovery trip starts with registration in Catbalogan at 7 AM and a public bus to Calbiga, where the permit is collected. A habal-habal takes you to Barangay Panayuran, followed by a trek of about an hour to the Gobingob viewing deck and a short descent to the cave mouth. Inside you pass giant stalagmites and flowstones, stop for lunch in the "football field" chamber, then tackle a vertical descent and a wall of about 40 metres before reaching the Langun chamber and its bats. A jungle trek ends at Mapaso Spring, and the group is back in Calbiga around 6:30 PM. Expect a day of about 13 hours, counting the ride back.


What it costs


The Discovery trip is PHP 5,000 per person with a minimum of two, and children from eight years old can join if they are fit. The price covers permits, caving gear, transport, porters, a guide and photos. Booking needs a 50 percent advance that Trexplore lists as non-refundable and non-transferable, so lock in your dates only after you have checked the weather and your flights. The two-day version, camping at the Gobingob or Langun campsite, is PHP 10,000 per person. Rates can change without notice, so confirm at trexplore.ph.


Bring sturdy shoes with grip, a change of clothes, water and a small pack. The mud, wading and rope sections are real, and the technical parts call for proper helmets and harnesses, which is the reason to go with an operator that supplies them.


Getting There From Cebu


Tacloban is the gateway for both caves. Cebgo and Philippine Airlines fly Cebu to Tacloban in about an hour, and an early flight leaves time for Basey the same day. Cebu Pacific's own flights on this route ended in June 2026, so search by route rather than by airline. For how flights and ferries compare on cost, see our ferry versus flight budget guide.


The sea alternative is OceanJet's fast craft to Ormoc, which runs four times a day and takes about three hours, followed by a van of two and a half to three hours to Tacloban. It suits anyone already combining the trip with Kalanggaman Island, which shares the Ormoc leg. From Tacloban, jeepneys and vans to Basey leave from the New Bus Terminal in Abucay and take about 45 minutes across the San Juanico Bridge. For Calbiga, take a van toward Catbalogan or Calbayog and ask to be dropped in Calbiga.


Philippine Airlines has also flown Cebu to Calbayog since October 2025, but only on certain days of the week, so check the current schedule first. Tacloban remains the simpler base for both caves.


When to Go


Eastern Samar and the neighbouring Leyte coast have no dry season. Rain peaks from December to February and eases between March and May. Heavy rain in those wet months raises the Sohoton river and the park can close, so the calmest window is roughly March to June. Trexplore runs trips year-round except in bad weather, and rising water inside the caves can still turn a group back, so leave a spare day. Samar also lies in the typhoon belt, which is another reason to avoid tight connections home.


A Three-Day Way to Do Both


Fly to Tacloban early on day one, take the van to Basey for the Sohoton half-day, then continue to Catbalogan for the night, since Trexplore registers there at 7 AM. Day two is Langun-Gobingob. Fly home on day three with a margin for delays. Budget PHP 5,000 for Calbiga and a few hundred pesos for Sohoton, before transport.


If you only have one day, choose Sohoton. If you want a real underground expedition and can handle mud and ropes, give Calbiga the time it needs.
