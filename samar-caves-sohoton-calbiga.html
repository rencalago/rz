Samar Caves Guide: Sohoton, Calbiga and Langun-Gobingob

Ren Calago 21 October 2026

Samar sits on one of the biggest limestone landscapes in the country, and two caving trips dominate every plan: Sohoton Natural Bridge and Cave in Basey, and the Langun-Gobingob system in Calbiga. They are very different days out. Sohoton is a boat-and-kayak half day that ends with a short walk into a cave. Calbiga is a proper expedition in the dark, with helmets, wading and an optional night underground.


Pick Sohoton if you want something organised, affordable and doable in a day. Pick Calbiga if you want to crawl, swim and camp, and you can book several days ahead. This guide gives the fees, the permits, the routes in and the months when the rivers behave.


Neither place is a spontaneous stop. Sohoton needs a morning start and cash in hand, and Calbiga does not accept walk-ins at all. Both reward people who sort out the paperwork before they leave town.




Sohoton Natural Bridge and Cave in Basey


You register at the Basey tourism office in town, pay your fees there, then ride a motorised boat up the river to the park. The ride takes roughly 30 to 45 minutes between nipa palms and limestone walls. At the park you walk or paddle to the Natural Bridge, a limestone arch with the river running beneath it, and wade a short way to the cave entrance.


Inside, plan on 15 to 20 minutes each way past stalactites and roosting bats. Helmets and life vests are provided, and you pay for lighting. Many people say paddling back through the gorge is the best part of the day.


What Sohoton costs


The tourism office charges a bundle of small fees, so the total depends on how many people share the boat. The figures below are the commonly listed rates, and Basey can change them, so confirm at the counter before you pay.


  • Entrance: PHP 25 for Filipinos, PHP 200 for foreign visitors
  • Boat, return transfer to the park, 1 to 5 passengers: PHP 500
  • Full river cruise boat from Basey and back: about PHP 2,500 per boat
  • Environmental fee: PHP 50, community fee: PHP 150
  • Cave guide: PHP 300 per group, lighting: PHP 300
  • Kayak rental: PHP 50, kayak guide: PHP 150
  • A service surcharge of about 15% on the subtotal

A group of five sharing the standard boat usually lands between PHP 500 and PHP 800 each. The longer river cruise pushes that toward PHP 800 to PHP 1,200. Treat these as planning numbers, not quotes.


No separate DENR permit is normally needed for the standard short tour, because registration and the fees act as your permit. If you want to go beyond the main chamber, ask the tourism office first, since extra clearance may apply. The office opens around 8 AM and closes at 4 PM, so arrive early rather than gambling on the afternoon.


Calbiga and the Langun-Gobingob Cave System


Langun-Gobingob is the large one. The system has 12 chambers over roughly six to seven kilometres of explored passage and sits inside a protected area of about 2,968 hectares. It is often described as the largest cave system in the Philippines. An Italian caver, Guido Rossi, first explored it in 1987, and it opened to visitors in 1990 under the National Cave Act.


There are no walkways and no lights. You walk, scramble and wade, and the air inside is humid enough that a phone left out of a dry bag will suffer. Camping chambers exist for overnight trips, which is the main reason people pick Calbiga over any other cave in the region.


Tour formats


Trexplore the Adventures, based in Catbalogan, is the operator commonly used for guided trips, and entry is by guide only. Three formats are on offer:


  • Discovery day tour: four to six hours in the entrance chambers, with no camping
  • Overnight, two days and one night: camp inside the cave
  • Extreme, three days and two nights: a longer traverse with two nights underground, meant for fit travellers

The day tour is quoted at around PHP 3,500 per person for a minimum group of four or five, and that price typically covers permits, gear, meals, local porters, a cave master and a certificate. Longer private trips can run PHP 8,000 to PHP 12,000 per person depending on group size and food. Organised caving festivals sometimes list lower rates, so ask Trexplore what is scheduled. Tips for porters and guides are extra, and PHP 300 to PHP 500 per porter per day is a common figure.


Send your full name, age, emergency contact and a valid ID several days ahead. The operator arranges the mayor's permit, the barangay clearance and the DENR permit for the protected area, and group sizes are capped. This is not a trip for kids under about 12, anyone who dislikes enclosed spaces, or anyone without sturdy shoes that grip wet rock.


Getting to Samar


Samar's cave towns have no airport of their own, so you fly or sail to a gateway and finish by road. The caves are on opposite sides of the island, so the gateway you pick shapes the whole itinerary.


Via Tacloban: best for both caves


Tacloban has the most flights and the easiest connections. The Cebu to Tacloban flight takes a little over an hour, with promo fares starting around PHP 1,000 and regular fares reaching PHP 4,500. From the airport, a jeepney or tricycle gets you to the transport terminal for about PHP 50 to PHP 150.


For Sohoton, take a van or jeepney across the San Juanico Bridge to Basey, about an hour for roughly PHP 70 to PHP 150. Tell the driver you are heading for the tourism office. For Calbiga, a van toward Catbalogan along the Maharlika Highway takes one and a half to two hours for PHP 150 to PHP 250, followed by a habal-habal ride of about 30 minutes to the jump-off. Trexplore can arrange a van pickup if you book in advance.


Via Ormoc: a sea route


If Tacloban flights are full or expensive, take the fast craft to Ormoc in Leyte, a crossing of roughly three hours that costs around PHP 1,200 to PHP 1,600. A van then covers the two hours to Tacloban. Our Kalanggaman Island guide lays out the Ormoc sailings in more detail, since the same boats serve both trips.


Via Calbayog: the northern gateway


Calbayog suits travellers who want to see northern Samar as well. There is a short morning flight from Cebu, and overnight ferries run to Calbayog and to Catbalogan on a few weekdays. Check the current schedule before you plan around either, because ferry days and fares shift. From Calbayog, vans heading to Catbalogan pass through Calbiga in about an hour and a half, and vans to Tacloban can drop you at the Basey junction.


Compare the air, ferry and van legs before you commit, since a cheap flight can lose its advantage once you add transfers. Our ferry versus flight budget comparison shows how to weigh them.


Best Months for the Samar Caves


November to May is the dry window and the safer choice for both trips. Sohoton's river runs lower and calmer, and Calbiga's passages carry less water, which matters for camping. March to May is the hottest stretch above ground, though the cave stays cool at roughly 22 to 26°C.


June to October is a gamble. Heavy rain can raise the Sohoton River enough to suspend boat tours, and the river inside Langun floods quickly, which is why operators cancel when levels climb. Typhoon season in Eastern Visayas peaks from August to October, with flights and sailings sometimes cancelled. If you have to travel in the wet months, book Sohoton for early morning and leave two spare days for Calbiga.


Practical Tips Before You Go


Handle permits differently for each cave


Sohoton accepts walk-ins, but register at the Basey tourism office early in the day. Calbiga does not. Contact the operator or the Calbiga tourism office at least five to seven days ahead so permits and insurance paperwork are ready.


Take the local guides


Both caves require guides, and the fees stay in the communities that protect the sites. Do not try to skip them to save money. At Sohoton, the guide and the lighting person are checked at the entrance.


Pack for water and darkness


Wear aqua shoes or trekking shoes that drain, not flip-flops or heavy boots. Bring quick-dry clothes rather than cotton, a dry bag for your phone and power bank, and a headlamp with spare batteries. Overnight campers need a sleeping mat and a hammock or bedding, since there is no electricity beyond Calbiga town.


Bring cash and start early


Plan on cash for everything. There are no card terminals at the jump-offs, and ATMs are scarce outside the towns. A PHP 3,000 to PHP 5,000 float covers a Sohoton group day, and Calbiga's day tour needs more for personal spending. Leave before 8 AM to beat the midday heat on the road and the river.


Leave the caves as you found them


Sohoton's chambers hold archaeological material, so do not write on walls or collect anything. Calbiga is protected under the Cave Act, and damage is punishable. Skip flash photography near bats when your guide says so, and carry out every piece of trash.


One Day, Two Days or Both?


With one day from Tacloban, do Sohoton. It costs far less, the logistics are simple, and you can be back in the city for dinner. With two or three days and a head for adventure, book Langun-Gobingob and budget PHP 3,500 for the day tour or PHP 8,000 to PHP 12,000 for a camping trip.


You can fit both into a short trip. Spend the first day on the Sohoton tour and sleep in Basey or Tacloban, then travel to Calbiga for a Discovery tour on the second day.
