Metro Gaisano Bais City: What to Expect at the New Metro Store

Ren Calago 25 Aug 2025 Updated 22 October 2026

Metro opened its new store in Bais City on 15 August 2025, putting a supermarket and a department store under one roof in a city whose shoppers used to head to Dumaguete for anything bigger than a grocery run. The official name is Metro Store and Supermarket Bais, run by Metro Retail Stores Group, the company behind the Metro Gaisano stores. Most people in town simply call it Metro Gaisano, or just Metro.


We visited on a Friday, about a week after the grand opening. It was calmer than opening week, when traffic around the building was heavy, but there was still a steady stream of shoppers, and one of the restaurants was full.


Here is what you will find inside, where the store is, and when to go if you would rather skip the busiest hours.





What's inside the Metro in Bais



Ground floor


The layout is easy to follow. Walk through the main entrance and Rib Shack is on your left. It was packed with diners when we were there. A hardware section is on the right, and the supermarket is straight ahead, with fresh food, groceries and household basics. Food stalls line the side hallways, and Executive Optical has a branch facing Rib Shack, which saves a trip to Dumaguete if you need new glasses.


Upper floor


Upstairs is a wide, open department store floor with clothing, electronics, school supplies and things for the home. The building has an open-ceiling design, and we expected it to feel warm up there. It did not. The air-conditioning keeps the floor cool, so it is a comfortable place to walk around on a hot afternoon.


Metro is not new to Bais. The company opened a hypermarket in the city back in 2016. The 2025 store adds a full department store, though, and that is why many locals see it as the city's first proper mall.


Where it is and how to get there


The store sits along the national highway that runs through Bais. Coming north from Dumaguete, it is on the left side of the road, a few steps from Jollibee. If you are riding a bus or jeepney, tell the conductor you are getting off at Metro, since everyone knows it as a landmark now. Tricycles around the city can drop you right at the entrance.


Parking is in front of the building. It is convenient, but it gets crowded at peak times, so come early or be ready to park a short walk away.


Coming from Dumaguete


Bais is on the main road north of Dumaguete, so buses and vans bound for Bais, Manjuyod or San Carlos all pass the store. Ask the conductor to drop you at Metro instead of the terminal. By car, the drive usually takes about an hour, more if you hit market-day traffic in the towns along the way. A shopping stop fits easily into a day spent around Bais or on the way to the north of the province.





The best time to go


During opening week, traffic officers had to keep cars moving on the road outside, and thanks to them we got through without much of a delay. Things have settled since then, but expect more people on weekends, around paydays on the 15th and 30th, and in the weeks before school opens and before Christmas. Weekday mornings are the quiet window.


Opening hours can change on holidays and during sales, so check Metro Retail's official Facebook page before you drive in from another town.


Why the store matters for Bais


Dumaguete has long been the commercial center of Negros Oriental. For families in Bais, that meant a trip of an hour or more each way for a big grocery run, school clothes or a new appliance. With Metro in town, much of that shopping can now stay local. Banks and restaurants were already gathering around the city center, and the store adds to that pull. Shoppers from neighbouring towns such as Manjuyod and Tanjay also get a closer option.


For visitors, Metro is a practical stop rather than an attraction. It is the place to stock up on water, snacks and sunscreen before a day on the water.


Quick tips for your visit


  • Bring a reusable shopping bag, since a big supermarket run adds up quickly.
  • Eat early. Rib Shack and the food stalls get busy around lunch and dinner.
  • Carry some cash as a backup in case card terminals are slow on a busy day.
  • If you need school supplies in June or gifts in December, shop on a weekday.

Make a day of it in Bais


Bais is better known for its sea life than its shopping. Pair a quick stop at Metro with dolphin watching and the Manjuyod sandbar, and buy food and drinks for the boat before you head to the port. If you are driving back south afterward, Victoria's Mountain View in Tanjay City is a short detour for coffee with a view.


Our advice for a first visit: go on a weekday morning, eat at Rib Shack or the food stalls, and finish your shopping before the afternoon rush.
