Mabinay Travel Guide: Caves, Nature and Things to Do

Mabinay is a quiet town in Negros Oriental, a short ride inland from Dumaguete, and it is best known for its caves. Most visitors pass through on the way to somewhere else, but the caves in the Bulwang area and the larger systems near the town centre are worth a day of your own. If you enjoy exploring underground, or you want a slower alternative to the busy coast, Mabinay is a good place to spend a day or two.


This guide covers the main caves, how to reach them from Dumaguete, what to bring, and a few other places nearby. Caves in the Philippines are not always signposted, and some sites have no posted fees or hours, so check with the local tourism office or a guide before you set out.


Getting to Mabinay


Buses and vans run from Dumaguete to Mabinay. Ask the bus conductor about the current fare and travel time before you set off. Ask the conductor to let you off at the Bulwang turnoff if you want to visit the caves there. From the Mabinay bus terminal, a tricycle takes about twenty minutes to reach the Bulwang area, and the driver will know the cave entrances.


If you are travelling from Dumaguete, plan an early start. Morning is cooler for walking, and the buses are quieter before midday.


Panligawan Cave, the Courting Cave


Panligawan Cave is the easiest cave to start with, and it is a good first experience for anyone who has not explored a cave before. A flight of stone steps leads down to the entrance, and daylight comes in through an opening in the dome-shaped roof. Local stories say that sweethearts once came here to meet, which is where the name Panligawan, or courting cave, comes from.


Wear proper shoes rather than slippers or sandals. The floor can be wet, and a grip will make the descent much safer.


Pandalihan Cave, the Marriage Cave


Pandalihan Cave is another shallow cave in the Bulwang area, with a large open ceiling that lets in plenty of light. It is popular with inexperienced hikers because it does not require special equipment, and it is one of the simplest caves to enter.


Take care in the entrance, since birds nest in the ceiling and the floor can be slippery. Watch out for millipedes as well. Some grow to about 25 centimetres, and when disturbed they can release a purplish liquid that stains the skin, so give them space.


Crystal Cave, the Honeymoon Cave


Crystal Cave is often described as the most beautiful accessible cave in Mabinay for beginners. It has stalactites and stalagmites, and a raised walkway guides you through the main chambers. At around 157 metres long and 12 metres deep, it is big enough to feel like a proper underground journey without being difficult.


Do not touch the formations, since the oils from your skin can damage them. The cave is also home to bats, frogs and small scorpions, so keep your hands to yourself and your eyes open.


Cayaso Cave


Cayaso Cave lies about a kilometre from the town centre, and it is one of the longest cave systems in the country. An underground river runs through the main hall, so expect water on the floor and bring footwear that can get wet. The cave is better suited to visitors who are comfortable with a longer, wetter walk.


Because the cave is large and the river is part of the route, a local guide is a good idea. Ask about the water level before you go, since it changes with the rains.


Odloman Cave, for Experienced Explorers


Odloman Cave is one of the longest cave systems in the archipelago, with large galleries linked by narrow passages. Entry can involve swimming across an underground river or dropping through a hole in the roof, so this is a place for experienced spelunkers and mountaineers. Do not attempt it alone, and go only with a guide who knows the route.


Plan for a full day, and bring a helmet, a strong light, spare batteries and a dry bag for anything you do not want to get wet.


What to Bring for a Day of Caving


Wear long trousers, a long-sleeved shirt and closed hiking shoes. Bring more water than you think you need, along with snacks, since there are few places to buy food near most caves. A torch is essential, even if the guide provides one, because a second light is a sensible backup.


Carry a plastic bag for rubbish, and leave the caves as you found them. Many formations take centuries to grow, and one careless visit can damage them.


Other Things to Do Around Mabinay


Caves are the main attraction, but the countryside around Mabinay is pleasant for a slow day. Walk the rural roads, stop at local food stalls and talk to people about farming and village life. If you have a second day, the town is also a practical base for exploring the inland roads of Negros Oriental.


The Balinsasayao twin lakes are a popular side trip, but they lie in Sibulan rather than in Mabinay itself. Allow an extra hour or two if you want to see them, and check the current entrance fees and boat rates when you arrive.


Practical Tips for Your Visit


Start early and carry cash, since many small stops do not take cards. Let someone know your plans, especially if you are going into a cave system. Ask at the Mabinay bus terminal or the municipal office about current guides, fees and conditions, because they can change from one season to the next.


Caving is possible throughout the year, according to local guides, but river levels change with the rain. Check the weather the day before, and be ready to change your plan if the water looks high.


Who Mabinay Suits


Mabinay suits adventurous travellers, families looking for a gentle first cave, and anyone who prefers quiet towns to busy resorts. Beginners can start with Panligawan or Crystal Cave, while experienced explorers can plan a longer trip to Cayaso or Odloman. If you like a slower pace, a day in Mabinay gives you time to explore without rushing.


Whichever cave you choose, respect the formations and the people who guide you. Caves are fragile, and the local communities who look after them deserve to be part of the story.
