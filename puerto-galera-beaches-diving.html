Puerto Galera Beaches and Diving: White Beach vs Sabang

Ren Calago 20 October 2026

Puerto Galera sits at the northern tip of Oriental Mindoro, a ferry ride across the water from Batangas, and Manila has treated it as the default beach-and-dive weekend for decades. Most visitors arrive with the same question: stay on White Beach or base in Sabang? The answer depends on whether your mornings will be spent on a dive boat or on the sand with a coffee.


The short version is this. White Beach suits sand, sunsets, easy swimming and cheaper meals. Sabang and Small Lalaguna suit divers who want a night dive, an early boat and a dive shop around the corner. The two coves are about 15 minutes apart by tricycle, so the choice is mostly about where you sleep, not what you can do.


Below you will find how the two coves differ, which dive sites are worth the money, how the Batangas ferry route works, what things cost in pesos and which months suit which kind of trip. Prices shift from year to year, so confirm them at the port, the dive shop and your resort before you pay.




White Beach: the easy one


White Beach, in Barangay San Isidro, is about a kilometre of light sand facing southwest. The water is shallow at low tide and outrigger boats sit parked at both ends. Behind the sand runs a row of grill restaurants, budget inns and mid-range resorts. In peak season the music starts around late afternoon, boat sellers pitch island-hopping trips in the morning and the sunset crowd fills the strip. It is not a quiet place. For calmer water, walk toward the western end near Aninuan or book a resort set back from the beach road.


Swim early, before the boats stir up the water. A half-day island-hopping trip to Coral Garden and Bayanan Beach runs about PHP 1,500 to 2,800 per small boat, split among four to six people. Paraw sailing at sunset costs roughly PHP 800 to 1,200 an hour. Serious dive boats do not leave from White Beach itself, so divers staying here take a short ride to Sabang or Muelle. From Balatero Port, White Beach is 10 to 15 minutes by e-trike or habal-habal.


Sabang and Small Lalaguna: the dive hub


Sabang is smaller, tighter and built around diving. The beach is narrow and no good for lounging, but the reef starts close to shore, and nearly every operator runs daily fun dives, night dives and technical dives. Small Lalaguna, just around the point, is a little calmer and holds several of the better dive resorts. Most of the 40-plus named dive sites are 5 to 15 minutes away by outrigger dive boat.


Non-divers are not shut out. Snorkelling at Small Lalaguna works best at high tide, shops sell Discover Scuba introductions, and a joiner snorkel trip to Coral Garden and the giant clams costs about PHP 600 to 1,000 a person. The bars here run late, so light sleepers should ask for a room facing away from the strip.


The dive sites that earn their boat fee


Canyons


This is the advanced site most people come for. Three underwater canyons sit at 25 to 35 metres, with strong current, schools of bannerfish and jacks, and the occasional white-tip reef shark. Dive it around slack tide, ideally on nitrox, and usually as the second or third dive of the day.


Sabang Wrecks


Three small wrecks lie at 18 to 20 metres in front of Sabang Bay. They make a good night dive, with mandarin fish, seahorses, frogfish and nudibranchs, and the depth is within reach of Open Water divers.


Coral Garden


Calm, protected and only 5 to 12 metres deep, this is where check dives and first-timers usually go. Expect brain coral, giant clams and the odd turtle. Snorkellers use it too.


Verde Island


Verde Island, a 45 to 60 minute boat ride east, is a day trip built around a wall that drops past 70 metres. Visibility often reaches 20 to 30 metres and fish life is dense. A trip usually means two or three dives plus lunch on the island, and most operators need at least four divers to run it. The Verde Island Passage is regarded as one of the richest stretches of ocean in the world for marine life, which is the reason this wall is worth the extra cost.


Hole in the Wall, The Hill, Sinandigan Wall and Ernie's Cave round out the list, with drifts and walls at 15 to 28 metres, sea fans, soft corals and plenty of macro life. Plan two days of diving if you can: one around Puerto Galera itself for Canyons and a Sabang Wrecks night dive, and one for Verde Island if the weather allows. Getting to Puerto Galera via Batangas


No airport serves the town, and the only practical route is by sea from Batangas. From Cubao or PITX in Manila, take a JAM Liner, JAC Liner or Ceres bus to the Batangas Grand Terminal, which takes two to three hours and costs about PHP 250 to 350. A jeepney or tricycle then covers the 10 minutes to the port for PHP 30 to 50. Bus-and-ferry combo tickets from Manila run about PHP 800 to 1,200.


Galerian, Island Water and Montenegro Lines sail from Batangas Port. Fastcraft take 1 to 1.5 hours and cost about PHP 500 to 700 one way, and RORO ferries take 1.5 to 2 hours at about PHP 400 to 500. Expect a terminal fee of PHP 30 to 100. Boats land at Balatero Port or Muelle Port, not at White Beach or Sabang, so anyone selling you a ticket that says otherwise is selling a transfer or a charter. From the port, a tricycle costs about PHP 150 to 300 to White Beach and PHP 250 to 400 to Sabang, with a PHP 50 to 100 surcharge after 6 PM. Some resorts send a pickup if you message your arrival time.


The first boats leave around 6:30 to 8:00 AM and the last around 5:30 to 6:00 PM. Afternoon seas are rougher, so take a morning boat. Visitors flying in from the Visayas or Mindanao land in Manila first. If your flight arrives after 2 PM, sleep in Manila or Batangas City and sail the next morning. What it costs in pesos


Tourists pay an environmental user fee at the port, listed at about PHP 100 to 120 a person, with lower rates for Mindoro residents. Keep the receipt, because some checkpoints ask for it. Some beaches charge a small snorkelling or barangay fee of PHP 20 to 50, and some dive shops add a marine park fee of PHP 100 to 150 a day when the boat visits a protected area.


A single fun dive with guide, tank, weights and boat costs about PHP 1,800 to 2,800. A five-dive package runs PHP 7,500 to 12,000 and ten dives cost PHP 14,000 to 22,000. Full gear rental is PHP 500 to 900 a day, and often free once you book five or more dives. A night dive adds PHP 300 to 800, and nitrox is PHP 300 to 500 a tank. The Verde Island day trip is PHP 4,500 to 7,500 a person. An Open Water course takes about three days at PHP 18,000 to 25,000, and a Discover Scuba introduction costs PHP 3,000 to 4,500.


A budget fan room on White Beach costs about PHP 1,200 to 2,500 a night. A beachfront air-conditioned room is PHP 3,500 to 7,000. Sabang and Small Lalaguna dive resorts charge PHP 3,000 to 8,500 with breakfast, and many sell room-and-dive packages. Carinderia meals cost PHP 150 to 300, and a dish at a beach restaurant is PHP 350 to 650.


Best months for beaches and diving


November to May is the best stretch overall. The northeast monsoon, locally called Amihan, brings sunny days and calm seas from December to April. December to February is the coolest and driest, around 24 to 30°C, and gets very busy over Christmas, New Year and Chinese New Year. March to May is hotter at 28 to 33°C and gives the calmest crossings to Verde Island. Holy Week is extremely crowded, with higher rates and full ferries.


June to September brings the Habagat, with afternoon downpours, choppier seas and the occasional cancelled ferry. Diving still runs, crowds thin out and the water is warm. Visibility is good all year and tends to peak from October to June. October and November are a decent shoulder period with fewer people and better room rates.


Tips before you go


Bring cash


ATMs in White Beach and Sabang exist but run out, and some charge a PHP 250 withdrawal fee. Ports, bars and dive shops prefer cash, and fees at the terminal are paid in cash even when your ferry was booked online.


Book Verde Island early in your stay


The trip needs four divers and calm weather. Put it on your second day, not your fifth, so a cancellation leaves room to try again. Ask what the price includes: boat, guide, tanks, weights, lunch and fees.


Plan for current


Canyons and several walls run with current. Bring or rent a dive computer and a surface marker buoy, and listen closely to the drift briefing. Skip diving with a cold, since boat rides are short and clinics are basic. Keep your hands off the coral and the shells on the beach, and follow lifeguard flags at White Beach.


So, White Beach or Sabang?


With three days and a beach-first plan, stay in White Beach, ride over to Sabang once for a fun dive or a Coral Garden snorkel, and keep one morning for island hopping. With four or five days and diving as the point, base in Sabang or Small Lalaguna, dive Canyons, Hole in the Wall and the Sabang Wrecks at night, and add Verde Island on the calmest day.


The trip takes a bus and a ferry on top of any flight into Manila, so it rewards a longer stay more than a quick hop. Travel between November and May, carry cash, book your first two dive days ahead and catch a morning boat.
