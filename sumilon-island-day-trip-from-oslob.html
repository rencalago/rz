Sumilon Island Day Trip from Oslob: Boats, Fees and What to Expect

Ren Calago 14 October 2026

Sumilon Island sits just offshore from Oslob, close enough that many visitors reach it in a single boat ride. It is a marine sanctuary, which is one reason the water looks as clear as it does and the reefs are still in good shape. For a day out from the whale shark coast, it offers a quieter contrast: a sandbar, some snorkelling and a slow afternoon in the shade.


This guide covers how the boats work, the fees that are listed, what to expect on the sandbar and reef, and the practical things to check before you book. Prices and boat times on this route vary, so treat the figures here as a starting point and confirm them with the boat operator at the port.


The island is small, and most of the day is spent on the water or on the sand. Plan around the tide and the sea conditions rather than around a fixed programme.



Getting to Sumilon from Oslob


Boats leave from the mainland at Bancogon in Oslob. Departures are listed at 8:00, 10:00, 12:00 and 2:00, with return boats leaving the island at 9:00, 11:00, 1:00 and 3:00. Treat those times as a guide, and ask the port office for the current schedule before you plan the day.


The crossing is short. The crossing takes about 20 minutes, though the actual time depends on the boat, the sea and how full it is. Boats are usually shared, and a shared banka is the cheaper choice over a private boat.


Oslob is also the place to base the trip if you are visiting the town's whale shark area. Our whale shark watching guide for Oslob covers that side of the day, and the island works well as a second activity on the same visit.


Fees and What the Day Costs


Boat fares are quoted at ₱1,500 per boat, plus a ₱50 environmental fee per person. A swim or snorkel package is quoted at an extra ₱2,200, and it is unclear whether that is per person or per boat. You may hear of a much lower solo fare, around ₱500, but that is not a published rate, so do not plan around it.


An entrance fee of ₱50 is paid on arrival. It may be the same as the environmental fee, so ask whether you will pay it once or twice. Keep cash in small notes, since the port and the island may not take cards.


If you prefer one fixed price, the resort on the island sells day passes that include boat transfers, lunch and snorkel gear. Some day passes include drinks and lunch. Compare it with a shared boat and buying your own lunch before you choose.


The Sandbar and the Reef


The sandbar is the island's best-known spot. Its shape changes with the season and the tide, so the sandbar you see at high tide may be much smaller than the one at low tide. Tide times vary by day, so check the tide table on the day and ask the boatman when the sand is likely to be most exposed.


Snorkelling is the other main draw. Four dive sites are known around the island, including Garden Eel Plaza and Julie's Rock. Water conditions change, so bring a rash guard and a reef-safe sunscreen, and ask the guide where the current is strongest before you go in.


Sumilon has been a marine sanctuary since 1974 and is also a marine protected area. Please follow any instructions from the boatman and the resort staff. Do not touch or stand on the coral, and take your rubbish back on the boat.


Staying on the Island


Most day visitors come for the afternoon and go back on the boat. If you want to stay overnight, the resort offers cottages, villas and glamping tents. Rates are not listed here, so check the rate directly with the resort before you book.


Plan for lunch and drinks, and check the toilets and shade in the resort's listing before you travel.


When to Go and What to Bring


The dry months from January to April are the best time. October to December brings moderate rain, and July to September is typhoon season. The sea can be rough on any day, so check the forecast the night before and be ready to change the plan.


Pack a dry bag for your phone and cash, a hat, sunscreen, water and a light towel. Wear shoes that can get wet, since the boat landing and the sand can be hot and sharp. If you are not confident in the water, a life vest is a sensible request at the port.

Who This Day Suits


Sumilon suits people who want a sandbar, a short boat ride and some snorkelling, without a long walk or a busy schedule. It is a good choice for families and for anyone who has already seen the whale sharks and wants a calmer afternoon. It is less suitable if you are prone to seasickness or dislike small boats in choppy water.
