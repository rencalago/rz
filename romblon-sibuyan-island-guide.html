Romblon and Sibuyan Guide: Marble, G2 and Cresta de Gallo

Ren Calago 21 October 2026

Romblon Province is a cluster of islands that have little in common. Romblon Island is a small harbour town with marble workshops and two old Spanish forts above the water. Sibuyan is mostly forest, with clear rivers and Mount Guiting-Guiting, a jagged 2,058-metre peak that sits among the hardest climbs in the country. Cresta de Gallo, a tiny islet off Sibuyan, has a long white sandbar and very little else.


This guide covers what each stop is like, the two realistic ways in, costs in pesos, the rules for climbing Guiting-Guiting and the months worth aiming for. The trip is slow to reach, and that is the main reason these islands are still quiet.


The short version: Cebu has no direct ferry to Romblon, so you connect through Manila, either on the overnight ferry out of Batangas or on a flight to Tablas. Plan four or five days and keep a spare one, because the sea decides the schedule more than the timetable does.




Romblon Island: Marble, Forts and Quiet Beaches


Romblon town wraps around a sheltered harbour, with Fort San Andres and Fort Santiago on the hills either side. The centre is small enough to walk. There is a 17th-century cathedral, a public market and a run of workshops where marble is cut, polished and carved by hand.


The marble is the reason to come. Blocks are white, grey and beige, with black and green pieces the rarer finds. Ask before you walk into a quarry or a workshop, then watch the carvers work. Small items are easy to take home: a mortar and pestle is around PHP 250 to PHP 600, and a 12 by 18 inch tile roughly PHP 800 to PHP 1,200. Sculptures cost more.


Climb to Fort San Andres for sunset. There is no entrance fee and the uphill walk takes about ten minutes. For a swim, Tiamban Beach is the easier one, while Bonbon Beach has a sandbar that stretches out at low tide. Divers will find a macro scene with the small critters Anilao is known for, and local shops charge about PHP 1,800 to PHP 2,500 a dive with gear. If you want the Batangas side of that story, read about diving in Anilao.


Getting around is cheap. A tricycle in town is PHP 20 to PHP 50 a person, a rented motorbike PHP 500 to PHP 700 a day, and a half-day tricycle tour of the beaches and marble shops about PHP 600 to PHP 1,000.


Sibuyan Island and Mount Guiting-Guiting


Sibuyan is often called the Galapagos of Asia. A large share of its forest is old growth, many of its plants and animals live nowhere else, and the rivers run clean enough that people swim in them without a second thought. The summit that dominates the island is Guiting-Guiting, usually shortened to G2.


Rules and Fees for the Climb


The jump-off is Barangay Tampayan in Magdiwang. Register at the DENR office in Magdiwang before you start, and expect a briefing. A guide is required, with one guide for every five climbers, and solo climbs are not allowed.


Budget roughly PHP 3,000 to PHP 4,000 for a guide and a similar amount for a porter. The environmental fee has been PHP 500 for Filipinos and PHP 1,000 for foreigners, with a small barangay tourism fee, port fee and climb certificate on top. Ask the DENR office whether a medical certificate is required for your dates, since requirements have changed from year to year.


This is not a first mountain. The ridge has exposed scrambling and sections where you rappel, and the weather can turn within an hour. Bring your own tent and cook set, around three litres of water a day, gloves and shoes with real grip. After rain the trail has leeches, so pack salt and gaiters. The mountain is normally closed for rehabilitation from about November to January, sometimes into February, so check the current advisory before you book any ferry. If you are building toward it, the Mount Pulag permits guide is a gentler place to practise the paperwork.


If You Are Not Climbing


There is plenty to do at ground level. Cantingas River is a good place to cool off, Cawa-cawa Falls and Lambingan Falls are short outings, and river swimming around Magdiwang costs little or nothing. Where a fee exists it is usually PHP 20 to PHP 50.


Cresta de Gallo: A Sandbar With No Resort


Cresta de Gallo sits off San Fernando on Sibuyan. There is a caretaker hut, coconut palms and no electricity. The reason to visit is the white sandbar along one side and the reef edge on the other, which you can walk in about twenty minutes and snorkel in an afternoon.


Boats leave from Azagra Port in San Fernando, a short tricycle ride from town (PHP 50 to PHP 100). A boat for four to six people costs about PHP 2,500 to PHP 3,500 for a day trip and PHP 3,000 to PHP 4,500 if you stay overnight, with the crossing taking 45 minutes to an hour and a half depending on the sea. Add an entrance or environmental fee of PHP 20 to PHP 100 a person. A homestay in Azagra can usually arrange the boat and the contact on the island.


Bring your own food and water. A small grilling fee applies if you cook, and camping is possible for a modest charge if you carry a tent. There are no bins, so everything you bring goes back to Azagra with you. Pair it with a Sibuyan climb as the reward day, or keep it as the one lazy day of the trip.


How to Get to Romblon and Sibuyan


Route A: Fly to Manila, Then the Batangas Night Ferry


This is the usual way for travellers coming through the Visayas and Mindanao. Cebu to Manila flights take about an hour and a half, with promo fares around PHP 1,200 to PHP 2,000 one way and regular fares PHP 2,500 to PHP 4,500. The 2GO ferry on the same route takes about 20 hours if you like travelling slowly.


From Manila, take a bus from Buendia, Cubao or PITX to Batangas Pier. Buses run every half hour or so, take two to three hours and cost PHP 250 to PHP 350. Starlite Ferries then sails in the late afternoon, usually 4:00 to 5:00 PM, reaching Romblon town in the small hours and continuing to Magdiwang on Sibuyan by morning. Montenegro Lines runs a separate evening sailing on some days.


Fares are roughly PHP 850 to PHP 1,000 in tourist class, PHP 1,000 to PHP 1,300 for an economy bed and PHP 2,500 to PHP 3,500 for a four-person cabin, plus a terminal fee. Montenegro also runs Batangas to Odiongan on Tablas, around PHP 816 to PHP 1,632 depending on class. Book online a few days ahead, then confirm at the port, because Fridays, Holy Week and December sell out.


Route B: Fly to Tablas


Tugdan Airport in Alcantara is the air gateway. Cebu Pacific has flown Manila to Tablas daily in recent seasons, and Sunlight Air has flown Clark to Tablas on Tuesdays and Saturdays with promo fares from about PHP 1,899 one way. Cebu has no direct flight to Tablas, so you connect, and schedules in this part of the country change, so check the airline before you commit.


From the airport, a van or jeep to Odiongan Port takes about an hour and costs PHP 100 to PHP 150. A RoRo then crosses to Romblon Island in three to four hours for PHP 500 to PHP 800. Small passenger boats link Romblon and Sibuyan, and some ferries reach Sibuyan through Odiongan.


Which Route to Choose


If time is tight and you can book early, fly to Tablas. If money matters more, take the Batangas night ferry: it replaces a hotel night, and you wake up at the destination. For a wider look at that trade-off, the ferry versus flight budget guide lays out the numbers.


Best Months and Weather


December to May is the drier stretch, and March and April are the driest and sunniest, which suits Cresta de Gallo and beach days. Between December and February the northeast monsoon (Amihan) makes the crossings rough, especially around Tablas Strait, so build slack into your dates. For G2, February to May gives the steadiest weather, while July to September is wet and slippery.


The southwest monsoon (Habagat) brings heavier rain from June to October, yet diving around Romblon is often good from March to October. If you are hopping between Romblon, Tablas and Sibuyan, avoid September to November, when rain peaks.


What a Trip Costs


These are per-person planning figures. Confirm them before you book.


  • Cebu to Manila return flight: PHP 2,500 to PHP 7,000 when booked three to four weeks ahead
  • Manila to Batangas bus, return: PHP 500 to PHP 700
  • Batangas to Romblon and Sibuyan ferry, return, tourist class: PHP 1,700 to PHP 3,000
  • Manila to Tablas flight, return, as the alternative: PHP 3,500 to PHP 6,500
  • Transfers around Tablas by van and ferry: PHP 1,000 to PHP 2,000
  • Rooms: PHP 800 to PHP 1,500 a night for a basic guesthouse, PHP 1,500 to PHP 3,500 for mid-range, PHP 3,500 to PHP 6,500 for boutique
  • Meals: PHP 150 to PHP 350 at a carinderia, PHP 300 to PHP 600 at a restaurant
  • Cresta de Gallo boat split four ways: PHP 625 to PHP 875 each
  • Guiting-Guiting arranged yourself: PHP 4,500 to PHP 6,500 plus transport, or PHP 6,500 to PHP 9,000 with an operator that includes the ferry, guides and food

For four days on Romblon Island with a Cresta de Gallo day trip, expect PHP 12,000 to PHP 18,000 a person on the ferry route, not counting the Cebu to Manila flight, or PHP 18,000 to PHP 28,000 if you fly to Tablas. Add PHP 3,000 to PHP 4,000 for G2. Seafood is fresh and usually cheaper than in Cebu City.


Where to Stay


On Romblon Island, most first-timers stay in or near the town, close to the port and the marble shops. Guesthouses and small resorts around Bonbon cost a little more and put you nearer the sand. Book ahead if you are travelling during the Biniray Festival in January.


If you fly into Tablas, sleep in Odiongan before taking the morning boat. On Sibuyan, Magdiwang is the practical base for G2 and the Cantingas River, and San Fernando works for Cresta de Gallo. Homestays in Magdiwang run about PHP 800 to PHP 1,200, some with a kitchen. Power can cut out away from the town centres, so carry a power bank.


Tips Before You Go


Book Weekend Ferries Early


The Batangas to Romblon sailing sells out on Fridays and before long weekends. If you miss it, sleep in Batangas City, where rooms near the pier cost PHP 1,000 to PHP 1,800.


Carry Cash


ATMs in Romblon town and Odiongan exist but are often offline, and Magdiwang has one. Withdraw in Manila or Cebu. Marble shops mostly prefer cash, though some accept GCash, and Cresta de Gallo has no signal at all.


Ship Heavy Marble Home


Shops can send large tiles and sculptures by RoRo to Manila or Cebu. Ask for a wooden crate, about PHP 300 to PHP 600. Small pieces fit in hand luggage, but check the airline's weight limit first.


Download Maps Before You Land


Globe and Smart work in the towns and drop out between them. Save an offline map of the province, and note that tricycle drivers tend to know the marble shops by the owner's name rather than a street.


Is Romblon and Sibuyan Worth the Extra Leg?


For someone who has already done Boracay, Palawan and Siargao, yes, because it feels like a different kind of trip. Romblon Island is for slow days and talking to carvers. Sibuyan is for rivers, forest and one serious summit. Cresta de Gallo is the quiet sandbar with no music playing.


Aim for March to May if you want calm seas and good hiking weather. Take the Batangas ferry to save money, or fly to Tablas to save time, and leave a buffer day either way.
