Cebu Ocean Park Guide: Tickets, Hours and Show Times

Ren Calago 8 Mar 2023 Updated 22 October 20260

Cebu Ocean Park is an oceanarium in the South Road Properties (SRP) in Cebu City, at the back of SM Seaside City Cebu and beside the San Pedro Calungsod Chapel. It is open daily from 10 AM to 6 PM, with the last ticket sold at 5 PM, and a visit takes two to three hours if you stay for the feedings and shows. This guide covers how to get there, what a ticket costs, when the shows run, which add-ons are worth your money and when to go. The new SM Seaside Cebu Arena is in the same area, so the park pairs well with a concert or show there.


How do you get to Cebu Ocean Park?


From anywhere in Metro Cebu, a taxi or Grab car will take you straight to SM Seaside City Cebu. If you would rather commute, take the MyBus to SM Seaside and exit through the mountain wing; the park is about three minutes on foot from there. Coming from Mactan takes longer because you cross the Cebu-Cordova Link Expressway and then deal with SRP traffic, so leave extra time on weekends.


Drivers can park at the mall. Parking costs little for the first few hours, but the lot can fill up on busy weekends, so arrive early and check the posted rate at the entrance.


How much are the tickets, and what are the opening hours?


The park lists online rates of PHP 750 on weekdays and PHP 900 on weekends and holidays. Tickets bought at the gate usually cost more than the online promo price, so booking ahead on a platform such as Klook saves money, which adds up fast for a family. Senior citizens and persons with disabilities get a discount on the regular rate, and very young children enter free. The park sets the exact age or height cutoff, so confirm it at the counter.


Rates change with promos and holiday seasons, so look at the park's official website or its Facebook page (cebuoceanparkofficial) before you go. The doors open at 10 AM and close at 6 PM every day, but the last ticket is sold at 5 PM, which leaves too little time for the shows. Plan to be inside by late morning at the latest.


What time are the feedings and shows?


The park's website lists three daily slots for its main programs: the main tank feeding at 11 AM, 1 PM and 3 PM, the amphitheatre bird show at the same hours, and the fur seal show at 12 PM, 2 PM and 4 PM. Schedules can shift with the day and the season, so check the board at the entrance and plan your route around it.


A simple order for the day is to walk the oceanarium first, catch the 11 AM main tank feeding and the noon fur seal show, then break for lunch and take the 1 PM bird show. That leaves the busiest stretch of the day for sitting down instead of queueing.


What is inside the park?


The centerpiece is the oceanarium, where a walk-through tunnel takes you under rays, sharks and reef fish. Beyond the big tank, the park has a Jungle Trek with reptiles such as iguanas, a Creepy Critters section with tarantulas and snakes, and touch pools. The park opened in 2019 as an affiliate of Manila Ocean Park.


Here's a small detail worth your time: every tank has a sign with the animal's name and a short description. Slow down and read them. You will learn more than you would by just looking at the glass.


Don't miss the main tank feeding


For me, this is the highlight of the whole park. The feeding area is a good spot to rest your legs between exhibits, and you can grab a bite while enormous fish are fed by mouth. Sitting still and watching those wide-mouthed sea giants chew through their treats is oddly relaxing. Get there 10 to 15 minutes early if you want a place near the glass.


Where to take the best photos


My personal favorite spot is the arched glass aquarium. Glass and low light are hard on phone cameras, so wipe your lens, hold it close to the glass without pressing on it, and check the park's rules on flash before you shoot.


Add-on experiences: fish spa, stingrays and water encounters


Several activities cost extra on top of the entrance ticket. The park lists a fish spa, lory feeding, a stingray encounter, a crocodile cage, SeaTREK (an underwater walk in the main tank) and Aqua Dining, an underwater meal that needs a reservation. Each one is priced separately and prices change, so ask at the counter and compare the total against your budget before you commit.


During our visit in April 2022, only a few people signed up for the close-up water encounter. Those who did got to swim around the colorful fish up close, which is a cool way to see them.


The fish spa is the one add-on I would also recommend. I tried the same kind of spa at Manila Ocean Park in 2008, and the painless tickle of the fish nibbling at my feet was fun and relaxing. I didn't try it here, but it is worth a few minutes.


Tips before you go


A weekday morning is the easiest time to visit: online prices are lower than on weekends and the crowds around the tanks are thinner. Weekends and school holidays get busy, especially around feeding times. Wear comfortable shoes, since you will be walking and standing for a couple of hours, and plan a proper meal at SM Seaside next door before or after your visit.


If the park is one stop in a longer trip, the Cebu 3-day itinerary covers the rest of the city. Families who want more animals can also compare it with Cebu Safari and Adventure Park in Carmen, which is a much longer drive north.


Is Cebu Ocean Park worth it?


It depends on who is going. For families with young children, it is one of the easiest hot-afternoon or rainy-day plans in Cebu City: everything is in one place, close to a mall, and the shows give the day some structure. For travelers on a tight schedule or budget, the entrance fee plus add-ons adds up quickly, and a few hours may be better spent elsewhere. Weekends are the least comfortable time to go, so pick a weekday if you can. Whichever day you choose, check the current ticket price and show times on the park's official page first.
