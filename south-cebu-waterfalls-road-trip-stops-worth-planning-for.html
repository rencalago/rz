South Cebu Waterfalls Road Trip: Stops Worth Planning For

South Cebu is full of waterfalls, and many of the best ones sit a long way from the main highway. Some are reached by a short walk from the road, while others need a motorbike ride, a steep trail or a local guide. Put together, they make a road trip that feels like a proper adventure rather than a single day out.


This guide follows the usual route from Cebu City down the southwest coast, with five stops worth planning around. Fees, transport prices and rules change, so treat the figures below as a starting point and check each site on the day you go.


Getting Started: The Route South


Most road trips begin at the Cebu South Bus Terminal, where air-conditioned buses run to Moalboal in about three to four hours. From there, tricycles and motorbikes reach the first falls in under an hour. Plan the trip as a loop, not a single line, so you can see the coast on the way down and the mountain roads on the way back.


The roads are steady rather than difficult, but some of the last stretches to the falls are rough. Hire a motorbike with a driver you trust, or agree on a tricycle price before you set off, so you are not negotiating at the trailhead.


1. Kawasan Falls, Badian


Kawasan Falls is the most famous stop on the route, and it is the one most visitors plan around. The falls are reached by a short walk from the car park, and the entrance fee is small. The main experience is canyoneering: you hike up the river, scramble over rocks and jump from limestone ledges into the blue water below.


Helmets and life vests are required, and a guided tour is the usual way to do it. Guided packages are commonly quoted around 2,000 to 2,100 pesos for a half-day trip, but check the current price before you go. Go in the early morning, when the water is calm and the crowds have not arrived yet.


2. Inambakan Falls, Ginatilan


Inambakan Falls is a quieter stop, tucked into the highlands above Ginatilan. A short motorbike ride from the town takes you to the jump-off point, and a set of moss-slicked concrete stairs leads down to the main drop. The waterfall falls about 100 feet into a deep, opaque basin, and it looks very different in the morning light.


Take care on the stairs, since they can be slippery after rain. Wear shoes with grip, not sandals, and keep your phone in a dry bag. Ask the locals about the lower pools if you want a swim.


3. Aguinid Falls, Samboan


Aguinid Falls is the adventure stop of the route. Here you climb the waterfall's limestone walls using ropes and footholds, with the milky white water running beside you. It is one of the most memorable climbs in the province, but it is not something to attempt alone.


A local guide is mandatory, and the guides are usually paid on a tip basis. Bring water, wear a shirt you don't mind getting wet and leave valuables at your accommodation.


4. Dao Falls, Samboan


Dao Falls is a narrow, towering gorge that opens onto a curved rock face pouring into a deep turquoise basin. The walk to it takes about 30 minutes along a path that crosses a steel bridge, and the last stretch can be slippery after rain. Once you arrive, the scale of the place is striking, and it is often quiet in the morning.


Go with a local guide if you are unsure of the route, and wear shoes you can walk in for the wade through the gorge.


5. Binalayan Falls, Bonbon, Samboan


Binalayan Falls is the easiest stop to fit in before the drive back. It is a two-layer falls, with a three-column cascade of about 30 feet, and it is sometimes called Triple-Drop Falls. The jump-off point is close to the main road, and the walk is short, which makes it a good finish to a long day.


Because it is so easy to reach, it can get busy in the afternoon. Aim for the morning if you want the pools to yourself.


Planning Your Road Trip


Start early. Most of the falls are best seen before the crowds arrive and the midday heat sets in. Pack more water than you think you need, plus sunscreen, a hat and a dry bag for your phone and cash. Carry small bills, since entrance fees and tricycle rides are usually paid in cash.


Plan two stops a day rather than all five. The walks, the rides and the swims take longer than they look on a map, and the drive between the sites is slower than the distance suggests. If you are staying in Moalboal, one day can cover Kawasan and Inambakan, with Aguinid, Dao and Binalayan saved for a second day in Samboan.


Who This Road Trip Suits


This route suits travellers who enjoy a bit of adventure, including canyoneering, climbing and gentle hikes. Confident swimmers and photographers will find plenty to enjoy, and families with older children can manage the easier stops. If you prefer calm, comfortable days, pick one or two falls and spend the rest of your time at the beach.


Whichever stops you choose, respect the water, follow the guides and leave each site as you found it. Waterfalls stay beautiful when visitors look after them.
