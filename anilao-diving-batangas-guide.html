Anilao Diving, Batangas: Costs, Seasons and Getting There

Ren Calago 20 October 2026

Anilao is not a beach holiday with a dive shop attached. It is a rocky stretch of coast in Mabini, Batangas, roughly 130 kilometers from Manila's airport, and nearly everything on it, from the resorts to the boats to the tricycle rides, exists for divers. It is often called the birthplace of Philippine scuba diving, and the reputation rests on what lives in the sand and rubble rather than on big reef walls.


Think small. More than 560 nudibranch species have been recorded in the area, and the dive sites are known for pygmy seahorses, frogfish, flamboyant cuttlefish and the occasional mimic octopus. Dives are slow, fairly shallow and guide-led, often an hour spent over what looks like bare sand until someone points out an animal the size of a fingernail. If your idea of a great dive is sharks and walls of fish, Anilao will feel quiet. If you like looking closely, few places in Luzon match it.


Below: what a dive weekend costs in pesos, the best months, how to get there by bus or private van, and a few details that save money or trouble.




What you actually dive: muck, macro and blackwater


Most sites are a 10 to 25 minute boat ride from the resorts. Secret Bay is the popular muck site and rewards patience. Twin Rocks works for both macro and wider scenes, Kirby's Rock has a wall, Sombrero Island is known for soft corals, and Beatrice gets stronger currents and swarms of anthias. The pier and several house reefs are used for night dives. Visibility usually sits around 12 to 15 meters, and surge at some sites makes photographing tiny animals harder than it looks.


Blackwater dives are the odd one out. You drift at night over deep water on a lit line while larval fish, jellies and other plankton animals rise toward the light. Resorts sell it as a surcharged night dive, which matters for your budget. A typical resort day has two morning dives, a break and two late-afternoon dives, often one at night, on boats carrying about four divers plus crew.


It is not all postcard material. Mabini has struggled with waste and plastic washing up, popular sites can have more boats than mooring buoys, and anchors damage coral when that happens. Stonefish also live in shallow water, so keep your hands to yourself.


Best months to dive Anilao


The main season runs from October to early June. November, December, April and May are usually the best months, though April and May are also the busiest, so book weekends and holidays early. January to March water cools to around 25°C compared with a typical 27.5°C, which feels chilly after an hour of hovering, but critters stay active and many macro divers pick these months anyway. July and August bring the monsoon. Sites stay reachable most days, but typhoon weeks can cancel boats, and some resorts discount rooms by around 30 percent in the July to September low season.


What a dive weekend costs


Per-dive prices with guide, boat, tank and weights run about PHP 2,000 to 2,500 per person for a small group. Night dives cost PHP 2,300 to 3,000 and blackwater about PHP 3,500 at one operator, with a minimum of two divers. A solo diver can pay as much as PHP 3,500 for an ordinary dive, because the boat cost is not shared. Full gear rental is roughly PHP 1,200 to 1,500 a day, a dive computer around PHP 500 and a torch PHP 200.


Rooms range from about PHP 2,500 for a basic one to more than PHP 11,000 for a deluxe room, with mid-range rooms around PHP 4,000 to 5,750 including breakfast for two. Outside the resorts there is little to eat or do, so ask what meals cost before you book. A marine or environmental fee of PHP 150 to 300 per diver per day is charged on top at many resorts, so ask if the quote already includes it, and if an annual pass makes sense for a long stay.


As a rough estimate, two nights with six boat dives and public transport from Manila comes to about PHP 18,000 to 27,000 per person before meals, gear rental and flights. Rate cards change each season, and some carry a temporary fuel surcharge, so get the current one in writing. Group size also changes the price. At one resort the blackwater surcharge falls from roughly USD 140 for a lone diver to USD 30 each for a group of four to six, so travelling with friends pays off.


How to get to Anilao from Manila


Plan on 2.5 to 3 hours by road in normal traffic. Fridays and pre-holiday days can stretch it to four hours, and flights landing after 6 PM hit the evening rush through southern Metro Manila.


The cheapest route is by bus. Buses from Buendia in Pasay, Cubao or PITX in Parañaque run to Batangas City Grand Terminal, operated by lines such as JAM Liner, ALPS, DLTB and Ceres. Fares run about PHP 180 to 275 for a ride of 2.5 to 3.5 hours. At the terminal, take a jeepney signed Mabini or Anilao for roughly PHP 40 to 60 and 40 to 60 minutes, then a tricycle to your resort. Agree on the tricycle fare before you set off. One way, that comes to about PHP 220 to 340 per person before the tricycle. Withdraw cash in Batangas City first, because ATMs are scarce along the coast and the nearest one is in Mabini.


Resort vans cost more but save time: one resort charges PHP 7,000 to 8,000 per van of up to six from NAIA or a Manila hotel, and PHP 3,000 from Batangas terminal. Split six ways, that is a little over PHP 1,000 each.


Coming from Cebu


There is no direct link, so it is a flight to Manila followed by the road. If you land in the evening, spend the night near Buendia or PITX instead of chasing a late bus. The ferry versus flight budget guide covers the Cebu to Manila leg. If a long haul is more than you want, Malapascua's thresher shark dives offer something different closer to home.


Before you book


Mind your flight home. DAN's guidance is a minimum 12-hour surface interval before flying after a single no-decompression dive, and at least 18 hours after several days of repetitive diving. Schedule your last dive accordingly and check your computer's no-fly time.


Taal Volcano sits in the same province, and a restless week can disrupt roads and plans, so check the PHIVOLCS bulletin before travel. The Tagaytay and Taal day trip guide explains the current rules around the volcano. Bring a torch even for day dives, keep your fins off the sand, and let the guide point out animals instead of moving them for a photo.


Is Anilao worth the trip?


Yes, if you want patient, detailed diving and can tolerate resort-only logistics. Come for two full dive days rather than one, bring a few friends to share boat and blackwater costs, and aim for a weekday outside April and May. Skip it if you want long swims over open reef, because Anilao's best moments happen within an arm's length of the sand.
