ASEAN Summit 2026 Manila: Road Closures and Holidays

Ren Calago 27 October 20260

The 49th ASEAN Summit and Related Summits run from November 16 to 18, 2026, with the Philippine International Convention Center in Pasay City as the main venue. Malacañang has made those three days, Monday to Wednesday, special non-working days across Metro Manila, and roads around the PICC and the Cultural Center of the Philippines are already partly closed. They close fully from November 9 to 20.


If you live in the capital, that means a surprise long weekend running from Saturday, November 14, to Wednesday, November 18. If you are flying in or out, staying near Manila Bay, or simply crossing the city that week, it means slower roads, tighter security and a few airport headaches. This guide sets out what has been announced so far, who it affects, and how to plan around it.


The Philippines chairs ASEAN this year. The 48th summit was held at Mactan in Lapu-Lapu City back in May, and the November meeting in Manila is the bigger one, bringing together Southeast Asian heads of government along with leaders from partner countries. That guest list explains the closed boulevards and the motorcade lanes.




The Dates at a Glance


  • October 24 to November 9: partial road closures around the PICC and CCP in Pasay
  • October 24 to November 20: no permits for fun runs, motorcades, processions and similar public events in Manila, Pasay, Parañaque, Makati and Taguig
  • November 9 to 20: full closure of the affected Pasay roads
  • November 16 to 18: the summit itself, and special non-working days in Metro Manila

Those are the windows announced so far. Security plans for an event this size tend to grow as the date gets closer, so check the advisories again in the week before you travel.


Which Roads Are Closed in Pasay


The Pasay City government has named the stretches to avoid: Macapagal Boulevard, Diokno Boulevard, the World Trade Center area and parts of Roxas Boulevard near the PICC and CCP complexes. People who work inside the restricted zone can get through by showing a valid company ID, though guards can still stop and check anyone.


Some designated bus lanes are also being turned into ASEAN lanes, kept clear for official vehicles and delegations. For ordinary drivers and bus passengers that means one lane fewer and longer waits, especially when a convoy needs the road. A motorcade can freeze an intersection for several minutes while it passes.


The city has not published a full list of detours. Its Traffic and Parking Management Office posts updates on Facebook, and Waze or Google Maps tend to pick up closures quickly once barriers go up. If your usual route crosses the bay area, plan for the slowest version of it.


The November 16 to 18 Holiday


Proclamation No. 1447 declares November 16, 17 and 18 special non-working days in the National Capital Region only. The stated reasons are to ease traffic and to make it easier to keep summit participants safe. Outside Metro Manila those are ordinary working days, which matters if your office is in the capital but you live in Cavite, Laguna or Bulacan.


For pay, the Department of Labor and Employment has applied the usual special-day rules in Labor Advisory No. 16, series of 2026. If you do not work, the default is no work, no pay, unless your company policy or union agreement says otherwise. If you do work, you get an extra 30 percent of your basic wage for the first eight hours. If the day is also your rest day and you work it, the extra is 50 percent. Overtime on these days earns another 30 percent of the hourly rate.


Flying In or Out That Week


The Civil Aeronautics Board has told airlines to expect possible changes to schedules and airport operations during the summit. It has asked carriers to keep changes to a minimum, warn passengers early through every channel they have, and give everything owed under the Air Passenger Bill of Rights. If you are booked for mid-November, look at your booking now instead of the night before.


A few habits help:


  • Make sure your phone number and email on the booking are current, so a schedule change actually reaches you.
  • If your flight is moved or cancelled, ask about free rebooking or a refund right away, before the next available seats are gone.
  • Leave for NAIA much earlier than usual. The airport sits close to the closed Pasay roads, and a convoy on the way can add a long wait.
  • Keep the airline's app or official page handy and check it the morning you leave.

Staying Near Manila Bay or the Mall of Asia


Hotels along Roxas Boulevard and in the Mall of Asia and Entertainment City area are the most exposed, since they sit inside or next to the closed zone. A hotel that hosts a delegation can bring in extra lobby screening and change its rules for other guests at short notice. If you have a booking there for November 9 to 20, message the hotel to ask how guests are being dropped off and whether any access rules apply.


Visitors with flexible plans might do better elsewhere. Our BGC and Makati guide covers areas that stay walkable and have plenty of places to eat close to the hotels, though traffic in and out of both business districts will still be heavier than usual during the holiday.


Using the Long Weekend


For many Metro Manila families, the summit has turned into an extra holiday. Saturday through Wednesday gives you five days, and with fun runs and processions on hold inside the capital, a lot of people will head out of town. Expect busier expressways on November 14 and on the evening of November 18, and book buses, ferries and resorts early.


Short trips need the least planning. A Tagaytay and Taal day trip keeps you well away from Pasay. If you are staying in the city instead, Intramuros, covered in our Manila in 24 hours guide, lies north of the security zone and makes an easy half day.


Plans for the summit may still change in the coming weeks. Keep an eye on the Pasay and MMDA advisories, leave extra time for every trip across the city, and treat that week as a good one to skip the bay area altogether.
