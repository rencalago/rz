Christmas in Dumaguete 2026: Lights, Masses and Markets

Ren Calago 3 November 20260

Christmas in Dumaguete is small enough to walk. The city's decorations sit in a tight loop around City Hall, Quezon Park, the cathedral and Rizal Boulevard, so a single evening on foot covers most of the season's lights, with grilled food and the sea breeze on the way. Add the dawn Masses, a mall bazaar or two and a market breakfast, and you have a December that feels local rather than staged.


This guide covers when Dumaguete's Christmas season actually starts, where the lights are, how Simbang Gabi and Misa de Gallo work at the cathedral, where to shop, and a simple plan for the last days before Christmas Eve.


Dates for 2026 are announced by the city, the malls and the parishes closer to December, so the timings below follow recent years. Check official pages a few days before you go.




Christmas in Dumaguete at a Glance


Christmas in Dumaguete 2026 at a glance

  • Mall season opens: mid-November, when the malls light their trees
  • City light-up: mid-December in recent years, around Quezon Park and City Hall
  • Simbang Gabi: evening Masses, 15 to 23 December
  • Misa de Gallo: dawn Masses, 16 to 24 December
  • Busiest days: 20 December to 2 January, when rooms and ferries fill

When the Season Starts


The malls go first. Filinvest Malls Dumaguete held its first Christmas tree lighting at its Central Plaza on 14 November 2024, and the malls tend to be fully in Christmas mode by the end of November.


The city's own celebration, Pasko sa Dumaguete, comes later. In the last two years the main light-up of the community Christmas tree has taken place in mid-December. In 2024 the city lit a 65-foot community tree, and the 2025 celebration was set for 15 December with a community belen, the nativity scene that sits alongside the tree. Expect a similar date in 2026, with a short programme, music and a crowd of families around the park.


Coming straight from fiesta week? Dumaguete's late-November festivities, including the Sandurot Festival and Charter Day, wind down just as the Christmas lights come on, so a long stay can take in both.


A Walking Loop of the Lights


Dumaguete's charm at Christmas is that you do not need a car. Start around 6:30 p.m. and give yourself two hours.


  • City HallThe government centre is decorated each year as part of Pasko sa Dumaguete and makes a good first stop.
  • Quezon ParkThe heart of the city's display, home to the community tree and belen. In 2019, 650 lanterns lit up the park.
  • Cathedral and belfryAcross the street from the park, the old stone church and its bell tower look their best in the evening light.
  • Rizal BoulevardThe lampposts along the seawall are dressed for the season, and the lights reflect on the water. End here for dinner.

Parking near the park and the boulevard gets tight in December. If you are staying downtown, walk. If you are coming from outside the centre, take a tricycle and get off a block or two early.


Simbang Gabi and Misa de Gallo at the Cathedral


The nine days of Masses before Christmas come in two forms. Simbang Gabi is the evening Mass, held from 15 to 23 December. Misa de Gallo is the dawn Mass, from 16 to 24 December. Completing all nine is a long-held tradition, and many families treat it as a promise.


In Dumaguete, the main place for both is the Saint Catherine of Alexandria Cathedral, beside Quezon Park, the oldest stone church on Negros. Dawn Masses usually begin between 4 and 5 a.m., and the evening Masses fall roughly between 6 and 7:30 p.m. Other parishes across the city hold their own schedules and are often less crowded. Parish Facebook pages post the times in early December, and some churches add a second dawn Mass when the first one overflows.


Arrive early if you want a seat, since the overflow often spills into the churchyard and onto the park. Dress modestly, with shoulders and knees covered. After the dawn Mass, follow the crowd to the vendors for puto maya, bibingka and a cup of hot sikwate, or walk to the public market's painitan stalls for a fuller breakfast.


Shopping, Bazaars and Food


Christmas shopping here is low-key. Nothing compares to the size of Cebu's night markets, but there is enough to fill an evening or two.


  • Mall bazaarsIn 2024, Filinvest ran a Christmas bazaar with Santa meet-and-greets every Sunday in December, plus evening events on Fridays and Saturdays. Watch for a similar calendar this year.
  • Parols and small giftsPop-up stalls around the park and the boulevard often sell parol lanterns and handmade items in the weeks before Christmas.
  • The public marketThe place for noche buena shopping, from fruit to rice cakes, and for an early breakfast at the painitan stalls.
  • Silvanas to take homeThe city's frozen cashew-meringue cookies are a favourite pasalubong. Order early, as the popular bakeries get busy in December.

A Plan for Christmas Week


  1. 0121 DecArrive and walk the lights

    Check in downtown, then walk from City Hall to Quezon Park and down to the boulevard for dinner.

  2. 0222 DecDawn Mass and market

    Join Misa de Gallo at the cathedral, eat at the market, then rest through the heat of the day.

  3. 0323 DecShop and the last Simbang Gabi

    Pick up silvanas and gifts, then attend the final evening Mass of the novena.

  4. 0424 DecChristmas Eve

    The last Misa de Gallo at dawn, a quiet afternoon, then noche buena with friends or at a restaurant that takes bookings.


If you want both cities, spend the first half of the novena in Cebu, where the lights and markets are bigger, then cross to Dumaguete for a calmer end to the week. Our guide to Christmas in Cebu 2026 covers that side of the trip.


Tips Before You Go


Before you book

  • Book rooms and seats earlyHotels near downtown fill from about 20 December to 2 January, and ferries and flights from Cebu get busy on the days before Christmas.
  • Plan around Christmas DayMany small shops and eateries close or open late on 25 December. Stock up on snacks and cash the day before.
  • Pack for church and rainBring clothes that cover shoulders and knees, a light rain jacket and a small torch for dark streets after the dawn Mass.
  • Carry small billsVendors outside churches and at the market rarely have change for large notes early in the morning.

For the trip itself, the Cebu to Dumaguete by sea or road guide compares the ferry and bus options so you can choose before the holiday rush.


Why Spend Christmas Here


Dumaguete does not try to outshine the bigger cities, and that is its appeal. The lights are close together, the Masses are part of everyday life, and the best evenings end on the seawall with something hot to eat. For a December that feels like home even if it is not yours, it is hard to beat.
