Dumaguete Is Getting Attention as a Pickleball Capital

Dumaguete has always had a reputation for being a relaxed city. Its universities, seaside boulevard, cafés, restaurants, and proximity to some of Negros Oriental's best destinations have made it popular with residents and visitors alike. But there is another part of the city's identity that is becoming increasingly difficult to ignore: pickleball.


In September 2026, Dumaguete's growing pickleball community received national attention after the Philippine Sports Commission expressed its support for the city's development of the sport. More than 300 courts are now reported across Dumaguete, with thousands more throughout Negros Oriental. That scale has put the city into a growing conversation about whether it could become one of the Philippines' major pickleball destinations.



Why Is Pickleball Growing So Quickly in Dumaguete?


Pickleball is relatively easy for newcomers to understand, especially for people who have played tennis, badminton, or table tennis. The smaller court and doubles format can make the sport feel approachable, while players can still develop their skills and compete at different levels.


That combination of accessibility and competition has helped pickleball attract different generations. People can play casually with friends, join organized games, or take the sport more seriously through tournaments and structured training.


Dumaguete's environment also appears to fit the social nature of the sport. The city has a strong community-oriented atmosphere, and pickleball gives people another reason to gather regularly. A court is not simply a place to exercise; it can become a meeting point for friends, families, and players who are new to the community.



More Than Just Another Sport


The most interesting part of Dumaguete's pickleball story may not be the number of courts. It is the community that has formed around them.


Philippine Sports Commission Chairman John Patrick Gregorio described pickleball in Dumaguete as a combination of community, health, and competition. Local officials have also pointed to its ability to bring different age groups together.


Dumaguete Mayor Manuel Sagarbarria noted that the city and province have an aging population and said pickleball is a suitable sport in that context. The sport has also been described by local officials as an activity that can be enjoyed by friends and families across different ages.


That broad appeal could be one reason the sport has been able to grow so quickly. Unlike activities that may be strongly associated with one particular age group or level of athletic ability, pickleball can accommodate casual players and competitive athletes within the same wider community.



National Attention Is Starting to Follow


The rapid growth of pickleball in Dumaguete has now attracted attention from the Philippine Sports Commission. In September 2026, the PSC pledged support for Dumaguete City as the sport continues to expand.


The commission's interest is not limited to pickleball. PSC Chairman John Patrick Gregorio said the organization also wants to see facilities for football, track and field, archery, and other disciplines developed to high standards.


Still, pickleball stands out because of how quickly its local footprint has expanded. Philippine News Agency reported more than 300 courts in Dumaguete and thousands more throughout Negros Oriental. ABS-CBN likewise reported that the city is looking to position itself as a pickleball center in the country.


This attention matters because grassroots popularity is only one part of building a lasting sports community. Facilities, coaching, competitions, and organized development can all determine whether today's enthusiasm becomes a sustainable local sports ecosystem.



Could Pickleball Become Part of Dumaguete Tourism?


Dumaguete already has a strong tourism identity. Visitors come for the city's food, boulevard, cafés, universities, and access to destinations around Negros Oriental and nearby islands.


Pickleball could eventually become another reason for people to visit, particularly if the city continues developing tournaments, training sessions, community events, and other organized competitions.


Sports tourism does not always require enormous international events. A regional or national tournament can bring players, coaches, families, and supporters into a city. Those visitors need accommodation, transportation, food, and places to spend their free time.


Dumaguete is already positioned to provide many of those experiences. A visitor could potentially combine a pickleball event with restaurants, cafés, the boulevard, diving, beaches, and trips to other parts of Negros Oriental.


If that pattern develops, pickleball could become connected to the city's existing tourism economy rather than operating separately from it.



A New Opportunity for Local Businesses


A growing sports community can create opportunities beyond the courts themselves. Coaches, court operators, sporting goods retailers, tournament organizers, food businesses, and other service providers can all potentially benefit from a larger player base.


There are also indirect opportunities. Players need places to eat and relax before or after games. Visitors attending tournaments need hotels, transportation, and other services. Local businesses located near sports facilities may benefit from the additional activity.


For entrepreneurs in Dumaguete, the growth of pickleball is therefore worth watching even if they have no plans to open a sports-related business. A strong sports community can create a wider network of economic activity around it.



The Community Side May Be the Biggest Story


It is easy to focus on the headline number of more than 300 courts. But numbers alone do not explain why a sport becomes part of a city's culture.


The community surrounding the courts may ultimately be more important. Regular games give people a reason to stay active, meet new people, spend time with friends, and participate in something outside of work or school.


That social component is particularly interesting in Dumaguete. The city is already known for its lifestyle and relatively relaxed pace. Pickleball fits naturally into that environment because it combines physical activity with a social experience.


The sport's appeal across age groups also gives it the potential to connect generations. Families can play together, older residents can remain active, and younger players can approach the sport competitively.



Is Dumaguete Really the Pickleball Capital?


The phrase "pickleball capital" is increasingly being used to describe Dumaguete, including by national media and in reporting about the city's growing sports community.


However, it is useful to separate the nickname from what can actually be documented. There is no need for a formal title to recognize what is happening on the ground: more than 300 courts have been reported across Dumaguete, thousands more are reported throughout Negros Oriental, and the Philippine Sports Commission has publicly acknowledged the city's rapid growth in the sport. :contentReference[oaicite:3]{index=3}


Whether the city ultimately establishes itself as a lasting national center for pickleball will depend on what happens next. Continued participation, quality facilities, organized tournaments, coaching, and player development will all play a role.


The current momentum, however, is already significant enough to make pickleball part of the conversation about Dumaguete's future.



Dumaguete's Pickleball Story Is Just Beginning


Dumaguete's pickleball boom is an interesting new chapter for a city already known for its food, universities, seaside atmosphere, and proximity to some of the Philippines' well-known island destinations.


More than 300 courts represent more than infrastructure. They represent a growing network of players, families, coaches, businesses, and communities built around a sport that has found a strong local audience.


The next stage will be particularly interesting. If Dumaguete can continue developing its facilities and competitions while maintaining the community atmosphere that helped the sport grow, pickleball could become an increasingly recognizable part of the city's identity.


For visitors, it could eventually become another reason to stop in Dumaguete. For residents, it is already becoming another way to stay active and connected. And for local businesses, it may represent a growing niche within the city's wider lifestyle and tourism economy.


For now, one thing is clear: pickleball is no longer a small side story in Dumaguete. With hundreds of courts, a growing community, and national attention, the city has become one of the places to watch as pickleball continues to develop in the Philippines.
