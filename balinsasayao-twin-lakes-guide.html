Balinsasayao Twin Lakes: A Day Hike and Boat Ride from Dumaguete

Ren Calago 13 October 2026

Balinsasayao Twin Lakes sit in the hills above Sibulan, roughly an hour's drive from Dumaguete. Two lakes lie side by side, one on each side of a narrow ridge, and the walk up to them through the forest is the part many visitors remember. Expect a quiet place with cool air, birdsong and boats on still water, rather than a resort with a fixed plan.


This guide covers how to get there from Dumaguete, what the park charges, what there is to do on the water and on the trail, and when the weather is kinder. Prices and rules at the park change, so confirm the details with the park office before you set out.


Because the park sits at around 800 metres above sea level, it is cooler than the city. Bring a light layer for the drive back down, even on a hot day.



Getting There from Dumaguete


No public transport goes directly to the park. The usual route is to take a tricycle or bus from Dumaguete to Sibulan, then a motorcycle or jeepney up the road to the entrance. The access road is about 14.5 kilometres west of Dumaguete, and the drive takes roughly an hour from the city.


If you do not want to change vehicles, a habal-habal can be chartered from the city. Expect to pay about ₱600 to ₱800 for the trip, though the price depends on the rider and the season, so agree on it before you leave. If you plan to hike and come back the same day, agree on a pickup time with the rider before you start.


Entrance Fees and Rentals


The entrance fee is small. It has been listed at ₱50 per person and is described as minimal, so check the current amount at the gate and ask what it covers. Registration takes place before the short trek to the lake.


Boat and kayak rentals cost extra. One listing gave boat rental at about ₱250 an hour and kayaking at about ₱150 an hour, and a trekking guide at about ₱300 for up to three people. These figures are older than the entrance fee, so treat them as a guide and settle the rate before you get in the boat.


The Lakes and the Trail


A short walk takes you from the registration point to Lake Balinsasayao. The larger lake is said to be about 90 metres deep and covers around 76 hectares. Lake Danao is smaller and shallower, at about 58 metres deep and around 30 hectares. A narrow ridge separates the two lakes.


The trail to the lakes passes through dense forest, crosses streams and goes near waterfalls. Distances for the trail are quoted differently and there is no clear difficulty rating, so judge the walk by your own fitness and the state of the trail after rain. Wear closed shoes with a good grip, since the path can be wet and slippery in the shaded sections.


There are two routes: a trek to Lake Danao, or a boat across to Lake Balinsasayao followed by a trek to a three-storey viewdeck. Ask the park office which route is open that day before you set out.


What to Do on the Water


Boating and kayaking are the easiest ways to see the lakes up close. Swimming is not allowed, and drones are banned in the park, so leave the drone at home and keep your phone out of the water.


Birdwatching is worth the time if you are patient. The park is home to species that are rare or endemic to Negros, including the Negros bleeding heart and the Visayan wrinkled hornbill, according to one account. Keep your voice down on the trail and move slowly near the shore.


When to Go


The dry months, roughly November to May, are the time to go, since the trails are more accessible then. The rainy months bring greener forest and more waterfalls, but the path can be slippery, so check conditions with the park before you go. Check the forecast the day before, and keep a spare day if the trip is part of a short stay.


The park opens around 8:00 in the morning and closes at 5:00 in the afternoon. Plan to arrive by late morning at the latest, so you have time for the hike, a boat ride and the drive back before the light fades.


Who Should Plan Ahead


The trail suits people who are comfortable walking on uneven ground for a couple of hours. It is not ideal for anyone with a knee or hip problem, or for anyone who would struggle with a steep climb in the heat. Families with young children can still enjoy the boat ride and the lake shore, but keep them close to the water and within sight at all times.


Pack more water than you expect to need, along with insect repellent, a hat and snacks. The park has few places to buy food, so bring lunch. Take your rubbish back down with you, and stay on the marked trails.
