What's Happening in Dumaguete This October? Events and Things to Watch

Ren Calago 30 September 20260

October is shaping up to be one of the busiest months of the year in Dumaguete and Negros Oriental. While the city already has its usual restaurants, boulevard activities and weekend attractions, this October brings a much bigger calendar of festivals, competitions, cultural activities, food events and community gatherings.


The biggest event on the calendar is Buglasan 2026, but it is not the only reason to keep an eye on Dumaguete this month. Several activities begin before the main festival opens on October 15, giving locals and visitors plenty to see even during the first half of the month.



Buglasan 2026 Will Be the Main Event


If there is one event that will define October in Dumaguete, it is Buglasan. The 2026 Buglasan Festival is scheduled from October 15 to 25, bringing together cities and municipalities from across Negros Oriental for an 11-day celebration.


This year's theme is “Different Stories. One Aspiration. One Negros Oriental,” reflecting the festival's role in bringing together the different communities of the province. The program includes street dancing, cultural competitions, entertainment, sports, food activities, agricultural displays and trade events.


October 1 Starts the Build-Up


You do not necessarily have to wait until October 15 to find something happening. A Feel the Beat TikTok Dance Challenge is scheduled from October 1 to 25, extending across the same period as the main festival activities.


Other activities are also scheduled before the official opening. On October 10 and 11, Buglas Camp Fest will take place in Dauin, while the Negros Oriental Destination Pickle Tournament – Club Wars Edition is scheduled in Sibulan.


The pickleball event is particularly interesting because the sport has already become part of Dumaguete's growing sports story. It gives October another connection between local sports, tourism and community activities.


The National Museum Will Have a New Exhibition


Art and culture will also have a place in the October calendar. From October 13 to November 15, the National Museum of the Philippines–Dumaguete is scheduled to host the Teizo Suzuki exhibition, “Microcosm in Bowl.”


That makes it an interesting option for people who want something different from the larger festival crowds. It also means visitors staying in Dumaguete beyond the Buglasan dates will still have a cultural attraction to check out.


October 14 Is Another Busy Day


The Buglasan calendar begins to get noticeably busier on October 14, with the Gandang NegOrense 2026 Queensize Edition pre-competition scheduled at Robinsons Dumaguete.


This is also a good example of how the festival is spread across different parts of Dumaguete rather than being limited to one venue. Robinsons Dumaguete, Freedom Park, the boulevard, sports facilities and other locations all become part of the wider October activity.


October 15 to 25: The Big Festival Period


The official Buglasan celebration opens on October 15 at Freedom Park with the ribbon-cutting ceremony and opening of the LGU Booths Fair Competition. The evening program also includes the Battle of Dance Supreme and the Hara sa Negros Oriental pre-competition.


October 16 is expected to be one of the busiest days. The schedule includes the Buglasan Painitan at Pantawan and Dumaguete Boulevard, the civic-military parade, the decorated umbrellas competition, Lechonfest, the opening of the Buglasan Trade Fair, street dancing and the Festival of Festivals field presentation and showdown.


October 16 has also been declared a province-wide holiday, making it an especially important date for people planning to attend the festivities.


There Is More Than Street Dancing


One of the interesting things about Buglasan is the variety of activities surrounding the main competitions. October 17 brings the marching band competition, Parade of Lights and pyromusical competition.


The following days include the Battle of the Bands, Singing Idol, vocal duet and Balitaw competitions, as well as tourism and hospitality skills competitions.


Food also gets its own spotlight. The festival program includes Lechonfest and Lutong Ato – “The NegOrense Flavors,” while the Buglasan Trade Fair gives visitors another chance to see products and specialties from different parts of Negros Oriental.


Sports Will Also Be Part of October


October will not be limited to cultural performances and concerts. The Buglasan calendar includes boxing, dancesport, bodybuilding and push bike competitions, adding a strong sporting element to the month.


There is also the Negros Oriental Destination Pickle Tournament in Sibulan earlier in the month. For visitors who enjoy active travel, this creates another reason to look beyond the usual sightseeing activities.


What Visitors Should Expect Around Dumaguete


With so many events happening close together, October is likely to feel different from a normal month in Dumaguete. More people will be moving around the city, particularly around Freedom Park, the Capitol area, Dumaguete Boulevard and other festival venues.


Visitors should also expect schedules and traffic conditions to change around major parades and street events. If you are attending a specific activity, it is worth checking the latest schedule before leaving because event times and venues can still change.


October Could Be a Good Month to Explore More of Negros Oriental


One advantage of visiting during Buglasan is that the festival provides a reason to explore beyond Dumaguete itself. Participating communities bring their own culture, food, products and traditions into the provincial celebration.


That makes October more than simply a festival season. It is also an opportunity to see how different parts of Negros Oriental come together in one city while still keeping their own identities.


So, What's Worth Watching This October?


If you are planning to spend time in Dumaguete this October, the biggest dates to remember are October 10 and 11 for the early sports and community activities, October 13 for the opening of the museum exhibition, October 14 for the pre-competition event, and October 15 to 25 for the main Buglasan celebration.


The biggest crowds will likely gather around the major festival activities, but the smaller events may be just as interesting for people who want a different experience. Between food, sports, culture, entertainment and local products, October gives Dumaguete one of its fullest event calendars of the year.


For locals, it is a month to watch what is happening around the city. For visitors, it may be a good time to experience Dumaguete and Negros Oriental beyond the usual tourist itinerary.
