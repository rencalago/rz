Alimyon Kapehan San Jose: Mountain Cafe Near Twin Lakes

Ren Calago 25 Feb 2023 Updated 22 October 2026

Alimyon Kapehan is a Filipino-style mountain cafe in San Jose, Negros Oriental, about 20 minutes from the main highway. It works as a coffee or meal stop if you are heading to Balinsasayao Twin Lakes, with a view of the mountains, a pond and the feel of a native home.


This guide covers what the place is like inside, how the access road and parking behave, and how to fit a meal here into a Twin Lakes day. It draws on visits in 2018 and early 2023. Opening hours and prices change, so confirm them with the cafe before you drive up.


The short version: come for a slow breakfast with a view, not for a quick roadside stop.



What It Is Like Inside


You leave your shoes at the staircase before going in, which gives the place a comfortable, at-home feel. On our early 2023 visit we ordered the typical Filipino breakfast, and it was as good as we remembered.



A pond runs through the grounds, and a small bridge crosses it to a second dining spot. It is a good place for a relaxed meal and for sitting a while without watching the clock. Walk across the bridge and compare the two dining areas before you settle on a table.


The Road and the Parking Lot


The cafe sits about 20 minutes off the main highway. Compared with our first visit in 2018, we saw few improvements in early 2023, apart from a spacious new parking lot. The lot is not fully cemented, and loose small rocks make it a challenge for a vehicle that is not 4x4. Take the last stretch slowly, and if you are driving a low car, be ready to reposition rather than force a spot.


About San Jose, Negros Oriental


San Jose is a small town of 14 barangays, about 14 kilometres from Dumaguete, with slopes that climb from the coast toward the interior and views across the Tañon Strait. It is known locally as the pineapple capital of the region, and it serves as the gateway to Balinsasayao Twin Lakes, which is why a cafe in these hills makes sense as a stop for travellers heading up.


The calendar matters if you plan to visit in May. The Ayuquitan Festival falls on 7 May and the town fiesta on 10 May, with street dancing as a highlight, so expect busier roads and fuller tables around those dates.


Getting There from Dumaguete


No public transport goes directly to Twin Lakes, so most visitors to this side of the hills go by their own vehicle, a rented motorbike or a chartered habal-habal. The Twin Lakes guide mentions about ₱600 to ₱800 for a habal-habal charter to the park, and the price depends on the rider and the season. If you plan to stop at the cafe, tell the rider before you agree on a rate and ask whether waiting time is included.


Ask the cafe for its exact location pin or directions before you leave Dumaguete, so you are not relying on a map app alone, since a wrong turn on a country road costs time. Once you leave the main highway, count on roughly 20 minutes to reach the gate.


Fitting It Into a Twin Lakes Trip


The park has few places to buy food, as the Balinsasayao Twin Lakes guide explains, so a proper meal at Alimyon is a practical part of the day. The drive from Dumaguete to the park alone takes about an hour, so leave early and decide ahead of time whether you will eat before the lakes or on the way back.


If you are building a longer loop, the Negros Oriental weekend getaways guide places Twin Lakes alongside other budget-friendly trips, and our Sakada Cafe and Farm guide covers another countryside cafe stop in Pamplona.


A Sample Half-Day Plan


This is a suggestion, not a fixed schedule, so adjust it to your pace. Leave Dumaguete early in the morning, since the drive to the park alone takes about an hour. Stop at Alimyon for a late breakfast or early lunch, then continue to the lakes for a boat or kayak ride and a short walk. Plan the return trip before the afternoon light fades, because hill roads are easier to handle with good visibility.


If Twin Lakes is not on your list, Alimyon also works as a stand-alone morning out. Go for breakfast, spend an hour or two by the pond, and be back in Dumaguete by early afternoon.


Who It Suits, and Who Might Skip It


Alimyon suits travellers who like slow meals, mountain views and a homely setting, and it is easy to pair with a family day out. Keep an eye on young children around the pond and the bridge, since there is open water on the grounds.


It is less suited to anyone in a hurry, to travellers without their own transport, or to visitors who want a polished resort-style cafe. The access road, the partly cemented parking area and the shoes-off rule at the staircase mean it takes a little effort, and that effort is part of what makes it feel like a countryside stop rather than a roadside one.


Before You Go


Call or message the cafe to confirm opening hours and prices, especially for a weekday visit, since small mountain cafes can change their schedule. Plan on an hour or two at the table, because the setting is made for lingering, and wear socks you do not mind showing, since shoes come off at the staircase.
