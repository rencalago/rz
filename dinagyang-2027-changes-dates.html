Dinagyang 2027: What's Changing and the Dates So Far

Ren Calago 26 October 20260

Dinagyang 2027 is going back downtown. At the 100-day countdown launch on 24 October 2026 at SM City Iloilo, the Iloilo Festivals Foundation Inc. (IFFI) confirmed that every judging area for the tribes competition will sit in the City Proper district, that banderitas will hang over the streets again, and that the religious side of the festival will get more room. The main festival days are 22 to 24 January 2027.


Those changes answer complaints from the last edition, when the competition was spread across the city and the street decorations disappeared. Below is what went wrong, what organizers are changing, the dates already set, and what it all means if you are thinking of booking a trip to Iloilo City for January.


If you are still deciding between the two big January festivals in Western Visayas, start with our guide comparing Ati-Atihan and Dinagyang. This post sticks to Iloilo and to the news from the countdown.





Why 2026 drew complaints


For the January 2026 festival, organizers moved part of the judging away from the old downtown route. Tribes performed at the Freedom Grandstand, at a judging area on Luna and Huervana streets in La Paz, and at the Iloilo Sports Complex, which seats roughly 7,000 people. Festivalgoers gave the Sports Complex mixed reviews, mostly because of the viewing experience and the distance from City Proper.


IFFI president Dr. Ronald Latap later admitted the split caused problems with traffic and logistics, and stretched security across too many sites. The foundation had also scrapped the banderitas, citing safety, the environment and single-use plastic. A festival without its overhead flaglets felt bare to many Ilonggos, and the criticism stuck. At the countdown, Latap promised that the 2027 edition would be better, more peaceful and more organized.



What changes for Dinagyang 2027



All five judging areas move back to City Proper


The tribes competition returns to a carousel-style route with five judging areas, all downtown: the Iloilo Freedom Grandstand, the Iloilo Provincial Capitol, Quezon and Ledesma streets, Mabini and De Leon streets near the Iloilo Terminal Market, and Iznart Street near the Iloilo Central Market. Security will be concentrated in the same district instead of being split across the city.


For a visitor, this matters more than any other change. Every performance happens within a compact part of town, so you can walk between areas and catch a tribe more than once without taking a taxi across the city.



Banderitas are back, with greener materials


Colourful banderitas will return over parts of General Luna, Quezon and Ledesma streets. JM Basa Street will get lanterns made from indigenous materials instead. Sponsors are being asked to use paper, native or biodegradable materials, which is how organizers plan to bring back the look without the plastic waste that led to the ban.



Nine competing tribes, including some old names


Seven tribes competed in 2026. In 2027 there will be nine: Pan-ay, Villahanon, Paghidaet, Salognon, Bola-Bola, Ilonganon, Silak, Taga-Baryo and Hugyaw. Bola-Bola returns after 17 years away, Villahanon comes back after a two-year break, and Hugyaw is competing for the first time. The Binirayan Festival contingent from Antique will also perform, though not for a prize, since its story does not fit the warrior theme of the competition. Separately, 11 groups have said they intend to join Kasadyahan sa Kabanwahanan, the contest for performers from Iloilo's towns.



More room for the Santo Niño


Dinagyang began in 1967, after a replica of the Señor Santo Niño de Cebu was brought to Iloilo City, and the devotion is still the reason the festival exists. For 2027, IFFI is working with San Jose Placer Parish on a bigger fluvial procession, a solemn foot procession and an expanded religious sadsad. If you want to see the festival as Ilonggo devotees experience it, these are the days to be in town.



The evening parade becomes a community event


The ILOmination, which used to be staged as a performance, is being turned into a street light parade. It and the Sponsors Mardi Gras will draw in universities and local institutions. The Sadsad sa Calle Real has been redesigned too, with barangays from all seven city districts taking part through the Liga ng mga Barangay. The Drum and Lyre Competition also returns.



Dates already on the calendar


Here is what IFFI has announced so far:


  • 16 December 2026: Pamuklaw
  • 8 January 2027: opening salvo of the tribes competition
  • 22 January 2027: fluvial procession and solemn foot procession
  • 23 January 2027: Drum and Lyre Competition in the morning, then Kasadyahan sa Kabanwahanan; Grand Religious Sadsad; Sponsors Mardi Gras at 4 pm and the ILOmination parade at 6 pm
  • 24 January 2027: the fourth Sunday of January, the day Dinagyang traditionally builds towards

Start times and the full programme usually come out closer to January, so check the IFFI and Iloilo City pages again before you travel.



Planning a trip around the new layout


Book a room early, and book near downtown. With every judging area in City Proper, hotels within walking distance of the Freedom Grandstand and Iznart Street will be in demand. Staying in City Proper, or close enough to walk in, saves you from fighting road closures on the busiest days.


Plan to walk. Expect closed streets around the route, packed sidewalks and few taxis willing to drive into the crowd. Comfortable shoes, water, a hat and sunscreen will do more for you than any app. Keep your phone and wallet in a front pocket or a small zipped bag.


Pick your judging area. The Freedom Grandstand and the Provincial Capitol are the most formal settings. The street areas near the markets are closer to the crowd and noisier. Arrive well before the first tribe reaches your spot if you want a view, because good places fill quickly.


Signal should hold up better. IFFI says it will not recommend signal jamming this time. Mobile data can still slow down when thousands of people share the same few streets, so agree on a meeting point with your group before the parade starts.


Add a day or two. The festival weekend is a good reason to stay longer. Iloilo is one of the best food cities in the Visayas, and our Iloilo and Guimaras food trip covers what to eat between performances. If you want quiet after the drums, the white-sand coves of Islas de Gigantes are in the north of the same province.



Still to be announced


Grandstand seating, ticket prices, exact parade routes and road closure maps have not been released. These usually come out in December or early January. Until then, a refundable hotel booking is the safer choice, and the Iloilo City tourism office can help with the details once the final programme is out.


Iloilo was recognised for Dinagyang at the 2026 ATOP Pearl Awards, when the 2025 edition was named the country's best cultural tourism festival. This year organizers are betting that a return to the old downtown format, with its flaglets and processions, is what will keep it there.
