Sipalay's White Sand Beaches and the Long Road to Get There

Ren Calago 03 June 2026 Updated 22 October 2026

Sipalay, at the southern end of Negros Occidental, has some of the best white sand on Negros Island. For anyone living in Negros Oriental or staying in Dumaguete, it is the nearest place to find the kind of beach that Cebu, across the strait, is known for. The trade-off is distance: about three to four hours by car from Dumaguete, and longer by bus.


This guide sticks to the beaches and the drive. If you also want to visit the island sanctuary nearby, our Sipalay and Danjugan Island guide covers the bookings and boat rules.


We drove to Sipalay one April as part of a loop around Negros Island: Tanjay, then Santa Catalina and Bayawan City, and on to Sipalay for a night. The next day we headed north to Bacolod for another night and came home through Don Salvador Benedicto. The whole loop came to about 500 km.





Poblacion Beach, the town's public beach


The town beach was the first surprise. It was clean, and you can tell the local government works to keep it that way. Families had set up camping chairs along the sand to wait for sunset, which is the main event here. Sipalay faces west toward the Sulu Sea, so on a clear evening the sun sinks straight into the water.


It is also the budget option. You can stay in town, walk to the beach, and eat at the food park nearby.


Seafood restaurants by the mangroves


Near town, a row of seafood restaurants sits along the edge of a mangrove area. We never learned what locals call the strip, but after a long drive it was a great first stop, with plenty of choices for a family table. Ask your tricycle driver for the seafood restaurants by the mangroves and they will know where to go.


Punta Ballo


Punta Ballo is a cove a short tricycle ride from town, lined with dive resorts. The beach is long, the water is calm enough for swimming in good weather, and the sunsets are just as good as in town. Most visitors get onto the beach through a resort, so plan on paying for a meal, day use or a room. Some resorts are built into the hillside with a lot of steps, which is worth knowing if you are travelling with older relatives.


Sugar Beach


Sugar Beach, in Barangay Nauhang, is the most secluded stretch. There is no road straight onto the sand. You ride a tricycle to Nauhang and then cross by small boat, and the resorts there can arrange the crossing for you. Choose it when you want quiet more than convenience.


Tinagong Dagat


Tinagong Dagat means "hidden sea" in Hiligaynon, and it lives up to the name. It is a few minutes' drive from the public beach, a sheltered spot of green water with small islets covered in trees rising out of it. The view is the reason to go. The resort at the site charges a small entrance fee.


Perth Paradise Resort and the rough road


The view from Perth Paradise Resort was the best of our trip, with islets offshore and open water in every direction. We would bet both sunrise and sunset are worth staying for. Getting there was another matter. The last stretch of road was rough, and with a full back seat our car kept scraping on large rocks. Everyone had to get out and walk a few metres so the car could clear the worst part.


It was still worth it. If your car sits low, message the resort about the road first, or leave the car in town and hire a tricycle for the final stretch. The resort is known for its infinity pool and offers day use, so you do not have to stay the night to enjoy the view. Ask for current rates when you message them.


Island hopping


We ran out of time for island hopping, but it is one of the main things to do in Sipalay. Boat tours around Punta Ballo and Campomanes Bay take in small islets, quiet beaches and snorkelling spots. Book through your resort or ask at the city tourism office, and agree on the price per boat before you set out.


Getting there and practical tips


  • By car from Dumaguete, plan on three to four hours along the coastal road through Bayawan.
  • By bus, trips from Dumaguete or from the Ceres South Terminal in Bacolod take five hours or more, and some routes need a transfer along the way.
  • ATMs are limited in Sipalay, so withdraw cash in Dumaguete or Bacolod before you go.
  • October to June is the drier, calmer stretch. April and May are the hottest and busiest months.
  • Heading north to Bacolod after Sipalay, as we did? Our Bacolod food trail is a good next stop.

Is Sipalay worth the drive?


As a day trip, no. For two nights or more, yes. Sipalay works best as one stop on a longer route around Negros, with Bayawan on one side and Bacolod on the other. The beaches are wide, the sunsets are dependable in the dry months, and the long road keeps the crowds smaller than at the better-known beaches in the region.
