Cebu travel guides

Cebu is a long, narrow island in the middle of the Visayas, and most trips start at Mactan-Cebu International Airport or the ports of Cebu City. The city is the busy part: old streets, churches and Carbon Market downtown, malls and hotels uptown, and the hills of Busay just above it. The three-day itinerary is a good first plan, and the heritage walk covers the old quarter on foot.

Leave the city and the island splits into two very different trips. The south is for the classic day trips: Oslob for whale sharks and Sumilon Island, Moalboal for the sardine run, Badian for canyoneering at Kawasan Falls, and Dalaguete for the climb up Osmeña Peak. Plan on long bus rides from the South Bus Terminal, and start early, because the road along the coast is slow after mid-morning. The north is quieter and better for staying a few nights: Bantayan Island for flat white-sand beaches, Malapascua for diving with thresher sharks, and the Camotes Islands to the east for lakes, caves and empty coves.

Timing matters more than most visitors expect. The driest months are usually from about January to May, while the second half of the year brings more rain and the chance of typhoons, which can cancel boats at short notice. January is also Sinulog month, when Cebu City fills up and hotel prices rise. The month-by-month guide goes through what each part of the year is like.

The guides below are newest first, with the most useful starting points at the top. Fees, boat schedules and rules at places like Oslob change, so check the current details with the operator or the town tourism office before you go. Crossing to Negros? The Dumaguete and Negros Oriental pages pick up where this one ends, and the Visayas guides cover Bohol, Siquijor and the islands farther out.
