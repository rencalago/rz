Venice Grand Canal in McKinley Hill, Taguig: A Practical Visitor Guide

Ren Calago 14 Sep 2024 Updated 22 October 20260

Venice Grand Canal is a Megaworld lifestyle mall inside McKinley Hill, Taguig, built around a 200-metre indoor canal with gondolas, bridge replicas and a bell tower. It opened in September 2015, and it makes one of the easier half-day outings in Metro Manila if you want something other than another standard mall. This guide covers the gondola ride, where to eat, how to get there and when to dodge the crowds.


We went in December 2023, a few weeks before Christmas. The place was packed from late afternoon into the evening, which is when groups meet up for dinner around the canal. The boat ride is the part we still remember: a slow loop under the bridges that lasts a few minutes, and a welcome change from walking yet another mall corridor.


The setting does most of the work. Pastel facades face each other across the water, restaurant tables sit right at the canal edge, and the lighting changes the whole mood after dark. Treat it as a photo stop and dinner spot first, and a place to shop second.





What you will find inside


The canal is 200 metres long and 15 metres wide, and the gondolas are moored under a copy of the Rialto Bridge. A smaller footbridge doubles as a love-lock spot for couples. Outside, the Venice Piazza has a replica of the St. Mark's Campanile bell tower, and the open square is where the McKinley Hill Christmas parade and other events take place. In the late afternoon, pigeons gather in the piazza for feeding time, which small kids tend to love.


The mall has three floors above ground and a basement. Besides the restaurants, there is a supermarket, a bookstore, fitness and wellness shops, a cinema on the third level and a Catholic chapel, St. Raphael the Archangel, on the same floor. The mall runs a pet pass system, so ask the concierge first if you plan to bring a dog.


The gondola ride


Most first-timers come for the gondola. The ride is short, a few minutes around the canal, which suits families with young children and couples who want a photo more than a long cruise. Expect a queue on weekend evenings and throughout December. Rates change, so read the price posted at the ticket booth near the bridge before you line up, and ask how many passengers each boat takes if you are in a group.


If photos matter to you, ride in the early afternoon. The queue is shorter and daylight shows off the facades. An evening ride has more atmosphere, but the boats fill up quickly once the dinner crowd arrives.





Where to eat


Food is half the reason to go. Restaurants line the canal on the ground floor, so you can eat with the water a few steps away. The mall has had Italian places such as Ponte Amore and Toni and Sergio, Tim Hortons for coffee, Mitsuyado Sei-Men for tsukemen, and the country's first Fundador Café. Tenants change over time, so take that as a starting list and walk the whole canal before you pick a table.


On weekends, book ahead if the restaurant takes reservations, or sit down before 6 PM. The canal-side tables are always the first to go.


How to get to Venice Grand Canal


The mall is on Upper McKinley Road in McKinley Hill, next to Bonifacio Global City. Driving is the simplest option, and there are about 1,000 parking slots, although they fill up fast on December weekends.


On public transport, a common route is to get to Market! Market! in BGC, take a city bus that ends at a terminal inside McKinley Hill, then walk around 400 metres to the mall. Routes and fares shift, so plan the trip on Sakay.ph or a similar route planner before leaving. Grab cars and taxis are the easy choice from Makati or BGC, and our guide to BGC and Makati for first-time visitors explains how to move around that side of the city.


When to visit


Weekday afternoons are the calmest. Late afternoon to evening is the rush, especially on Fridays and weekends. December is the peak, when the Christmas decorations and the parade draw big crowds and the parking gets tight. Go early for photos, or go after dark for the lights and accept the wait.


Holiday hours and event dates change from year to year. Check the mall's official social media pages before you travel, particularly around Christmas and New Year.


Tips for a smoother visit


  • Line up for the gondola first, then eat. The dinner rush and the boat queue peak at the same time.
  • Wear comfortable shoes. Between the canal, the piazza and the upper floors, you will walk more than you expect.
  • Bring a light jacket if you plan to catch a film on the third floor.
  • Set a meeting point at the bridge if you are with kids or a big group. The canal-side walkways get crowded in the evening.

Is it worth the trip?


Venice Grand Canal is not Venice, and it does not pretend to be. What it gives you is an easy evening: a short boat ride, a meal by the water and a walk around a piazza that looks nothing like the rest of Metro Manila. If you only have a couple of days in the capital, pair it with a day around Intramuros or a Binondo food crawl for a more local side of the city.
