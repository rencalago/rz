Tide

Arrive at low tide

Ask your boatman when low tide falls and build the route around it. Animasola and the Tinalisayan sandbar both need the water down, and a good boatman will order the stops so you catch each one at the right moment.

Time of day

Leave by 8:00 AM

Set off from San Pascual between about 5:30 and 8:00 AM. The wind picks up in the afternoon and the water between the islands gets choppy. An early start also gets you onto Animasola before other boats arrive and before the rock heats up.

Season

March to May is calmest

November and December are quieter but can still bring late storms. From June to October, the southwest monsoon and typhoons often lead to no-sail orders, and a cancelled crossing can strand you on either side.

A few habits that keep a short trip relaxed.

01 Traffic

One area per day

Baguio's roads are narrow and jam easily. Staying in one part of the city each day saves hours in taxis.

02 Timing

Start early

Popular viewpoints and gardens get crowded by mid-morning on weekends. Being out by 7:30 makes a big difference.

03 Weather

Dress in layers

Mornings and evenings are cool year-round and cold from December to February. A light jacket is useful even in summer.

04 Money

Carry small bills

Taxis, market stalls, taho vendors and smaller attractions mostly want cash in small notes.

Near Burnham Park, with other branches

Local institution

Café by the Ruins

Shuntug Road, near City Hall

Started in 1988 by a group of Baguio artists, including BenCab, on the site of the Garden Theatre, a 1912 outdoor cinema. After a fire in 2017, it reopened at its original site in 2018. The menu leans on Cordillera ingredients and Benguet coffee, and the garden seating is pleasant at lunch.

Order: kamote bread, a Filipino breakfast plate, local coffee
