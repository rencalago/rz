Osmeña Peak to Kawasan Falls: Cebu's Classic Traverse

Osmeña Peak and Kawasan Falls sit on opposite sides of the mountains in southern Cebu, and one of the island's best-loved hikes links them. The traverse starts in the cool highlands of Mantalongon in Dalaguete, climbs to the jagged summit of Osmeña Peak at 1,013 metres, then drops west through farms, forest and river valleys to the turquoise pools of Kawasan in Badian.


It is a long day rather than a technical one. The summit itself is a short climb from the trailhead, while the descent to Kawasan takes most of the day. Many visitors do the two as separate trips by road, but walking from one to the other shows you a side of Cebu that few day-trippers see.


This guide covers getting to the jump-off, the summit, the traverse, the shorter options, and what to pack.




Getting to the Jump-Off


From the Cebu South Bus Terminal, take a bus bound for Oslob or Santander and ask to get off at Dalaguete. The ride takes about two hours, depending on traffic, and an air-conditioned bus costs roughly ₱106 to ₱130 each way.


From the highway, habal-habal drivers take you up the winding road to Mantalongon and the trailhead in around 30 minutes or more. Expect to pay about ₱200 to ₱400 per motorbike for a round trip, so agree on the price and the pick-up time before you set off. If you plan to traverse to Kawasan, you only need a one-way ride.


Leaving Cebu City around 4 am puts you at the summit close to sunrise. For a gentler start, an afternoon arrival avoids the worst of the midday heat.


The Summit Climb


At the trailhead, register and pay the entrance fee. Expect to pay somewhere between ₱50 and ₱150 per person, depending on whether a guide is included, and have small notes ready. If you plan to camp near the top, there is an extra camping fee of around ₱50, and you need to bring your own tent.


From there, the path to the top is only about 1.3 km. Most people reach the summit in 15 to 30 minutes, though the last stretch is steep and rocky. Small stalls along the way sell water and snacks.


The view is the reward: rows of sharp, grass-covered limestone peaks, with the sea visible on clear days. Early mornings often bring a layer of cloud below the ridges. Allow at least 45 minutes to an hour at the top to take it all in.


The Traverse to Kawasan Falls


The full route crosses the island from east to west. It starts on the Dalaguete side in Mantalongon and finishes in Barangay Matutinao, Badian, where Kawasan Falls tumbles down toward the coast. Plan for a full day on foot. Depending on your pace and how long you stop, the walk from the peak to the falls takes roughly five to eight hours.


Hire a local guide for the traverse. The route crosses farmland, small villages, forest and river valleys, and the path is not always obvious to someone walking it for the first time. Arrange the guide at the trailhead or through your accommodation, and confirm the fee, the start time and the meeting point the day before.


Water sources along the trail are limited, so carry more than you think you need and refill wherever your guide points out a safe source. The trail ends near the upper levels of Kawasan Falls, a welcome place to cool off before the final walk down to Matutinao and the main road, where buses run back north along the west coast to Cebu City.


Some people choose to camp near the summit and start the traverse at first light. The campsite can fill with thick fog, so pitch a sturdy tent and keep your gear dry.


Shorter Ways to Do Both


If a full day of walking is too much, you can still see both places in one day. A habal-habal from Osmeña Peak to Kawasan takes about two hours and costs around ₱1,000 per motorbike for two passengers. Many tour operators also offer packages that combine the summit with canyoneering at Kawasan.


For the water side of the trip, our Kawasan Falls canyoneering guide covers the jumps, the guides and the gear. If you would rather keep driving south, the south Cebu waterfalls road trip lists more stops nearby.


When to Go


December to May is the most dependable period, with drier trails and a better chance of clear views. August and September are the hardest months, with muddy paths and frequent cloud. Weekdays, and especially early weekday mornings, are much quieter than weekends.


Check the forecast the night before. After heavy rain, the descent to Kawasan can turn slippery and streams along the way can rise, so it is better to wait a day than push on.


What to Pack


Wear shoes with good grip; the limestone is sharp and the descent is long. Bring at least two litres of water per person for the traverse, plus snacks, a hat, sun protection and a light jacket for the cool summit air. A dry bag keeps your phone safe near the water, and swimwear is worth packing for the pools at the end.


Mobile signal is weak in the mountains, so download an offline map before you leave and tell someone your plans. Carry cash in small notes for fees, food and transport back.


A Full Day From Peak to Pool


The traverse packs much of southern Cebu into a single walk: highland farms, a sharp summit, quiet villages and one of the island's most famous waterfalls at the end. It asks for an early start, a guide and steady legs, and it gives back a very different view of Cebu from the one most visitors take home.
