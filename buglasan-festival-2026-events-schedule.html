Buglasan Festival 2026: Events, Schedule and What to Expect

Ren Calago 11 October 2026 Updated 18 October 20260

Updated 18 October 2026

The 2026 festival winners are now listed below. The schedule and venues are unchanged.

Buglasan is Negros Oriental's flagship cultural festival, and in 2026 it runs for eleven days, from 15 to 25 October, with most of the action centred on Dumaguete City. The organisers have given this year's festival the theme "Different Stories. One Aspiration. One Negros Oriental," and the programme is packed: parades, street dances, pageants, music, food contests and a fireworks finale. This guide walks through the main events, what each part of the festival feels like, and a few practical habits that will make your visit easier.

Key Dates at a Glance


The festival opens on Thursday, 15 October, with the ribbon-cutting for the LGU booths fair at Freedom Park. The biggest street events fall on the first weekend, the nights of 16 to 18 October carry the parades and competitions, and the programme winds down with the closing ceremonies on Sunday, 25 October.

  • 15 to 25 October: the main festival period
  • 16 October: Civic-Military Parade and the Buglasan Festival of Festivals street dancing
  • 17 October: Parade of Lights and the Pyromusical competition
  • 25 October: closing ceremonies, the ABS-CBN Kapamilya Caravan and fireworks

Times and venues come from the published programme, which the organisers say may change. Check the latest version before you plan your day.


The Opening Days


The first few days are a good time to take in the local side of the festival. On Thursday, the LGU booths fair opens at Freedom Park, and each booth shows the tourism spots, products and traditions of its town or city. Judging of the booths runs through the festival, with the final judging on 24 October, so the displays are worth a second look later in the week.


Friday, 16 October, is the busiest day. It starts early with the Buglasan Painitan, a communal meal at Pantawan and Dumaguete Boulevard at 5:30 in the morning, followed by the Civic-Military Parade at 8:00 with a decorated umbrellas competition. The Lechonfest competition at Freedom Park begins at 10:00, and the Festival of Festivals street dancing takes over the streets of Dumaguete at 3:00 in the afternoon. In the evening, the field presentation and showdown competition moves to the Gov. Mariano F. Perdices Memorial Coliseum.


Arrive early for the street dancing. The crowds gather quickly along the route, and the best spots near the start fill up well before the first contingent appears.


Parades, Street Dances and Lights


The street events are the heart of the festival, and they are where the energy of the whole province shows. The Festival of Festivals brings performances from towns and cities across Negros Oriental, each presenting its own signature celebration. The dancers wear bright costumes, the drums carry down the street, and the contingents move from one stage to the next.


Saturday, 17 October, brings the Parade of Lights. Floats are judged as they travel from the Provincial Capitol to Dumaguete Boulevard, and the evening ends with the Buglasan Pyromusical competition at the boulevard, where fireworks are set to music. The parade is also a good chance to see the illuminated floats up close before the crowds press in.


Because parade routes and closures can change each year, check with the organisers or the local government before you travel, and allow extra time to walk between venues. Parking in the city centre can be difficult during the parades, so a tricycle or a short walk from your hotel is often the simplest choice.


Music, Pageants and Performances


Music runs through the whole festival, and the evening programme at Freedom Park gives you plenty to choose from. The Battle of the Bands takes place on 18 October, followed by the Singing Idol competition on 19 October and the Vocal Duet competition on 20 October. The Balitaw competition on 20 October is a chance to hear the traditional courtship songs of the province performed live.


The pageants are also a major part of the schedule. The Gandang NegOrense 2026 grand competition takes place on 18 October, and the Hara sa Negros Oriental grand competition is on 23 October at the Lamberto L. Macias Sports and Cultural Center. Dance lovers can look forward to the Battle of Dance Supreme on the opening night and the Buglasan Dancesport Competition at Freedom Park on 22 October. The Buglasan Music Fest at Pantawan closes the music programme on 24 October.


Food, Farms and Fairs


Food is one of the easiest ways to feel part of the celebration. The Lechonfest competition on 16 October brings roast pig from competing teams to Freedom Park, and the Lutong Ato competition on 23 October, titled "The NegOrense Flavors," celebrates local cooking. If you want to shop as well as eat, the Negros Oriental Buglasan Trade Fair at Robinsons Dumaguete runs from 16 to 22 October and showcases local products.


The agricultural side of the festival is covered by the Buglasan Family Farm Congress at Quezon Park on 22 and 23 October, which runs from 9:00 in the morning to 6:00 in the evening. It is a quieter part of the programme and a useful stop if you are interested in local farming and produce.


Sports and Youth Events


The festival also has a strong sporting and youth programme. The Buglasan Marching Band competition takes place on 17 October at the Lamberto Macias Sports and Cultural Center, and the boxing competition runs at Pantawan and Dumaguete Boulevard on 17 and 18 October. Cyclists can watch the push bike competition on Dumaguete Boulevard on 24 October, and the bodybuilding competition follows the same afternoon. On 21 October, the Inter-School Tourism and Hospitality Skills Competition at the Negros Oriental Convention Center brings students together to show off skills such as tour guiding, cake decorating and cooking.


The Closing Days


The final weekend is a relaxed way to end the festival. The closing ceremonies, awarding and appreciation luncheon begin at 11:00 on Sunday, 25 October, at the Plenary Hall of the Negros Oriental Convention Center. In the evening, the ABS-CBN Kapamilya Caravan performs at Freedom Park at 8:00, and the night ends with a fireworks display at 10:00 on the Provincial Capitol grounds.


If you only have one evening to spare, make it the last one. The crowds are big, but the atmosphere is warm, and the fireworks are a fitting finish to eleven days of celebration.


Getting Ready for the Festival


October is still part of the rainy season in Negros Oriental, so pack a light rain jacket or a compact umbrella, and wear shoes you can walk in for hours. Carry some cash, because food stalls and small vendors often prefer it, and keep a power bank charged for your phone. Dumaguete's streets get busy in the evenings, so keep your bag in front of you and your valuables close.


Book your accommodation early if you plan to stay over, especially for the first weekend. Freedom Park and Dumaguete Boulevard are the central hubs, so a hotel nearby saves a lot of walking and taxi waiting. Check the official programme on the day you plan to attend, since times and venues can change at short notice.


Who Buglasan Suits


Buglasan suits anyone who enjoys a lively celebration. Families will find the lights, the food and the afternoon events easy to enjoy. Music lovers can follow the competitions from one night to the next, and photographers will find the costumes, the floats and the fireworks hard to resist. Quieter travellers can still enjoy the trade fair, the farm congress and the booths, which are calmer than the evening events.


Whatever you choose, plan a few must-see events rather than trying to attend everything. Eleven days is a long festival, and the best memories often come from one good parade, one shared meal or one song you did not expect to love.
