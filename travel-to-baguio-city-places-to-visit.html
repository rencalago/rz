5-Day Baguio Itinerary With a Toddler: Places We Visited

Ren Calago 12 Sep 2024 Updated 22 October 2026

Baguio is one of our favourite cities, so when our son Enzo turned three, we went up the mountain to celebrate. The whole trip ran nine days: four in Metro Manila and five in Baguio. We planned every stop in advance on TripIt, from meals to sights, and managed about 85 percent of the list.


This page is that plan, cleaned up so you can borrow it. You will find our day-by-day itinerary below, followed by what each place is, current entrance fees, and the small adjustments that made five days in Baguio work with a toddler in tow.

Getting to Baguio and Getting Around


We rode up on Joybus, the executive coach service of Genesis Transport, from its Pasay terminal. Both of its classes have an onboard restroom, which matters more than legroom when your seatmate is three. Expect roughly six to seven hours for a daytime trip and a bit less at night. Our 7 AM departure had us checking in at the hotel by 3 PM. For the ride home we took Victory Liner. Buses on this route can sell out on weekends and long holidays, so book your seats early for those dates.


Our base was Travelite Express Hotel, and we moved around by taxi. Baguio's centre is compact but steep, and with a small child, short taxi hops beat walking uphill. For two meals we simply ordered in through GrabFood.


The Places on Our List and What to Expect


Fees and hours below were current as of October 2026. They change, so confirm at the gate before you go.


Baguio Cathedral


Our Lady of the Atonement Cathedral sits on a hill above Session Road, and it bookended our trip: Mass on the day we arrived and again on Enzo's birthday. Entry is free. If you only want to look around, time your visit between Masses; the schedule is posted on the cathedral's Facebook page.


Wright Park and The Mansion


These two face each other across a long reflecting pool, which makes them an easy pair. Wright Park is open around the clock and free, and the horses at the stables are popular with kids. Across the road, The Mansion, the former residence of the American governors-general, now houses the Presidential Museum. It opens Tuesday to Sunday from 9 AM to 6 PM, closed on Mondays, and admission is free.


Mines View Park and Good Shepherd Convent


Mines View Park looks out over the hills and old mining areas of Itogon. It opens from 6 AM to 8 PM, with entry at ₱20 for adults, ₱10 for children aged 3 to 12, and free for kids two and under. Arrive early, before the tour vans, for a clearer view. A short ride away, the Good Shepherd Convent is best known for its ube jam, sold alongside strawberry jam, peanut brittle and cookies made by its training centre. Lines can be long in peak season, and buying here is cheaper than from resellers in town.


Baguio Botanical Garden


The garden is laced with short walking paths and has an Igorot village display, so it is easy to cover at a child's pace. It is open daily from 7 AM to 7 PM. Entry is ₱110 for adults and ₱27.50 for children 12 and under.


Camp John Hay and the Bell Amphitheater


We planned an unhurried morning at Camp John Hay: pancakes, coffee, a picnic under the pines and a walk to the Bell Amphitheater. The Bell House grounds open daily from 9 AM to 5 PM. Adults pay ₱85, while students, seniors and PWDs pay ₱40, and children under four feet tall enter free. If you want more pine forest, the free Yellow Trail nearby runs about 1.5 kilometres. For how the camp feels on a quiet weekday, see our piece on whether Baguio is quieter and cheaper right now.


Igorot Stone Kingdom


This cultural park celebrates Igorot heritage through stonework, with castle-like walls and terraces modelled on the rice fields of the Cordillera. It is a newer attraction than most on this list and very photogenic. Hours are 7 AM to 6 PM daily. Regular admission is ₱130, children aged 4 to 12, seniors and PWDs pay ₱100, and kids three and under go free. Good shoes help on the stone paths.


La Trinidad Strawberry Farm


Just outside Baguio in La Trinidad, Benguet, the strawberry farm run by Benguet State University is free to enter; picking and anything you buy are paid separately. The picking season usually runs from November to May, so outside those months expect to shop for fruit and treats rather than pick your own. We had strawberry taho and strawberry ice cream on the spot. Go early, as we did at 8 AM, before the heat and the buses arrive.


Colors of StoBoSa


Also in La Trinidad, this is a hillside of roughly 150 to 200 homes painted as one giant mural. The name combines three sitios in Barangay Balili: Stonehill, Botiwtiw and Sadjap. Residents and volunteers painted it in 2016 with sunflower and rainbow designs. It costs nothing to see and pairs naturally with the strawberry farm on the same morning.


Burnham Park, the Night Market and Baguio Country Club


Burnham Park is the city's central park, free and open all day, with a lake where you can rent boats. We went twice: once at dusk, and again late on Enzo's birthday for a night boat ride and a stroll. The night market on Harrison Road, right beside the park, runs from about 9 PM to 2 AM and is the place for bargain clothes and street food. Earlier that evening we took the kids to the bubble park at Baguio Country Club, part of its holiday attractions. Seasonal setups like this change each year, so check what is on before you go.


Tips for Doing Baguio With a Toddler


Start early, stop by lunch. Every busy day on our plan began between 6:30 and 8:30 AM and ended at the hotel by mid-afternoon. Mornings are cooler, crowds are thinner, and a child who naps in the afternoon has energy for an evening outing.


Pair nearby stops. Wright Park, The Mansion, Mines View and Good Shepherd sit close together, as do the strawberry farm and StoBoSa. Grouping them cut our taxi time and kept each day to one area.


Pack for cold. Baguio sits far higher than Manila and nights can be chilly, especially from December to February. Bring a jacket for everyone, and a blanket for the bus.


Leave slack. We finished about 85 percent of our list, and the rest is saved for next time. Keep a few stops optional so a tired afternoon doesn't sink the whole plan.


Planning Your Own Trip


Five days was enough to cover the classics at a toddler's pace, with time to rest. If you can choose your dates, think about crowds: February brings the flower festival and some of the busiest weekends of the year, so read our guide to Panagbenga in February before booking then. With extra days to spare, you can continue north into the mountains; our Sagada caves and hanging coffins guide covers what to know.
