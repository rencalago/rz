Lake Sebu Guide: T'boli Culture, Falls and Zipline

Ren Calago 22 October 2026

Lake Sebu is a highland town in South Cotabato where three lakes, a chain of waterfalls and one of Mindanao's best-known weaving traditions sit within a short motorcycle ride of each other. It is the home of the T'boli, and most of what makes the trip worth the airfare comes from that: t'nalak cloth, brass gongs, beaded crowns and boatmen who paddle dugout canoes standing up.


For a Cebu traveler, this is a two-night trip, not a day trip. You fly to General Santos, ride a van uphill, and spend the next day on the water, at the weaving houses and over the falls on a zipline. Nothing in the Visayas feels quite like it, which is the point.


Below: how to get there from Cebu, what each sight offers, rough costs, and when to go. Prices here are budget ranges that differ between operators and seasons, so confirm the amount at the gate before you pay.




How to Get to Lake Sebu from Cebu


There is no practical sea or road link, so you fly. Cebu Pacific and Philippine Airlines have both operated nonstop Cebu to General Santos (GES) flights of roughly 1 hour 25 minutes. Schedules and fares change, so check both airlines before you book, and take the morning departure if you can, because the land leg is the part that runs out of daylight.


From the airport, a multicab or tricycle takes you to Bulaong Terminal in General Santos. Vans to Lake Sebu leave from there, usually for PHP 150 to PHP 200 and about two hours on the road. They depart only when full, so the wait can run from a few minutes to a couple of hours, and the last direct vans can leave early in the afternoon.


The backup is the relay: a Yellow Bus from Bulaong to Marbel (Koronadal City), then a bus to Surallah, then a jeepney or habal-habal up to Lake Sebu. It costs a similar amount in total but takes longer, and public transport thins out in the late afternoon. If you land after midday, sleep in General Santos and go up in the morning. Coming from Davao instead, buses from Ecoland Terminal to Marbel take around four hours before the same last leg. Our Davao travel guide covers that side of Mindanao.


The Lakes at Dawn


Lake Sebu is the largest of the town's three lakes, covering about 354 hectares, with Lake Seloton and Lake Lahit nearby. The classic early-morning activity is a ride in an owong, the narrow dugout canoe, to see lotus flowers open around sunrise. A boatman can meet you at the shore around 6 AM, and it is best to arrange the ride the day before through your resort. A lotus boat has run about PHP 300 for two or three people, so agree on the price first.


Be realistic about the bloom: it is a morning sight, it depends on the day and season, and it is not a staged garden. Come for the quiet light on the water and treat the flowers as the bonus. Resorts around the lake, including Punta Isla, Sunrise Garden and El Galong, put rooms from roughly PHP 1,000 to PHP 1,200 for two, and basic dorm-style beds cost much less. Grilled tilapia from the lake is the standard dinner, and it pairs well with an early night.


T'boli Culture: T'nalak and the Dreamweavers


T'nalak is a cloth woven from abaca fiber, dyed with natural pigments, and patterned with designs weavers say come to them in dreams. The best-known name is Lang Dulay (1928 to 2015), who was named a National Living Treasure in 1998 for keeping the traditional patterns alive. Her weaving center stands at her family home in Lamdalag, Sitio Tbong, and the province has helped fund its upkeep. The family and weavers there continue her work.


Plan a morning for culture. Visit the Lang Dulay center and the School of Living Traditions, where you can see abaca being prepared and cloth taking shape on the loom, and add the T'boli Museum for brass instruments, beadwork and ceremonial dress. Bring small bills for entry fees and donations. Ask before photographing weavers, and do not touch cloth that is still on the loom. If you want to buy t'nalak, buy directly from the weaver, because price depends on size and pattern, and a full panel is a real investment.


Two festivals are worth knowing. The Helobung Festival is Lake Sebu's own T'boli celebration, and recent editions have run around late May to June, so confirm the date with the municipal tourism office before building a trip around it. The T'nalak Festival, held in July, takes place in Koronadal City, the provincial capital, rather than in Lake Sebu.


Seven Falls and the Zipline


The Hikong falls run in a chain through the hills, and only the first two are easy for casual visitors. Hikong Alo (Falls 1) is reached by habal-habal or a long staircase. Hikong Bente (Falls 2) is a short walk down from the entrance gate. Falls 3 to 7 are trekking territory and need a local guide, so budget a half day and sturdy shoes. The entrance fee has been around PHP 20.


The zipline is the main draw for most Cebuanos. It has two lines across the gorge, a long one of about 700 to 750 meters and a shorter return of about 400 meters, with views over several falls on the way. Operators describe it as among the highest in Southeast Asia, at around 600 feet. The fee has been quoted anywhere from PHP 250 on weekdays to PHP 350 on busy days, with photo packages at PHP 100 to PHP 200 extra, so carry a bit of margin. The province funded an upgrade to the Falls 5 landing station in 2025 aimed at safety and capacity. Weather can still delay rides, so keep your morning flexible. If you liked the Mindanao-style thrill, compare it with the Dahilayan Adventure Park guide.


When to Go and What to Pack


The town is cool year-round compared with the lowlands. February to April is generally the clearest stretch for views and dry steps at the falls. In wetter months the stairs get slick, the trek to the lower falls turns muddy, and the zipline can pause. Bring a light jacket for evenings, shoes with grip, and cash for boats, guides and small fees, since resort payment options vary. Mobile signal is generally workable in town.


A Two-Night Plan and Whether It Is Worth It


Day one: morning flight to General Santos, van to Lake Sebu by afternoon, sunset by the lake and tilapia for dinner. Day two: lotus boat at dawn, weavers and the museum by mid-morning, Falls 1 and 2 and the zipline after lunch. Day three: a morning van back and an afternoon flight home. A shared room and local transport, without flights, usually keep this to the low thousands of pesos per person, and our Philippines travel budget guide helps with the airfare side.


If your idea of a long weekend is a beach, Bohol and Negros are far closer. If you want cool mountain air, living textile traditions and a zipline that crosses waterfalls, Lake Sebu earns the plane ride and the extra hours on the road.
