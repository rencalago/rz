BGC and Makati for First-Timers: Stay, Eat, Get Around

Ren Calago 20 October 2026

Metro Manila has a reputation for traffic, heat and confusing commutes, and first-time visitors often brace for the worst. Two districts soften the landing: Bonifacio Global City (BGC) in Taguig and the Makati Central Business District. They sit roughly 4 to 6 kilometres apart, both have proper sidewalks, both run on ride-hailing apps, and both let you eat, shop and see a museum without learning the jeepney routes on day one.


The two areas have different personalities. BGC is the newer one, a planned grid of glass towers, numbered streets, murals and open plazas that feels designed for walking. Makati is older and leafier, built around Ayala Avenue, with mature trees, weekend food markets, a serious history museum and the Poblacion bar district on its edge.


This guide covers getting in from NAIA, what each district does best, where to eat at different budgets, how to move between them, what a day costs, and which one to pick as a base if you only have a weekend.




Getting from NAIA to BGC or Makati


Domestic flights land at Ninoy Aquino International Airport, and the terminal depends on your airline, so check the booking before you fly. Budget 20 to 45 minutes from the gate to the arrivals curb once bags are collected. The airport has free Wi-Fi, which matters more than it sounds: book your car while still inside, because signal can be patchy in the pickup bays.


There are four realistic ways into the business districts.


Grab car


The simplest option for a first visit. Off-peak, expect roughly PHP 250 to 450 to BGC and PHP 200 to 350 to Makati, with fares climbing during the 5 PM to 8 PM rush. Travel time swings from about 20 minutes on a clear road to well over an hour on a Friday evening. Type the building name, not just the street, so the driver finds the right drop-off.


Yellow airport taxi


The yellow metered taxis queue at arrivals and give receipts. A ride to Makati or BGC typically lands in the PHP 350 to 550 range depending on traffic. Skip anyone in the arrivals hall offering a flat price in an unmarked car.


UBE Express bus


The cheapest air-conditioned direct ride. UBE Express runs from the terminals to stops near Glorietta in Makati and Market! Market! in BGC, at around PHP 150 to 200 per person, paid to the conductor. It runs roughly from early morning until about 10 PM, with gaps of 30 to 60 minutes between trips, so it suits a light traveller who isn't in a hurry. Check the current schedule before you count on it for a late arrival.


Taxi plus BGC Bus


A taxi to a bus terminal, then a BGC Bus to your hotel, saves a little money and costs a lot of patience. Most first-timers are better off on Grab.


If you arrive in the evening rush and the app shows surge pricing, waiting 15 minutes inside the terminal with a coffee often brings the fare back down.


What BGC is good for


BGC was a military camp before it became a planned city, which explains the straight streets and generous sidewalks. It is one of the few places in Metro Manila where an evening walk feels like the main event rather than a way to get somewhere.


Bonifacio High Street and Central Square form the spine of the district: a long open-air strip of shops and cafés with lawns down the middle. Early morning, before about 8 AM, it belongs to joggers and dog walkers. After dark it fills with families and office workers, and the murals around the side streets change from time to time, so it rewards a slow loop.


The Mind Museum near JY Campos Park is BGC's big indoor attraction, with galleries on the atom, life, earth, the universe and technology. Listed admission is around PHP 750 for a timed visit, with cheaper promo tickets sometimes sold online. It has historically closed on Mondays and Tuesdays, so check the opening days and book ahead, as walk-in queues can be long on weekends.


Manila American Cemetery and Memorial sits just outside the commercial core. Entry is free, the grounds are calm and immaculate, and morning is the best time before the heat builds.


For errands and air-con breaks, SM Aura, Uptown Mall and Market! Market! are all a short ride apart. Market! Market! is where you go for groceries, bottled water and food-court meals at local prices, typically PHP 150 to 300 for tapsilog or roast chicken with rice.


On High Street, casual café and restaurant mains usually run PHP 350 to 650. At the top end, BGC has tasting-menu restaurants where dinner easily passes PHP 1,500 per person, so check menus online before booking.


What Makati is good for


Makati feels more lived-in. The office towers are older, the trees are bigger, and the district has more to do on a weekend morning.


Ayala Triangle Gardens is a free park wedged between Ayala Avenue, Makati Avenue and Paseo de Roxas. It's pleasant for an early walk any time of year and gets its own light show during the Christmas season, usually starting around mid-November.


Ayala Museum, beside Greenbelt, is the strongest reason to give Makati a full day. Its collection of pre-colonial gold and its historical dioramas are a good crash course in the country's past, and two hours inside also gets you out of the midday sun. Ticket prices vary by exhibit, roughly PHP 300 to 650 for adults, so check the current rate on the museum's page.


Salcedo Saturday Market at Jaime Velasquez Park and Legazpi Sunday Market at Legazpi Park both run from about 7 AM to 2 PM. These are the most fun meals in the district: grilled meats, bagnet, fresh lumpia, paella, kakanin, vegan bowls and cold brew, mostly in the PHP 100 to 300 range per dish. Bring small bills and a reusable bag, and go early before the best stalls sell out.


Greenbelt and Glorietta are linked by covered walkways, which makes a rainy afternoon easy. Inside Landmark, the food centre is one of the cheaper sit-down options in the CBD.


Poblacion, a short ride from the CBD, is where Makati loosens up at night, with bars, small restaurants and rooftop spots packed into narrow streets. It's livelier and grittier than BGC, so stick to busy streets and book a Grab home.


Moving between the two


Off-peak, a Grab between BGC and the Makati CBD usually costs PHP 120 to 230 and takes 15 to 30 minutes. In rush hour the same trip can triple in time.


The BGC Bus is useful for short hops inside BGC and for the link toward Ayala, with fares in the PHP 15 to 40 range. It gets crowded around office hours and has no luggage space. The MRT-3 Ayala station connects toward Glorietta, which works for a light day bag but not for suitcases or the 7 to 9 AM crush.


Walking works well inside each district. Use marked crossings and expect turning cars to push their luck.


A realistic daily budget


Leaving aside your flight and hotel, a careful traveller eating at markets and food courts and taking one or two rides can get by on about PHP 1,200 to 2,000 a day. Add café lunches, a museum and a sit-down dinner, and PHP 2,500 to 4,000 is more comfortable.


Hotel prices in both districts range from around PHP 2,500 a night for mid-range business hotels to well past PHP 6,000 at the luxury end, with serviced condos somewhere in between. Rates jump during holidays and big conventions, so book early. Weather, safety and small things that help


November to April is the drier half of the year, and December to February is the most comfortable for walking. March to May is hot, so plan parks for the early morning. From June to October, afternoon downpours are common and traffic gets worse, though malls and museums keep a trip workable.


Both districts have visible private security and are generally comfortable for visitors, but ordinary city sense applies. Keep your phone in a front pocket at the markets, stay on lit main roads late at night, and use the app rather than hailing cabs on EDSA.


Neat casual clothes are fine almost everywhere, though a few upscale restaurants turn away slippers and sleeveless shirts. Download offline maps for both districts before you leave the airport. Which one should you stay in?


For a short first visit, base yourself in BGC. The walkability, newer hotels and easy evenings make it the gentler introduction, and Makati is close enough for a day out.


A good weekend shape: arrive Friday, walk High Street that evening, spend Saturday morning at Salcedo Market, then Ayala Triangle and Ayala Museum, and head back to BGC before the late-afternoon traffic. Use Sunday for the Mind Museum or the Legazpi market before your flight.
