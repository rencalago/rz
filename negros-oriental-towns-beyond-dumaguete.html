Beyond Dumaguete: 5 Negros Oriental Towns Worth the Detour

Most people who come to Negros Oriental spend their days in Dumaguete, and that makes sense. The city has the restaurants, the guesthouses and the bus connections. But the province stretches well beyond its capital, and a handful of towns within a few hours of the city offer waterfalls, a forest lake, a river that locals swim in and a highland ridge with wide views.


This guide covers five of them: Bayawan, Siaton, Guihulngan, Pamplona and Canlaon. For each one, it lists the access details we could confirm, along with the parts that are still unclear. Travel times and fares are approximate and change with the season and the bus schedule, so treat them as a starting point and check locally before you travel.


Planning a day trip into the hills or along the south coast? Our 5 places near Dumaguete worth the drive covers the nearer options, so this guide focuses on the places further out.


Bayawan: Waterfalls Beside the Highway


Bayawan City sits on the southwest coast, roughly two to three hours from Dumaguete by Ceres bus. Several Ceres lines serve the route, and the Ceres terminal in Bayawan is the place to ask about onward connections.


The main reason to come is Niludhan Falls. The entrance sits right beside the highway, and a short flight of steps leads down to the water. Entry is free, and the site has benches, toilets and a washing area. There are no shops nearby, so bring water and food.


Getting there takes a little planning. From Bayawan, one option is a habal-habal (motorcycle taxi) to the barangay of Dawis, which takes about an hour. Another is a Ceres bus north toward Mabinay, followed by a motorcycle ride. Fares reported online vary widely. Round-trip motorcycle costs are quoted at ₱500 to ₱700, but it is not always clear which leg that covers, so agree on the price before you set off.


The falls look their best in the rainy months, which runs roughly from June to November, when a heavy downpour can make the flow dramatic. In drier weeks the water runs thinner, but on a clear morning the spray often catches small rainbows.


Siaton: A Forest Lake That Needs a Guide


Siaton lies toward the southern tip of the island. Its best-known spot is Lake Balanan, a lake at about 285 metres above sea level in Sitio Balanan. It is described as a lake of around 62 acres, surrounded by forest, waterfalls and mountains.


The route uses a van and a boat, so most visitors go on a guided day tour. Operator listings put the drive from Dumaguete at roughly an hour and a half. From there, a van and then a banca (outrigger boat) across to the lake's northern side. The full day runs five to six hours and includes a trek of about four kilometres under forest canopy, with views of three waterfalls on the far shore.


One operator lists a price of US$197 per group, with entrance fees, lunch, bottled water and a guide included. The operator offers free cancellation if you cancel at least 24 hours before the start time. Prices change, so confirm the current rate directly with the operator.


Opening times and the best season are not clear from the information available. Before you book, ask the operator how conditions look after heavy rain, since both the boat crossing and the trail depend on the weather.


Costs also drop quickly as the group grows. A party of four or more spreads the per-person rate far better than two people sharing a single day.


Guihulngan: A River Beside the Road


Guihulngan City is in the north of the province. From Dumaguete, an air-conditioned bus takes about three and a half hours, while a V-hire van takes about three. Once you reach the city, the route into the highlands is the next leg.


The spot to aim for is the Kansalakan River, which runs in a village between Banwague and Balogo. It is roughly 137 km from Dumaguete, and the route to it is more about the road than the walk. The river runs close to the main road, so you can reach the water without a trek. Locals use the pools for bathing, and the rocky cliffs are a popular spot for jumping.


The same journey can take in Hinakpan Mystical Hills, a group of conical hills that is often paired with the river. From Guihulngan town, the leg to Hinakpan takes about 45 minutes by motorcycle.


We found no published safety guidance for the river, and little on how the water changes after rain. Ask people in the village before you jump, and skip the cliffs if the water looks high or brown.


Pamplona: The Highland Town With a Ridge to Climb


Pamplona lies about 37 km northwest of Dumaguete. It is a farming town of around 41,000 people, spread across 16 barangays, and its Sakada farms are a draw for food lovers. We covered that side in our guide to going to Sakada to eat, so the rest of this section looks at the parts that get less attention.



Café Alicia, pictured above, is a café in the town of Pamplona, open from 6 am to 8 pm.


Agriculture is the town's main livelihood. Coconuts, sugar and bananas fill the fields, with corn, rice and root crops grown alongside, and many households also keep cattle, pigs, chickens and goats. Driving through, you get a direct view of how the province makes its living.


Nabalabag Ridge, at about 800 metres in barangay San Isidro, is the town's best-known viewpoint. It looks out over the neighbouring towns and the plains below. The walk up and the road conditions were not described in detail, so ask a local about a habal-habal to the ridge, and whether the road holds up in the rainy season.


The town also hosts the Yamog Festival every 12 October. If you plan a visit around it, check the programme with the municipal office in the weeks before, because dates and events can shift.


Canlaon: The Town on the Volcano's Slopes


Canlaon City occupies the northwest tip of the province, in highland country on the slopes of Mount Kanlaon, the highest peak on Negros island.


Mount Kanlaon is an active volcano, and its alert level has changed several times in recent years. The most recent report we could find, from June 2026, put it at Alert Level 2. Check the current level with Phivolcs before you go, and follow any access limits they set for areas near the crater.


When the volcano is calm, the Mount Kanlaon Natural Park is the usual draw, with hiking and sunrise views as the main reasons to visit. Quipot Falls is also listed among the town's waterfalls, but we could not confirm its access route, fees or hours. Ask the Canlaon tourism office before you plan a visit.


Planning Around the Distances


These five towns lie in different directions, so pick one or two for each day rather than trying to chain them together. Bayawan and Siaton are both in the south of the province. Guihulngan lies north, and Pamplona and Canlaon are both northwest of Dumaguete.


Bring small notes for fares and habal-habal rides, since roadside stalls and motorcycle drivers rarely have change for large bills. Carry water and sun protection at every stop, and download offline maps before you leave Dumaguete, because signal can be weak in the hills.


If you want a lake that sits closer to the city, our Balinsasayao Twin Lakes guide covers the nearer option. For the nearer waterfall stops, the 5 places near Dumaguete worth the drive list is a good starting point.


Before You Go


Check the road and weather conditions before you set out for the highlands, and confirm any tour booking directly with the operator. Prices, bus times and the status of falls and trails can change from one month to the next, so a short call or message the day before will save you a wasted trip.
